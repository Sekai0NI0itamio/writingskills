# General: How an Idea Moves Through a Paragraph

A paragraph is a **dependency chain**: each move hands the next a handle. Remove a move and the next becomes unjustified. The patterns below are replicable skeletons — same shape, different content.

---

## 1. Biology Reasoning Flow — "finish prior mechanism → named substitute → chemistry → re-pair → hypothesis → IV/DV"

### Move-by-move (P1–P4)
- **P1 — finish prior mechanism → flip condition → name substitute.** Anchor end of prior mechanism (carry-over). Flip with *"whereas the inverse will…"* — same actors, swapped ratio, opposite organ. Name the test substance by role: *"X is a Y that can act as a substitute for Z."*
- **P2 — chemistry → natural source → functional parallel → additive.** (a) physical state + solubility; (b) quoted chemical spec with a *number* (12-carbon, 80%, MW); (c) source, dominant source last ("mainly…"); (d) *"Like [standard], [test] can [verb] to [outcome]."*; (e) *"It also [verb₂] and [verb₃]."*
- **P3 — hypothesis** (single comparative sentence).
- **P4 — variables.** IV by role (*"the variable that is being adjusted and tested is…"*); DV by measurement + anatomical landmark (*"the [unit] of the [specimen] from [landmark]…"*).

Each move unlocks the next: unfinished mechanism → unmotivated substitute; no chemistry → unjustified solubility; no source → equivalence reads thin; no re-pairing → hypothesis has no mechanism; no second effect → hypothesis only predicts "some" not "faster."

### Skeleton A — finish prior mechanism + name substitute
> "[End of prior mechanism]. [whereas/in contrast] the inverse will [opposite effect]. [Test substance] is a [category] that can act as a substitute for [standard agent] and have similar effects ([citation])."
- **Slots:** (1) restated outcome of prior mechanism; (2) flip clause with swapped condition; (3) identity + equivalence claim.
- **Original:** "…stabilizing the molecules in the leaf… whereas the inverse will promote the development of the shoot (Britannica). Lauric acid is a type of saturated fatty acid that can act as a substitute for BAP and have similar effects."
- **Demo:** "Stabilising Rubisco activase under heat stress, whereas prolonged exposure accelerates leaf senescence. Salicylic acid is a plant phenolic that can act as a substitute for methyl jasmonate and trigger comparable defence responses."

### Skeleton B — chemistry + source + functional parallel + additive
> "[Test substance] is [state] at [condition] but is [solubility property]. 'The molecule is [chemical description with quantitative specifier]' and can be found in [biological sources], mainly [dominant source] ([citation]). Like [standard agent], [test substance] can [mechanism verb] to [outcome]. It also [verb₂] and [verb₃]."
- **Slots:** (1) physical-state sentence; (2) quoted chemical spec with number; (3) source clause ending on dominant source; (4) "Like X, Y can…" functional parallel; (5) "It also…and…" additive.
- **Original:** "Lauric acid is a solid at room temperature but is very soluble in boiling water. 'The molecule is a saturated medium-chain fatty acid with a 12-carbon backbone' and can be found in plant and animal oils, mainly coconut and palm kernel oil (NCI). Like BAP, lauric acid can react alongside auxins to impede early stage senescence. It also boosts meristematic tissue growth and promotes development."
- **Demo:** "Chitosan is a powder at room temperature but dissolves readily in dilute acetic acid. 'The polymer is a linear β-1,4-linked glucosamine with ~80% deacetylation' and can be found in crustacean shells and fungal cell walls, mainly shellfish waste (PubChem). Like methyl jasmonate, chitosan can prime salicylic-acid signalling to reduce lesion size. It also elevates chitinase activity and reinforces the cuticle."

### Skeleton C — directional hypothesis + IV/DV
> **HYPOTHESIS:** The [treatments] with the [comparative] amounts of [IV] will result in [comparative] [DV].
> **IV:** The variable that is being adjusted and tested is the [IV quantity] being put into the [system].
> **DV:** The [unit] of the [specimen] from [anatomical landmark]…
- **Original:** "The solutions with the higher amounts of lauric acid will result in faster germination and development. IV: …the concentration of lauric acid being put into the soil. DV: The length of the plant specimen from apical…"
- **Demo:** "The agar plates with the higher concentrations of NaCl will result in shorter radicle extension. IV: …the molarity of NaCl being dissolved into the agar. DV: The length of the radicle from the seed coat to the root cap."

### Replication steps
1. Finish the established mechanism including the *opposite* condition; use *"whereas the inverse will…"* to flip the ratio — gives the substitute something to substitute *for*.
2. Define-by-role: *"X is a [category] that can act as a substitute for Y."* Weak verbs ("resembles") don't commit to equivalence.
3. Cite parenthetically, end of clause (not footnote).
4. Chemistry = state + solubility, then quoted spec with a *number* — the number makes it measured, not vague.
5. Natural source, dominant source last ("mainly…").
6. Re-tell original pairing in substitute terms: *"Like [standard], [test] can [verb] to [outcome]."* — the load-bearing sentence.
7. Stack a second benefit with *"It also…"* — one effect only makes the hypothesis fragile.
8. Hypothesis = one comparative sentence, committed direction (no hedging).
9. IV by *function*; DV by *unit + anatomical landmark* — the "from [landmark]" forces a reproducible protocol.

### Vocabulary (biology reasoning)
- **Cause/consequence:** "give rise to."
- **Contrast/concession:** "whereas the inverse will…" (the flip-the-ratio connector).
- **Equivalence:** "Like [X],"; "act as a substitute for" (load-bearing, not "is similar to").
- **Identity:** "is a [category]" (definition-by-role).
- **Occurrence:** "can be found in…, mainly…".
- **Mechanism verbs:** "can react alongside," "impede," "boost," "promote."
- **Stacking addition:** "It also…" (second effect without breaking rhythm).
- **Evidence:** parenthetical citation at clause end ("(NCI)").

## 2. Worked-Calculation Flow — "dependency-chain calculation"

Each move **unlocks the next computation**. Sequence: section label → claim → headline formula + symbol definitions → subset selected → stage signpost → formula restated with one symbol plugged → variable definitions for remaining symbols → algebraic-move verb sentence → simplified form → term-by-term expansion → collapse + named intermediate (e.g. *variance*) → inverse operation recovers base quantity (σ, SD) → plug-back into headline formula → plain-English interpretation (often %).

### Skeleton A — Definition → instance → procedure
**Shape:** `[LABEL]. [Subject] has [feature] based on [statistic] ([abbr]). [Stat] is defined as [formula]; where [symbols]. [Taking/Using] the data of [subset], the [statistic] can be calculated.`
- **Original:** "PROCESSED DATA. The raw data has error bars based on the standard error of the mean (SEM). SEM or s̄x = σ/√n where σ represents a sample's standard deviation and n is the amount of data points. Taking the data of the 40ml shoot length, the SEM can be calculated."
- **Demo:** "NORMALISED YIELDS. The reaction output has confidence limits based on the standard error of the proportion (SEP). SEP = √(p(1−p)/n) where p is the observed proportion and n is the trial count. Taking the data of the 50 °C trial, the SEP can be calculated."

### Skeleton B — Stage label → one substitution → algebraic move
**Shape:** `[The first/next] step is [gerund]. [Formula]. The mean can be calculated as [substituted mean]. [Variable] is equal to [definition]. [Algebraic verb]-ing both sides simplifies the equation. [Simplified formula].`
- **Original:** "The first step is finding σ. σ = √(1/(N−1) Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²). x̄ = 3.914. N is equal to the number of specimens, 7. Squaring both sides simplifies. σ² = Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²/(N−1)."
- **Demo:** "The first step is finding the variance. Var = (1/(n−1)) Σ(xᵢ − μ)². μ = 15. n is equal to the number of readings, 5. Squaring each deviation simplifies. Var = Σ(xᵢ − μ)²/(n−1)."

### Skeleton C — Expansion → collapse → reverse operation
**Shape:** `[Expression] = ([each term]) / ([constant]). Completing all operations on top and bottom, the fraction becomes [result]. The [intermediate name] is [value]. [Inverse-verb]-ing both sides means that [quantity] = [recovered value].`
- **Original:** "Σ(xᵢ − x̄)²/(N−1) = (3.1−3.9)² + (3.7−3.9)² + … / (7−1). Completing all operations, the variance is 0.17476. Square rooting both sides means that σ = 0.418."
- **Demo:** "Σ(pᵢ − p̄)²/(n−1) = (0.12−0.20)² + (0.18−0.20)² + (0.22−0.20)² + (0.28−0.20)² / (4−1). Completing all operations, the variance is 0.016. Square rooting both sides means that SD = 0.1265."

### Skeleton D — Plug-back → result → interpretation
**Shape:** `Now the value for [intermediate] can be plugged into [headline formula]. [Symbol] is [value], so the equation becomes [fully substituted] = [final number]. This means that [plain-English meaning], often as a [percentage/bound].`
- **Original:** "Now σ can be plugged into s̄x = σ/√n. n is 7, so s̄x = 0.418/√7 = 0.158. This means the error for that group is 15%."
- **Demo:** "Now σ can be plugged into CI = 1.96·σ/√n. n is 30, so CI = 1.96·0.42/√30 = 0.150. This means the 95% confidence half-width is 0.15 units."

### Replication rule
Every algebraic step is **named as an action**; nothing is computed before its prerequisite is shown.

### Vocabulary (worked calculation)
- **Sequencing:** "The first step is finding…"; "The next element that is needed is…"
- **Plug-back:** "Now the value for…"
- **Subset narrow:** "Taking the data of [X]"
- **Symbol definition:** "where σ represents…"; "N is equal to…"
- **Algebraic action:** "Squaring both sides simplifies the equation"
- **Inverse conclusion:** "Square rooting both sides means that σ ="
- **Expand→collapse:** "Completing all of the operations on the top and bottom, the fraction becomes"
- **Plain-English:** "This means that…" (render as %, bound, threshold)
- **Operational:** "must have … subtracted from them and then multiplied"; "repeated for all data points and groups"
- **Culmination:** "Therefore, [STAT] = … = T"

## 3. Raw Data, Uncertainty, and Citation Flows

### Raw-data flow (Baseline-Then-Deviation) — 7 steps
1. Data-type header (*"Qualitative Data:"* / *"Quantitative Data:"*).
2. IV's most obvious sensory feature (colour/smell/texture) — anchors cause.
3. Earliest positive sign in control + lowest group on a **named day** — anchors t1.
4. Sustained positive outcome in control at a **later** time point — normal trajectory.
5. Deviation in highest 1–2 groups with *"compared to those in the [control] groups."*
6. Visible physical link (subjects take on IV's sensory property) on a **named day** — supplies mechanism.
7. Quantitative block: caption table first (DV name + denominator), then numbers.

### Raw-data skeleton — Qualitative block + Table caption
- **Header + caption shape:** *"Qualitative Data:"* → *"Data Table [n]: A raw data table containing the [DV] in each trial [/N]"*
- **Original:** "Data Table 1: A raw data table containing the number of seeds germinated in each trial."
- **Demo:** "Data Table 1: A raw data table containing the mass of precipitate (g) formed in each trial."
- **Qualitative demo:** "The solutions with a higher concentration of copper (II) sulphate had a faint turquoise tint. The leaf surfaces of plants in the 0 and 10 mg/L solutions showing signs of new bud formation as soon as the third day. The plants in the 0 mg/L solution were already showing steady leaf expansion by the end of the fortnight. The leaves … in the 40 and 60 mg/L groups showed minor curling at the margins compared to those in the 0 mg/L groups. The plants in the higher concentration groups appeared to take on the turquoise tint of the copper (II) sulphate solutions by day 6."

### Uncertainty flow — Tolerance Catalogue → Per-Instrument → Aggregation
- **Move 1:** Catalogue every instrument's margin in parallel phrasing (reader holds raw tolerances).
- **Move 2:** Per instrument — (a) name quantity + sample + instrument in past tense, (b) optionally justify the chosen value with one rule-sentence, (c) display vertical fraction × 100 = result %.
- **Move 3:** Aggregate — sum contributions into "maximum percentage uncertainty of X %."

### Skeleton A — Tolerance Catalogue
**Shape:** bridging sentence + 2–4 identical "margin of error of ±X unit" lines.
- **Original:** "Margin of error in the mass balance: ±0.001g / ±0.5ml / ±0.4ml."
- **Demo:** "Margin of error in the digital thermometer: ±0.1 °C / in the 100 ml beaker: ±1.0 ml / in the top-loading balance: ±0.01 g."

### Skeleton B — Single-Instrument Percentage Uncertainty
**Shape:** passive procedure sentence → primed calculation block.
- **Original:** "25ml of each NiCl2 was measured using the 50ml graduated cylinder, thus the percentage uncertainty for the same would be the following: 0.4 / 25 × 100 = 1.60 %."
- **Demo:** "30.0 cm³ of each sugar solution was measured using the 25 cm³ pipette, thus the percentage uncertainty for the same would be the following: 0.06 / 30.0 × 100 = 0.20 %."

### Skeleton C — Justified Substitution
**Shape:** procedure → causal rule → primed calculation.
- **Original:** "The mass balance used to weigh the NiCl2 … The smaller the measured value, the higher the uncertainty. Substituting the minimum measured mass (30mg) into the same we get the following: 0.001 / 0.03 × 100 = 3.33%."
- **Demo:** "The 25 cm³ pipette was used to deliver the acid. The smaller the delivered volume, the higher the percentage uncertainty. Substituting the minimum pipetted volume (5.00 cm³) into the same we get the following: 0.06 / 5.00 × 100 = 1.20 %."

### Skeleton D — Aggregation Verdict
**Shape:** gerund + reporting verb + value.
- **Original:** "Adding together all the uncertainties, we get the maximum percentage uncertainty (%) of 5.12%."
- **Demo:** "Adding together all the uncertainties, we get the maximum percentage uncertainty (%) of 2.45%."

### Citation flow — Funnel: identity → container → locator → verification
Author → title → container (vol./no./year/pages) → DOI → access date. Each slot supplies info the next depends on.
- **Multi-author journal:** *"[Last, First, et al.] ['Title.' Journal Name, vol. X, no. Y, Year, pp. N–N., doi:XX. Accessed D Month Year.]"* — "Baruah, Nijara, et al. 'Influence of Heavy Metals on Seed Germination…' Water, Air, & Soil Pollution, vol. 230, no. 12, 2019, pp. …, doi:10.1007/s11270-019-4329-0. Accessed 15th May 2020."
- **Single/two-author journal:** one author → no "and"; two → ", and" before second; "Kapoor, Rohit, and Asha Menon. 'Carbon Sequestration…' Estuarine, Coastal and Shelf Science, vol. 245, 2020, p. 106891., doi:10.1016/j.ecss.2020.106891. Accessed 7 July 2023."
- **Institutional report:** institutional chain in full; title with embedded year; "National Water Quality Monitoring Authority, Ministry of Jal Shakti, Government of India. Annual Report on Trace Metals in Major River Basins - 2021. Accessed 5 June 2023."

## 4. Methods / Procedural Vocabulary

- **Imperative verbs:** Gather, Label, Place, Pipette, Weigh, Transfer, Stir, Repeat, Measure.
- **Temporal anchors:** "prior to pressure cooking"; "Continue stirring until…"; "Every two days"; "Then" (arithmetic ladder: "Then pour… 20 ml… 30 ml… 40 ml").
- **Completion triggers:** "until the powder has completely dissolved"; "until the solution becomes clear"; "Mix until the lauric acid is saturated."
- **Specification:** "should be equal to one cup"; "1M HCl"; "at 17 psi"; "at room temperature (15-30 °C)"; "about 15-20 minutes, never over 20 psi."
- **Standardisation:** "Plant 1 seed per pot"; "3 fronds per well"; "in the center of the pot."
- **Allowance:** "Masking tape and/or a permanent marker can be used."
- **Backward-pointing nouns:** "the solution" / "each pot" / "the specimen" — each sentence's subject is the prior sentence's product.
- **Linker:** "and" chains sub-actions; comma-chains doses in ladders.

**Rule:** Methods stays imperative and clean — no "according to" / "this suggests"; reasoning belongs in Discussion.

## 5. Raw-Data Vocabulary

- **Sequencing/scope:** "The same steps can be repeated"; "This should be repeated for the other N [cases]."
- **Observation verbs:** "showing signs of germination" (behavioural); "showed minor irregularities in form" (morphological); "appeared to take on" (hedged visual).
- **Comparison:** "compared to those in the [control] groups"; "higher concentration groups" vs. "0 and [low] mg/L".
- **Time-pin:** "as soon as the second day"; "by day 4"; "by the end of the week."
- **Causal logic:** positional (timeline + dose-band order) — connectives not explicit in raw data.

# Methods & Procedures

## A. Lab Method Paragraph Patterns

### Pattern 1 — Quantified Controls → Materials → Safety/Setup → Preparation → Troubleshooting
The flow: one quantified control per sentence (volume, solute amount, substrate fill, density, shared environment) closes the variable set. Materials are then enumerated in fixed groups (liquids → powders → tools → glassware → hardware → PPE → containers → biologicals → instruments). Safety arrives as paired imperatives (gather+PPE; hair+shoes). Setup delivers labelling, grid geometry, tool alternatives, placement. Preparation runs measure-and-add then corrective rinse, supplemented and stirred "until dissolved." The block closes with a conditional rescue branch ("if not X, then use Y and verb, verb, verb until Z"). Each sentence's product is the next sentence's input.

### Pattern 2 — Prep → Define IV → Apply IV → Introduce Subject → Control Confounds → Measure DV → Close
S1 opens with an imperative naming vessel + material. S2 sets sterilisation parameters (temp/time/pressure + ceiling cap). S3 delivers a fixed-volume substrate dose with spatial targeting. S4 prepares a solution (mass-in-volume-at-temperature with saturation endpoint). S5 lays an arithmetic dose ladder across labelled rows. S6 homogenises. S7 introduces the organism at fixed density ("N per unit"). S8 states maintenance (interval + volume). S9 caps duration. S10 splits data collection into count + measure, organ-by-organ. S11 closes with safety/disposal.

## B. Method Paragraph Skeletons

### Skeleton A — Quantified Control Variable Enumeration
`[Variable] should be equal to [qty unit].` / `[Container] should have [qty] of [substance] mixed.` / `[Container] has to be filled with [qty] of [substrate].` / `[N] [organism] per [unit].` / `All [subjects] should be exposed to the same [factor A] and [factor B].`
- **Fill:** one control per sentence; close the variable set before moving on.
- **Original:** "The amount of water in each molar solution should be equal to one cup… All of the plants should be exposed to the same light source and temperature."
- **Demo (enzyme assay):** "The volume of buffer in each tube should be equal to 2 mL. / Each tube should have 0.5 mL of enzyme diluted in it. / Each 10 mL cuvette has to be filled with 3 mL of substrate. / One substrate concentration per cuvette. / All cuvettes should be exposed to the same incubation time and temperature."

### Skeleton B — Grouped Materials List
`[MATERIALS]` — grouped noun phrases each carrying a quantity: liquids → powders/chemicals → branded preparations → tools → glassware → hardware → PPE → containers → biologicals → instruments.
- **Fill:** preserve group order; quantity travels with the noun.
- **Original:** "1000ml Sterile Water / Murashige and Skoog Medium 5L / 1 1000 ml beaker / 1 Pressure cooker at 17 psi / 1 pair of gloves / 30 pots 1 pt of soil / 30 seeds of brassica rapa / 1 balance."
- **Demo (photosynthesis):** "500 mL distilled water / 5 g sodium bicarbonate / 1.0 mL bromothymol blue / 1 dissolved oxygen probe / 1 1000 mL beaker / 1 LED source at 0–800 lux / 1 pair of gloves / 5 250 mL bottles with 200 mL water / 5 sprigs of *Elodea* 5 cm / 1 light meter."

### Skeleton C — Safety + Labelling Compound Imperative
`[Verb] materials and [verb] on [PPE].` / `[Verb] up [body part] and make sure to [verb] [PPE].` / `[Verb] each [unit] with [labels] corresponding to each [group]. Each [group] should have [N] [units] and [N] per [other grouping]. [Tool] and/or [alt tool] can be used. Place them in a [adj] area with access to [resource].`
- **Fill:** pair imperatives; tool alternatives offered with "and/or"; location specifies a resource.
- **Original:** "Gather materials and put on gloves. Tie up hair and make sure to wear closed toe shoes. Label each row of pots with the numbers 1-5… Masking tape and/or a permanent marker can be used. Place them in a wide area that has access to light."
- **Demo (pH germination):** "Gather materials and put on gloves. Tie up hair and make sure to wear a lab coat. Label each Petri dish with numbers 1-6 corresponding to each pH. Each dish should have 10 seeds and 5 dishes per treatment. Pipettes and/or droppers can be used. Place them in a controlled area with access to a 25 °C incubator."

### Skeleton D — Mix → Correct → Condition → Troubleshoot
`[Verb] [vol] of [solvent] and [verb] [mass] of [solute] at [range].` / `[Verb] the [vessel] with [solvent] to remove trace residues of [contaminant].` / `[Verb] [additions] prior to [stage]. [Verb] until [solute] has completely [verb-ed].` / `If [solute] does not [verb] then [verb] [reagent] and [verb], [verb], and [verb] until [outcome] becomes [adj].`
- **Fill:** corrective rinse precedes supplementation; the conditional branch closes the block.
- **Original:** "Take 600 ml of distilled water and add 4.54 g of MS medium at 15-30 °C. Rinse… Add heat stable supplements prior to pressure cooking. Continue stirring until the powder has completely dissolved. If the powder does not dissolve then use HCL and mix, heat, and rotate until the solution becomes clear."
- **Demo (starch agar):** "Pour 100 mL distilled water and add 1 g agar at 20-25 °C. Rinse the flask to remove trace residues. Add indicator dye prior to autoclaving. Continue swirling until the agar has completely suspended. If not, use a magnetic stirrer and heat, stir, and rotate until the solution becomes translucent."

### Skeleton E — Numbered Procedure with Embedded Parameters
`[Verb substrate into vessel]. [Sterilise at T/t/P, never above X]. [Pour fixed vol, spatial target]. [Mass-into-vol-at-temp until endpoint]. [Arithmetic dose ladder across rows]. [Mix to homogenise]. [N organism per unit]. [Every X, verb Y]. [Observe Z days]. [Count + measure, anatomy split]. [Safely discard].`
- **Fill:** embed all numerics inline; homogenise between dose ladder and subject introduction; close with disposal.
- **Original:** lauric-acid procedure S6–S13 (as quoted).
- **Demo (*Lemna* salt assay):** "Dispense Hoagland's into flasks and autoclave at 121 °C for 15 min, never above 20 psi. Pipette 10 ml per well, centred. Weigh 0.5 g NaCl into 500 ml medium at 25 °C; mix until dissolved. Add 0, 10, 20, 30, 40 mM NaCl to columns 1–5. Vortex. Transfer 3 *Lemna* fronds per well. Every 2 days, replace with 5 ml. Record for 14 days. Count frond number and measure frond area. Safely discard and clean up."

### Skeleton F — Mass-into-Volume-at-Temperature with Endpoint
`Take the bottle of [X], and pour [mass] [unit] into [volume] of [solvent] heated to [temp] °C. Mix until the [solute] is [saturated/dissolved/homogenised].`
- **Fill:** mass precedes volume; endpoint verb specifies terminal state.
- **Original:** "Take the bottle of lauric acid, and pour 25 grams into 1200 ml of water heated to 37 C. Mix until the lauric acid is saturated."
- **Demo:** "Take the bottle of boric acid, and pour 4 g into 200 ml distilled water heated to 60 °C. Mix until fully dissolved."

### Skeleton G — Graduated Dose Distribution with Homogenisation Close
`Pour [V1] into row N. Then pour [V2] into row N+1, [V3] each for row N+2, and [V4] each for row N+3. [Stir/mix the substrate].`
- **Fill:** arithmetic series across rows; close with one homogenisation verb.
- **Original:** "Pour 10 ml into the second row. Then pour 20 ml into the third row, 30 ml each for the fourth, and 40 ml each for the fifth. Stir the dirt."
- **Demo:** "Pour 5 ml water into column 1. Then pour 10 ml of 10 mM into column 2, 15 ml into column 3, and 20 ml into column 4. Agitate the agar."

### Skeleton H — Maintenance Schedule with Duration Cap
`Every [interval] days, [verb] the [subject] with [volume] for each [unit]. Repeat the process and observe for [duration] days.`
- **Fill:** interval + per-unit dose; cap with total observation window.
- **Original:** "Every two days, water the plants with 300 ml for each plant. Repeat the process and observe for 45 days."
- **Demo:** "Every three days, feed the *Daphnia* with 2 ml algal suspension per beaker. Repeat and observe for 21 days."

## C. Calculation Method Patterns

### Pattern A — Long Calculation Block (multi-paragraph)
The spine: (1) display master equation → (2) plain-English statement of what's needed → (3) "where" clause glossing symbols with two parallel instances → (4) one worked case with raw values shown → (5) "The same steps can be repeated" sweep across remaining cases → (6) mirror sweep onto a parallel variable with "These steps can be repeated with [second variable]" → (7) one-sentence signpost naming the next prerequisite ("The total means … are needed") → (8) parallel delivery of values ("For A, this is X, and for B it is Y") → (9) procedural-imperative sentence ("Proceed to subtract … and then square it") → (10) executed long arithmetic = headline number → (11) temporal hinge ("Then") opening the next formula block.
**Why this order:** equation establishes the spine; one concrete case proves the method; repetition sweeps rather than re-derives; signposts prevent number ambush; imperatives separate rule from execution; "Then" mirrors the mathematical order of decomposition (SSM → SSE → SS total).

### Pattern B — Worked-Procedure Block (short, three-paragraph)
The spine: (1) restate the formula in words as a verbal recipe ("must be subtracted … squared and added together") → (2) name the specific subset the recipe will be demonstrated on → (3) lay out the full algebra term-by-term with shared constant at full precision → (4) print the intermediate total → (5) "This should be repeated for the other N [cases], and then all of the values are to be added together" → (6) "Therefore, STAT = r₁ + r₂ + … + r_N = T."
**Why this order:** verbal rule precedes instance (reader checks math against contract); instance precedes extension (proof-of-concept first); extension precedes aggregation (summation is justified only after all parts are computed).

## D. Calculation Paragraph Skeletons

### Skeleton 1 — Equation + Symbol Glossary Opener
`[Display equation]. [Plain-language statement of what must be calculated first]. [Symbol] represents [general meaning] where [instance 1] represents [thing] and [instance 2] represents [thing].`
- **Fill:** Slot 1 = centred formula, no verb. Slot 2 = "must be calculated/needed" flagging the next sub-quantity. Slot 3 = "[symbol] represents … where [i₁] = … and [i₂] = …" — two parallel clauses joined by "and".
- **Original:** `SSm = ∑(xk − x̄)². The mean of the different factors, or group intervals, must be calculated. x̄k represents the mean of the different factors where DV1 represents the root growth and DV2 represents the shoot growth.`
- **Demo (χ² on fertiliser trial):** `χ² = ∑((O − E)² / E). The expected counts for each cell must be calculated. Eij represents the expected count for cell (i, j) where row 1 = fertiliser A applied and row 2 = fertiliser B applied, and column 1 = seedlings germinated.`

### Skeleton 2 — One Worked Case, Then Sweep
`[Specific case]'s ([role], e.g. control group) [quantity] is equal to ([raw values]) / [n] = [decimal]. The same steps can be repeated to give [label 2] = [v], [label 3] = [v], …, and [label N] = [v]. These steps can be repeated with the [second variable] to give [parallel list with switched prefix].`
- **Fill:** Slot 1 = full arithmetic expression, raw values listed, divided by n, long decimal, "is equal to". Slot 2 = "same steps can be repeated" hinge + comma list `[label] = [value]`. Slot 3 = "These steps can be repeated with the [second variable]" hinge + parallel list with prefix switch.
- **Original:** `Factor 1's (the control group) mean of the first dependent variable is equal to (4.1 + 4.4 + 4.6 + 5 + 5.1 + 5.2 + 5.4) / 7 = 4.82857143. ### Skeleton 3 — Signpost + Procedure + Execution
`The [next quantity] for [variable(s)] [is/are] needed. For [A], this is [X], and for [B] it is [Y]. [Imperative: "Proceed to"/"Next,"] [operation 1] and then [operation 2] [each/the cases]. Then [worked arithmetic, case by case] = [final result].`

Proceed to subtract the total means from each of the factor's means and then square it. Then add all of the values together to get the sum. The first dependent variable's SSm = … = 5.870416359.`

- **Fill:** Slot 1 opens with "Then", names next quantity, "using the equation" reintroduces a display formula in the same typographic style as the opener.

### Skeleton 5 — Rule-Statement Paragraph
`In order to get [X], the [operation] of the [items] must be [verb-ed] from the [reference] of their respective [items] [verb-ed] and added together. This will be demonstrated with [the specific case].`

## E. Express-Idea Vocabulary Groups

**Priming a calculation [method]:**
- "the percentage uncertainty for the same would be the following:" — primes a worked calculation that follows immediately.
- "Substituting … into the same we get the following:" — echoes earlier formula, primes next block.
- "Adding together all the uncertainties, we get" — announces summation verdict.

**Stating tolerance / evidence catalogue [method]:**
- "[Instrument]: ±[value][unit]" — repeated verbatim so reader scans a list.

**Cause / mechanism — conservative-value rule [method]:**
- "The smaller the measured value, the higher the uncertainty." — comparative correlation stated as general rule before dependent substitution.

**Instrument-to-role assignment [method]:**
- "[Instrument] was used to [verb] the [sample]" — pairs apparatus with the variable it measured.

**Displayed calculation [method]:**
- "[margin] / [value] × 100 = [result] %" — vertical fraction, two-decimal result, units dropped when context already implies them.

**Citation slot labels [general]:**
- "vol.", "no.", "pp." (multi-page), "p." (single page), "doi:" / "DOI:", "Accessed D Month Year", "et al." — fixed markers that signal each slot.

**Authorship scope specification [general]:**
- "et al." — flags additional authors beyond lead.
- ", and" with comma — marks second of exactly two authors.

## F. Explanation / Replication Patterns

### Pattern 1 — Maximum Percentage Uncertainty Walkthrough [method]
1. Announce in one carry-over line that equipment uncertainties will now be calculated.
2. List every instrument's absolute margin of error in identical phrasing.
3. For each instrument (procedure order): procedure sentence naming quantity + sample + instrument.
4. If the chosen measured value is non-obvious, insert one rule-sentence ("The smaller the value, the higher the uncertainty.") before the substitution.
5. Display vertical fraction × 100 = result %.
6. Repeat steps 3–5 per instrument.
7. Close with single aggregation sentence summing contributions into "maximum percentage uncertainty of X %."

### Pattern 2 — Citation Entry Walkthrough [general]
1. Name creator (inverted individual or full institution).
2. Quoted article title ending in period (or italicised work title).
3. Container + vol./no./year/pages as comma list.
4. DOI prefixed "doi:" (case-matched to source).
5. Close with "Accessed D Month Year."
Each step hands the next by dependency; repeating the funnel for every source yields a uniform list.

## Data, Calculations & Results

### Paragraph Flow Patterns

#### Pattern A — Worked-Calculation Bridge [data] / [general]
Move sequence: **box prior result → flag next dependency → define next term → state rule → generalize → demonstrate → box new result.**
Each move hands the reader exactly one job; no move can be skipped.

**Slot template with fill instructions:**
- [box prior result] — recap the numerical outcome from the preceding paragraph, stating value + units, so the reader doesn't have to flip back. *Fill: "From the data above, the initial mass was recorded as **[X g]**."*
- [flag next dependency] — explicitly name the variable whose effect on the system must now be quantified, so the reader knows what is about to be derived. *Fill: "To determine how **[variable Y]** influences the system, the next calculation must be performed."*
- [define next term] — give a one-sentence operational definition of the term being introduced, with symbols. *Fill: "**[variable Y]** is defined as the change in **[quantity]** per unit **[quantity]**."*
- [state rule] — write the formula using symbols, then restate it in words. *Fill: "Using the relationship **[equation]**, where **[symbol]** represents **[meaning]**…"*
- [generalize] — point out the qualitative trend so the reader can predict outcomes without re-running the math. *Fill: "This shows that as **[IV]** increases, **[DV]** **[direction of change]**."*
- [demonstrate] — plug a specific set of numbers into the rule to produce one concrete worked answer. *Fill: "For example, when **[variable A] = [value]** and **[variable B] = [value]**, the calculation yields **[result with units]**."*
- [box new result] — end with a clearly stated numerical conclusion (boxed or bolded) so the reader's eye lands on the answer. *Fill: "Therefore, **[final value + units]**, confirming that **[one-sentence takeaway]**."*

**Different-idea demonstration (different IV/DV, different numbers):**
- [box prior result]: "From the data above, the initial volume of gas collected was **[12.0 cm³]**."
- [flag next dependency]: "To determine how **[temperature]** influences the rate of gas production, the next calculation must be performed."
- [define next term]: "**[Rate]** is defined as the change in **[volume]** per unit **[time]**."
- [state rule]: "Using the relationship **[Rate = ΔV / Δt]**, where **[ΔV]** represents the change in volume and **[Δt]** represents the elapsed time…"
- [generalize]: "This shows that as **[temperature]** increases, the **[rate]** **[increases]**."
- [demonstrate]: "For example, when **[ΔV = 6.0 cm³]** and **[Δt = 30 s]**, the calculation yields **[0.20 cm³/s]**."
- [box new result]: "Therefore, **[the rate at this temperature = 0.20 cm³/s]**, confirming that **[higher temperatures produce gas at a faster rate]**."

#### Pattern B — Raw Data Section [data]
Move sequence: **categorise data type → describe IV's sensory feature → early positive in control/low → sustained control outcome → deviation in high group(s) → visible mechanism (IV absorbed) → switch to quantitative header → caption table.**
Each move sets up the next by giving the reader one anchor (cause, time, baseline, deviation, link) before requiring a judgment.

**Slot template with fill instructions:**
- [categorise data type] — name what kind of data the paragraph will present (qualitative/quantitative, raw/calculated) so the reader knows how to read it. *Fill: "**[Qualitative]** observations were recorded for each trial of **[experiment]**."*
- [describe IV's sensory feature] — paint one picture of how the IV looked, smelled, or felt at the start, anchoring the reader in the raw setup. *Fill: "At the start of the trial, **[IV]** appeared as **[colour/texture/odour]**."*
- [early positive in control/low] — state what the lowest-level condition showed in the first phase of the trial, giving the reader a baseline anchor. *Fill: "In the **[control / lowest-concentration]** group, a **[small/clear]** change was visible within **[first time interval]**."*
- [sustained control outcome] — confirm that the low-level outcome held across the full duration, establishing stability. *Fill: "This effect was sustained throughout the **[duration]**, with no notable further change."*
- [deviation in high group(s)] — contrast the control with the highest-concentration or experimental group, pinpointing when and how the outcome diverged. *Fill: "However, in the **[highest-concentration]** group, a **[larger/different]** change appeared at **[later time interval]**, diverging from the control trend."*
- [visible mechanism (IV absorbed)] — explain the physical or chemical reason the IV caused the deviation, so the reader links cause to effect. *Fill: "This deviation occurred because **[IV]** was absorbed by **[reactant/tissue]**, which **[observable consequence]**."*
- [switch to quantitative header] — pivot the paragraph to numbers, signalling that the qualitative observations will now be measured. *Fill: "To quantify these observations, the data was tabulated as follows:"*
- [caption table] — end with a descriptive caption that names IV, DV, units, and number of trials, so the table can stand alone. *Fill: "Table 1: **[DV]** (units) measured across **[number]** trials for varying levels of **[IV]**."*

**Different-idea demonstration (different IV/DV, different observations):**
- [categorise data type]: "**[Qualitative]** observations were recorded for each trial of the **[enzymatic reaction]**."
- [describe IV's sensory feature]: "At the start of the trial, **[the substrate]** appeared as a **[clear, colourless liquid]**."
- [early positive in control/low]: "In the **[lowest-enzyme]** group, a **[small]** amount of **[product]** was visible within **[the first 30 seconds]**."
- [sustained control outcome]: "This effect was sustained throughout the **[5-minute]** observation period, with no notable further change."
- [deviation in high group(s)]: "However, in the **[highest-enzyme]** group, a **[much larger cloudiness]** appeared at **[1 minute]**, diverging from the control trend."
- [visible mechanism (IV absorbed)]: "This deviation occurred because **[the enzyme]** bound rapidly to **[the substrate]**, which **[accelerated product formation visible as cloudiness]**."
- [switch to quantitative header]: "To quantify these observations, the data was tabulated as follows:"
- [caption table]: "Table 1: **[Absorbance readings (AU)]** measured across **[3]** trials for varying concentrations of **[enzyme]**."

### Express-Idea Vocabulary (Data, Calculations & Results)

**Sequencing connectives** — show order of operations or measurements: *first, next, subsequently, finally, throughout, at the start, at the end, in the initial interval, by the end of the trial.*

**Quantitative framing connectives** — introduce a number or formula: *the value of X was calculated as…, the data shows that…, from the equation…, using the relationship…, the calculation yielded…, this gives a value of….*

**Contrast/deviation connectives** — flag where data breaks the trend: *however, in contrast, whereas the control…, a deviation appeared at…, unlike the low group, the high group showed…, this diverged from….*

**Cause-effect (mechanism) connectives** — link IV to observed result: *because, as a result, this occurred due to, this was caused by, the IV was absorbed by, the reaction proceeded because, this led to….*

**Generalization connectives** — lift a single result to a rule: *therefore, this suggests that, overall the trend shows, in general, this implies that, this can be generalized as….*

**Boxed-result phrasing** — phrase to introduce a clearly marked conclusion: *therefore the result is X, confirming that…; the final value equals X, showing that…; it can be concluded that X, because….*

**Table-caption phrasing** — phrase to label a data table so it stands alone: *Table 1: [DV] (units) measured across [N] trials for varying levels of [IV].*
