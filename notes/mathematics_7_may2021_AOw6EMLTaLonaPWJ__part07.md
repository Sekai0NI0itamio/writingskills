## What This Section Does (content sequence)

A reference list in this IB coursework performs one job — anchor every claim earlier in the essay to a credible source — and it does it in the **order in which the body cites those sources**. The sequence is:

1. **Cultural/context citation first** ([6]) — because the essay's opening framing of a regional anxiety anchors a real-world hook.
2. **Methodological source next** ([7]) — because once the phenomenon is named, the method used to measure it must be justified.
3. **Peer-reviewed theoretical sources** ([8] → [9] → [10]) — because theory must be both formulaic and definitional, and a textbook gives the canonical definition.
4. **Geographic/visual sources** ([11] → [12]) — because the concrete study sites must be locatable.
5. **Mathematical theorem sources** ([13] → [14]) — because the calculus machinery used in the box-counting argument needs its own authority.
6. **Cross-disciplinary application last** ([15]) — because closing on a paper *outside* the immediate domain (physiology, not geography) widens the claim that the method is generalisable.

**Why this order:** it mirrors the rhetorical arc of the body — context → method → theory → setting → calculation → transferability. Another student can replicate the sequence by listing their sources in the order the *argument* needed them, not alphabetically.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A — Single citation entry**
`[N] Author, A. (Year). "Title of article/chapter." Journal/Publisher, URL/DOI.`

1. **What each slot holds:**
   - `[N]`: bracketed numeral, continuing from the body.
   - `Author, A. (Year).`: surname + initials, year in parentheses, ending with period.
   - `"Title of article/chapter."`: quoted, sentence-case, terminating period inside the quotes.
   - `Journal/Publisher,`: italicised in print, plain comma here.
   - `URL/DOI.`: hyperlink or doi string, terminating period.
2. **HOW to fill with a DIFFERENT idea:** Pick the exact source you cited in the body; copy author name, year, title, and stable URL/DOI directly from the source; preserve the punctuation; do not alphabetise — keep citation order.
3. **Original filled version:** *"[7] Karperien, A. '"BoxCounting."' National Institutes of Health, imagej.nih.gov/ij/plugins/fraclac/FLHelp/BoxCounting.htm."*
4. **Demonstration fill (different idea):** *"[7] Smith, J. (2021). '"Measuring tree-canopy complexity."' Urban Forestry, doi.org/10.1000/xyz123."* — same skeleton, new subject.

**SKELETON B — Block of same-domain citations**
`Several sources clustered by sub-topic to build an authority chain (tool → paper → textbook).`

1. **What each slot holds:** three or more entries of Skeleton A, all drawn from one sub-field, ordered from most specific tool to most general textbook.
2. **HOW to fill:** find one practical tool source, one peer-reviewed paper, one textbook; place them consecutively so the body can quote them in escalating generality.
3. **Original filled version:** entries [7] (NIH plugin), [8] (Chen 2013), [9] (Shirali 2014), [10] (Feder 2013) — tool → formulae paper → worked-example paper → textbook.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

Because this is a bibliography, the "vocabulary" is structural rather than connective.

- **Sequencing / ordering markers:** bracket-number sequencing — *"[6] 'Anglo-Scottish Anxieties.'"*, *"[8] Chen, Y. (2013)."*, *"[15] Glenny, R. W., et al."* — moves the reader through citations in the order they were needed.
- **Authority / evidence handling verbs:** none appear as verbs in the list; the *form* itself does the work of authority — *'"BoxCounting."' National Institutes of Health, U.S. Department of Health and Human Services'*, *'Springer Science & Business Media'* — institutional names substitute for verbs like "according to."
- **Specification / version-locking:** parenthetical year stamps — *'Chen, Y. (2013)'*, *'Shirali, S. A. (2014)'*, *'Feder, J. (2013)'* — pin each claim to its exact edition.
- **Locating / retrieval markers:** URLs — *'imagej.nih.gov/ij/plugins/fraclac/FLHelp/BoxCounting.htm'*, *'www.whitman.edu/mathematics/calculus-online/section16.04.html'* — let the reader trace the claim back.
- **Plurality / collective handling:** *'et al.'* in *'Glenny, R. W., et al.'* — compresses a multi-author paper.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern this section relies on is **citation-as-evidence-trail** — every idea in the body is followed by a bracketed numeral that points to a source. The order of the numerals reproduces the *logical* order of the claims.

Step-by-step replication:

1. **Write the body first.** Decide each claim you make (definition, formula, example, application).
2. **For each claim, pick the *single* source you will cite.** Prefer: a primary tool/paper for methods, a peer-reviewed paper for theory, a textbook for canonical definitions, a figure source for visuals.
3. **Number sources in the order they first appear in the body.** Do not alphabetise. Use square brackets and continuous numbering: [1], [2], [3]…
4. **Standardise the entry format** to the skeleton above: `Author. (Year). "Title." Publisher, URL/DOI.` Keep punctuation identical across every entry — a bibliography's authority comes from uniformity.
5. **Group same-domain sources consecutively** (tool → paper → textbook) so the reader sees a chain of authority when they scan down.
6. **Close with one cross-disciplinary source** if you want to signal transferability — the final entry should widen the lens.
7. **Re-number nothing at the end.** If you insert a new citation in the body, renumber the entire list so body and bibliography stay synchronised; never edit only the list.
