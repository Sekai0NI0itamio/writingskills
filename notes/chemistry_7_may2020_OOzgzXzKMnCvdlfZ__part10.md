## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1** (governing formula + single substitution)
1. **Setup / transition** — *"law was used as follows:"* — announces the equation that follows; hands the reader by naming WHAT is coming next (the formula).
2. **Substitution / evidence** — *"n = 101 000 × 0.01 ÷ 1 8.314 × 348"* — plugs measured values into the formula; hands the reader by completing the arithmetic, so the reader expects the answer next.
3. **Result / verdict** — *"n = 0.35 mol [of O2 produced]"* — gives the numerical answer with units and a label of what it represents; hands the reader to the question "what do I do with this for the rest of the data?".

**Paragraph 2** (generalization + stoichiometric correction)
1. **Generalization / scope** — *"This formula was used to find values for n at all other temperatures."* — lifts the single calculation to cover the full dataset; hands the reader to WHY the raw n is not yet the final quantity.

1. **Definition** — *"The rate of reaction (r) was calculated by dividing the amount of moles of H2O2 reacted by the average time"* — defines r by its formula; hands the reader by naming an operation, so an example is expected.
2. **Transition / pointer** — *"An example calculation at 348K is displayed below:"* — signals a worked instance; hands the reader by pointing to the substitution.
3. **Substitution** — *"r = [0.35×2] ÷ 10.4"* — re-uses the previous answer inside a new formula; hands the reader to the final result.
4. **Result / verdict** — *"r = 0.067moldm-3s-1"* — final answer with composite units; closes the chain.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **worked-example calculation block**, and its move sequence is:

1. **Name the governing formula** (what equation will be applied). *Why:* anchors the reader to the physical law before any numbers appear.
2. **Substitute one set of measured values** (one temperature). *Why:* makes the formula concrete and shows units being tracked.
3. **State the result with units + a label** of what it physically represents. *Why:* converts a number into a chemical meaning.
4. **Generalize** — say the same formula was applied to every trial. *Why:* justifies that one example is enough.
5. **Stoichiometric / structural adjustment** — explain WHY the raw result needs scaling (molar ratio, conversion factor). *Why:* bridges from the measured species (O₂) to the chemically relevant species (H₂O₂).
6. **Define the derived quantity** (e.g. rate) by naming what is divided into what. *Why:* the reader now knows the target.
7. **Show a second worked example**, embedding the previous result into the new formula. *Why:* proves the chain end-to-end.
8. **Final answer with composite units.** *Why:* closes with the quantity the rest of the IA will use.

Replicable sequence for any quantitative IA: **formula → substitution → labelled result → generalize → adjust for stoichiometry/structure → define target quantity → re-substitute using previous answer → final answer with units.**

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / pointing forward**
- *"law was used as follows"* — announces the formula that follows.
- *"An example calculation at 348K is displayed below"* — signals a worked instance.

**Cause / consequence (As-clause → action)**
- *"As the equation for the decomposition… has a molar ratio of 1:2… the value of n was multiplied by 2"* — reason embedded in "As…" leading to the arithmetic adjustment.

**Specification / scope-setting**
- *"at all other temperatures"* — bounds the formula's reach.
- *"at 348K"* — narrows the worked example to one condition.
- *"of O2 produced"* — labels what the number represents.

**Evidence handling (worked-instance markers)**
- *"This formula was used to find values for n"* — asserts coverage of the dataset without re-showing each calculation.
- *"An example calculation at 348K is displayed below"* — selects one representative trial as proof.

**Explanation / transformation verbs**
- *"was used as follows"* — introduces a governing equation.
- *"was multiplied by"* — stoichiometric scaling verb.
- *"was calculated by dividing"* — definition verb naming the operation.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern is a **worked-calculation cascade** (definition → single worked example → generalization → stoichiometric adjustment → derived-quantity definition → second worked example that re-uses the previous answer). Steps to replicate with any new topic:

1. **Name the governing law/equation** in one short clause ("X was used as follows:"), then display the symbolic formula on its own line.
2. **Substitute one trial's measured values** on the next line, keeping units visible inside the expression.
3. **Give the numerical answer** on the next line, with its unit and a bracketed label telling the reader what the number physically means.
4. **Generalize** in a new sentence: state that the same formula was applied to every other trial / condition.
5. **Add the stoichiometric or structural adjustment** in one complex sentence: an "As…" clause stating the ratio or conversion factor, then the operation (multiply/divide by N) that converts the raw quantity into the chemically relevant one.
6. **Define the derived quantity** in a new sentence by saying it "was calculated by dividing [A] by [B]" — name what goes on top and what goes on the bottom.
7. **Point to a second worked example** ("An example calculation at [specific condition] is displayed below:") and re-substitute, **embedding the previous answer in brackets** so the reader sees the chain physically carry forward.
8. **End with the final answer with composite units**, so the reader can see the unit analysis worked.

The whole pattern's logic: **show one calculation completely, then prove the chain holds by feeding its output into the next formula in the chain.**
