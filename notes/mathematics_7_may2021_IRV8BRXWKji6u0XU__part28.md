## What This Section Does (content sequence)

A References section performs this ordered sequence of content moves **per entry**, then repeats it:

1. **Author(s)** — identifies WHO wrote the source. Comes first because academic citation indexing is by author surname.
2. **Title** — identifies WHAT work is cited. Comes second so the reader immediately knows the cited piece.
3. **Container / publication venue** — identifies WHERE the work lives (journal, book, website). Comes third to locate the source.
4. **Date** — identifies WHEN the work appeared. Comes fourth for temporal anchoring.
5. **Access information** — DOI, URL, or access date. Comes last so a reader can verify and retrieve the source.

This order is replicated identically across all eight entries. Generalised: for any IB coursework topic, the student lists every cited source in the order each first appears in the body, applying the same five-slot template to each entry.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

References sections do **not** use argumentative connectives (no "however", no "therefore"). Instead, they use bibliographic markers grouped by job:

**Sequencing:** none — entries are ordered by first appearance in the body, not signalled.

**Cause / consequence:** none — references do not assert causal links.

**Contrast / concession:** none.

**Specification (narrowing the source):**
- "vol. 8," in "Regular and Chaotic Dynamics, vol. 8," — narrows to one journal volume.
- "2nd ser.," in "Periodico di Matematica per l'Insegnamento Secondario, 2nd ser.," — narrows to the second series of an old journal.
- "pp. 140–143," in "vol. 3, pp. 140–143," — narrows to exact pages cited.

**Evidence handling (verification markers):**
- "Date Accessed:" paired with "12/28/2020" in the Galperin entry — timestamps the verification.
- "[Online]. Available:" paired with "https://www.ibdocuments.com/..." — flags retrievability.
- "doi:" paired with "10.1070/RD2003v008n04ABEH000252" — provides a permanent digital locator.
- "doi:" paired with "10.1080/00031305.2012.687494" — same locator job for the Ver Hoef entry.

**Explanation verbs:** none — references do not define or unpack terms.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern this section relies on is **identification → location → access** — a *verification chain* rather than an explanation chain. To cite any new source in this format, follow these steps:

1. **Identify the author(s):** write surname first, then initials; for multiple authors, separate with commas and use "and" before the final name (e.g. "M. Aigner, Z. G. M., and K. H. Hofmann").
2. **Identify the title:** place article or chapter titles inside quotation marks; leave book or journal titles without quotes (e.g. "'Analysis, buffon's needle problem,' in Proofs from the Book.").
3. **Locate the source:** name the journal in full, OR use "in [Book Title]" for chapters, OR name the publisher for books (e.g. "Springer, 2018").
4. **Date the source:** write the year of publication, optionally preceded by month (e.g. "Jan. 2003") or volume/series/page information for older journal articles (e.g. "vol. 3, pp. 140–143, 1901").
5. **Provide access:** for online sources, append "Date Accessed: [date], [Online]. Available: [URL]"; for journal articles with permanent IDs, append "doi: [number]". This closes the chain and lets the reader retrace the research trail.
