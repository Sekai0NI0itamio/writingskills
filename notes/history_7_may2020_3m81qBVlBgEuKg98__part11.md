## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern used:** artefact → precursor → framing → direct analysis → parallel instance → historical extension → impact claim. This is a *layered evidence assembly* pattern: each source adds one analytical layer around a central object.

1. **Name the primary artefact** — identify the object, film, document, or event your idea centres on and cite it first so the reader has a concrete referent.
2. **Trace its precursor** — cite one source that shows what came before the artefact (a novel, a precedent, a earlier version), establishing that the artefact has a history.
3. **Frame its cultural function** — cite a source that labels what the artefact *does* (propaganda, myth, protest), converting it from object to argument.
4. **Analyse its content directly** — cite a source that reads the artefact's internal features (imagery, rhetoric, structure), deepening the critique.
5. **Introduce a parallel instance** — cite a source from a different but analogous context to show the pattern recurs, widening the claim's scope.
6. **Extend the timeline** — cite a source that places the artefact in a later historical moment, showing its effects outlive its creation.
7. **Close with an impact claim** — cite a source that argues the artefact shaped something later, completing the cause-to-effect arc and giving the section its conclusion.
