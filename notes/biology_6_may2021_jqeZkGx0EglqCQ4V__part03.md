## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1 (Steps 6–13: continuous procedural block)**

- **S1** — *Action specification* (numbered imperative, container + parameters): "Dispense the mediums into suitable closed containers" — hands the reader forward because a prepared substrate now needs a sterilisation step to justify what follows.
- **S2** — *Parameter-bound action* (time/temperature/pressure): "pressure cook at 121-125 C for about 15-20 minutes" — specifies the previous move's conditions, so the reader expects the next sentence to deploy the sterilised medium.
- **S3** — *Ceiling constraint* (safety upper bound): "never over 20 psi" — caps the prior instruction; hands the reader to a new, post-sterilisation phase.
- **S4** — *Dosing action* (volume + spatial targeting): "Evenly pour 10ml of the solution into each pot of soil" — shifts register from prep to application, prompting the next move to define the active solution.
- **S5** — *Solution preparation* (mass + volume + temperature): "pour 25 grams into 5 into 1200 ml of water heated to 37 C" — defines what the "solution" in S4 actually is, so the reader now anticipates dose variation.
- **S6** — *Saturation condition* (endpoint qualifier): "Mix until the lauric acid is saturated" — closes the prep move; the next sentence must distribute this solution.
- **S7** — *Dose gradient* (row-by-row escalation): "pour 10 ml each into the second row … 20 ml … 30 ml … 40 ml" — answers "how is the solution used?" with a controlled variable; the next move must plant a subject into that variable.
- **S8** — *Mixing action* (homogenisation of treatment into substrate): "Stir the dirt" — finalises setup; the reader now expects the biological subject to be introduced.
- **S9** — *Subject introduction* (count + placement): "Plant 1 seed per pot" — introduces the independent organism; hands forward to maintenance.
- **S10** — *Maintenance schedule* (interval + volume): "Every two days, water the plants with 300 ml" — sustains the subject over time, so the next move must specify duration.
- **S11** — *Observation endpoint* (duration closure): "observe for 45 days" — closes the temporal frame; next move must say what is recorded.
- **S12** — *Data collection* (counting + measuring, dual variable): "Observe how many new seedlings … Measure the length of the roots and stem" — defines the dependent variables, prompting a close-out.
- **S13** — *Procedural close / safety* (disposal + cleanup): "Safely discard and clean up lab materials" — terminates the procedure.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Container preparation with sterilisation parameters** — sets the sterile baseline so later biological readings are not confounded.
2. **Substrate dosing (fixed volume)** — establishes the growth medium state before treatment.
3. **Treatment-solution preparation (mass, volume, temperature, saturation endpoint)** — defines the IV.
4. **Dose gradient across rows** — operationalises the independent variable.
5. **Substrate mixing** — ensures even distribution so dose is the only varying factor.
6. **Biological subject introduction (1 seed per pot)** — standardises the starting organism.
7. **Maintenance regime (interval + volume)** — controls nuisance variables.
8. **Duration cap** — fixes the experimental window.
9. **Data collection (count + length, organ-by-organ)** — operationalises the DVs.
10. **Disposal / safety close** — lab compliance bookend.

Order rationale: **prep → define IV → apply IV → introduce subject → control confounds → measure DV → close**. The sterilisation must precede dosing; dosing must precede treatment preparation; treatment must precede subject introduction; subject must precede maintenance; maintenance must precede measurement; measurement precedes disposal. Each move constrains the conditions under which the next is valid.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / enumeration:** "Then pour 20 ml … 30ml each pot for the fourth row, and 40ml each pot for the fifth row" (arithmetic dose ladder via "Then" + comma chain).
- **Specification / targeting:** "in the center of the pot" (spatial anchor on the dosing sentence).
- **Parameter anchoring:** "about 15-20 minutes, never over 20 psi" (range + ceiling in one clause).
- **Endpoint / condition verbs:** "Mix until the lauric acid is saturated in the water" (defines completion of a step).
- **Standardisation cue:** "Plant 1 seed per pot" (one organism, one unit — fixes density).
- **Maintenance / repetition framing:** "Every two days … Repeat the process of watering" (interval + "Repeat the process of").
- **Observation / measurement verbs:** "Observe how many new seedlings are in each pot and then pull out the specimen. Measure the length of the roots and stem separately" (count → extract → measure, organ-split).
- **Procedural close:** "Safely discard and clean up lab materials" (safety bookend).
- **Conjunction "and" as procedural linker:** used in nearly every imperative to chain sub-actions ("Dispense the mediums … and pressure cook", "pour 25 grams … and pour", "water the plants with 300 ml for each plant").
- **Pronoun-driven continuity:** "the solution" / "each pot" refer back to the previously prepared reagent and vessel, doing the hand-off work between sentences.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The section uses a **sequential procedural explanation** pattern: each sentence is an imperative action whose parameters are fully stated in the same sentence, and whose output (a prepared/treated object) becomes the input the next sentence assumes. The reader never has to ask "where did that come from?" because every prior step's product is the next step's starting material.

To explain a NEW procedure with the same pattern:

1. **State the first imperative with its full parameters** (vessel, material, quantities). Do not defer numbers.
2. **Add a conditioning/sterilisation clause as a sub-clause of the same or next sentence** — temperature, time, and an explicit ceiling. This protects the validity of every later measurement.
3. **Introduce the active reagent as "the solution" only after you have defined it** — first the preparation sentence (mass-into-volume-at-temperature, with a saturation/dissolution endpoint), then a dosing sentence that uses "the solution" as a backward pointer.
4. **Make the independent variable a numbered, arithmetic dose ladder** spread across labelled groups ("second row", "third row" …). This makes the experimental design legible to the reader in one sentence.
5. **Insert one short homogenisation imperative** between dosing and organism introduction, so the reader knows the treatment is evenly distributed.
6. **Introduce the biological subject with a fixed density** ("1 seed per pot" / "3 fronds per well"). Density must be a numeral, not a phrase.
7. **State maintenance as interval + volume + per-unit** in a single sentence, and follow immediately with a duration cap in the next sentence.
8. **Split data collection into a count move and a measurement move**, and if anatomy allows, separate organs ("roots and stem separately").
9. **Close with a safety/disposal sentence** that mirrors the opening's lab-register tone.
10. **Throughout: chain actions with "and", and use backward-pointing nouns** ("the solution", "each pot", "the specimen") so each sentence's first phrase is the last sentence's product. The logic path is then enforced by referential continuity, not by transition words.
