## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This section type is a **bibliography / works-cited list**, so its moves are not argument-moves but **source-curation moves**. The generalized sequence is:

1. **Local / official primary source first** — establishes the closest, most authoritative data for the immediate study context. Sets up credibility before broadening.
2. **Mirror-topic peer-reviewed source** — adds a review article covering the opposite side of the intake spectrum, so both deficiency and excess are covered.
3. **Methodology / chemistry source** — introduces how iodine is measured or chemically characterised, supporting the Methods section.
4. **Accessible physiology explainer** — provides a plain-language mechanism source for non-specialist markers.
5. **Deficiency-side clinical review** — supplies the negative-outcome evidence the essay weighs against excess.
6. **Local clinical study** — re-anchors to the same geography as Move 1 but with patient-level data.
7. **Mechanistic / biochemistry paper** — supplies the molecular mechanism (e.g. antioxidant role) needed for deeper explanation.
8. **Industry / grey literature** — adds a non-academic perspective, showing the issue matters commercially.
9. **Global health authority (WHO topic page)** — elevates authority to international public-health level.
10. **Global health authority (WHO technical document)** — closes with a heavyweight methodology reference, signalling the bibliography is exhaustive.

**Why this order:** authority escalates from local-population → peer review → methodology → popular → clinical → mechanistic → industry → international body, while **deficiency and excess are interleaved** so the reader sees both sides of the iodine story. Geography alternates local (Hong Kong) and global to keep the local study anchored without becoming parochial.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section does **not** run an explanation pattern such as *definition → unpack → example → implication*. It runs a **citation-credibility ladder pattern**: each entry is a *step* on a credibility ladder from local-population data up to global-health authority, with topic-pairs (deficiency vs excess) interleaved.

**Step-by-step replication for a NEW topic (e.g. "Vitamin D intake in UK adults"):**

1. **Open with a local-official primary source.** Find the closest national body that has measured the variable in your population (e.g. Public Health England). Format as: *report title*, body, date, URL, access date. *Why:* gives the essay its immediate data anchor.
2. **Add a peer-reviewed review article on the upper bound of intake.** Pick a review covering excess / toxicity. Format as: Author et al. "Title." Journal vol. X, Y (year): pp. doi:. *Why:* establishes the upper end of the intake story.
3. **Add a methodology / chemistry source for the analyte.** Find a paper describing how the substance is quantified. *Why:* supports the Methods section's analytical choices.
4. **Add a plain-language physiology explainer.** Use an institutional Q&A page (e.g. NHS Inform). Phrase the title as a question. *Why:* gives non-specialist markers a digestible mechanism source.
5. **Add a deficiency-side clinical review.** Find a review on deficiency health consequences. *Why:* balances the excess-side article in Step 2.
6. **Add a same-geography clinical study.** Return to UK / local clinical data. *Why:* pairs with Step 1 to triangulate the local population.
7. **Add a mechanistic biochemistry paper.** Find a paper on the molecular role of the substance (e.g. vitamin D receptor binding). *Why:* deepens explanation beyond clinical observation.
8. **Add an industry / grey-literature report.** Pick a commercial-insurance or trade-association analysis. *Why:* shows real-world economic stakes.
9. **Add a WHO topic page.** Use WHO as the global-authority anchor. *Why:* elevates from local to international.
10. **Close with a WHO technical / methodology document.** End on the highest-authority source. *Why:* signals the literature scan is exhaustive.

This ladder — *local data → peer review → methodology → lay explainer → deficiency review → local clinical → molecular mechanism → industry → WHO topic → WHO technical* — is the replicable idea-expression pattern of the section.
