## What This Section Does (content sequence)

A "hook-then-formalisation" move sequence:

1. **Personal encounter** (1st) — grounds the method in lived discovery so the reader feels the journey.
2. **Stated doubt with general principle** (2nd) — manufactures tension by invoking a familiar rule the method seems to violate.
3. **Resolution of doubt** (3rd) — releases tension and licenses the technical exposition; the reader is now primed to *accept* what follows.
4. **Definition + semantic claim** (4th) — names the function and asserts what its evaluation counts, converting the curious method into a usable object.
5. **Pointer to tabulated evidence** (5th) — defers demonstration to a visible artefact, the natural endpoint of a "solution reveal" section.

The order matters because each move **earns the right** to the next: the personal story gives permission to define, the doubt-then-acceptance gives motivation to look at the evidence.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / time-anchoring:** "While researching" (sets scene in past); "Initially" (positions first belief); "after looking into it" (marks later verification).
- **Cause / consequence:** "as generally a derivative is supposed to 'decrease' a function" — "as" introduces the rule that *causes* the doubt.
- **Contrast / concession:** "However, after looking into it, I was astounded" — single pivot word that overturns the prior clause.
- **Specification / definition:** "The formula is defined as:" — formal marker that the next symbol block is canonical.
- **Evidence handling:** "are shown in the table below" — defers justification to a non-prose artefact.
- **Explanation verbs:** "defined as" (names); "is" (equates formula with quantity — implicit modelling verb).

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses the **"hook-then-formalise" pattern** — personal narrative → general-principle objection → acceptance → symbolic definition → evidence-pointer.

To replicate with a new idea, take these numbered steps:

1. **Pick a moment of personal discovery** — open with "While researching…" and name both the problem and the unexpected method you met.
2. **State the intuitive objection** — write "Initially, I thought… because…" and articulate the general rule that the new method appears to violate, in lay terms.
3. **Issue a clean reversal** — single sentence beginning "However," confirming the method works; this earns the reader's attention for the formal bit.
4. **Give the canonical definition** — one sentence with "is defined as:" followed by the symbolic equation, immediately mapped onto the *real-world quantity* it counts ("and the value of [thing] is: [expression]").
5. **Hand off to a visible artefact** — finish with "The first [N] [evaluations/values] … are shown in the table/figure below:" so the reader knows proof is coming, not more prose.
