## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **context → anomaly → personal motivation → problem statement** opener. The order matters:

1. **Goal of the system** — names what "solved" means so the rest has a reference point.
2. **Rules / mechanics** — sets the boundary of legality before introducing a famous breach.
3. **Historical anchor + citation** — lends authority and pins the topic in time.
4. **Unpack the specific anomaly** — the reader needs the concrete action before feeling the consequence.
5. **Surprising verdict** — the pivot that creates a question.
6. **Lead question ("why?")** — opens the investigation.
7. **Broadening question ("any others?")** — converts a curiosity into a research direction.
8. **Personal scenario raising stakes** — turns theory into a problem the writer faced.
9. **Functional payoff of the setup** — justifies the writer's project.
10. **Concrete problem encountered** — delivers the obstacle the investigation must solve.

This is the classic IB exploration opener: define → cite → surprise → personalise → surface the problem.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

Pattern used: **definition → rules → famous historical case → unpack → surprising verdict → question cascade → personal stakes → functional payoff → concrete problem.**

1. **Name the success condition** in one to-clause (what does "done" look like?).
2. **State one sharp rule** constraining legal moves (use "only" or "cannot").
3. **Anchor with a famous dated event** and a named case; attach a citation.
4. **Unpack the specific action** of that case on the canonical state (one verb).
5. **Deliver a counter-intuitive verdict** flagged with "Interestingly," or similar.
6. **Pivot to a "why?" question** introduced by "This led to…".
7. **Broaden with a "Furthermore," question** that asks if the anomaly is general.
8. **Drop into a personal scenario** using past tense and "when I attempted to…".
9. **Justify the setup** with a "which allowed me to…" benefit chain (two payoffs).
10. **Surface the concrete problem** with a quantified failure introduced by "because".

Following these ten moves in order reproduces the "objective + historical hook + personal motivation + obstacle" opener that an IB exploration uses to launch its investigation.
