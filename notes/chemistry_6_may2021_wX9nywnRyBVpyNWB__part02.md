## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Justification of the investigation** — first, because a reader of a Background section needs to know *why* this topic is being opened up; it is the licence for the next paragraph.
2. **Top-level definition of the device** — establishes the noun of the section ("Galvanic cells").
3. **Synonym/category bracket** — so the reader who knows it as "voltaic" is not lost.
4. **Mechanism clause attached to the definition** — defines *why* the device produces a value.
5. **Definition of the smaller structural unit (half-cell)** — zooms into the building block.
6. **Linking property (standard electrode potential) introduced** — bridges structure to the number used later.
7. **Procedural standardisation** — pins the number down (reference electrode, fixed T).
8. **Interpretive sentence** — converts the number into a chemical meaning.
9. **Directional/quantitative rule** — converts meaning into a comparative statement usable in analysis.

The order is *justification → device → mechanism → unit → measurement → standardisation → interpretation → rule*. Each move sets up the next: justification licences the topic; the device name licences the mechanism; the mechanism licences the structural unit; the structural unit licences the standardisation; the standardisation licences interpretation; interpretation licences the comparative rule. Replicate by always moving **claim → cause → component → quantification → standard → meaning → comparative consequence**.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**Skeleton A — "Literature-justification opener"**
SKELETON: "it is [time-frame adjective] to carry out this investigation as certain literature point out research in '[quoted gap statement]' ([source])."

1. **Slots:** Slot 1 = adverb of timeliness ("contemporarily", "currently", "increasingly"); Slot 2 = a verbatim gap claim from a source ("high temperature is still lacking…"); Slot 3 = an author/source bracket. Grammatically: concessive clause + noun ("research in…") + parenthetical citation.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** Slot 1 — pick a word that frames when the investigation matters now; Slot 2 — find one quoted sentence in a paper that names a missing study area and copy it verbatim; Slot 3 — name the author in brackets. Use past/present tense ("is") to keep it as ongoing relevance.
3. **Original fill:** "it is contemporarily relevant to carry out this investigation as certain literature point out research in 'high temperature is still lacking and researchers need to put more efforts to promote its development' (Xu and Wang)."
4. **Demo fill (different subject — perovskite photovoltaics):** "it is increasingly relevant to carry out this investigation as certain literature point out research in 'long-term stability under operational conditions remains the principal barrier to commercialisation' (Sert and Snaith)."

**Skeleton B — "Definition-with-synonym-and-mechanism"**
SKELETON: "[Term] (also known as [synonym]) [verb] [output] from [cause-process] due to [micro-mechanism]."

1. **Slots:** Slot 1 = primary technical noun; Slot 2 = alternate name in brackets; Slot 3 = verb of generation/production ("generate", "produce", "yield"); Slot 4 = measured quantity (Ecell, rate, yield %); Slot 5 = named chemical/process category ("spontaneous redox reactions"); Slot 6 = mechanistic phrase ("movement of electrons", "diffusion of ions").
2. **How to fill:** pick a device/phenomenon whose output you want to define; immediately supply its synonym; name the broad process class that drives it; finish with the particle/charge-level reason. Keep all six slots inside ONE sentence.
3. **Original fill:** "Galvanic cells (also known as voltaic cells) generate Ecell from spontaneous reduction-oxidation (redox) reactions due to the movement of electrons."
4. **Demo fill (different subject — polymer electrolyte membrane fuel cell):** "Polymer electrolyte membrane fuel cells (also known as PEMFCs) generate electrical current from spontaneous redox reactions involving hydrogen and oxygen due to the movement of protons across the membrane."

**Skeleton C — "Unit-construction-then-property"**
SKELETON: "[Units] are made by [construction verb]-ing [component A] into [component B]. The relationship between [component A] and [component B] is represented by [property name] ([unit])."

1. **Slots:** Slot 1 = plural name of the small unit ("Half cells"); Slot 2 = verb of assembly ("made by depositing", "constructed by immersing"); Slot 3 = first physical part ("a solid metal"); Slot 4 = second physical part ("a solution of its own metal ions"); Slot 5 = the measurable relationship ("the relationship between the solid metal and the solution"); Slot 6 = the symbol and SI unit (E°, V).
2. **How to fill:** name the sub-unit you just defined, describe how it is physically assembled in two parts, then immediately introduce the symbol that links those two parts. Two sentences, cause-then-symbol.
3. **Original fill:** "Half cells are made by depositing a solid metal into a solution of its own metal ions. The relationship between the solid metal and the solution of its metal ions is represented by standard electrode potentials (V)."
4. **Demo fill (different subject — enzyme kinetics):** "Enzyme–substrate complexes are made by binding an enzyme into the active site of its substrate molecule. The relationship between the substrate concentration and the reaction velocity is represented by the Michaelis constant (mM)."

**Skeleton D — "Standardisation-then-interpretation-then-rule"**
SKELETON: "These values are calculated when [unit] is attached to a [reference standard], which has a [reference property] of [value] at [condition] ([source]). [Property] provides a [adjective] measure of [underlying quality]. The more [comparative adjective] [property], the [comparative adjective] [underlying quality]."

1. **Slots:** three sentences. S1 — Slot 1: reference setup clause; Slot 2: name of standard; Slot 3: fixed value; Slot 4: fixed condition (T, p, time). 2. **How to fill:** S1 — find the *reference object* that makes your number reproducible, state its fixed value and condition; S2 — translate the number into the chemistry behind it using "provides a [adj] measure of…"; S3 — convert the interpretation into a monotonic rule. Three sentences, three jobs.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / procedural:**
- "These values are calculated when" — sets up the standardisation step.
- "at 298K" — pins the procedure to a fixed condition.
- "In a galvanic cell" — applies the rule to the named device.

**Cause / consequence:**
- "due to the movement of electrons" — mechanism clause attached to the definition.

**Specification / category bracketing:**
- "(also known as voltaic cells)" — synonym bracket.
- "in question" — points the property back to the named metal ion.
- "is represented by standard electrode potentials (V)" — names the symbol and unit.

**Evidence handling:**
- "certain literature point out research in 'high temperature is still lacking…'" — quotes the source as a gap claim.
- "(Xu and Wang)" / "(Brown and Ford)" — parenthetical citations attached to claims.

**Definition / explanation verbs:**
- "generate Ecell from" — defines output and source.
- "is represented by" — equates the relationship with a quantity.
- "provides a quantitative measure of" — converts a number into an interpretation.
- "is defined as / is made by" (implicit shape: "Half cells are made by depositing…") — construction verb.

**Comparative / rule-of-thumb:**
- "The more positive a standard electrode potential, the greater the reduction potential" — monotonic comparative rule.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses the **"device → mechanism → component → measurement → standard → interpretation → comparative rule"** explanation pattern (definition-then-unpack-then-standardise-then-apply). To explain a NEW idea:

1. **Name the device/phenomenon and bracket a known synonym** so a reader can recognise it.
2. **State the output the device produces and the broad cause** in the same sentence (verb of generation + process category).
3. **Add the micro-mechanism clause** ("due to …") so the cause has a reason.
4. **Define the smallest sub-unit and how it is physically assembled** in a single sentence.
5. **Link that sub-unit to a measurable property** with its symbol and unit in the next sentence.
6. **Standardise the property** by naming the reference object, its fixed value, and its fixed condition — in one sentence ending with a citation.
7. **Interpret the property** in a "provides a [adj] measure of [quality]" sentence so the number gains meaning.
8. **Convert the interpretation into a monotonic comparative rule** using a "the more X, the more Y" sentence so the idea becomes usable in analysis.

Each step hands the reader to the next: definition licences mechanism, mechanism licences component, component licences measurement, measurement licences standard, standard licences interpretation, interpretation licences rule.
