## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1 — Equipment Margin-of-Error Catalogue**
- Sentence 1 (continuation from previous section): *carry-forward context* — "equipment utilized will be calculated." Sets up that what follows are the numerical margins. Hands to next sentence by **delivering the object** of the calculation just announced.
- Sentence 2: *stated datum (evidence)* — "Margin of error in the mass balance: ±0.001g". Supplies the first instrument's tolerance. Hands to next by **parallel specification** (another instrument follows the same template).
- Sentence 3: *stated datum (evidence)* — "Margin of error in the 250 ml graduated cylinder: ±0.5ml". Same slot, second instrument. Hands to next by **parallel specification**.
- Sentence 4: *stated datum (evidence)* — "Margin of error in the 50 ml graduated cylinder: ±0.4ml". Third and last tolerance. Hands to next paragraph by **trigger** — the reader now has the numbers, so the next move shows them applied.

**Paragraph 2 — Percentage Uncertainty for the 25 ml Volume Measurement**
- Sentence 1: *procedure + basis (claim)* — "25ml of each NiCl2 was measured using the 50ml graduated cylinder, thus the percentage uncertainty for the same would be the following:" Names the volume, names the instrument, and primes the reader for a calculation. Hands to next by **calculation handoff** ("the following:").
- Sentence 2: *worked calculation (evidence)* — inline fraction "0.4 / 25 × 100 = 1.60 %". Substitutes the margin from the catalogue into the percentage formula. Hands to next paragraph by **new instance** — a different instrument and a different measured value.

**Paragraph 3 — Percentage Uncertainty for the Minimum Mass**
- Sentence 1: *procedure + variable-cause statement (claim + mechanism)* — "The mass balance used to weigh the NiCl2 … The smaller the measured value, the higher the uncertainty." Introduces the instrument and the principle that justifies the choice of the smallest mass. Hands to next by **operationalising** the principle just stated (Substituting…).
- Sentence 2: *worked calculation (evidence)* — "Substituting the minimum measured mass (30mg) into the same we get the following:". Hand-off by **formula echo** ("the same") and by priming ("the following:").
- Sentence 3: *worked calculation (evidence)* — inline fraction "0.001 / 0.03 × 100 = 3.33%". Concludes the calculation. Hands to next paragraph by **new instance** — the third and final instrument.

**Paragraph 4 — Percentage Uncertainty for the 250 ml Adjustment**
- Sentence 1: *procedure (claim)* — "The 250ml graduated cylinder was used to adjust the NiCl2 solutions to the final volume of 1 litre." States the role of the third instrument. Hands to next by **reminder of its tolerance** (continuing the parallel structure).
- Sentence 2: *stated datum (evidence)* — "The 250ml graduated cylinder has a margin of error of ±0.5ml." Hands to next by **calculation handoff** ("the following:" is implicit from the layout).
- Sentence 3: *worked calculation (evidence)* — inline fraction "0.5 / 250 × 100 = 0.2 %". Hands to next paragraph by **aggregation promise** (next move combines all results).

**Paragraph 5 — Aggregation and Final Verdict**
- Sentence 1: *aggregation + verdict (claim)* — "Adding together all the uncertainties, we get the maximum percentage uncertainty (%) of 5.12%." Collapses the three contributions into one headline number, satisfying the section's aim stated in its title.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

The move order is **catalogue → per-instrument application → aggregation → verdict**:

1. **Instrument tolerance catalogue first.** State every margin of error in one block so the reader knows the raw tolerances before any calculation begins. This sets up the substitution step later.
2. **Per-instrument calculation, in the order the instruments were used in the procedure.** For each: name the instrument, name what was measured, display the substitution into the percentage formula. Repeating this unit per instrument keeps each uncertainty auditable on its own.
3. **One explanatory sentence per calculation when the choice of value is non-obvious** (e.g. why use the minimum mass). This justifies the substitution before showing it.
4. **Aggregation last.** Sum the percentages and announce the combined maximum percentage uncertainty. This delivers the single number the section title promised.

Why this order works: catalogue before use → reader holds the data; per-instrument before sum → each contribution is traceable; justification before substitution → the chosen value is defensible; sum last → the verdict is a clean deduction, not an assertion.

### SKELETON A — "Tolerance Catalogue Paragraph"
**Slot template:**
> [Carry-over sentence from previous section stating the calculation is now done]. [Instrument A] [noun phrase] had/has a margin of error of ±[value][unit]. [Instrument B] [noun phrase] had/has a margin of error of ±[value][unit]. [Instrument C] [noun phrase] had/has a margin of error of ±[value][unit].

1. **What each slot holds:**
   - Slot 1: a past-tense bridging sentence announcing the upcoming calculation.
   - Slots 2–4: identical structures of [Apparatus name] + [brief role] + "margin of error of ±X unit", listed in parallel.
   - Grammatical shape: noun phrase + "margin of error of ±" + number + unit, repeated 2–4 times.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** list every measuring instrument you used; copy its absolute precision straight from the manufacturer's spec or your lab handout; write one sentence per instrument using the same wording template so they read as a set.
3. **Original filled version:** "equipment utilized will be calculated. Margin of error in the mass balance: ±0.001g / Margin of error in the 250 ml graduated cylinder: ±0.5ml / Margin of error in the 50 ml graduated cylinder: ±0.4ml".
4. **Demonstration fill with a new idea:** "The tolerances of the calorimetry apparatus used will now be stated. Margin of error in the digital thermometer: ±0.1 °C. Margin of error in the 100 ml beaker: ±1.0 ml. Margin of error in the top-loading balance: ±0.01 g."

### SKELETON B — "Single-Instrument Percentage Uncertainty Paragraph"
**Slot template:**
> [Volume/mass] of each [sample] was measured using the [instrument], thus the percentage uncertainty for the same would be the following: [margin] / [measured value] × 100 = [result] %.

1. **What each slot holds:**
   - Slot 1: a procedure sentence (past tense) naming the measured quantity, the sample type, and the instrument.
   - Slot 2: a primed worked calculation (fraction × 100) presented as a block.
   - Grammatical shape: passive procedure sentence → labelled calculation.
2. **How to fill:** state what you measured and with what; take the margin from your catalogue; divide by the actual measured value; multiply by 100; write it as a stacked fraction.
3. **Original filled version:** "25ml of each NiCl2 was measured using the 50ml graduated cylinder, thus the percentage uncertainty for the same would be the following: 0.4 / 25 × 100 = 1.60 %."
4. **Demonstration fill with a new idea:** "30.0 cm³ of each sugar solution was measured using the 25 cm³ pipette, thus the percentage uncertainty for the same would be the following: 0.06 / 30.0 × 100 = 0.20 %."

### SKELETON C — "Justified Substitution Paragraph"
**Slot template:**
> [Instrument] was used to [action]. [Principle linking the chosen value to the result]. Substituting [chosen value] ([reason for choice]) into the same we get the following: [margin] / [chosen value] × 100 = [result] %.

1. **What each slot holds:**
   - Slot 1: past-tense procedure statement.
   - Slot 2: a general rule that explains why a particular measured value is the most conservative (worst-case).
   - Slot 3: a primed worked calculation using that conservative value.
   - Grammatical shape: procedure sentence → general causal/conditional sentence → calculation.
2. **How to fill:** name the instrument; state the conservative rule (smallest/largest value gives the largest % uncertainty); pick the extreme measured value; substitute into the same percentage formula.
3. **Original filled version:** "The mass balance used to weigh the NiCl2 … The smaller the measured value, the higher the uncertainty. Substituting the minimum measured mass (30mg) into the same we get the following: 0.001 / 0.03 × 100 = 3.33%."
4. **Demonstration fill with a new idea:** "The 25 cm³ pipette was used to deliver the acid. The smaller the delivered volume, the higher the percentage uncertainty. Substituting the minimum pipetted volume (5.00 cm³) into the same we get the following: 0.06 / 5.00 × 100 = 1.20 %."

### SKELETON D — "Aggregation and Maximum Paragraph"
**Slot template:**
> Adding together all the uncertainties, we get the maximum percentage uncertainty (%) of [sum] %.

1. **What each slot holds:**
   - Slot 1: a single summation sentence in present tense.
   - Grammatical shape: gerund phrase (Adding together) + noun (all the uncertainties) + reporting verb (we get) + object noun (the maximum percentage uncertainty) + value.
2. **How to fill:** add the percentages you obtained in the per-instrument blocks; quote the sum to two decimal places; mark it as the maximum (worst-case).
3. **Original filled version:** "Adding together all the uncertainties, we get the maximum percentage uncertainty (%) of 5.12%."
4. **Demonstration fill with a new idea:** "Adding together all the uncertainties, we get the maximum percentage uncertainty (%) of 2.45%."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / priming a calculation**
- "the percentage uncertainty for the same would be the following:" (paragraph 2 sentence 1) — primes a worked calculation that follows immediately.
- "Substituting … into the same we get the following:" (paragraph 3 sentence 2) — echoes an earlier formula and primes a worked calculation.
- "the maximum percentage uncertainty (%) of 5.12%" (final sentence) — closes with a summation verdict.

**Specification / role assignment**
- "The mass balance used to weigh the NiCl2" (paragraph 3 sentence 1) — assigns the instrument to the variable it measured.
- "The 250ml graduated cylinder was used to adjust the NiCl2 solutions" (paragraph 4 sentence 1) — assigns the instrument to its procedural role.

**Cause / mechanism (conservative-value rule)**
- "The smaller the measured value, the higher the uncertainty." (paragraph 3 sentence 1) — comparative correlation stated as a general rule before the substitution that depends on it.

**Evidence handling / displayed calculation**
- "0.4 / 25 × 100 = 1.60 %" (paragraph 2 sentence 2) — fraction presented vertically, percentage quoted to two decimals.
- "0.001 / 0.03 × 100 = 3.33%" (paragraph 3 sentence 3) — same template, units dropped because value is already in grams.
- "0.5 / 250 × 100 = 0.2 %" (paragraph 4 sentence 3) — same template, one decimal because value is small.

**Stating tolerance (evidence catalogue)**
- "Margin of error in the [instrument]: ±[value][unit]" (paragraph 1 sentences 2–4) — repeated three times with identical phrasing so the reader can scan the list.

**Aggregation verb**
- "Adding together all the uncertainties, we get" (final sentence) — single verb phrase that announces the sum.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses the **tolerance-catalog → justified substitution → per-instrument percentage → summation** pattern.

To explain the maximum percentage uncertainty of a new measurement, follow these steps in order:

1. **State the aim in one carry-over line** that announces you will now calculate the percentage uncertainty of the equipment used (e.g. "The tolerances of the apparatus used will now be calculated.").
2. **List every measuring instrument and its absolute margin of error in one block**, using the identical phrasing "[Apparatus]: ±[number][unit]" so the reader can see the raw tolerances at a glance.
3. **For each instrument, write a short procedure sentence** naming the quantity measured, the sample, and the instrument used (past tense, passive acceptable).
4. **If the chosen measured value needs justifying** (because you are using the smallest or largest value rather than the mean), add a one-sentence rule ("The smaller the measured value, the higher the uncertainty.") immediately before the substitution.
5. **Display the substitution as a vertical fraction × 100 = result %**, using the margin from step 2 and the chosen measured value; quote the result to two decimals.
6. **Repeat steps 3–5 for every instrument**, in the order the instruments appear in your procedure.
7. **Finish with one aggregation sentence**: "Adding together all the uncertainties, we get the maximum percentage uncertainty (%) of [sum] %."

The reader can now audit each contribution independently and see the final worst-case percentage in a single line.
