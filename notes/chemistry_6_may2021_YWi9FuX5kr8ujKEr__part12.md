## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This section's ordered moves are: **1. Author/Creator → 2. Title of Work → 3. Publication Details (place, publisher, year) → 4. Specific Locator (page range or URL) → 5. Access Date.**

The order matters because each element sets up the next:

- **Author first** establishes WHO is responsible for the information, which the reader needs to judge credibility before reading what was said.
- **Title second** answers WHAT was said — required once the authority is known.
- **Publication details third** situate the work in time and by whom, dating and authenticating the title.
- **Locator fourth** pinpoints the EXACT portion consulted, because a citation must be reproducible.
- **Access date last** proves the source was live and unchanged on that date, closing the citation.

A student replicating this for a different topic would: sort entries alphabetically by author/organization surname, then within each entry apply the five-move sequence.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**Skeleton A — Web source with corporate author:**
   SKELETON: "[Organization]. "[Article Title]." [Short Publisher Tag], [Publication Date], [Full URL]. Accessed [Day Month Year]."

1. Slots & shapes: organization (proper noun phrase, no period inside); article title (in quotation marks, sentence case); publisher abbreviation (single capitalized word); date (Day Month Year or Month Year); URL (full https path); access date (Accessed + Day Month Year).
2. How to fill with a different idea: pick an organization with an article-style web page; copy the exact page title into quotation marks; abbreviate the site to a single recognizable token; record when the page itself was posted; paste the canonical URL; record the day you consulted it.
3. Original: Environmental Business Specialists. "PH Testing in Wastewater Treatment." EBS, 16 Dec. 2010, www.ebsbiowizard.com/ph-testing-in-wastewater-treatment-964/. Accessed 11 Feb. 2021.
4. Demonstration fill (different idea): World Health Organization. "Lead in Drinking Water." WHO, 15 Mar. 2022, www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/lead-in-drinking-water. Accessed 4 Jun. 2023.

**Skeleton B — Print textbook with corporate/personal author:**
   SKELETON: "[Author Surname], [First Initial(s)]. [Book Title]. [Edition], [Place of Publication], [Publisher], [Year], pp. [X]–[Y]."

1. Slots & shapes: surname first, comma, initials with periods (Last, F.G.); italicized book title; edition numeral with "ed."; city; publisher; year; page range with "pp."
2. How to fill: pick a textbook you actually opened; invert the author's name; copy the title exactly; record the edition you used; record city and publisher as on the title page; record the copyright year; record the inclusive page numbers you consulted.
3. Original: Bylikin, Sergey, et al. Chemistry Course Companion. Great Clarendon Street, Oxford, United Kingdom, Oxford University Press, 2014, pp. 209–226.
4. Demonstration fill (different idea): Brown, Catrin, et al. Chemistry for the IB Diploma. 2nd ed., London, Hodder Education, 2014, pp. 145–167.

**Skeleton C — MSDS / datasheet PDF:**

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

This section is non-prose (citation list), so connectives/conjunctions are absent. The "vocabulary" that expresses the citation idea consists of structural tokens, grouped by job:

- **Authority attribution tokens:** "et al." (Bylikin, Sergey, et al.), "The Editors of" (The Editors of Encyclopaedia Britannica) — mark collective or institutional authorship.
- **Title-isolating tokens:** quotation marks ("Water Hardness and PH.", "PH Testing in Wastewater Treatment.") — visually separate article titles from book titles.
- **Edition/place markers:** "Third ed.," "Second ed.," "Great Clarendon Street, Oxford" — pin the specific print run consulted.
- **Locator markers:** "pp. 209–226," "pp. 303–304," "pp. 283–321" — point to the exact pages used.
- **Date markers (publication):** "2014," "16 Dec. 2010," "31 July 2017" — date the source itself.
- **Verification tokens:** "Accessed 11 Feb. 2021," "Accessed 10 Feb. 2021," "Accessed 5 Jan. 2021" — stamp when the URL was confirmed live.
- **URL tokens:** full https paths immediately follow the comma after publisher/year, with no connective — the comma itself does the linking job.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section does NOT explain an idea in the explanatory sense — its pattern is **attribution → location → verification**, not definition → unpack → example. To replicate the citation logic for a new bibliography:

1. **Identify the responsible party.** Write the author or organization exactly as you want it alphabetized (surname first for persons; full name for organizations).
2. **Isolate the work.** Place a short work (article, webpage, video) in quotation marks; leave a long work (book, report, MSDS) in plain text or italics. Keep the title sentence-case for articles, title-case for books.
3. **Pin the version.** For books, add edition, place, publisher, and year in that order, separated by commas. For web sources, state the publication or last-updated date.
4. **Give the locator.** For print, write "pp. X–Y" or "p. X." For online, paste the full URL immediately after the date, with no connective.
5. **Stamp verification.** End every online source with "Accessed [Day Month Year]." — this closes the citation by proving the source was retrievable on that date.
6. **Alphabetize the whole list** by first letter of the entry (surname for individuals; organizational name otherwise), so the reader can scan for any cited work.

This sequence — *who, what, where/when, where to find it, when you checked* — is the replicable idea-expression pattern of a bibliography.
