## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1** (all four numbered lines function as one procedural unit):

1. **Sentence 1 — material/quantity claim.** "3 bottles of same water sample were collected from a nearby pond shown in Figure 1." Specifies *what* and *where*. Hands the reader forward by raising an implicit question — *why three, and what comes next with them?* — which the next sentence answers.
2. **Sentence 2 — first procedural step with justification.** "Bottles were left in open air overnight for oxygen saturation." Names the action ("left in open air overnight") and in the same clause explains the mechanism ("for oxygen saturation to ensure initial dissolved oxygen concentration would be known"). The "to ensure…" clause hands the reader forward by signalling the preparatory phase is done; a *next* action is now due.
3. **Sentence 3 — sealing step with consequence rationale.** "The lids were replaced, and water samples were stored in air-tight bottles." Action stated, then reason ("to prevent any air bubbles from being formed"). Closes the preparation loop; hands the reader to the experimental phase by changing the temporal marker to the following day.
4. **Sentence 4 — setup phase with controlled-variable specification.** "The following day, marked as day 1, water samples were carefully poured into 6 separate 250cm3 volumetric flasks." Introduces the day label, names the action ("poured into… flasks"), then enumerates the labelling system ("A, A1, B, B1, C, C1") and the independent-variable manipulation ("pH in Flask A… was kept unaltered… lowered by… raised by…"). The text is cut off mid-specification, but the move is clear: having set up the day, the writer is now unpacking the *controlled vs. varied* conditions in parallel triplet form, which would hand the reader to the measurement procedure.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

A method section in IB Bio EE style moves in this order, and each move sets up the next:

1. **Source + location of sample** (so the reader knows *what* is being tested and *where* it came from — sets up the need to standardise it).
2. **Standardisation of the sample** (saturation, sealing — sets up that any later change is the IV's effect, not noise).
3. **Storage to preserve the standardised state** (air-tight bottles — sets up transfer into the test apparatus without altering the starting condition).
4. **Labelled distribution into replicate flasks** (naming A/A1, B/B1, C/C1 — sets up the controlled-vs-varied split).
5. **Independent variable manipulation per flask** (control, raised, lowered — sets up that only pH changes between groups).
6. *(Implied, cut off)* **Dependent variable measurement procedure** — would be set up by the triplets above.

The logic: *source → fix starting state → preserve state → distribute replicates → vary one thing → measure*. A student replicating this on, say, soil salinity and seed germination, would collect soil from one site, dry and sieve it to a uniform texture, seal it to keep moisture constant, divide into labelled pots (S, S1, T, T1, U, U1), keep one pair as control, raise salt in one pair, lower it in another, then measure germination rate.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**Skeleton A — Source establishment**
```
SKELETON: "[N] [units] of same [sample type] were collected from a [location] shown in Figure [#]."
```

1. **Slot 1 — `[N]`**: a small integer (3, 5, 10). **Slot 2 — `[units]`**: container noun ("bottles", "petri dishes", "core samples"). **Slot 3 — `[sample type]`**: the material being tested ("water sample", "leaf disc", "soil sample"). **Slot 4 — `[location]`**: a nearby concrete site ("nearby pond", "school field", "local river"). **Slot 5 — `Figure [#]`**: cross-reference to a figure that anchors the location.
2. **How to fill differently**: pick a real local site, decide how many replicates you can realistically handle, name the container, then draft as one past-tense passive clause ending with a figure pointer.
3. **Original**: "3 bottles of same water sample were collected from a nearby pond shown in Figure 1."
4. **Demo fill (different idea)**: "5 leaf discs of same spinach sample were collected from a local market stall shown in Figure 2."

---

**Skeleton B — Standardisation with purpose clause**
```
SKELETON: "[Containers] were [treatment verb]-ed [time/manner phrase] for [mechanism] to ensure [measurable outcome] would be known (source)."
```

1. **Slot 1 — `[Containers]`**: plural noun matching Skeleton A's unit. **Slot 2 — `[treatment verb]`**: action that fixes the variable ("left", "heated", "rinsed", "dried"). **Slot 3 — `[time/manner phrase]`**: concrete duration or condition ("overnight", "at 25 °C for 2 hours"). **Slot 4 — `[mechanism]`**: the physical/chemical reason ("oxygen saturation", "thermal equilibrium"). **Slot 5 — `[measurable outcome]`**: the starting value you need locked down ("initial dissolved oxygen concentration", "baseline mass"). **Slot 6 — `(source)`**: parenthetical citation showing the method is grounded.
2. **How to fill differently**: identify the one variable that must be equal at t = 0, then ask *what physical process will lock it?* Verb-tense that process, attach a duration, and end with a citation that justifies the procedure.
3. **Original**: "Bottles were left in open air overnight for oxygen saturation to ensure initial dissolved oxygen concentration would be known (Kognity)."
4. **Demo fill (different idea)**: "Cores were oven-heated at 60 °C for 24 hours for moisture evaporation to ensure initial dry mass would be known (Kognity)."

---

**Skeleton C — Preservation rationale**
```
SKELETON: "The [closures] were [action verb]-ed, and [samples] were [stored verb] in [container type] to prevent [interference] from being [verb form]-ed."
```

1. **Slot 1 — `[closures]` / `[samples]`**: paired nouns matching the prior sentence. **Slot 2 — `[interference]`**: the thing you do not want introduced ("air bubbles", "microbial growth", "evaporation"). **Slot 3 — `[verb form]`**: gerund showing the dynamic form of that interference ("formed", "established").
2. **How to fill differently**: name the closing action (lids, parafilm, caps), name the storage vessel, and complete the *to prevent* clause with whatever would invalidate the standardised state.
3. **Original**: "The lids were replaced, and water samples were stored in air-tight bottles to prevent any air bubbles from being formed."
4. **Demo fill (different idea)**: "The caps were sealed, and leaf discs were stored in damp Petri dishes to prevent any wilting from being initiated."

---

**Skeleton D — Labelled replicate distribution with parallel IV manipulation**
```
SKELETON: "The following day, marked as day [#], [samples] were [transfer verb]-ed into [N] separate [volume]-[vessel type] – [label list], ensuring no [artefact]. [Variable] in [Pair 1] was [state verb], [Variable] in [Pair 2] was [manipulation verb]-ed by [treatment] and [Variable] in [Pair 3] was [manipulation verb]-ed by [treatment]…"
```

1. **Slots**: a temporal marker ("day 1"), the transfer action ("poured", "transferred"), the replicate count (matching 2× the condition count), the volume + vessel, the label list in pairs (X, X1…), an "ensuring no artefact" clause (mirrors the previous sentence's concern), then a tripartite parallel listing that contrasts control vs. raised vs. lowered.
2. **How to fill differently**: fix the number of conditions first (control + 2 treatments → 6 flasks in pairs), then write the three IV clauses in identical grammatical shape ("was kept…", "was lowered by…", "was raised by…") so the parallelism itself signals the experimental design.
3. **Original**: "The following day, marked as day 1, water samples were carefully poured into 6 separate 250cm3 volumetric flasks – A, A1, B, B1, C, C1, ensuring no formation of air bubbles. pH in Flask A and A1 was kept unaltered, pH in Flask B and B1 was lowered by adding 6 drops of HCl and pH in Flask C and C1 was raised by adding…"
4. **Demo fill (different idea)**: "The following day, marked as day 1, soil samples were carefully weighed into 6 separate 50 cm³ pots – S, S1, T, T1, U, U1, ensuring no loss of moisture. Salinity in Pot S and S1 was kept at ambient, salinity in Pot T and T1 was raised by adding 2 g of NaCl and salinity in Pot U and U1 was lowered by rinsing with distilled water."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / time-marking**: *"The following day, marked as day 1"* — uses a temporal anchor to pivot from preparation to procedure.
- **Purpose / mechanism (explanation verbs)**: *"for oxygen saturation to ensure initial dissolved oxygen concentration"* — chains a physical mechanism to a measurement outcome inside one *for… to ensure…* clause.
- **Causation / prevention rationale**: *"to prevent any air bubbles from being formed"* — uses *to prevent* + passive gerund to express a negative consequence the design must avoid.
- **Specification / distribution labelling**: *"into 6 separate 250cm3 volumetric flasks – A, A1, B, B1, C, C1"* — uses an em-dash and paired-letter system to specify replicates compactly.
- **Parallel IV-manipulation phrasing**: *"was kept unaltered, … was lowered by adding … drops, … was raised by adding"* — uses three grammatically identical past-passive clauses to make the controlled-vs-varied design visible in syntax.
- **Cautious-procedure adverb**: *"carefully poured … ensuring no formation of air bubbles"* — pairs an adverb with an *ensuring* clause to flag procedural care without naming the threat again.
- **Evidence handling / citation placement**: *"(Kognity)"* — places the source in parentheses at clause-end, attaching it to the action rather than to a claim, signalling method provenance.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern this section uses to explain *how* the experiment works is a **preparation → justification → preservation → replicated-manipulation chain**, where every action carries its own embedded *because*. To reproduce it for a new idea:

1. **State the source concretely.** One sentence: quantity, what it is, where it came from, with a figure reference. (Establishes *what*.)
2. **Perform one standardising action and embed its mechanism.** Use a *for [process] to ensure [measurable baseline]* clause so the reader sees both what you did and why the baseline is now trustworthy.
3. **Seal the standardised state.** Add a *to prevent [interference] from being [verb]-ed* clause that explicitly names the failure mode you are guarding against.
4. **Re-mark time.** Use "The following day, marked as day 1" (or equivalent) to signal the experimental phase has begun.
5. **Distribute into labelled replicates.** Name the count, the vessel, and the label pairs (X, X1, X2…) in a single em-dash list, then add an *ensuring no [artefact]* clause that echoes the previous sentence's concern.
6. **Unpack the IV in three grammatically parallel clauses.** Control state → increased by → decreased by, all in the same past-passive shape, so the reader sees the design in the syntax itself. Each clause names the reagent, the dose, and the target flask.
7. *(Implied next)* **Hand to the measurement step.** The triplet of conditions is the setup; what follows is the DV readout over time.

The logic the reader is walked through is: *where it came from → why its starting value is trustworthy → why that value survives until measurement → what changes between groups → (what is measured)*. Each sentence both *does* a procedure and *justifies* that procedure, so no sentence is purely descriptive — every action carries its own embedded *because*.
