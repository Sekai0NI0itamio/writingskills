2. **State the observed trend** in the current graph with an in-equation cue (negative coefficient). *Why second:* the reader needs to know what shape of behaviour is being defended.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / cause-consequence**
- *"therefore the linear equation will be used"* — locks in a methodological choice as a direct consequence.
- *"therefore when applying an increase in temperature"* — sets up a conditional causal chain.
- *"By doing so the changes in heat…are minimized"* — "By doing so" compresses the prior mechanism into a single causal pivot.

**Contrast / concession**
- *"however the reactants are favoured"* — concedes a counter-direction (heat minimised *but* reactants favoured).

**Specification / quantification**
- *"to a greater extent"* — specifies magnitude of the directional shift.
- *"an R2 value of 0.9909"* — converts a verbal claim into a numerical specification.

**Evidence handling**
- *"since no literature was found to support this idea"* — "since" introduces a justification; absence-of-evidence is itself treated as evidence.
- *"because its equation has a negative coefficient"* — "because" anchors the verbal trend in an in-equation cue.
- *"The trendline suggests that a negative linear correlation exists"* — "suggests" softens a claim that the data alone cannot prove.
- *"according to Le Chatelier's Principle"* — "according to" is the authority-handling connective that lets a named principle do the explanatory work.

**Explanation verbs / definitional moves**
- *"is not a good representation of the relationship"* — modelling-verdict verb.
- *"A galvanic cell is always exothermic and spontaneous"* — definitional copula, two permanent properties.
- *"equilibrium shifts to the left favouring endothermic reaction"* — directional mechanism verb + outcome verb.
- *"this is reflected in the average Ecell and 𝐾𝑐"* — reflective verb that maps theory back onto measured quantities.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern name:** *Statistical Validation → Cross-Case Precedent → Theory Bridge → Mechanism → Implication on Measured Variables.*

This is a **"lab-number-to-principle"** explanation: the trend is locked in *before* the theory is invoked, so the theory reads as an explanation rather than a justification.

**Step-by-step replication on a NEW idea:**

1. **Lock the model.** Open by disposing of any rival curve/polynomial, citing a specific type of evidence you searched for and didn't find ("no literature…", "no datasheet…"). State your chosen model in the *consequence* clause ("therefore the [X] will be used"). Do not yet explain *why* the model is correct — only that the rival is rejected.
2. **Name the trend in the new graph.** Use the template "[Graph N] shows a [direction] correlation between [A] and [B] because its equation has a [sign] coefficient on 𝑥." The "because" must point to a numerical cue inside the equation — never to the scatter points alone.
3. **Quantify the strength.** State the R² value to four decimal places, then attach a single strength adjective ("the correlation is strong"). This converts a visual claim into a defensible statistical claim.
4. **Borrow precedent from a parallel case.** Write "For the same reasons as [earlier graph], [chosen model] will be taken for further analysis instead of [alternative]." This avoids re-arguing the modelling choice twice.
5. **Unify both findings.** Write one generalising sentence that packages both graphs' trends into a single unit that theory must explain ("Both [trend A] and [trend B] are supported by scientific theory"). This sentence does no explanation — it only sets up the next move.
6. **Define the system and run the principle.** In one long sentence, state the system's two permanent properties ("[System] is always [P1] and [P2]"), apply a perturbation ("therefore when applying [change]"), invoke a named principle ("according to [Principle]"), and predict the directional response ("[system] [shifts/tilts/moves] favouring [outcome] to [extent]"). Keep all five pieces in one sentence — splitting them weakens the causal chain.
7. **Map back to your measured variables.** End with "By doing so [consequence 1] however [consequence 2], this is reflected in the [measured quantity 1] and [measured quantity 2]." The "this is reflected in" verb is the hinge that converts theoretical language back into lab data.
8. **Hand forward.** If the paragraph continues, open the next sentence with the next measured quantity ("The [next variable] created in the…"), so the reader is led forward by variable name rather than by topic name.
