## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

### Paragraph 1
1. **Recommendation move (continuation signal):** *"He also urged the government to invest the carbon tax revenues"* — opens with "also," linking back to a prior point made by the speaker; sets up a policy demand. Hands the reader to the next sentence via **concession**, because a recommendation needs the tension that complicates it.
2. **Concession move (quoted voice):** *"we have to make this transition away from coal … but there are workers and communities"* — pairs a "yes, but" structure; the "but" announces the obstacle the recommendation must address. Hands the reader forward by **specifying** who is at stake in that "but."
3. **Implication move (solution re-stated):** *"Those [carbon tax] revenues should be used to make sure the poor are not worse off."* — narrows the broad recommendation to a specific target group (the poor). Hands the reader to the next paragraph by **shifting scale outward** (from workers/poor to the country as a whole).

### Paragraph 2
1. **Context move (scale-up factual backdrop):** *"South Africa gets some 90 per cent of its electricity … from coal, a high emissions source."* — quantifies the dependency that makes the recommendation urgent. Hands forward by **establishing the gap** between current reality and the Paris commitment that follows.
2. **Commitment move (policy target):** *"As its contribution to the Paris Agreement, it committed to peak emissions between 2020 and 2025"* — names the official trajectory. Hands forward by **temporal pivot** ("Since that 2015 pledge") which becomes the trigger for the next sentence.
3. **Evidence move (market mechanism):** *"wind and solar power generators have started to undercut new coal plants on price"* — shows the transition is already economically driven, not just policy-driven. Hands forward by **parallel contrast** introduced with "At the same time."
4. **Counter-evidence move (persistent problem):** *"citizens face rolling blackouts as ageing, poorly maintained coal plants fail to meet demand."* — the "same time" sets up a contradiction: progress on price coexists with unreliability. Closes the section on unresolved tension.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This section is a **policy-context paragraph pair** following a quoted expert. The sequence is:

1. **Expert recommendation** — what the figure wants done (the "ask").
2. **Concession within the quote** — acknowledge what complicates the ask (workers, communities, poor).
3. **Targeted implication** — narrow the ask to a specific redistributive use (carbon tax revenues → protect the poor).
4. **National-scale factual scene-setter** — quantify the current fossil dependency.
5. **International policy commitment** — anchor the country's stated trajectory.
6. **Market evidence of change** — show forces already moving in the recommended direction.
7. **Counter-evidence of difficulty** — show that change is incomplete/contradictory.

The **why** of this order: the speaker's authority is established *before* the facts; facts are ordered from **dependency → commitment → progress → persistent problem**, so the reader moves from "why change is needed" to "why change is possible" to "why change is still hard." That arc lets the recommendation in sentences 1–3 feel both justified and unfinished.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

### Skeleton A — "Authority → concession → targeted solution"
- **SKELETON:** "[Authority figure] also urged [decision-maker] to [broad policy action] in [specific lever]. '[Yes, X must happen], but [stakeholders affected by X],' [he/she] said. 'Those [lever resources] should be used to make sure [vulnerable group] are not worse off.'"
- **Slot grammar:** slot 1 = past-tense reporting clause with "also"; slot 2 = direct quote with "but"; slot 3 = "Those … should be used to make sure …" modal recommendation.
- **How to fill with a different idea:** slot 1 — pick a named expert who has *already* spoken earlier and add a related demand; slot 2 — pick a transition/policy with an obvious cost-bearer; slot 3 — pick a revenue or tool and a group that could be hurt.
- **Original:** *"He also urged the government to invest the carbon tax revenues in expanding access to clean energy … 'Yes, we have to make this transition away from coal … but there are workers and communities that depend on the coal value chain.' … 'Those [carbon tax] revenues should be used to make sure the poor are not worse off.'"*
- **Demonstration fill (different topic):** *She also urged the ministry to redirect sugar-levy revenues into school meal programmes. "Yes, we have to curb sugary-drink consumption, but there are smallholder cane farmers tied to this industry," she said. "Those revenues should be used to make sure rural households are not worse off."*

### Skeleton B — "Dependency stat → policy pledge → market mechanism → persistent flaw"
- **SKELETON:** "[Country] gets [X%] of its [resource] from [problematic source], a [adjective] source. As its contribution to [international agreement], it committed to [trajectory: peak/plateau/decline]. Since that [year] pledge, [new actors] have started to [mechanism that undercuts old actors]. At the same time, [public] face [problem] as [old system] fails to [deliver]."
- **Slot grammar:** slot 1 = present-tense statistic with appositive descriptor; slot 2 = "As its contribution to … it committed to …"; slot 3 = "Since that [year] pledge … have started to …"; slot 4 = "At the same time, [subject] face [noun phrase] as …".
- **How to fill with a different idea:** slot 1 — find a country and a percentage dependency on a dirty/scarce resource; slot 2 — name the relevant international agreement and a peak/plateau/decline curve; slot 3 — pick the clean/cheap disruptor and what it undercuts; slot 4 — pick the public-facing failure that runs in parallel.
- **Original:** *"South Africa gets some 90 per cent of its electricity … from coal … As its contribution to the Paris Agreement, it committed to peak emissions between 2020 and 2025 … Since that 2015 pledge, wind and solar power generators have started to undercut new coal plants on price … At the same time, citizens face rolling blackouts as ageing, poorly maintained coal plants fail to meet demand."*
- **Demonstration fill (different topic):** *Brazil gets roughly 65 per cent of its transport fuel from road diesel, a high-polluting source. As its contribution to the Glasgow Fuel Declaration, it committed to cap diesel sales by 2030, then plateau and decline. Since that 2021 pledge, electric two-wheeler manufacturers have begun to undercut petrol mopeds on total cost of ownership, disrupting the petrostate distribution model. At the same time, commuters face gridlock as underfunded bus networks fail to absorb displaced car trips.*

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / continuation:** *"He also urged the government to invest"* — adds to a prior point already on the table.
- **Concession (yes-but):** *"Yes, we have to make this transition away from coal … but there are workers"* — concedes the policy while flagging its cost.
- **Specification / narrowing:** *"Those [carbon tax] revenues should be used to make sure the poor are not worse off."* — narrows a broad ask to a group + a tool.
- **Causal/contextual framing:** *"As its contribution to the Paris Agreement, it committed to peak"* — frames the pledge as causally tied to an international duty.
- **Temporal pivot:** *"Since that 2015 pledge, wind and solar power generators have started"* — uses "Since" to trigger a cause-and-time link.
- **Parallel contrast:** *"At the same time, citizens face rolling blackouts"* — introduces a coexisting contradiction.
- **Evidence-handling verbs:** *"have started to undercut new coal plants on price"* — present perfect + "started to" marks an ongoing market shift as evidence.
- **Explanation/appositive labelling:** *"a high emissions source"* — appositive that classifies the previously named fuel.
- **Quoted-voice markers:** *"he said"* — frames the concession and the implication as direct speech, lifting authority.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern is **authority quote → national scene-setter → trajectory pledge → market mechanism → stubborn counter-problem.** It is an **"expert demand, justified and complicated by facts"** structure. To replicate it:

1. **Open with an authority figure who has already spoken** ("also urged …"). Use a reporting verb in the past and a policy lever (tax, revenue, fund) as the object.
2. **Insert a direct quote that concedes the difficulty.** Use the "Yes, … but …" shape so the obstacle is named inside the same sentence as the goal.
3. **Add a second short quote that specifies who must be protected** and ties it back to the lever from step 1 (re-use the noun: "Those revenues …").
4. **Step back to national scale** and give one quantitative dependency on the old resource (X% from Y).
5. **Link that dependency to an international commitment** ("As its contribution to [agreement], it committed to peak/plateau/decline"). Name the years.
6. **Use "Since that [year] pledge"** to introduce the market evidence that change is already happening — pair a new actor with a verb of undercutting/competitiveness.
7. **Close with "At the same time"** and a counter-evidence sentence showing the old system still failing the public, so the recommendation in steps 1–3 reads as both necessary and incomplete.
