## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This section is a **bibliography / references page** (APA-style for journal articles, with mixed web-source formatting). The ordered content moves are:

1. **Alphabetical placement of source by author surname** — gives readers an indexed trail back into the in-text citations.
2. **Author listing** — names establish the authority / origination of the evidence.
4. **Year in parentheses** (or date string) — locks the source to a temporal point, signalling to the reader which edition / version is being cited.
5. **Article / book / webpage title** — tells the reader what claim the source supports.
6. **Journal / publisher / institutional source** — situates the claim in a venue with known editorial standing.
7. **Volume, issue, page numbers OR DOI / URL** — gives the precise locator so the reader can verify.
8. **Access date** — signals that the source was retrieved online on that day (especially for web sources).

**Why this order:** a bibliography must let a reader re-find the source from any one piece of information. Author → year → title → venue → locator → access date moves from broadest identifier (who wrote it) down to most specific (where on the page / when retrieved). Reversing volume before title, for instance, would break the convention and frustrate verification.

**Generalised replicable sequence for any bibliography:**
- 1 author surname-led anchor → 2 date → 3 work title → 4 container (journal/publisher/site) → 5 locator (vol/pages/DOI/URL) → 6 access date.

This section does **not** explain an idea. It supplies verifiable evidence hooks that other sections in the paper refer back to via in-text citation. The closest pattern it follows is **citation-as-evidence-handling**, and the steps to reproduce it are:

2. **Choose a citation style** (APA in this case) and stick to it; mixed styles are a red flag.
3. **For each source, identify its container type** — is it a journal article, book, book chapter, web page, or institutional report? The container dictates which slots you fill.

9. **Apply consistent capitalisation and punctuation** — title case for titles in this paper's style, but sentence case for article titles; period at end of every entry.
10. **Verify each entry resolves** — open every URL, confirm every DOI; an inaccessible citation undermines the paper.
