## What This Section Does (content sequence)

For a Table of Contents in an IB investigation, the ordered moves are:

1. **Front matter (figures + acronyms).** Orients the reader to visuals and jargon before any reading is required.
2. **Introduction → Research Question → Hypotheses → Spatial Context.** Moves from broad framing → the focused question → the testable claims → the place. Each move narrows the reader's attention.
3. **Methods of Investigation → Data Collection Methods → Data Collection Procedure → Method 1…5.** Moves from section header → generic approach → generic execution → enumerated specific tools. The order is general-to-specific so the reader knows the category before encountering instances.
4. **Quality and Presentation of Data → Example Calculation 1 → Example Calculation 2 → primary instrument → environmental measure → specific readings.** Establishes competence by showing workings first, then layers evidence from qualitative (questionnaire) to environmental (EIA) to quantified (decibels, footfall).
5. **Conclusion.** Single header signalling synthesis of all preceding data.
6. **Evaluation → Strengths → Limitations → Respective Improvements → Extension.** A retrospective arc: what worked → what didn't → fixes tied by the word "Respective" → forward projection.
7. **Works Cited.** Final reference layer that retrospectively underwrites every prior claim.

Why this order works: each move sets a category whose sub-items the reader will then walk through in the body of the paper. Word counts in parentheses act as proportion signals, telling the reader where the cognitive weight lies (here, overwhelmingly in the Data section).

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

This Contents page is sparse in connectives because it is structural rather than argumentative. The "idea-moving" vocabulary it does use falls into these jobs:

- **Sequencing / numbering:** "Method 1", "Method 2", "Method 3", "Method 4", "Method 5" — labels instruments in body order so the reader can match contents to body pages.
- **Specification (parenthetical clarification):** "Acronyms (21 words)", "Introduction (211 words)", "Questionnaires (Adjective Survey)", "Environmental Impact Assessment", "Urban Greenery Percentage" — uses parentheses or compounding to specify exactly which instance or how much.
- **Anaphoric back-reference (binding repairs to weaknesses):** "Respective Improvements" — the word "Respective" binds each improvement to a previously listed limitation.
- **Forward projection:** "Extension ................................................................................................................................................. 39" — a one-word forward-looking label that signals "outside this paper."
- **Worked-example labelling:** "Example Mann Whitney Calculation", "Example Spearman's Rank Calculation" — the noun "Example" tells the reader this entry shows workings, not results.
- **Word-count as proportion signal:** "(21 words)", "(211 words)", "(220 words)", "(1405 words)", "(195 words)", "(444 words)" — the parenthetical numbers function as scope/weight markers, telling the reader where cognitive effort will be densest (1405 words in the Data section).

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This Contents page relies on the pattern: **structural enumeration with proportional weighting and binding back-references.** To replicate it for a new IB investigation:

1. **List front matter first.** Name any non-text aids (maps, figures, photos) and any glossary/abbreviation list, each with a brief word count.
2. **Frame the inquiry as a narrowing triad.** Section header → driving question → testable claims → the specific place/context. Each sub-entry should be a noun phrase, not a sentence.
3. **Generalise the method before enumerating it.** Write one method section header, then two generic sub-headers (e.g., "Data Collection Methods" + "Data Collection Procedure"), then number the individual instruments (Method 1 … Method n) in the order they appear in the body.
4. **Lead the evidence section by demonstrating competence.** Place two "Example [Statistic] Calculation" entries first so the reader sees worked technique before raw results.
5. **Layer evidence from qualitative to quantitative.** Primary instrument (with parenthetical descriptor) → broad environmental/secondary measure → specific measurable quantities.
6. **Mark synthesis with a single-word chapter.** "Conclusion" signals everything is now being drawn together.
7. **Build the evaluation as a retrospective arc with binding language.** Use "Strengths", "Limitations and Weaknesses", "Respective Improvements" (the word "Respective" is what links the fixes back to the flaws), and "Extension".
8. **Close with sources.** "Works Cited" sits last and retrospectively underwrites every prior claim.
9. **Apply proportional weighting throughout.** Place the largest expected word count on the section that carries the most data; signal the weight in parentheses so the reader knows where the cognitive load sits before reading a single body page.
