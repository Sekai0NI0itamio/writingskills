## What This Section Does (content sequence)

The ordered content moves this section makes:

1. **Historical origin claim** (who/when the field began) — *sets up why the problem matters by linking it to a foundational moment.*
2. **Caveat on the origin** (the name didn't yet exist) — *sets up that this is a retrospective label, not a self-described field.*
3. **Motivating problem named** — *sets up the reader's expectation that a specific puzzle will be unpacked.*
4. **Problem statement (the verb of the puzzle)** — *sets up the constraints by defining what action is in question.*
5. **Enumerated constraints** (first / second) — *sets up that a visual aid is now needed, since the conditions are abstract.*
6. **Figure pointer** — *closes the paragraph because the figure will carry the spatial detail.*

The *why this order*: origin → motivating problem → action being questioned → rules of the action → picture of the action. The sequence always moves from the broadest historical frame to the smallest concrete artefact, so each sentence narrows what the reader has to hold in mind before the visual takes over.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A (Origin paragraph)**

> "[Authority figure] [did founding action] in [year], even if at the time [he/she] did not [use the modern term], [the action] was the beginning of [a new field/discipline]. At that time there was a problem that [affected a community], [name of problem]."

- **Slot 1** — *founding action in past simple with date*. Concrete detail: pick a year and a verb of creation (published, proposed, introduced).
- **Slot 2** — *concession on the modern label* in past simple. Concrete detail: name the term that exists today but didn't then.
- **Slot 3** — *retroactive significance* — claim it started a field.
- **Slot 4** — *temporal pivot* ("At that time") introducing the motivating puzzle and its community.
- **Original fill:** *"In 1736 Euler published the first paper about graph theory, even if at the time he did not call it graph theory, this paper was the beginning of a new field in mathematics. At that time there was a problem that divided mathematicians, the Königsberg bridge problem."*
- **Demo fill with different idea:** *"In 1905 Einstein published four papers on what he called the electrodynamics of moving bodies, even if at the time he did not use the term relativity, this work was the beginning of modern theoretical physics. At that time there was a problem that puzzled physicists, the constant speed-of-light observation."*

**SKELETON B (Problem-definition paragraph with two conditions and a figure)**

> "This problem asks if it was possible to [perform the central action] [of object] (where [optional geographic/physical aside]) [in/with some constraint]. But there are two conditions, the first one [constraint A]. And the second [constraint B]. In Figure [X] you have the image of [the scene] at that time and [its key features]."

- **Slot 1** — *problem restatement in "asks if it was possible to…"* — frame as a yes/no question of feasibility.
- **Slot 2** — *parenthetical aside* for historical/geographic detail in present/past mixed tense.
- **Slot 3** — *enumeration opener* ("But there are two conditions") — signals paired list.
- **Slot 4** — *first condition* — "the first one [prohibition/requirement]".
- **Slot 5** — *second condition* — "And the second [paired requirement]".
- **Slot 6** — *figure pointer* — "In Figure X you have the image of [setting] and [feature list]".
- **Original fill:** *"This problem asks if it was possible to cross the seven bridges of Königsberg … in a single trip. But there are two conditions, the first one you cannot pass two times the same bridge. And the second that you must end in the same point which you began. In Figure 2 you have the image of the city at that time and its bridges."*
- **Demo fill with different idea:** *"This problem asks if it was possible to deliver letters to every house in the village using one walk, even with the snow blocking some lanes, without retracing your steps. But there are two conditions, the first one you cannot skip any house. And the second that you must finish at the post office. In Figure 3 you have the image of the village in winter and its roads."*

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / enumeration:** *"the first one you cannot pass two times the same bridge"* and *"And the second that you must end in the same point which you began"* — paired "the first one … And the second" pattern.
- **Concession (within the opening):** *"even if at the time he did not call it graph theory"* — "even if at the time he did not" pattern.
- **Temporal pivot:** *"At that time there was a problem that divided mathematicians"* — "At that time there was a problem that".
- **Contrast / rule-introducer:** *"But there are two conditions, the first one"* — "But there are two conditions".
- **Specification / restatement:** *"This problem asks if it was possible to cross"* — "This problem asks if it was possible to".
- **Evidence handling / reference:** *"In Figure 2 you have the image of the city"* — "In Figure [N] you have the image of".
- **Explanatory verbs (inherited claim):** *"was the beginning of a new field"* — passive "was the beginning of" used to declare significance.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses the **historical-origin → motivating problem → problem statement → paired constraints → figure anchor** pattern. To replicate it on a NEW idea:

1. **Open with an origin claim** — name a person and a year and a founding action (verb of creation) in past simple.
2. **Add a label caveat** — "even if at the time [he/she] did not call it [modern term]".
3. **Declare significance** — "[the action] was the beginning of [a field/discipline]".
4. **Pivot temporally** — "At that time there was a problem that [affected a group], [problem name]."
5. **Restate the puzzle** — "This problem asks if it was possible to [verb] [object] [context]."
6. **Introduce paired rules** — "But there are two conditions, the first one [rule A]. And the second [rule B]."
7. **Anchor visually** — "In Figure [N] you have the image of [the scene] and [its features]."

Each step hands forward by *narrowing scope*: world → field → puzzle → action → rules → picture. The reader never has to hold more than one new piece per sentence.
