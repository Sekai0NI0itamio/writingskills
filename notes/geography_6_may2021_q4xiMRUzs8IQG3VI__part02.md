## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Figure number + dash** – provides a unique identifier for each visual.  
2. **Descriptive title** – tells the reader what the figure shows (e.g., “Bid‑Rent on modified Burgess model”).  
3. **Source in parentheses** – attributes the graphic to a creator or agency (e.g., “(URA)”, “(Google)”, “(Roberts)”).  
4. **Page reference (after …)** – locates the figure in the document (e.g., “4”, “5”, “10”).  

**Why this order?**  
- The figure number anchors the item in the sequence.  
- The title gives immediate context, allowing the reader to anticipate the visual’s purpose.  
- Citing the source satisfies academic integrity and lets the reader verify the origin.  
- The page number enables quick retrieval, completing the reference loop.

*Generalizable rule:* Any “Table of Figures” should list items chronologically, using **[Number] – [Title] ([Source]) … [Page]**.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

### Skeleton A – Basic Figure Entry  
**Slot template:**  
```
[Figure number] – [Descriptive title] ([Source]) … [Page number]
```

1. **Slot 1 – Figure number**  
   - *Grammatical shape:* “Figure X” where X is an integer.  
   - *How to fill:* Start at 1 and increment by 1 for each new visual.  

2. **Slot 2 – Descriptive title**  
   - *Grammatical shape:* Noun phrase (often includes a location or variable).  
   - *How to fill:* Choose a concise phrase that names the type of data or map shown; include key variables (e.g., “Bid‑Rent on modified Burgess model”).  

3. **Slot 3 – Source**  
   - *Grammatical shape:* Parenthesized proper noun or abbreviation (e.g., “(URA)”, “(Google)”).  
   - *How to fill:* Cite the original creator, agency, or database; use the same format as the source appears in the text.  

4. **Slot 4 – Page number**  
   - *Grammatical shape:* Number placed after a line of dots (“……”) or directly after the parentheses.  
   - *How to fill:* Locate the page where the figure first appears in the text and write that number.  

**Original filled version (from text):**  
*“Figure 2 – Bid‑Rent on modified Burgess model (Pun‑Cheng)……5”*  

**Demonstration fill (different idea):**  
*“Figure 12 – Temperature distribution map of coastal Kenya (UNEP)……9”*

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Explanation pattern:** **Enumerative List** (Figure‑by‑Figure catalogue).  

1. **Assign a sequential figure number** – start at 1 and increase by 1 for each new visual.  
2. **Write a concise descriptive title** – capture the type of data or visual (e.g., map, graph, photograph) and any key variables or locations.  
3. **Cite the source** – place the creator, agency, or database in parentheses directly after the title. If multiple sources contributed, add another set of parentheses.  
4. **Indicate the page number** – after a line of dots or a space, write the page where the figure first appears in the text.  
5. **Maintain chronological order** – list figures in the order they are introduced in the document, ensuring the reader can follow the development of the argument.

*Following these steps will produce a clear, academically rigorous Table of Figures for any independent investigation.*
