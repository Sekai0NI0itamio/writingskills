## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1 — Bailey (scholarly journal article)**
- Move 1 — Author (provenance opener): "Bailey, Thomas A." Hands to title by answering *who* wrote the work, the obligatory first field.
- Move 2 — Title (work identifier): "Japan's Protest Against the Annexation of Hawaii." Hands to container by answering *what* was written.
- Move 3 — Container + location (publication venue): "The Journal of Modern History 3, no. 1 (1931): 46-61." Hands to access date by specifying *where and when* the work sits.
- Move 4 — Retrieval date: "Accessed February 1, 2020." Hands to URL by timestamping the moment of access.
- Move 5 — Persistent identifier: "www.jstor.org/stable/1872141." Hands to next entry by closing the record with a verifiable path.

**Paragraph 2 — Blount (primary government document hosted online)**
- Move 1 — Author: "Blount, James H." Hands to title via standard surname-first ordering.
- Move 2 — Long descriptive title: "Report of U.S. Special Commissioner James H. Blount to U.S. Secretary of State Walter Q. Gresham Concerning the Hawaiian Kingdom Investigation." Hands to repository because the title already names the document's office of origin.
- Move 3 — Original date + repository: "Hawaiian Kingdom - Blount's Report, July 17, 1893." Hands to publisher by anchoring the original publication moment.
- Move 4 — Hosting site: "The Hawaiian Kingdom." Hands to access date by attributing the digitised copy.
- Move 5 — Access: "Accessed January 10, 2020." Hands to URL by marking retrieval.
- Move 6 — URL: closes the record.

**Paragraph 3 — Collins (book with catalogue identifiers)**
- Move 1 — Author with publication year: "Collins, Stephen (2014)." Hands to title by fusing authorship and date into one field.
- Move 2 — Title with colon subtitle: "Taking Hawaii : How Thirteen Honolulu Businessmen Overthrew the Queen of Hawaii in 1893, With a Bluff." Hands to publisher because the title is now closed.
- Move 3 — Publisher only: "Open Road Media." Hands to identifiers because location/series are omitted.
- Move 4 — Standard numbers: "ISBN 978-1-4976-3808-2. OCLC 874921510." Hands to access date by establishing catalogue reach.
- Move 5 — Access: "Accessed February 1, 2020."

**Paragraph 4 — Kinzer (short book citation)**
- Move 1 — Author: "Kinzer, Stephen." Hands to title by the standard pivot.
- Move 2 — Title: "Overthrow: Americas Century of Regime Change from Hawaii to Iraq." Hands to imprint because no subtitle extends the work.
- Move 3 — Place + publisher + year: "New York: Times Books, 2007." Hands to next entry — imprint closes the record (no URL because no online retrieval occurred).

**Paragraph 5 — "History of Labor in Hawai'i" (web-only source with no author)**
- Move 1 — Title standing in for author: "History of Labor in Hawai'i." Hands to publisher because no personal author exists.
- Move 2 — Institutional publisher: "University of Hawaii." Hands to access by attributing the hosting institution.
- Move 3 — Access date: "Accessed January 10, 2020." Hands to URL as the only verifiable anchor.
- Move 4 — URL: "https://www.hawaii.edu/uhwo/clear/home/HawaiiLaborHistory.html." Closes the entry.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Alphabetical ordering by author surname** — sets up the reader's expectation that any source can be located by name (e.g. *Bailey*, *Blount*, *Chamberlain*…).
2. **Complete bibliographic record per entry** — author, title, container, date, identifier — sets up verifiability so the reader can audit each claim in the essay.
3. **Source-type variety in fixed slots** — books, journal articles, primary government documents, web pages, edited memoirs, archival newspapers — sets up breadth-of-research credibility without commentary.
4. **Persistent identifiers at the end of each record** — URL, DOI stub, ISBN/OCLC — sets up the "you can check this" handshake that closes every entry.
5. **Access dates only for online sources** — signals which records needed retrieval (web) versus which exist physically (books).

**Why this order:** alphabetical first because a Bibliography is a retrieval tool, not an argument; complete fields second because readers scan for the field they need (author or title); type variety third because diversity of evidence types is *shown*, not *argued*; identifiers last because they close each unit and let the reader leave the bibliography with the next step (a link) in hand.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A — Scholarly journal article**
"[Author Last, First]. "[Article Title]." [Journal Name] [Volume], no. [Issue] ([Year]): [Pages]. Accessed [Date]. [URL/DOI]."

1. Slot 1 = author surname + comma + given name (proper noun pair). Slot 2 = article title in quotes (noun phrase). Slot 3 = journal name in italics + volume number + issue ("no. X") + year in parentheses + colon + page range. Slot 4 = "Accessed" + full date. Slot 5 = URL.
2. **To fill differently:** pick a peer-reviewed article on any subject; pull author, exact article title, journal title, vol/issue/year/pages from the article header; copy the stable JSTOR/DOI link; record today's date.
3. **Original fill:** "Bailey, Thomas A. "Japan's Protest Against the Annexation of Hawaii." The Journal of Modern History 3, no. 1 (1931): 46-61. Accessed February 1, 2020."
4. **Demonstration fill with a different idea:** "Smith, Jane. "The Ecology of Coral Bleaching." Marine Biology Quarterly 12, no. 3 (2019): 204-220. Accessed October 5, 2024. https://doi.org/10.1234/mbq.2019.012."

**SKELETON B — Modern book with publisher imprint**
"[Author Last, First]. [Book Title]. [City]: [Publisher], [Year]."

1. Slot 1 = author. Slot 2 = book title (no quotes, italicised in print). Slot 3 = "City: Publisher, Year" imprint.
2. **To fill differently:** take any single-author monograph; mirror the title page; use the city and publisher exactly as printed; use the copyright year.
3. **Original fill:** "Dijk, Cees van. Pacific Strife: The Great Powers and Their Political and Economic Rivalries in Asia and the Western Pacific (1870-1914). Amsterdam: Amsterdam University Press, 2015."
4. **Demonstration fill with a different idea:** "Brown, Liam. The Architecture of Memory. London: Verso, 2018."

**SKELETON C — Primary government/historical document hosted on a digital archive**
"[Author/Office]. "[Document Title]." [Archive Name], [Original Date]. [Host Site]. Accessed [Date]. [URL]."

1. Slot 1 = originator (person or office). Slot 2 = document title in quotes. Slot 3 = archive or collection name + comma + original publication date. Slot 4 = hosting organisation. Slot 5 = "Accessed" + date. Slot 6 = URL.
2. **To fill differently:** pick a primary document (treaty, report, speech) that has been digitised; preserve the original date even though you accessed it digitally; name the digital host separately from the original archive.
3. **Original fill:** "Blount, James H. "Report of U.S. Special Commissioner James H. Blount to U.S. Secretary of State Walter Q. Gresham Concerning the Hawaiian Kingdom Investigation." Hawaiian Kingdom - Blount's Report, July 17, 1893. The Hawaiian Kingdom. Accessed January 10, 2020."
4. **Demonstration fill with a different idea:** "Turing, Alan. "On Computable Numbers." Proceedings of the London Mathematical Society, 1936. Internet Archive. Accessed November 12, 2024. https://archive.org/details/turing1936."

**SKELETON D — Web-only source with no individual author**
"[Title of Page]. [Institutional Publisher]. Accessed [Date]. [URL]."

1. Slot 1 = page or article title standing in for author. Slot 2 = institutional or organisational publisher. Slot 3 = "Accessed" + date. Slot 4 = full URL.
2. **To fill differently:** pick a web page whose authorship is institutional; cite the page title first, the institution second, then record the access date and full https URL.
3. **Original fill:** "History of Labor in Hawai'i. University of Hawaii. Accessed January 10, 2020."
4. **Demonstration fill with a different idea:** "Climate Change Indicators. NASA. Accessed March 2, 2024. https://climate.nasa.gov/indicators/."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Source-type markers (category labels that slot a record into a genre):** "The Journal of Modern History" (journal container), "Digital History" (web archive label), "Biographical Directory of the U.S. Congress" (institutional directory), "Chronicling America RSS" (newspaper aggregator).
- **Edition / role markers (clarify who did what to the work):** "Edited by Andrew Farrell" (editor credited), "ISBN 978-1-4976-3808-2" (standard book number), "OCLC 874921510" (world catalogue number).
- **Container / venue punctuation (the grammar that locates a work):** colon after city "New York: Times Books", comma after volume "3, no. 1", parentheses around year "(1931)", colon before pages "46-61".
- **Retrieval language (the only time-stamped connective in a Bibliography):** "Accessed February 1, 2020"; "Accessed January 10, 2020"; "Accessed February 6, 2020"; "Accessed March 8, 2020" — every web record closes with the same *Accessed [Month Day, Year]* phrase, producing the rhythm of "you can check this on this date."
- **Authority / institutional attribution:** "The Hawaiian Kingdom" (host of primary doc), "University of Hawaii" (host of labour history page), "Constitutional Rights Foundation" (host of debate article).
- **Imprint / publisher shorthand:** "Honolulu: Univ. of Hawaii Pr." (abbreviated publisher); "Pub. Co." (abbreviated imprint); "FORGOTTEN BOOKS" (reprint imprint).

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

A Bibliography does not "explain an idea" — it **establishes provenance**. The pattern is *Identity → Location → Verification*. To build a bibliography entry for any new source, execute these moves in order:

1. **Identify the originator.** Write the author (surname, given name) or, if no author exists, the institutional owner of the page. This sets up every later field.
2. **Name the work.** Choose the punctuation that signals genre: quotation marks for article/short document titles; italicisation for standalone books and journals.
3. **Locate the work in its container.** State the journal + volume + issue + year + pages, OR the city + publisher + year for a book, OR the archive/site for a primary document. This is where readers trace the source.
4. **Date the original publication.** Always include the year of *creation* (e.g., "(1931)", "2007", "July 17, 1893") — distinct from the retrieval date that follows.
5. **Stamp the retrieval.** Add "Accessed [full date]" only if the source lives online; this proves when the live version was consulted.
6. **Pin the persistent identifier.** Close with URL, DOI, ISBN, or OCLC number so the reader can verify or relocate the source without contacting you.

Execute 1 → 6 in that order for every entry; vary slots 2–3 by source type using Skeletons A–D above. The "explanation" being delivered is not an argument but a chain of custody: *who made this, what it is, where it lives, when it appeared, when I checked, where you can check too.*
