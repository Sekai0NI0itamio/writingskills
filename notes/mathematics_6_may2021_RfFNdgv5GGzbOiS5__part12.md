## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1** (3 sentences)

1. **Carry-over context fragment** — *"in red colors."* A left-over descriptor continuing from the previous page (specifying how data are visually marked). Hands the reader forward by establishing which visual element the upcoming statistics refer to (cause/specification — colour labels which group on the graph).
2. **Surprising observation / claim** — *"The box-whisker plot, surprisingly, has a mean with the same value as the Q1, 82."* Reports a specific numerical finding and flags it as unexpected. Hands the reader forward by raising a "why is this noteworthy?" question that the next sentence continues answering (consequence — the surprise invites more stats).
3. **Additive specification** — *"Besides, it has a range of 9 years and an IQR of 1 year."* Layer in two more numerical descriptors of the same plot using "Besides." Hands the reader to the next paragraph by completing the central-tendency/spread description, so the writer now pivots to what is *outside* this compact box (contrast — from typical range → atypical points).

**Paragraph 2** (5 sentences; final one cut off)

1. **Outlier identification** — *"There are two outliers in the distribution in total, belonging to Saudi Arabia (75 years) and the US (79 years), exceeding the lower limit of the box-whisker plot."* Names how many outliers, names the two cases with their values, and locates them relative to the plot. Hands the reader forward by signalling that these cases will now be interrogated (cause — outliers invite explanation).
3. **Method/transition marker** — *"Further research was carried out to find the reasons for this for both countries."* Announces the move from description to explanation. Hands the reader forward by promising reasons, which the next sentence begins to deliver (answer — sentence 3 fulfils the promise of "reasons").
4. **First sequenced cause** — *"Firstly, for Saudi Arabia, it was found that the country has newly joined the team of developed countries due to its high income from oil exports."* Uses "Firstly" to mark the start of the explanation block; "due to" supplies a sub-cause (oil income → developed status). Hands the reader forward by introducing a *cause* that demands a *consequence* in the next sentence (cause → effect).
5. **Causal consequence** — *"Therefore, the healthcare system of the country is still in the process of improvement."* Uses "Therefore" to draw the implication of the previous sentence for the variable of interest (life expectancy / healthcare). Hands the reader forward to the second country via "Besides" (specification — moving from SA to the next instance).

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **discussion-of-results** section sitting between a graph and the next point. The ordered content moves are:

1. **Statistical summary of the typical case** — central tendency and spread (mean, range, IQR) reported with surprise marked. *Why first:* you must establish what "normal" looks like before outsiders become visible.
2. **Identification of atypical cases** — count the outliers, name the countries, give their values, locate them on the plot. *Why next:* now the reader can see the contrast against the summary just given.
3. **Methodological pivot** — "Further research was carried out…" announces the move from observation to investigation. *Why needed:* signposts that interpretation, not more numbers, is coming.
4. **First causal explanation (sequenced)** — "Firstly, for [country], it was found that [cause] due to [sub-cause]." *Why first:* gives a concrete country-specific story rather than an abstract claim.
5. **Logical consequence** — "Therefore, [downstream implication for the dependent variable]." *Why next:* links the country-level cause back to the statistical outcome.
6. **Hand-off to second case** — "Besides, [country 2]…" replicates steps 4–5 for the other outlier.

Replication logic: summary → outliers → research pivot → cause per case → consequence per case. The reader is never given an explanation without first seeing *what* is being explained.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**Skeleton A — Compact statistical summary paragraph**
1. *What each slot holds:*
   - Slot 1: a leftover visual-context fragment (e.g. colour/position marker) — noun phrase, often elliptical.
   - Slot 2: a specific numerical finding about the plot, prefaced by an evaluative adverb ("surprisingly," "interestingly").
   - Slot 3: one or two further numerical descriptors of the same plot, introduced by "Besides."
2. *How to fill with a different idea:* Slot 1: paste in whatever visual cue you used in the figure (colour label, axis tag). Slot 2: pick one statistic (mean, median, mode) and compare it to another (a quartile, the maximum) — make the comparison numerically exact and worth reacting to. Slot 3: add one spread measure (range, IQR, standard deviation) with units.
3. *Original filled version:* *"in red colors. The box-whisker plot, surprisingly, has a mean with the same value as the Q1, 82. Besides, it has a range of 9 years and an IQR of 1 year."*
4. *Demonstration fill:* *"above the x-axis. The scatter graph, notably, has a mode with the same value as the minimum, 14 cm. Besides, it has a range of 6 cm and an interquartile range of 2 cm."*

**Skeleton B — Outlier-investigation paragraph**
1. *What each slot holds:*
   - Slot 1: total count of outliers + the two specific instances with values + their position relative to the plot.
   - Slot 2: "Further research was carried out to find the reasons for this for both countries." (research-pivot sentence in simple past passive).
   - Slot 3: "Firstly, for [Country A], it was found that [cause] due to [sub-cause]." — sequenced, country-named, with embedded sub-cause.
   - Slot 4: "Therefore, [consequence for the variable of interest]." — causal connective, simple present progressive for ongoing state.
2. *How to fill with a different idea:* Slot 1: identify exactly how many anomalies you found and pin each to a labelled case. Slot 2: keep the research-announcement sentence as a pivot. Slot 3: for the *first* case, give a socio-economic reason plus a deeper cause ("due to X"). Slot 4: state the downstream consequence that ties back to the graph's variable.
3. *Original filled version:* *"There are two outliers in the distribution in total, belonging to Saudi Arabia (75 years) and the US (79 years), exceeding the lower limit of the box-whisker plot. Further research was carried out to find the reasons for this for both countries. Firstly, for Saudi Arabia, it was found that the country has newly joined the team of developed countries due to its high income from oil exports. Therefore, the healthcare system of the country is still in the process of improvement."*
4. *Demonstration fill:* *"There are two low points in the distribution in total, belonging to Portugal (12 cm) and Greece (13 cm), falling below the lower whisker of the box plot. Further research was carried out to find the reasons for this for both countries. Firstly, for Portugal, it was found that the region suffered a prolonged drought during the sampling period due to shifting atmospheric pressure patterns. Therefore, the local groundwater levels are still recovering."*

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing:** *"Firstly, for Saudi Arabia"* — opens the first case in an ordered list of explanations.
- **Cause / consequence:** *"due to its high income from oil exports"* — embedded sub-cause inside the first-explanation sentence; *"Therefore, the healthcare system …"* — single-word sentence opener that converts cause into consequence.
- **Specification / addition:** *"Besides, it has a range of 9 years"* — appends more numerical detail; *"Besides, the"* — sets up a second addition at paragraph end.
- **Reactive / evaluative framing:** *"The box-whisker plot, surprisingly, has a mean…"* — adverb in comma position flags the finding as counter-intuitive.
- **Evidence handling:** *"it was found that the country has newly joined…"* — passive reporting verb distancing the writer from the claim.
- **Method announcement:** *"Further research was carried out to find the reasons for this for both countries."* — past-passive verb phrase marking the shift from description to investigation.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The section relies on an **observation → identification → research-pivot → per-case cause → per-case consequence** pattern (essentially a *contrast-then-cause-chain* explanation). To replicate with a new idea:

1. **Summarise the typical case in numbers.** Pick one central-tendency statistic and one spread statistic, state both with units, and decide whether to react with "surprisingly" or "interestingly."
2. **Pile on one more numerical detail** using "Besides" so the reader feels the dataset is fully described before anomalies appear.
3. **Switch to outliers.** State the count in total, name each labelled instance with its value, and locate it relative to the plot ("exceeding the lower limit…").
4. **Insert a research-pivot sentence** in past passive ("Further research was carried out to find the reasons for this for both [cases]"). This sentence exists only to licence the explanation that follows.
5. **Open the first case with "Firstly, for [Case A]…"** and give a country/instance-specific reason containing an embedded sub-cause ("due to X").
6. **Close the case with "Therefore, [downstream consequence]."** The consequence must touch the same variable the graph measured (healthcare ↔ life expectancy; groundwater ↔ measurement values).
7. **Begin the second case with "Besides, for [Case B]…"** and repeat steps 5–6 so the reader sees the explanation is systematic, not anecdotal.
