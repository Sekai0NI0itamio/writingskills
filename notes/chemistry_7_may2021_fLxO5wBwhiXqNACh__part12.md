## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Calculate moles of the titrant (Na₂S₂O₃)** — establishes the known starting quantity from concentration and volume; this value is the foundation every subsequent step depends on.
2. **Calculate total moles of I₂ added** — establishes the complete pool of the second reactant before any consumption; provides the minuend for the subtraction to come.
3. **Determine moles of I₂ consumed by thiosulphate** — applies the 1:2 stoichiometric ratio to find how much I₂ reacted in the first redox step; yields the subtrahend.
4. **Find moles of I₂ remaining for the second reaction** — subtracts consumed I₂ from total I₂; isolates the portion that actually reacted with caffeine.
5. **Determine moles of caffeine that reacted** — applies the I₂:caffeine stoichiometric ratio (2:1) to the remaining I₂; converts the iodine remainder into the analyte's moles.
6. **Convert caffeine moles to mass** — multiplies by molar mass (194.19 g/mol) and scales to milligrams; transforms the abstract mole quantity into a weighable mass.
7. **Calculate percentage purity** — divides the pure caffeine mass by the total extracted sample mass and multiplies by 100; expresses the final result as a percentage.

*Why this order:* each step's output is the next step's input — the chain is strictly sequential and non-reversible; you cannot find purity without first finding mass, cannot find mass without moles, cannot find caffeine moles without knowing how much I₂ remained, and cannot know remaining I₂ without knowing both the total and the consumed amounts.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern name:** Sequential stoichiometric chain — each calculation's output feeds the next via subtraction or division by a stoichiometric coefficient, terminating in a mass-to-percentage conversion.

**Replication steps:**

1. State the known titrant quantity as concentration × volume and divide by 1000 to obtain its moles in scientific notation.
2. State the total amount of the second reactant (concentration × volume ÷ 1000) to establish the complete initial pool.
3. Divide the first reactant's moles by its stoichiometric coefficient in the balanced equation to find how many moles of the second reactant were consumed.
4. Subtract the consumed moles from the total moles to find the remainder that proceeded to the next reaction.
5. Divide the remaining moles by the second reactant's stoichiometric coefficient to convert them into moles of the target product.
6. Multiply the product's moles by its molar mass and by 1000 (if converting to mg) to obtain a mass value.
7. Divide the pure mass by the total sample mass, multiply by 100, and state the result as a percentage to complete the explanation.
