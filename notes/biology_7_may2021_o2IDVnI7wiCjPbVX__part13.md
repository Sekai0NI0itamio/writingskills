## What This Section Does (content sequence)

A Bibliography does not advance an argument with sentences; it advances **citation coverage** with entries. The ordered moves are:

1. **Author block first** — establishes who is being credited. Names come before everything else because, in academic referencing, authorship determines the entry's identity.
2. **Year next** — temporal anchor: "(2017)", "2020", "(n.d)". The year lets a reader immediately gauge the source's currency relative to the essay.
3. **Title in third position** — declares what the work is about. In journals the title sits inside quotation marks; in books/reports it stands unquoted. The title's placement after authorship follows the convention "who, when, what."
4. **Container/source in fourth position** — names the journal, book series, or website. This move sets up whether a DOI or a URL will follow.
5. **Volume/issue/pages/URL in fifth position** — locational data that lets the reader physically retrieve the work.
6. **DOI in sixth position** (when available) — a permanent identifier; appears for journal articles and some datasets.
7. **Access date in final position** — closes the citation and proves the source was live when consulted; mandatory for web sources. The access date functions as the "handing" move that signals the entry is finished.

The order matters because each move narrows the reader's search — *who → when → what → where → how to find it → proof of consultation*. Removing any move breaks retrieval.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A — Journal article (Ahmed entry)**

1. *Slot 1 (author block):* `[Surname1, Given1 et al.]` or `[Surname1, Given1 & Surname2, Given2.]` — noun phrase, surname-first, "et al." for ≥3 authors.
2. *Slot 2 (year):* `"[Year]"` placed inside or right after the author block.
3. *Slot 3 (article title):* `"[Title]."` — sentence-case inside double quotes.
4. *Slot 4 (journal + volume + issue + article ID):* `[Journal name] vol. [N],[N] [Article#].` — italic journal, "vol." abbreviation.
5. *Slot 5 (full date):* `[DD Month YYYY],` — day-month-year.
6. *Slot 6 (DOI):* `doi:[identifier].`
7. *Slot 7 (access date):* `Accessed [DD Month YYYY].`

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / formatting markers**
- `"vol. [N], [Issue] [Article#]."` → container-and-locator notation; **Quoted:** "vol. 16,17 3165."
- `"[DD Month YYYY],"` → publication-date stamp; **Quoted:** "30 Aug. 2019,"
- `"doi:[identifier]."` → permanent-ID marker; **Quoted:** "doi:10.1371/journal.pone.0079276."
- `"Available at:"` → retrieval verb for web sources; **Quoted:** "Available at: https://www.cdc.gov/malaria/about/biology/index.html."

**Cause/consequence markers** — none used (a bibliography does not assert causal links).

**Contrast/concession markers** — none used.

**Specification / subordination**
- `"[Surname], [Given] & [Surname], [Given]."` → joining multiple authors with ampersand; **Quoted:** "Austin, Kelly & Bellinger, Megan & Rana, Priyokti."
- `"et al."` → abbreviating a long author list; **Quoted:** "Beck-Johnson, Lindsay M et al."
- `"and"` → alternative connector for two authors; **Quoted:** "Bauhoff, Sebastian, and Jonah Busch."

**Evidence-handling / sourcing language**
- `"Accessed [DD Month YYYY]."` → proof of live consultation; **Quoted:** "Accessed 16 March 2021."
- `"(n.d)"` → flag for undated web sources; **Quoted:** "GoodData Documentation. (n.d)."

**Explanation verbs** — none (no definitions or mechanisms appear in a bibliography).

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The bibliography relies on an **identifier-construction** pattern — the idea is not *explained* but *located*. To cite a new source using the same logic:

1. **Start with the author(s).** Write surnames first, given names or initials after; use "et al." once the count exceeds three. State the block as a noun phrase ending in a full stop.
2. **Anchor with the year.** Place it in parentheses for journal/report style (e.g., `(2017)`) or separated by a full stop for older book style (e.g., `1999.`). Use `(n.d)` when the source gives no date.
3. **State the title.** Use double quotation marks for article-length works; leave unquoted for books, reports, and webpages. End the title with a full stop.
4. **Name the container.** For journals, italicise the name and follow with `vol. [N]`, issue, and either an article number or page range. For books, give the publisher. For websites, give the domain.
5. **Add a permanent locator.** Prefer the DOI in `doi:[string]` form; for online-only sources, insert `"Available at:"` followed by the full URL.
6. **Close with the access date.** Write `Accessed [DD Month YYYY].` This last move signals to the reader that the entry is complete and hands them to the next citation block.
