## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

The Contents page is one continuous navigational block, so I treat each thematic cluster as a "paragraph."

**Cluster 1 — Preliminaries**
1. Sentence/entry: "Acknowledgements ……… iii" — **move: navigational placement** — quote: "Acknowledgements …" — *does*: signals non-content front matter. Hands the reader to the next entry by **adjacency** (a second prelim listing follows immediately).
2. Entry: "Table of Figures …" — **move: navigational placement** — quote: "Table of Figures …" — *does*: confirms two preliminaries exist before the body, then hands the reader forward by **convention shift** — the next Roman numeral begins the actual investigation.

**Cluster 2 — Section I (set-up)**
1. Entry: "I. Fieldwork Question and Geographic Context" — **move: framing claim** — quote: "Fieldwork Question and Geographic Context" — *does*: announces the dual purpose (a question + a place). Hands the reader to the next entry by **specification**, because the heading promises the question is broken down next.
2. Entry: "a. Hypotheses" — **move: specification** — quote: "Hypotheses" — *does*: converts the "question" half of the heading into testable statements.
3. Entry: "b. Relation to Syllabus" — **move: justification/authority** — quote: "Relation to Syllabus" — *does*: anchors the work to the course. Hands the reader forward by **cause** — once authority is cited, the place must now be introduced.

5. Entry: "d. Geographic Theory" — **move: theoretical setup** — quote: "Geographic Theory" — *does*: layers the conceptual model over the now-defined site, completing the umbrella heading and handing the reader to Methods by **logical necessity** — theory established, procedure is next.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

For a Table of Contents of an IB Geography fieldwork report, the ordered content moves are:

1. **Preliminaries** (Acknowledgements, Table of Figures) — sets housekeeping before the body; lets the reader locate the study's accountability and visuals instantly.
2. **Question umbrella + hypotheses + syllabus link** — converts the abstract "fieldwork question" into testable statements and shows the work is course-required. *Why this order:* justification (syllabus) and testability (hypotheses) must be visible before the site is described.
3. **Study-area geography → geographic theory** — moves from *where* to *what conceptual model applies*. *Why this order:* the place must exist before theory can be layered onto it.
4. **Methods — equipment first, then measurement variables in field-order, then sampling strategy** — declares tools before procedure, records variables in the order they appear in the field, and ends with reliability reasoning. *Why this order:* a reader should be able to imagine the fieldworker picking up tools, then logging data, then defending how they sampled.
5. **Results — one section per hypothesis, in the same order as the methods variables** — preserves a 1-to-1 methods/results map so each test can be cross-checked.
6. **Conclusion** — synthesises all three hypothesis verdicts.
7. **Evaluation** — critiques methodology and limitations after the verdict is stated; only then is the reader allowed to challenge it.
8. **References → Appendices (raw → test → threshold → workings)** — closes with verifiable evidence in the order a sceptical reader would follow it.

The overarching principle: **each move sets up the reader's right to question the next move** — theory before method, method before result, result before verdict, verdict before critique, critique before raw evidence.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**Skeleton A — Umbrella Heading with Specifications**
SKELETON: "[Roman numeral]. [Broad Theme combining a question and a setting]. a. [First specification of the question]. b. [Justification/authority link]. c. [Definition of the setting]. d. [Theoretical model applied to that setting]."

1. **Slots & shapes:**
   - Slot 1: Roman numeral + noun phrase that names *both* an inquiry and a place (compound noun phrase).
   - Slot 2: Letter "a" — the inquiry reduced to testable statements (plural noun).
   - Slot 3: Letter "b" — an external authority or curriculum link (noun phrase).
   - Slot 4: Letter "c" — physical/environmental setting (noun phrase + prepositional phrase "in [place]").
   - Slot 5: Letter "d" — the conceptual framework (noun phrase).
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** Slot 1: pick a broad theme that pairs a *thing you will investigate* with *where you will investigate it* (e.g. "Urban Heat and the CBD"). Slot 2: turn the investigation into 2–4 hypotheses. Slot 3: identify the syllabus strand or external body it serves. Slot 4: name the city's morphology. Slot 5: name the urban-climate model (e.g. Urban Heat Island theory).
3. **Original filled version:** "I. Fieldwork Question and Geographic Context … a. Hypotheses … b. Relation to Syllabus … c. Geography in Study Area … d. Geographic Theory."
4. **Demonstration fill:** "I. Urban Heat Question and CBD Context. a. Hypotheses (asphalt vs albedo, building height vs retention, traffic vs temperature). b. Relation to Syllabus (Option G — Urban Environments). c. Urban Morphology in Central Madrid. d. Urban Heat Island Theory."

**Skeleton B — Methods Block (materials → variables → reliability)**
SKELETON: "[Roman numeral]. Methods of Investigation. a. Equipment. b. [Variable 1]. c. [Variable 2]. d. [Variable 3]. e. [Variable 4]. f. [Derived/composite variable]. g. Sampling Strategies."

1. **Slots & shapes:**
   - Slot 1: Roman numeral + "Methods of Investigation" (header).
   - Slot 2: "Equipment" — tool list (plural noun).
   - Slots 3–6: individual variables in the order they are measured in the field (noun phrases).
   - Slot 7: derived/composite variable.
   - Slot 8: "Sampling Strategies" — plural noun phrase signalling reliability reasoning.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** Slot 2: list physical tools (thermometer, anemometer, camera). Slots 3–6: pick 4 variables in the order you would record them at a single site (e.g. surface temperature, air temperature, wind speed, sky view factor). Slot 7: pick a composite (e.g. mean radiant temperature). Slot 8: state sampling logic (transects, times of day).
3. **Original filled version:** "II. Methods of Investigation … a. Equipment … b. Particle Size and Shape … c. Velocity … d. Wet Width … e. Wet Depth … f. Wetted Perimeter … g. Sampling Strategies."
4. **Demonstration fill:** "II. Methods of Investigation. a. Equipment (infrared thermometer, hand-held anemometer, hemispherical lens). b. Surface Temperature. c. Air Temperature. d. Wind Speed. e. Sky View Factor. f. Mean Radiant Temperature. g. Sampling Strategies (10 transects, 12:00–14:00 to control for solar angle)."

**Skeleton C — Results Section Per Hypothesis**
SKELETON: "[Roman numeral]. Hypothesis [n] – [Key variable or composite being tested]."

1. **Slots & shapes:**
   - Slot 1: Roman numeral incremented per hypothesis.
   - Slot 2: "Hypothesis" + cardinal number — the count matches its methods entry.
   - Slot 3: en-dash + the exact variable name that appeared in the methods block (noun phrase, identical wording).
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** Slot 1: number results sections in the same order as the methods variables. Slot 2: keep the word "Hypothesis" + count. Slot 3: copy the methods variable name verbatim so the reader can locate the test instantly.
3. **Original filled version:** "III. Hypothesis 1 – Particle Size and Shape … IV. Hypothesis 2 – Hydraulic Radius … V. Hypothesis 3 – Velocity."
4. **Demonstration fill:** "III. Hypothesis 1 – Surface Temperature … IV. Hypothesis 2 – Sky View Factor … V. Hypothesis 3 – Air Temperature."

**Skeleton D — Terminal Pair (Verdict then Critique)**
SKELETON: "[Roman numeral]. Conclusion. [Roman numeral + 1]. Evaluation."

1. **Slots & shapes:**
   - Slot 1: header "Conclusion" (single noun).
   - Slot 2: next Roman numeral + header "Evaluation" (single noun).
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** Slot 1: synthesise the verdict across all hypotheses. Slot 2: critique methodology, sampling bias, and equipment accuracy — only after the verdict is on the page.
3. **Original filled version:** "VI. Conclusion … VII. Evaluation."
4. **Demonstration fill:** "VI. Conclusion (asphalt albedo drives the strongest temperature anomaly). VII. Evaluation (limited to one summer window; anemometer accuracy ±0.3 m/s)."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

This section expresses logic through **structural sequencing and parallel naming** rather than connectives, because a TOC is navigational. Grouped by job:

- **Sequencing (ordinal + Roman/letter increments):** "III. Hypothesis 1 – Particle Size and Shape"; "IV. Hypothesis 2 – Hydraulic Radius"; "V. Hypothesis 3 – Velocity" — the numerals enforce the order in which tests are read.
- **Causal/Consequential ordering:** "II. Methods of Investigation … III. Hypothesis 1" — methods placed *before* results, signalling *method → outcome* causality.
- **Contrast/Parallel structure:** "a. Hypotheses … b. Relation to Syllabus … c. Geography in Study Area … d. Geographic Theory" — lettered sub-points at the same indent signal peer-level support.
- **Specification (narrowing the umbrella):** "I. Fieldwork Question and Geographic Context" then "a. Hypotheses" — the lettered entry *specifies* the umbrella's question half.
- **Authority/Justification naming:** "b. Relation to Syllabus" — the heading itself signals an external anchor.
- **Definition-via-placement:** "c. Geography in Study Area" — placing "Geography" before "in Study Area" defines the lens before the site.
- **Derived-composite marker:** "f. Wetted Perimeter" — placed last in the methods block to signal it is *built from* d and e.
- **Evidence-deposit markers:** "Raw Data … Spearman's Rank Correlation Coefficient … Critical Values … Calculations" — each appendix label names a *type* of evidence in the order it is consulted.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern this Contents page relies on is **anticipatory scaffolding**: every heading is named in the order a sceptical reader will need it, so that the document's full argument (question → theory → method → result → verdict → critique → evidence) is mapped *before any prose is read*. To replicate:

1. **State the inquiry and its setting in one umbrella heading.** Write the dual-purpose noun phrase first (e.g. "[Phenomenon] Question and [Place] Context").
2. **Convert the inquiry into testable statements as the first sub-point.** Use a plural noun ("Hypotheses") so the reader sees there is more than one.
3. **Insert an authority link second.** Name the syllabus, option, or external framework so the work is justified before any context is given.
4. **Define the setting third.** Use a noun phrase of the form "[Discipline] in [Place]" so the lens precedes the site.
5. **Layer the theoretical model fourth.** End the intro block with the conceptual framework that will be tested.
6. **Open a Methods block; list equipment before variables.** Tools must be visible before the procedure that uses them.
7. **Order variables in field-recording sequence.** Place the *derived/composite* variable last so it visibly depends on the earlier ones.
8. **Close Methods with a reliability sub-point.** "Sampling Strategies" signals that the reader may now trust (or question) the data.
9. **Mirror each methods variable with a Hypothesis results section.** Use identical wording and increment the count so the reader can match test to measurement.
10. **Pair Conclusion with Evaluation.** The verdict appears first; the critique appears immediately after, in its own numbered section.
11. **Deposit evidence last, in workflow order.** Raw data → statistical test → threshold table → workings, so a reader can verify the argument step-by-step in the same sequence the student built it.
