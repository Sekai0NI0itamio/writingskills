## What This Section Does (content sequence)

For a **method/procedure sub-section that introduces a quantitative technique and derives its working equation**, the moves in order are:

1. **Bridge from prior analytical observation** — close the previous sub-argument (a graph form) and signal that a different kind of evidence is now needed.
2. **Propose the technique** — name the method and its key reagents in one sentence ("could be employed" — hedged because the design is still being argued).
3. **Explain what the technique physically measures** — so the reader knows what variable the experiment actually delivers.
4. **List the required knowns** — the volume/concentration values that must be in hand.
5. **Justify the role of the unusual reagent** — explain *why* a seemingly extra substance (HCl) is in the procedure; this sets up the stoichiometric trick.
6. **Signal the derivation** — "could be calculated as follows".
7. **Write the working equation** — one or two lines of definition in a box, separate from prose.
8. **Chain of ∴-marked deductions** — each step rearranges or substitutes from the previous, anchored to the chemical premise stated in step 5.
9. **Final rearranged form** — the equation the experimenter will plug numbers into, given its own number.

Why this order: each move hands the reader the **one piece of information** the next move needs. The justification of HCl (step 5) is *delayed* until after the procedure is described so it lands as the chemical premise that powers the derivation (steps 7–9). If it came first, the procedure would feel unmotivated; if it came last, the derivation would float.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**Skeleton A — "Bridge into a new technique"**
> "From (previous equation number), we can see that [analytical consequence]. Finally, in order to determine [target quantity], [method] could be employed."

1. *What each slot holds:*
   - Slot 1: a parenthetical reference to the prior equation (numeric).
   - Slot 2: a short "what the equation implies" clause (will/shall + result).
   - Slot 3: the quantity the next paragraph will resolve (noun phrase).
   - Slot 4: a passive method description with hedged modal ("could be employed / could be used / may be performed").
2. *How to fill with a different idea:* Take any experiment that has both an analytical step (a graph/formula) and an experimental step (a measurement). Use slot 1 to point back at the analytical step's equation; slot 2 states the consequence of that equation; slot 3 names the new quantity the experimental step will yield; slot 4 names the experimental technique in the passive.
3. *Original filled version:* "From (5), we can see that plotting time against will result in a straight line of positive gradient k. Finally, in order to determine the concentration of the reactants…titrations using the titrant sodium hydroxide in the presence of excess HCl could be employed."

**Skeleton B — "Justify a quirky reagent, then derive"**

1. *Slot shapes:* Slot 1 = method name + hedged conditional ("would indicate"); slot 2 = bulleted-style list of required values (volume, concentration, sample); slot 3 = a sentence with "The purpose of X is to…i.e. to…" that states both role and mechanism; slot 4 = a transition sentence that launches a derivation.
2. *How to fill:* Pick any analytical method that uses an *auxiliary* substance (a quench, a buffer, an indicator, an internal standard). **Skeleton C — "∴ chain derivation"**
> "[Quantity] is simply expressed as [equation]. Based on [chemical premise just stated], it can be deduced that [stoichiometric relation]. ∴ [rearranged form]. ∴ [substituted measurable form]. ∴ [final rearranged form] (eq number)."

1. *Slot shapes:* Slot 1 = a boxed-style "is simply expressed as" line (definition, c = n/V shape); slot 2 = "Based on…it can be deduced that" (links derivation to the chemically-justified reagent role); slots 3–5 = three "∴" lines, each a single algebraic step from the previous; final slot = an equation number.
2. *How to fill:* State a fundamental relation (moles = concentration × volume, absorbance = εlc, etc.). Anchor the first ∴ step to the role you justified in Skeleton B's slot 3 (this is what makes the math *follow from the chemistry*). Each subsequent ∴ is purely algebra — rearrange, substitute knowns, then isolate the target variable. Number the final form.
3. *Original filled version:* "The concentration of the reactants…is simply expressed as: c = n/V. Based on the purpose of HCl, it can be deduced that: nHCl = n + nNaOH. ∴ c = (nHCl − nNaOH)/V. ∴ c = (VHCl cHCl − VNaOH cNaOH)/V. ∴ 1/c = V / (VHCl cHCl − VNaOH cNaOH) (6)."

Based on the purpose of the standard solution, it can be deduced that: Astd/Aunk = cstd/cunk. ∴ cunk = cstd · (Aunk/Astd). ∴ cunk = cstd · Aunk / Astd. ∴ Aunk = (εl cstd / Astd) · Aunk (a no-op here — better: Aunk/Astd as the working ratio) (4)."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / transition**
- "Finally, in order to determine the concentration of the reactants" — opens the last method proposal after earlier ones.
- "From the measured volume of NaOH" — launches the derivation block.

**Cause / consequence / deduction**
- "Based on the purpose of HCl, it can be deduced that" — anchors the math to the chemically-justified reagent role.
- "∴" (used four times in succession) — marks each pure algebraic consequence.
- "therefore the concentration of the reactants could be calculated" — the implicit promise fulfilled by the derivation.

**Specification / clarification**
- "i.e. to stop the reaction from proceeding" — refines the stated function into a mechanism.
- "alongside the sample volume of the reaction mixture" — appends a required known.

**Soft proposal / hedging (signalling that design is still being argued)**
- "could be employed" — first mention of the titration method.
- "would indicate the volume of a known concentration of NaOH solution" — conditional explanation of what the titration *would* do.
- "must be known" — requirement stated with authority.

**Function / purpose verbs**
- "The purpose of … is to serve as" — explicit role labelling for an auxiliary reagent.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern this section uses is: **bridge-from-prior-step → propose technique → mechanism → required knowns → justify auxiliary reagent → launch derivation → ∴-chain to working equation**. Replicate for a new idea as follows:

1. **Pick an equation from earlier in the IA** whose analytical consequence you have already stated. Open with "From (X), we can see that [consequence]." This closes the prior thread in one sentence.
2. **Switch register** with "Finally, in order to determine [target quantity]," and name the new technique + key reagents in a passive clause ("could be employed / used / performed").
3. **Explain what the technique physically reports** — the one measurand it delivers ("would indicate the volume / voltage / absorbance of …").
4. **List the calibration inputs** the reader must already have ("The volume and concentration of X, alongside Y must be known").
5. **Justify the auxiliary substance** with a "The purpose of X is to …, i.e. to …" sentence — this is the single most important sentence because it becomes the *chemical premise* of the derivation.
6. **Transition into math** with "From the measured [output], [target] could be calculated as follows" — then box a one-line definition (e.g. c = n/V).
7. **Link the chemistry to the algebra** with "Based on [the role stated in step 5], it can be deduced that [stoichiometric/physical relation]."
8. **Write three "∴" lines in succession** — each one a *single* algebraic step from the previous (rearrange → substitute measured quantities → isolate target). Number the final form so it can be referenced later.

The discipline to maintain: each sentence must give the reader exactly the *one* fact the next sentence needs. The justification in step 5 is intentionally delayed until step 7 so it reads as the premise of the math, not as a digression.
