# Idea Flow Notes: biology_7_may2021_f2EKQVzK41czTZ1f — and UV radiation are skin damage, sunburns and the increased risk of developing skin cancer

## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1** (continuation of risk list, 3 sentences)
- S1: citation finishing a list — "as well as heatstroke and dehydration" → DOUBLES a category (adds a second related risk to an already-running list). Hands to S2 by **consequence**: now that two more risks are named, the next move must be the response.
- S2: response — "In order to avoid the risks mentioned" → DOUBLES a mitigation (names the purpose). Hands to S3 by **fulfilment of purpose**: once the aim is stated, the specific actions follow.
- S3: specification of actions — "the use of protection measures such" → DOUBLES an instantiation (lists concrete measures matching the stated aim). Hands to nothing (paragraph ends with the compliance commitment implicit).

**Paragraph 2** (ethical + environmental declaration, 2 sentences)
- S1: negative claim — "no intentional or unintentional harm" → DOUBLES a blanket assurance (a single flat denial covering three categories). Hands to S2 by **authority citation**: the reassurance needs a basis.
- S2: authority basis — "The investigation was planned according to" → DOUBLES a compliance citation (a formal source backing the prior claim). Hands to nothing (section ends).

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is the **tail end of a "Safety, Ethics, Limitations" (or Methodology closing) section**, plus a **data-table bridge**. The ordered moves are:

1. **Risk enumeration (residual)** — finish listing the specific physical/environmental hazards still not yet covered (UV damage, then heat/dehydration). Order: most directly experiment-relevant first (sun-related), then environmental extremes second — because the reader first needs to know what the *set-up* exposed the experimenter to, then what the *outdoor context* added.
2. **Mitigation enumeration** — name the protective behaviours used against those risks. Order follows the risk list 1-to-1 so the reader can match each risk to a control. *Why this order:* risk → response is the natural justification chain; flipping it would make the protection measures feel unmotivated.
3. **Ethics declaration (negative)** — a single blanket statement that nothing was harmed. Placed *after* physical risk because in IB coursework the reader expects physical safety first, then ethical assurance.
4. **Ethics declaration (positive, source-backed)** — cite the IB guideline the procedure followed. Placed *after* the negative claim because a flat denial sounds hollow without an authority behind it.
5. **Data table transition** — the very next page opens "Table 3. Raw data table" which is the **evidence handoff**: now that procedure and ethics are closed, the reader is shown what was actually measured.

A student replicating this with a different topic (e.g. a chemistry IA on reaction rates) would: (1) close the risk list with two paired hazards and a citation, (2) write "In order to avoid…" and list two specific PPE/control actions, (3) write the negative ethics line covering humans/animals/environment, (4) add the IB-guideline citation, (5) place the raw data table immediately after.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**Skeleton A — "Risk-tail + Mitigation" paragraph**
SKELETON: "[Citation] as well as [secondary risk category] (Citation). In order to avoid the risks mentioned the use of [protection category] such as [concrete item 1] as well as the [repeated action 2] were utilized."

1. **Slot 1** — the secondary risk category, grammatically a noun phrase parallel to whatever the previous sentence listed; **Slot 2** — a parenthetical citation tying the secondary risk to a source; **Slot 3** — a purpose clause opening with "In order to avoid" that retroactively justifies everything; **Slot 4** — a protection noun phrase (e.g. "protection measures", "safety equipment"); **Slot 5** — the first concrete example (a hat, a glove); **Slot 6** — a second concrete action phrased as a gerund (drinking, ventilating).
2. **Fill instructions:** slot 1 = pick the *next* physical hazard not yet named in the section (must be one the set-up caused, not a generic one). Slot 2 = attach a real source. Slot 3 = write the purpose clause in the past tense of an actual paper. Slot 4 = name a *category* of control, not the item itself. Slots 5–6 = one tangible object + one repeated behaviour, joined with "as well as" to mirror the source.
3. **Original fill:** "(Jakuboski, 2015) as well as heatstroke and dehydration … In order to avoid the risks mentioned the use of protection measures such as hats and sunscreen as well as the repeated drinking of water were utilized."
4. **Demo fill (chemistry IA on acid–base titration):** "(ScienceDirect, 2020) as well as chemical burns from sodium hydroxide splashes. In order to avoid the risks mentioned the use of protective equipment such as safety goggles and lab coats as well as the repeated rinsing of the workstation were performed."

**Skeleton B — "Blanket-ethics + Authority" paragraph**
SKELETON: "Ethical and environmental: [flat negative claim covering three categories] was imposed on [category 1], [category 2] or [category 3] during the course of this investigation. The investigation was planned according to [authority name] [year]."

1. **Slot 1** — a label fragment "Ethical and environmental:" acting as the paragraph's topic signpost; **Slot 2** — a negative absolute ("no … harm", "no … damage", "no … breach") with a doubled modifier ("intentional or unintentional", "direct or indirect"); **Slot 3** — a three-item list of the protected parties (humans, animals, environment — or subjects, data, school property); **Slot 4** — a compliance verb ("planned according to", "conducted in line with", "reviewed under"); **Slot 5** — the named authority (IB, school board, ethics committee).
2. **Fill instructions:** slot 2 = use a *negated noun* ("harm", "damage", "interference") plus a paired adjective to sound thorough. Slot 3 = list exactly three nouns of ascending scope (people → other life → surroundings). Slot 5 = name a real institutional guideline, not a generic phrase.
3. **Original fill:** "Ethical and environmental: no intentional or unintentional harm was imposed on humans, animals or the environment … The investigation was planned according to IB ethical and safety guidelines."
4. **Demo fill (psychology IA on memory recall):** "Ethical and environmental: no direct or indirect distress was caused to participants, data or the school setting during the course of this investigation. The study was designed in accordance with BPS ethical guidelines for research with minors (2018)."

**Skeleton C — "Data table opener"**
SKELETON: "Table [N]. [descriptor of table contents] with [outliers/markers] marked in [colour/method]"

1. **Slot 1** — table number; **Slot 2** — a brief content descriptor naming the variable measured; **Slot 3** — a flagging phrase ("with outliers marked", "with trial 4 excluded", "with anomalous values highlighted"); **Slot 4** — the visual method ("in yellow", "with an asterisk", "in italics").
2. **Fill instructions:** slot 2 must state *what* the columns hold, not the conclusion. Slot 3 must be present so the reader knows to look for the markers before scanning the numbers.
3. **Original fill:** "Table 3. Raw data table with outliers marked in yellow."
4. **Demo fill:** "Table 2. Reaction time dataset with participant 7 excluded, marked in grey."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / listing**
- "as well as" — "(Jakuboski, 2015) as well as heatstroke and dehydration" (adds a parallel item to a running list)
- "such as" — "protection measures such as hats and sunscreen" (introduces concrete instances of a just-named category)
- "as well as the repeated drinking" (chains a second action type in the same mitigation list)

**Purpose / consequence**
- "In order to avoid the risks mentioned" (purpose clause that retroactively justifies the immediately following list)

**Specification / instantiation**
- "such as" — same as above; functions here as example-introducer after a category noun

**Authority / source handling**
- "(Jakuboski, 2015)" (parenthetical citation appending a risk claim)
- "(OSH Answers, 2021)" (second parenthetical citation, same role)
- "planned according to" — "planned according to IB ethical and safety guidelines" (compliance verb attaching the prior claim to an institution)

**Negation / absolute claim**
- "no intentional or unintentional harm" (a doubled-negation construction that signals completeness of the assurance)

**Framing / labelling**
- "Ethical and environmental:" (a colon-terminated signpost that names the paragraph's category before the claim lands)

**Visual / data-handling**
- "marked in yellow" — "outliers marked in yellow" (specifies the visual encoding used inside the table that follows)

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses the **"enumerate-then-mitigate" pattern**, backed by an **"assure-then-cite"** pair. To replicate the pattern for any new IA closing section, follow these steps:

1. **Name the second-tier risk.** Pick a hazard that *was not yet listed* in the prior paragraphs. Attach a citation in parentheses so the risk feels sourced, not asserted.
2. **Link it with a parallel connector.** Use "as well as" to chain it onto the risk that was already on the page; the reader now reads the two as a single list.
3. **Open a purpose clause with "In order to avoid".** This phrase is the hinge: it converts the risk list into a *problem statement* that demands a *response*, and it telegraphs to the reader that mitigations are coming.
4. **Name the mitigation category, then two examples.** Write a *category noun* ("protection measures", "safety controls") and immediately unpack it with "such as" + one tangible object + "as well as" + one repeated behaviour. The double-as-well-as mirrors the risk list, so the reader feels the response is complete.
5. **Drop a colon signpost for the next category.** Use a two-word label ("Ethical and environmental:", "Limitations:", "Sources of error:") to switch paragraph topic without a transition sentence.
6. **Issue a doubled-negative blanket claim.** State what was *not* done, using two paired modifiers ("intentional or unintentional", "direct or indirect") to sound exhaustive.
7. **Cite the governing authority.** End the section with "planned according to" / "conducted under" + the specific institutional guideline. This converts the previous sentence's reassurance from opinion into compliance.
8. **(Optional) Place the raw data table immediately after.** The phrase "Table N. [content] with [flags] marked in [encoding]" gives the reader a one-sentence bridge from procedure to evidence.

The logic path the reader walks: *one more risk was present → we took specific actions against it → nothing ethical was breached → and here is the institutional rule we followed → here is the data to judge us by.*
