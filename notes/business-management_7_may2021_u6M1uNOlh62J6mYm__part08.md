## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1 (Figure 1 / Figure 2 block): survey question + day preference**

- Sentence 1 — **figure-caption framing**: "Figure 1: Would the person consider sports lessons…" — frames the survey item by quoting the question, which then hands the reader into the answer graph because a question naturally invites a response.
- Sentence 2 — **data description**: "This graph shows the days at which…" — restates what the figure shows; hands forward by promising a specific result.
- Sentence 3 — **verdict (top result)**: "The most popular choice was Wednesday…" — delivers the headline answer; hands forward by giving a ranked comparison that the next sentence will complete.

**Paragraph 2 (Figure 3 block): time preference**

- Sentence 1 — **figure-caption framing**: "This graph shows the times at which…" — re-anchors with a new variable (time), the natural next axis of a scheduling survey after day.
- Sentence 2 — **verdict (top result)**: "The time range of 15:00-16:30 was the most preferred." — names the winning slot; hands forward by introducing a ranked second place that mirrors the day block's structure.
- Sentence 3 — **specification of runner-up**: "Similarly, 16:30-18:00 was the second most popular option." — the word "Similarly" signals the reader should expect the same top-two pattern just used, keeping the rhythm of the previous paragraph.

**Paragraph 3 (Figure 4 block): cost preference, defined population**

- Sentence 1 — **figure-caption framing**: "This graph shows the results of the questionnaire, for which was aimed at parents and students of the school." — names the figure and its sample, handing forward into a population refinement.
- Sentence 2 — **scope definition (unpack)**: "In the result below, the word 'adult' is defined to be respondents who are at the age of 19+." — defines a working term; the reader expects a consequence (why this definition matters).
- Sentence 3 — **exclusion justification**: "Students were excluded in these results due to their dependency in finance and costs…" — gives the cause for the prior definition, handing forward to the actual finding.
- Sentence 4 — **verdict**: "The graph below shows that the most preferred cost range is 800 − 1000 TL." — delivers the headline number, closing the paragraph by satisfying the claim set up in sentence 1.

**Paragraph 4 (Figure 5 block): age vs sport**

- Sentence 1 — **figure-caption framing + claim**: "This graph shows the relationship between age and sports preferences…" — announces the cross-tabulation; hands forward because a relationship claim must be unpacked.
- Sentence 2 — **evidence unpacked (instance 1)**: "Kickboxing especially attracted younger individuals." — delivers the first pole of the relationship.
- Sentence 3 — **evidence unpacked (instance 2 + contrast)**: "Pilates, on the other hand, was preferred the most by older individuals." — "on the other hand" is a contrast hinge, handing forward by closing the age polarity.
- Sentence 4 — **caveat / exclusion note**: "One person was also excluded from the results as they did not want to disclose their age." — methodological footnote; terminates the paragraph.

**Paragraph 5 (Figure 6 block): gender vs sport**

- Sentence 1 — **figure-caption framing + claim**: "This graph shows the relationship between gender and sports preferences…" — mirrors the age paragraph's opener, signaling parallelism; the reader expects the same pattern (polarity unpacked).
- Sentence 2 — **evidence unpacked (top result)**: "Pilates was the most liked sports by the majority, with this majority mostly being female." — delivers one pole and qualifies it with the demographic skew, closing the paragraph by satisfying the relationship claim.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

A "results / data presentation" section made of repeating mini-blocks, one per figure. The moves are:

1. **Figure-caption framing** (what the figure shows). Sets up the reader's expectation that a concrete claim is about to follow.
2. **Population / scope definition** (only when the figure filters respondents — e.g. age 19+ adults). Necessary before any verdict can be trusted, so it must come before the data claim.
3. **Top-result verdict** (the single most preferred option). The reader needs one anchor number before being asked to compare.
4. **Runner-up / second-place specification** (the next-ranked option). Logical because the survey is rank-ordered; the runner-up contextualises the winner.
6. **Cross-tabulation unpack** (when the figure relates two variables — age or gender against sport): one pole (e.g. younger → kickboxing), then contrast ("on the other hand") with the opposite pole (older → pilates). Order matters: the contrast marker only works once pole 1 is established.
7. **Methodological exclusion / caveat** (e.g. non-disclosing respondent). Comes last in a paragraph because it is housekeeping, not a finding.

The repeating-block architecture lets the reader predict each paragraph; the only variation is whether a block carries a definition move (because the sample is filtered) or a cross-tabulation move (because the figure compares two variables). A student copying this sequence should: caption → (if needed) define who is in the count → state the winning option → state the runner-up → (if cross-tab) contrast two poles → (if relevant) note exclusions.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**Skeleton A — Single-variable ranking (used for day, time, cost paragraphs)**

```
SKELETON: "Figure X: [survey question wording]. This graph shows the [variable] at which the respondents were most [attribute] in [context]. The most [attribute] choice was [winner], followed closely by [runner-up]."
```

1. **Slot 1 — survey question wording.** A noun-phrase question, the kind a respondent would answer. Grammatically a clause headed by a modal/auxiliary ("Would the person consider…", "Which day…").
2. **Slot 2 — "This graph shows the [variable]…"** A clause that names the measured axis (day / time / cost). Past tense "were most [attribute]" or present "shows". This slot promises a ranked result.
3. **Slot 3 — winner.** A concrete option ("Wednesday", "15:00-16:30", "800-1000 TL").
4. **Slot 4 — runner-up.** A second concrete option, introduced by "followed closely by" or "Similarly… was the second most…"

**How to fill with a DIFFERENT idea:** pick a scheduling question with three or more discrete options. Slot 1: write the question exactly as it appeared on the questionnaire. Slot 2: name the axis being ranked (price, duration, location). Slot 3: name the modal answer with the raw count if useful. Slot 4: name the second-place answer using the same connectives ("followed closely by", "Similarly").

**Original filled version:** *"Figure 1: Would the person consider sports lessons with trained professionals? This graph shows the days at which the respondents were most comfortable in attending the lessons on. The most popular choice was Wednesday, followed closely by Monday."*

**Demonstration fill with a different idea:** *"Figure 1: Which platform do students use most for revision? This graph shows the apps from which the respondents were most likely to revise. The most common choice was YouTube, followed closely by Quizlet."*

**Skeleton B — Filtered-sample ranking (used for the cost paragraph, which excludes students)**

```
SKELETON: "This graph shows the results of the questionnaire, [for which was aimed at sample]. In the result below, the word '[term]' is defined to be respondents who [criterion]. [Group] were excluded in these results due to [reason]. The graph below shows that the most preferred [variable] is [winner]."
```

1. **Slot 1 — figure caption + raw sample.** Names who was surveyed overall.
2. **Slot 2 — working-term definition.** A formal definition in present tense "is defined to be". Required when the figure counts a subset.
3. **Slot 3 — exclusion justification.** A causal clause "due to [reason]". Pairs with slot 2: defining the kept group forces explaining why the others were dropped.
4. **Slot 4 — verdict.** A "most preferred [variable] is [value]" sentence that closes the paragraph.

**How to fill with a DIFFERENT idea:** use when a survey figure reports only one demographic (e.g. only paying customers, only voters, only adults). Slot 1: state who was originally asked. Slot 2: define the kept subgroup with a numeric or categorical cut-off. Slot 3: justify why the dropped group was dropped (financial dependence, ineligibility, irrelevant). Slot 4: give the modal preference of the kept subgroup.

**Original filled version:** *"This graph shows the results of the questionnaire, for which was aimed at parents and students of the school. In the result below, the word 'adult' is defined to be respondents who are at the age of 19+. Students were excluded in these results due to their dependency in finance and costs… The graph below shows that the most preferred cost range is 800-1000 TL."*

**Demonstration fill with a different idea:** *"This graph shows the results of the questionnaire, which was aimed at all members of the gym. In the result below, the word 'active member' is defined to be respondents who attended at least once per week. Casual visitors were excluded in these results due to their irregular attendance patterns. The graph below shows that the most preferred class time is 18:00-19:00."*

**Skeleton C — Cross-tabulation with contrastive poles (used for age and gender paragraphs)**

```
SKELETON: "This graph shows the relationship between [variable A] and [variable B] listed in the questionnaire. [Category 1] especially attracted [pole of variable A]. [Category 2], on the other hand, was preferred the most by [opposite pole of variable A]. [Optional: methodological exclusion note]."
```

1. **Slot 1 — relationship claim.** "shows the relationship between X and Y" — sets up a polarity that the next sentences must deliver.
2. **Slot 2 — pole 1 + category.** "Category 1 attracted pole of A" — verb "attracted" or "was chosen by".
3. **Slot 3 — pole 2 + category with contrast hinge.** "Category 2, on the other hand, was preferred the most by opposite pole of A." The "on the other hand" only works after pole 1 is on the page.
4. **Slot 4 — exclusion note.** "One [unit] was also excluded… because [reason]." Placed last; can be dropped if not applicable.

**How to fill with a DIFFERENT idea:** use whenever a figure cross-tabulates two variables where one splits the sample into two visible groups. Slot 1: name both variables. Slot 2: pick the category that skews toward one pole. Slot 3: pick a second category that skews toward the opposite pole, using the "on the other hand" hinge to make the contrast explicit. Slot 4: add only if some respondents could not be classified.

**Original filled version:** *"This graph shows the relationship between age and sports preferences listed in the questionnaire. Kickboxing especially attracted younger individuals. Pilates, on the other hand, was preferred the most by older individuals. One person was also excluded from the results as they did not want to disclose their age."*

**Demonstration fill with a different idea:** *"This graph shows the relationship between year group and music taste listed in the questionnaire. Heavy metal especially attracted Year 12 students. Classical, on the other hand, was preferred the most by Year 13 students. One person was also excluded from the results as they did not want to disclose their year group."*

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / ranking connectives**
- "followed closely by" — *"The most popular choice was Wednesday, followed closely by Monday."* (positions runner-up right after winner)
- "Similarly… was the second most…" — *"Similarly, 16:30-18:00 was the second most popular option."* (signals parallel ranking structure)

**Contrast hinge**
- "on the other hand" — *"Pilates, on the other hand, was preferred the most by older individuals."* (pivots from pole 1 to pole 2 inside a cross-tabulation)

**Definition / scope-setting**
- "is defined to be" — *"the word 'adult' is defined to be respondents who are at the age of 19+."* (formal working-term definition before a verdict)
- "due to" — *"Students were excluded… due to their dependency in finance and costs…"* (causal justification for an exclusion)

**Evidence-handling / framing**
- "This graph shows…" — *"This graph shows the days at which the respondents were most comfortable…"* (recurring figure-caption stem, one per paragraph)
- "the relationship between X and Y" — *"the relationship between age and sports preferences listed in the questionnaire."* (signals cross-tabulation)
- "was preferred the most by" — *"Pilates, on the other hand, was preferred the most by older individuals."* (passive construction that links a category to a demographic pole)

**Verdict verbs**
- "was the most preferred" — *"The time range of 15:00-16:30 was the most preferred."*
- "especially attracted" — *"Kickboxing especially attracted younger individuals."* (intensifier + verb; marks the stronger pole)

**Exclusion verbs**
- "were excluded" — *"Students were excluded in these results…"*
- "did not want to disclose" — *"…as they did not want to disclose their age."* (reason clause for an exclusion)

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses a **figure-caption → definition (if needed) → headline verdict → contrastive unpack** pattern, organised in repeating blocks. To explain a NEW data figure the same way:

1. **Caption the figure literally.** Write "This graph shows the [variable]…" or "This graph shows the relationship between [variable A] and [variable B]…" before any verdict. This sentence does no analysis — it only tells the reader what axis the figure plots.
2. **If the figure filters the sample, define the kept group first.** Use "is defined to be respondents who [criterion]" and immediately justify the cut with "due to [reason]." Skip this step when the figure uses the full sample.
3. **State the single most preferred option as one short verdict sentence.** Use "The most [adjective] choice was [winner]." Do not yet list everything — one anchor number first.
4. **Add the runner-up using a ranking connective.** Use "followed closely by" for the same variable, or "Similarly, X was the second most…" to keep the rhythm of the previous paragraph.
5. **If the figure cross-tabulates two variables, unpack the relationship as two poles.** Deliver pole 1 ("Category A especially attracted Pole-1"), then pivot with "Category B, on the other hand, was preferred the most by Pole-2." The hinge word only works after pole 1 is on the page, so the order is fixed.
6. **Close with an exclusion note if any respondents could not be classified.** Use "One [unit] was also excluded… because [reason]." Place it last so it does not interrupt the finding.
7. **Repeat steps 1-6 for each figure, in the order the figures appear.** The repeating structure lets the reader predict each paragraph, which is what makes the section readable.
