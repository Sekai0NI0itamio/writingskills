## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section does *not* explain an idea in the discursive sense — it is a citation list — so the pattern to replicate is the **citation-construction pattern**, not an explanatory pattern. Steps:

1. **Classify the source** — is it a peer-reviewed article, a working paper, a book, a web page, or a personal communication? The classification dictates the slot order.
2. **Identify the author in surname-comma-first format** — never invert; this is the bibliographic anchor.
3. **Transcribe the title verbatim** — preserve original capitalisation and punctuation; enclose articles/report titles in quotation marks, journal titles in italics.
4. **State the container** — the journal name, the institutional publisher, or the website.
5. **Add the locator** — `vol./no./pp.` for journals, `Working Paper No.` for institutional papers, page number for books, URL for web sources.
6. **Date the source in the format matching its genre** — bare year `(2011)` for journals, full date `17 June 2020` for web/news, full date `Oct 2, 2020` for interviews.
7. **Add a retrieval marker for online sources** — `"Retrieved [date]. [URL]"` to lock in the access moment.
8. **Number consecutively across pages** — let the integer chain, not a new heading, carry the reader across the page break; the page number stands alone.
