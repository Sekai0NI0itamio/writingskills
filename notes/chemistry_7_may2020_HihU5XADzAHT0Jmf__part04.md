## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **procedure paragraph with embedded justification and endpoint definition**. The ordered moves a method section of this type makes:

1. **Apparatus + mixture setup** — establishes the container, the reactants, and the thermal environment (water bath) so the reader knows the starting state.
2. **Reaction trigger** — starts the clock and the stirring so that all subsequent t-values are measured from a known zero.
3. **Sampling protocol** — states interval, volume, and replicate count; defines what counts as a "data point."
4. **Justification of replication** — answers the implicit "why more than one?" with a reliability/random-error argument.
5. **Quench/preparation of each aliquot** — stops the reaction so what is measured is the state at t, not a moving target.
6. **Indicator + titration step** — performs the analytical measurement.
7. **Endpoint definition** — operationalises a vague word ("endpoint") into an observable colour change so a second reader could repeat it.
8. **Apparatus detail** — short, parenthetical rig notes that prevent ambiguity.
9. **Raw data capture** — what is read off the burette.
10. **Aggregation + formula reference** — collapses replicates to a mean and routes to a downstream equation.
11. **Time-series extension** — applies the same procedure across all t-values.
12. **Condition replication** — applies the same procedure across all T-values.

**Why this order works:** setup → trigger → sample → justify → process → measure → define endpoint → record → aggregate → extend in t → extend in T. Each move creates the *precondition* for the next (e.g., without the clock starting in Move 2, the intervals in Move 3 are meaningless; without the colour definition in Move 7, the burette reading in Move 9 is not reproducible). A student replicating this sequence with any kinetic/sampling experiment (e.g., following a reaction by quenching aliquots, by conductivity, by colorimetry) keeps the same skeleton.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

### SKELETON A — "Sampling with epistemic justification"
`[A short apparatus/setup clause]. A timer was started and the mixture [stirred/equilibrated] for [X seconds]. At [regular/equal] time intervals of [Δt], [n] [volume] samples of the [reaction mixture/solution] were [withdrawn/taken] using a [instrument]. More than one sample was taken in order to increase the reliability of the measurements and minimise random error.`

- **Slot grammar:** S1 = past passive setup clause; S2 = past passive trigger with duration adverbial; S3 = adverbial + cardinal + volume + past passive; S4 = purpose-clause justification ("in order to …").
- **How to fill with a different idea:** Pick a kinetic or time-resolved experiment (e.g., enzyme assay, iodine clock, cooling curve). Choose a Δt, a sample volume, and n ≥ 3. Keep the "in order to … reliability … random error" sentence **verbatim or near-verbatim** — that exact justification move is the load-bearing piece.
- **Original fill:** "hydroxide were mixed … A timer on the stopwatch was started and the solution was stirred … At equal time intervals of five minutes, three 10cm³ samples … were measured using a 25cm³ pipette. More than one sample was taken in order to increase the reliability of the measurements and minimise random error."
- **Demo fill with a different idea:** "Substrate and enzyme were mixed in a 25 cm³ beaker held in a 30 °C water bath. A timer was started and the mixture was stirred for 15 seconds. At equal time intervals of one minute, three 5 cm³ samples of the reaction mixture were withdrawn using a 10 cm³ pipette. More than one sample was taken in order to increase the reliability of the measurements and minimise random error."

### SKELETON B — "Action with embedded endpoint definition"
`Each sample was then added to [volume] of [reagent] in a [vessel]. After adding [indicator], the sample was [measured by technique] using [titrant/instrument]. The [technique] was stopped when the endpoint was reached—that is, when [observable change].`

- **Slot grammar:** S1 = past passive + reagent prep; S2 = past passive indicator + technique; S3 = past passive + dash-clause "that is, when [visible change]."
- **How to fill with a different idea:** Keep the **dash + "that is, when …"** construction — it is the defining rhetorical move. The visible change must be a colour/state shift detectable by eye. Replace titrant with any back-titration, colorimetric, or conductivity endpoint.
- **Original fill:** "Each sample was then added to 10cm³ of 0.04moldm⁻³ hydrochloric acid … in a conical flask. After adding one drop of phenolphthalein, the sample was titrated using sodium hydroxide. The burette was stopped when the endpoint of titration was reached—that is, when the colour of the entire sample turned pink/purple."
- **Demo fill with a different idea:** "Each sample was then added to 20 cm³ of starch solution in a conical flask. After adding two drops of DCPIP, the sample was titrated using sodium thiosulphate. The burette was stopped when the endpoint of titration was reached—that is, when the blue colour of the entire sample disappeared to colourless."

### SKELETON C — "Capture → aggregate → route to formula"
`The [initial and final] [quantities] were recorded and the difference found. The [mean/average] [quantity] was then calculated from the [n] samples, which was used to calculate the values according to ([equation number]).`

- **Slot grammar:** S1 = past passive capture + subtraction; S2 = past passive aggregation + non-defining relative clause routing to a numbered equation.
- **How to fill with a different idea:** Use any quantity that comes from a difference of readings (titre, absorbance before/after, mass before/after) and any downstream equation. The numbered reference "(6)" can be replaced with the student's own equation label.
- **Original fill:** "The initial and final volumes of the NaOH in the burette were recorded and the difference found. The mean volume was then calculated from the three samples, which was used to calculate the values according to (6)."
- **Demo fill with a different idea:** "The initial and final absorbance readings of the colorimeter were recorded and the difference found. The mean absorbance was then calculated from the three samples, which was used to calculate the concentration values according to (3)."

### SKELETON D — "Closing two-axis extension"
`[The procedure] was carried out for the other [axis 1 values] until [boundary of axis 1]. Finally, the experiment was repeated for the remaining [n] [axis 2 values].`

- **Slot grammar:** S1 = past passive extension across axis 1 + temporal boundary; S2 = past passive replication across axis 2, fronted by the temporal connector "Finally."
- **How to fill with a different idea:** Name the two independent variables (e.g., time + temperature, concentration + pH, dose + time). The word "remaining" must always refer to the *second* axis.
- **Original fill:** "Titrations were carried out for the other time intervals until 30 minutes into the reaction taking place. Finally, the experiment was repeated for the remaining four temperatures."
- **Demo fill with a different idea:** "Readings were taken for the other concentrations until 0.08 mol dm⁻³ had been tested. Finally, the experiment was repeated for the remaining four pH values."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / order of operations**
- "Each sample was **then** added to 10cm³ of 0.04moldm⁻³ hydrochloric acid" — S5, marks step-order after sampling.
- "**Finally**, the experiment was repeated for the remaining four temperatures." — S12, closes the procedure.
- "**At equal** time intervals of five minutes, three 10cm³ samples … were measured" — S3, embeds cadence into the sampling step.

**Cause / purpose**
- "More than one sample was taken **in order to** increase the reliability of the measurements and minimise random error." — S4, the only explicit purpose clause; carries the section's epistemic justification.

**Specification / definition (the key "that is" move)**
- "The burette was stopped when the endpoint of titration was reached—**that is**, when the colour of the entire sample turned pink/purple." — S7, operationalises a vague noun ("endpoint") into a visible criterion.

**Temporal boundary**
- "Titrations were carried out for the other time intervals **until** 30 minutes into the reaction" — S11, defines the extent of the time series.

**Aggregation / reporting verbs**
- "The mean volume was **then calculated** from the three samples" — S10, names the aggregation action in past passive.
- "The initial and final volumes … **were recorded** and the difference **found**." — S9, bare past passive for routine actions.

**Equipping verbs (low-content filler that the student is allowed to keep brief)**
- "was held in place," "was poured in" — S8, apparatus grounding.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern used: PROCEDURE WITH EMBEDDED JUSTIFICATION AND EXPLICIT ENDPOINT DEFINITION (a "lab-method-with-reason-inline" pattern).**

To reproduce this pattern for any new kinetic / time-resolved / quenched-aliquot experiment:

1. **Name the vessel and the combined reactants in one past-passive clause** ("X and Y were mixed and placed in a [volume] [vessel] that remained in the [thermal environment]"). This sets the starting state.
2. **State the trigger in one sentence that pairs a clock with a mixing action and a short duration** ("A timer was started and the solution was stirred for [n] seconds"). This defines t = 0.
3. **State the sampling rule with three explicit parameters — interval, count, volume — in adverbial-fronted position** ("At equal time intervals of [Δt], [n] [volume] samples … were withdrawn using a [pipette]").
4. **Immediately justify the replicate count in a separate sentence using the fixed phrase "in order to increase the reliability … and minimise random error."** Do *not* bury this inside another sentence.
5. **State the quench/prep step, naming the second reagent, its concentration, and the receiving vessel** ("Each sample was then added to [volume] of [concentration] [reagent] in a [vessel]").
6. **State the analytical step as "indicator + technique"** ("After adding [indicator], the sample was [titrated/measured] using [instrument/titrant]").
7. **Define the endpoint with a dash and the literal phrase "that is, when …"** so the reader can repeat the stop-criterion. ("… was reached—that is, when the colour … turned [X].") This is the single most load-bearing sentence; do not omit it.
8. **Add a one-sentence apparatus note in past passive** if the rig is non-obvious ("The [instrument] was held in place using a [clamp/stand] and [reagent] was poured in through a [funnel]").
9. **State the raw capture as a bare past-passive pair** ("The [initial and final] [readings] were recorded and the difference found").
10. **Aggregate and route to a numbered equation** ("The mean [quantity] was then calculated from the [n] samples, which was used to calculate the values according to ([n])").
11. **Extend across the first independent variable with a temporal/spatial boundary** ("[The procedure] was carried out for the other [values] until [boundary]").
12. **Close by replicating across the second independent variable, fronted by "Finally"** ("Finally, the experiment was repeated for the remaining [n] [values]").
