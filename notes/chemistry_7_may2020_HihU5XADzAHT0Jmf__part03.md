## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1 (Independent Variable)**

1. **LABEL + DEFINITION** — "Independent Variable: The absolute temperature at which the reaction was carried out in Kelvin."
   → Hands the reader forward by stating *what* the variable is, which makes the natural next move the *specific values* that will be tested. The unit is given so the reader knows the measurement scale, setting up the list that follows.

2. **SPECIFICATION (enumeration of conditions)** — "Five conditions were chosen at intervals of 10: 293K, 303K, 313K, 323K and 333K"
   → Hands the reader forward because the reader now asks *why these intervals?* The paragraph delivers concrete values, which automatically raises the "why this gap?" question — a natural hand-off into justification.

3. **JUSTIFICATION (cause → consequence chain)** — "These intervals were chosen as to allow for significant variability… and hence noticeable changes in the rate constants, which would potentially allow for the construction of a graph with a clear linear trend"
   → Hands the reader forward by completing the IV block and pivoting implicitly toward the DV: once the reader knows what the IV will produce ("changes in the rate constants"), the next paragraph logically *must* define the DV. The phrase "rate constants" is the bridge.

**Paragraph 2 (Dependent Variable)**

1. **LABEL + DEFINITION** — "Dependent Variable: The rate constant of the second-order reaction in mol⁻¹ dm³ min⁻¹."
   → Hands the reader forward by stating the variable, its order, and its units — which raises the question "where do those units come from?" prompting the worked derivation.

2. **MECHANISM (worked derivation)** — "The units of the rate constant can be worked out by rearranging (4) for k and dividing moldm⁻³ min⁻¹ (units of rate) by mol² dm⁻⁶ (units of c²)."
   → Hands the reader forward by *proving* the units rather than asserting them — the section closes here because the DV is now fully specified, justified, and unit-verified. Nothing more needs to follow inside this section.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

A "Variables" section in an IB Chemistry IA makes content moves in this fixed order:

1. **Name + define the IV** — first, because until the reader knows what is being changed, every later piece (conditions, rationale, units) is unanchored.
2. **Enumerate the chosen levels/conditions** — second, because a defined variable in the abstract is not yet operationalised; concrete values make the experiment real and let the reader audit spacing.
3. **Justify the choice of levels** — third, because a reader who sees only a list will ask "why these gaps?"; the justification also previews the *outcome* (what the IV will produce in the DV), which sets up the DV paragraph.
4. **Name + define the DV** — fourth, placed *after* IV justification because the IV paragraph's final phrase ("rate constants") has now primed the reader for the DV's introduction.
5. **Derive or verify the DV's units** — last, because the DV has just been introduced with units, and IB examiners expect the units to be *shown* rather than asserted; this closes the section with rigour.

The order works because each move plants a hook (a unit, a list, an unstated reason, a term, a unit symbol) that the next move answers.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A (IV paragraph):**
"[Variable]: [Definition with units]. [N] conditions were chosen at intervals of [Δ]: [list of values with conversion]. These intervals were chosen as to allow for [property of spread], and hence [consequence on DV], which would potentially allow for [analytical output]."

- **Slot 1** (Label + definition): noun phrase naming it, then appositive/definition with units in parentheses. *How to fill:* state the IV's name after a colon, then a noun phrase stating what is measured and the SI/practical unit.
- **Slot 2** (Enumeration): "N conditions… intervals of X: list". *How to fill:* pick a small odd number (3–7), pick a constant step, list all values, optionally give a converted scale.
- **Slot 3** (Justification chain): "These intervals were chosen… and hence… which would potentially allow for…". *How to fill:* connect step size → variability in readings → clearer trend/graph/relationship. Use "hence" + "potentially" to show reasoning, not overclaiming.

*Original fill:* "Independent Variable: The absolute temperature at which the reaction was carried out in Kelvin. Five conditions were chosen at intervals of 10: 293K… These intervals were chosen as to allow for significant variability… and hence noticeable changes in the rate constants, which would potentially allow for the construction of a graph with a clear linear trend between T and ln k."

*Demonstration fill (different idea — pressure as IV for an enzyme-rate IA):* "Independent Variable: The partial pressure of oxygen bubbled through the reaction mixture in kPa. Six conditions were chosen at intervals of 10: 10, 20, 30, 40, 50 and 60 kPa. These intervals were chosen as to allow for a wide enough spread for oxygen to move from limiting to saturating, and hence measurable changes in initial rate, which would potentially allow for the construction of a Michaelis–Menten curve of v₀ against pO₂."

**SKELETON B (DV paragraph):**
"[Variable]: [The measured quantity] of the [order/type] reaction in [units]. The units [can be worked out / follow] by [operation] for [symbol] and [combining the units of A and B] ([units of A]) by [units of B] ([units of B])."

- **Slot 1** (Label + definition): colon, then measured quantity + order/type + explicit unit string. *How to fill:* name the DV, state its reaction order or context, give the unit with superscripts.
- **Slot 2** (Unit derivation): "The units can be worked out by… and dividing [rate units] by [concentration-squared units]". *How to fill:* show the algebra/operation that produces the unit, and label what each unit symbol means in plain English.

*Original fill:* "Dependent Variable: The rate constant of the second-order reaction in mol⁻¹ dm³ min⁻¹. The units of the rate constant can be worked out by rearranging (4) for k and dividing moldm⁻³ min⁻¹ (units of rate) by mol² dm⁻⁶ (units of c²)."

*Demonstration fill (different idea — rate constant of a first-order radioactive decay):* "Dependent Variable: The decay constant λ of the first-order disintegration in s⁻¹. The units of λ can be worked out by rearranging the rate law for λ and dividing counts s⁻¹ (units of activity) by counts (units of N), so the count-units cancel, leaving s⁻¹."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / enumeration:** "Five conditions were chosen" (lists levels of the IV with a count).
- **Cause → consequence:** "and hence noticeable changes" (slot 2 → slot 3 of the IV justification, slot-chain signature).
- **Purpose / aim statement:** "as to allow for" + "which would potentially allow for" (double "allow for" stacks intent and analytical payoff).
- **Specification / concrete data:** "293K, 303K, 313K, 323K and 333K" with bracketed Celsius conversion "20, 30, 40, 50 and 60 ℃".
- **Mathematical justification verb:** "can be worked out by rearranging… for k" (introduces algebra as proof rather than assertion).
- **Unit-handling:** "dividing moldm⁻³ min⁻¹ (units of rate) by mol² dm⁻⁶ (units of c²)" — pairs each symbolic unit with a plain-English gloss in parentheses.
- **Naming/labeling:** "Independent Variable:" / "Dependent Variable:" (colon-headed labels act as paragraph signposts).

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses the **Define → Operationalise → Justify → Derive** pattern. To explain a new variable block using the same pattern:

1. **Name the variable with a colon and define it in one noun phrase**, including its unit at the end (e.g., "X: the quantity measured in unit").
2. **Operationalise it by listing the concrete levels you will test** — give a count, a constant step, and the actual values; convert to a second scale if useful.
3. **Justify the step size in a single sentence using "as to allow for… and hence… which would potentially allow for…"** — the chain should run: step size → variability in readings → clearer analytical result (graph, curve, constant).
4. **Move to the dependent variable** with the same colon-and-noun-phrase template, including units.
5. **Derive the DV's units** rather than asserting them — show the rearrangement ("can be worked out by rearranging [equation] for [symbol]") and label each unit string in parentheses.
