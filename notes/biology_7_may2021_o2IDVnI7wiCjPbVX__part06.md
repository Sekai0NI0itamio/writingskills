## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The "explanation pattern" this section relies on is **dataset declaration by paired-variable announcement**: name two variables, name the per-unit denominator, then (in the sentence that would follow) enumerate the units. The pattern is *incomplete in this section* — only step 1 is present.

Step-by-step instructions to reproduce the pattern:

1. **Open with a closing-position sequencer** ("Finally," / "Lastly," / "In addition,") to signal this is the last block of data you will introduce.
2. **Name the first variable as "the values for [concrete measurable quantity]"** — use a noun phrase, not a clause; keep it short.
3. **Join the second variable with "and"** so the two variables are grammatically parallel; this parallelism signals to the reader the two will be analysed together.
4. **State the normalisation denominator** ("per 100,000 people," "per capita," "per year") immediately after the paired noun phrases, so the units of measurement are unambiguous.
6. **Finish the sentence by specifying the enumeration unit** ("for each country," "for each year," "for each site") — *this step is absent from the submitted section and is the structural gap that makes the sentence trail off.*
5. **Hand off to the next paragraph** (a table, a list, or a discussion) which will then supply the values for each enumeration unit one by one.
