## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section relies on the **Elimination → Survivor Verdict** pattern. The reader is shown that every alternative has been ruled out by a documented source, leaving one actor as the surviving option.

1. **List the rival actors or explanations** that the paragraph must eliminate (communist insurgency, radical Malay nationalism, citizenship-based mass mobilisation, etc.).
2. **For each rival, cite one source that documents its failure or danger** — alternate primary archive and secondary scholarship so the reader sees triangulation.
3. **Use the word *"only"* (or *"sole," "single viable"*)** in the verdict sentence; this word alone signals that the elimination chain has been completed.
4. **Qualify the surviving actor with an alignment-adjective** (*"British-friendly," "temperate," "pro-Western"*) so the reader sees *why* the dominant power found it acceptable.
5. **End on a gerund naming the status quo preserved** (*"preserving British rule," "containing Soviet influence," "maintaining parliamentary succession"*) — this gerund is what the whole argument was *for*.

The pattern is: **rival → rival → rival (each killed by a citation) → survivor with modifier → status quo preserved**. The footnote apparatus *is* the argument; the final sentence is only its name.
