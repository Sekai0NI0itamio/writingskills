## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern name**: Definition → Vector mapping → Ideal-case simplification → Real-case contrast with distributed-force definition → Named physical consequence → Additional-force introduction with definition.

**Replication steps for a new idea** (example: explaining Bernoulli's principle in fluid flow):

1. **State the principle and write its formula**: Name the governing principle in one clause and present its mathematical relation immediately after, e.g., "Bernoulli's principle, which states that an increase in fluid speed occurs simultaneously with a decrease in pressure, is summarized as P + ½ρv² + ρgh = constant."
2. **Define every symbol with a source**: Write a "Where…" clause assigning each variable a one-line definition and cite the reference, e.g., "Where P is the static pressure, ρ is the fluid density, v is the flow speed, and h is the height (Smith)."
3. **Map the vectors/quantities onto the physical system and simplify the ideal case**: State where each quantity acts in the setup, then introduce the ideal simplification (e.g., "The ideal streamline assumes no viscosity, so the total pressure is constant along the flow, thus no energy-loss terms appear in the diagram").
4. **Contrast the ideal case with the real case and introduce the additional quantity**: Use a contrast word to pivot, e.g., "While the real fluid experiences viscous dissipation, which is shown by the pressure drop denoted by ΔP," then define the new quantity as a sum or function of prior quantities.
5. **State the physical consequence and name the phenomenon**: Link the new quantity to an observable effect and give it a technical name with a second citation, e.g., "ΔP is the pressure loss that produces the curved streamlines known as turbulent wake (Jones)."
6. **Introduce any remaining forces or factors with a definition**: State the next factor in sequence (e.g., "The second effect acting on the fluid is the compressibility force") and define it by its mechanism in one clause, e.g., "Compressibility is the density-change response caused by pressure fluctuations above Mach 0.3."
