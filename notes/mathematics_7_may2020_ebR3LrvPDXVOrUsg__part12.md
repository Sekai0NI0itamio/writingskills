## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern name:** *Derivation → Attribution → Application → Achievement Enumeration → Forward Reference*

This pattern takes a derived result, validates it through naming and citation, connects it back to the original problems, lists its advantages over prior methods, and opens a new observation for the next section.

**Step-by-step instructions to explain a NEW idea with the same pattern:**

1. **Reference your prior work that justifies the current step.** State the proof, derivation, or method you already completed that makes the current result valid, using a brief noun phrase with a relative clause (e.g., "The differential equation, as solved in Section 3").

2. **Present the key result or formula.** State what you found, using a transition like "Therefore I found that:" or "This yields:" followed by the expression. If a second result follows algebraically from the first, link it with "and it follows that:"

3. **Name and attribute the result.** Identify the established concept or formula your result corresponds to, credit its original discoverer and date, and cite the shared method or principle that connects your approach to the original derivation.

4. **Connect the result back to the original problems.** State explicitly that the formula or method resolves the questions or challenges introduced earlier in the coursework, using a phrase like "Using this [equation/model], the [problems] have been solved."

5. **Enumerate the advantages in order.** List each gain your result achieves (e.g., broader applicability, faster computation, simpler procedure), specifying the scope of each advantage and contrasting it explicitly with the limitation of the previous method (e.g., operation count, number of steps, resource requirement).

6. **Synthesize the gains into a summary claim.** Combine the listed advantages into a single noun phrase statement (e.g., "A [advantage 1] and [advantage 2] have been reached") to close the achievement section cleanly.

7. **Point forward to the next observation.** State what new pattern, ratio, or question becomes visible through your result and indicate it will be explored in the following section, using "Finally, [observation] becomes apparent as will be seen in the next section."
