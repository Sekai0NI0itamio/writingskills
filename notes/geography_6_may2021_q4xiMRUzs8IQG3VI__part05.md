## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Timing first** — fix the dates and justify them by the variable being controlled. Sets up a clean temporal frame so every later measurement is anchored.
2. **Catalogue of instruments second** — list every tool, pair it with its site and the hypothesis it tests. This lets the reader see that each hypothesis has multiple converging methods.
3. **Transect note inline with the catalogue** — a clarifying footnote on the starred items, so spatial logic is visible without a separate paragraph.
4. **Procedure third** — name the single sampling approach used across all methods, justify it, instantiate it once concretely, then concede where it broke, and end on the resulting disadvantage.
5. **Order rationale:** *when → what → how-with-limits.* Putting timing before tools prevents the reader wondering why the dates matter; putting the procedure last means the catalogue raises the "how" question the procedure then answers. The closing concession earns credibility for the results that follow.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**Skeleton A — Timing justification (single-sentence paragraph)**
```
SKELETON: "[Specific dates/period] (both on the [day type]) were chosen so [dependent variable] didn't differ significantly by [varying factor]."
```
1. **Slots:** date phrase in past tense (noun phrase); bracketed day-type clarification (prepositional phrase); the measured outcome (noun phrase); the noisy factor being controlled (noun phrase). One sentence, cause-consequence shape ("were chosen so …").
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** pick a two-day window from your own study; name the day-type that controls your noise (weekday/weekend, season, tide); insert the dependent variable you are measuring; insert the external factor (time of day, weather, day) that you feared would skew it.
3. **Original:** *"21st and 22nd August (both on the weekend) were chosen so EI didn't differ significantly by day."*
4. **Demonstration fill (different idea):** *"2nd and 9th July (both on a Saturday) were chosen so litter mass didn't differ significantly by weekday commuting pattern."*

**Skeleton B — Parallel catalogue of methods**
```
SKELETON: "[Tool name] at [locations] (H#) … [Tool name]* (H# and H#)"
```
1. **Slots:** tool (noun); location cluster (place-list prepositional phrase); hypothesis tag in parentheses; optional asterisk to flag a shared rule; repeated in parallel lines so each line reads with the same rhythm.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** decide 4–6 instruments; group those that share a spatial rule and mark them with `*`; append `(H1)` or `(H1 and H2)` to each so the reader sees which question each instrument serves.
3. **Original:** *"Urban Questionnaires at 4 popular places for JE (Westgate, JEM, JCube and Jurong Lake Gardens) … Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA)* (H1 and H2)."*
4. **Demonstration fill:** *"Soil Moisture Probes at 5 riparian points along the River Ver (H2) … Invertebrate Kick-sampling* (H1 and H3)."*

**Skeleton C — Procedure paragraph (claim → justification → application → concession → disadvantage)**
```
SKELETON: "[Sampling approach] is used for all methods. This is advantageous as it [practical benefit] and ensured [methodological benefit]. For the [first instrument], it meant [one concrete action]. [Ideal interval/range] was attempted to some extent but due to [cause], this did not happen entirely. [Domain knowledge] is needed to [make adjustments], a disadvantage of this sampling."
```
1. **Slots:** sentence 1 = the named technique + scope ("for all methods"); sentence 2 = two-clause "as … and ensured …" reason; sentence 3 = "For the X, it meant Y" instantiation with a single verb of action; sentence 4 = concession with "but due to"; sentence 5 = verdict noun phrase ("a disadvantage of this sampling").
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** name the sampling type you used; list two genuine benefits (one logistical, one bias-reducing); pick one instrument and state the literal action (e.g., "every fifth quadrat"); admit where the ideal spacing slipped and give the real-world reason; close with the knowledge the gap forces the investigator to bring.
4. **Demonstration fill (different idea):** *"Stratified random sampling is used for all methods. This is advantageous as it is quick to organise and ensured representation across slope classes. For the clinometer readings, it meant one reading every 20 m of elevation change. Strict 20 m spacing was attempted to some extent but due to dense bracken, this did not happen entirely. Local terrain familiarity is needed to pick safe transect legs, a disadvantage of this sampling."*

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / scope** — *"is used for all methods"* — declares the technique's reach before justifying it.
- **Cause / consequence** — *"were chosen so EI didn't differ significantly by day"* — the connective `so` ties a decision to its controlled outcome.
- **Cause / reason** — *"due to variation in place of data collection"* — `due to` introduces the real-world cause of a procedural gap.
- **Justification** — *"This is advantageous as it is easy to carry out and ensured reduced sampling bias"* — `as` clause chains practical + methodological reasons.
- **Specification / instantiation** — *"For the questionnaire, it meant approaching every third person"* — `For the X, it meant Y` turns an abstract rule into one observable action.
- **Concession / partial failure** — *"to some extent but … this did not happen entirely"* — `but` paired with `to some extent` softens the gap from a total failure into an honest limit.
- **Verdict / cost** — *"a disadvantage of this sampling"* — closes the paragraph by naming the trade-off the method forces.
- **Spatial specification** — *"to be taken across transects in OR"* — `across transects in` locates where the starred instruments operate.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses the **"method choice → justification → concrete instance → concession → cost"** pattern. Use it whenever you must explain *why* you investigated something *the way* you did.

1. **Name the single technique** that governed your whole procedure (e.g., "Systematic sampling is used for all methods").
2. **Justify it with two reasons** joined by `as … and ensured …` — one practical (easy, quick, cheap) and one methodological (reduced bias, fairer coverage).
3. **Instantiate it once** with `For the [instrument], it meant [one observable action]` so the reader can picture the rule in the field.
4. **Concede the gap** with `to some extent but due to [real-world cause], this did not happen entirely` — admit where the rule slipped without abandoning it.
5. **Close on the cost** — end the sentence with the noun phrase `a disadvantage of this [sampling/approach]`, naming the extra knowledge or labour the gap forced on you.

Following steps 1→5 in that order gives the reader: *what you chose, why it was defensible, what it looked like, where it broke, and what the break cost you* — the same arc this section walks through.
