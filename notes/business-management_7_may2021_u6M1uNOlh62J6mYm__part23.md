## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This section functions as a **secondary-data-evidence block** for an IA that already established methodology elsewhere. The ordered content moves are:

1. **Mode-of-recording note** — flags that this interview was written rather than audio-recorded (sets up the reader's expectations about transcript fidelity).
2. **Language-translation note** — flags that the dialogue has been rendered into English (sets up why the transcript below is in English).
3. **Verbatim Q&A transcript** — student-question / employee-answer pairs alternate, each pair functioning as one unit of evidence.
4. **Embedded stage direction in parentheses** — locates non-textual artefacts (e.g. scanned catalogue) within the appendix.
5. **Topic progression inside the transcript** — moves from *physical catalogue evidence*, to *establishment timeline*, to *operational scope* (internal vs. external), to *philosophical mission*, to *concrete activities delivered*.

The reason for **this exact order**: each probe opens a new **category of business information**, moving from tangible artefact → historical fact → operational boundary → values → realised practice. The reader is moved from concrete and verifiable evidence (a catalogue, a founding date) toward more interpretive material (mission statement, activity history), which is exactly where the analysis that follows in the next section will pick up. The rhythm of probe-then-answer-then-(sometimes)-embedded-direction lets every question both **set up** and **justify** the answer that immediately follows.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**Skeleton A — Methodological caveats block (two-sentence setup before data)**

```
SKELETON: "However, [a deviation from the standard recording method] [was/were] [done instead] in [an alternative mode]. Moreover, the [data] for the purpose of this IA were [transformed in a stated way]."
```

1. *What each slot holds*:
   - Slot 1 (`However, …`): a **contrasting qualifier clause** in past tense, naming what did NOT happen (e.g. recording).
   - Slot 2 (`Moreover, …`): an **additive clarifier clause** that explains an applied transformation to the data (e.g. translation, anonymisation, transliteration).
2. *How to fill with a different idea*: Slot 1 — identify the one methodological compromise in your data-collection and frame it as the exception ("not audio, but written"; "not transcribed, but summarised"). Slot 2 — name the practical transformation you applied to the raw artefact so it could appear in the IA (translation, redaction, simplification). Both must be grammatically parallel — one concession, one clarification.
3. *Original filled version*: "However, the most crucial questions and answers were written instead in real time. Moreover, the questions and answers for the purpose of this IA were translated to English."
4. *Demonstration fill with a different idea*: "However, the participant declined to be audio recorded and was instead interviewed via written message exchange. Moreover, all responses for the purpose of this IA were anonymised to protect the participant's privacy."

**Skeleton B — Embedded parenthetical stage direction**

```
SKELETON: "(The [artefact] was [verb-ed] and [verb-ed] [locator phrase].)"
```

1. *What each slot holds*: a **parenthetical, passive-voice, past-participle clause** that tells the reader where to find a non-textual piece of evidence. Grammatically it is always one bracketed sentence, no capital, ending with a directional phrase ("into the pages below", "in Appendix B").
2. *How to fill with a different idea*: Decide on the single supplementary artefact (a scanned form, a photograph, a map) that supports the preceding answer. Express it as one passive past-participle instruction with a location phrase pointing the reader to where it is stored in the appendix.
3. *Original filled version*: "(The catalogue was scanned and attached into the pages below)"
4. *Demonstration fill with a different idea*: "(The signed consent form was photographed and inserted into Appendix C)"

**Skeleton C — Probe / Affirmation / Concessive-clarification beat (three turns)**

```
SKELETON: "Student: [a scoping probe about X]? Employee: [the core answer], however, [a second, partial case that complicates the answer]."
```

1. *What each slot holds*: a **single-line student question** (interrogative, present-tense framing of an inquiry about scope or expansion), followed by an **employee reply** whose second clause contains a concessive connector ("however", "although") introducing a secondary, narrower instance.
2. *How to fill with a different idea*: Slot 1 — phrase the student's question to ask about *where else* a practice occurs or *whether scope has extended*. Slot 2 — answer with a clear default ("No/None other than X") and then use a concessive clause to introduce a smaller, secondary instance that complicates the clean default. The rhetorical job is to show that the main case is exclusive, while still signalling a partial exception.
3. *Original filled version*: "Have you attempted to offer lessons in other establishments other than BISI? Employee: Non other than the school in Istanbul, however, in Izmir there is currently one concession happening with another business."
4. *Demonstration fill with a different idea*: "Student: Has the clinic offered its screening programme outside the main hospital? Employee: Not beyond the satellite clinic in Ankara, however, a single one-off pilot was run last summer at a local school."

**Skeleton D — Probe / Mission-statement beat**

```
SKELETON: "Student: What is the [organisation]'s mission and vision statement? Employee: To [verb1] and [verb2] to the [beneficiaries], whilst [verb3-ing] a [abstract noun]."
```

1. *What each slot holds*: a **two-clause employee reply** that defines the organisation's purpose through parallel infinitives ("to X and Y … whilst Z-ing"). Grammatically a purpose clause, three coordinated actions, with the third action subordinate via "whilst" or "while".
2. *How to fill with a different idea*: Pick three activities the organisation performs, two directly serving customers and one abstract/auxiliary. Keep the structure "To A and B … whilst C-ing" so the sentence reads as a values statement, not as a list.
3. *Original filled version*: "To offer cultural background and sports discipline to the customers, whilst promoting a healthy lifestyle."
4. *Demonstration fill with a different idea*: "To deliver early-language exposure and intercultural competence to the children, whilst nurturing long-term curiosity in the community."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Contrast/concession connectors**: "However, the most crucial questions and answers were written instead in real time."
- **Additive/clarification connectors**: "Moreover, the questions and answers for the purpose of this IA were translated to English."
- **Purpose/justification phrases**: "for the purpose of this IA" — used to license methodological transformations applied to the transcript.
- **Embedded stage-direction verbs (passive past participle)**: "was scanned and attached" — used to handle non-textual evidence inside the transcript.
- **Interrogative openings for probes**: "Do you have a catalogue I could see?", "How long is the business open for?", "Have you attempted to offer lessons in other establishments other than BISI?", "What is the business's mission and vision statement?", "What kind of physical activities did you offer in BISI before?"
- **Affirmative short-form replies**: "Yes, here it is." — minimal-confirmation register that hands off to embedded material.
- **Temporal benchmarking phrases**: "8 years ago" — establishes institutional age with a single dated anchor.
- **Exclusion + concession pairing**: "Non other than the school in Istanbul, however, in Izmir there is currently one concession happening" — a negation plus a contrasting partial instance used to map operational reach.
- **Values-statement infinitives**: "To offer cultural background and sports discipline … whilst promoting a healthy lifestyle" — "To … whilst …-ing" as a formula for stating mission.
- **Embedded quotation framing**: "Student:" / "Employee:" — turn-taking labels that organise dialogue as alternating evidence units.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section relies on a **methodological-caveat → primary-evidence-transcript** explanation pattern. To reproduce it for a new idea:

1. **Open with a methodological caveat that names the exception.** State in one sentence what did NOT happen in the standard way (no audio recording, no live observation), then specify what WAS done instead ("written in real time"). The job of this sentence is to set the reader's expectation about the form of evidence that follows.
2. **Add an additive clarifier that licenses the form of the evidence.** Use "Moreover," and name the applied transformation (translation, anonymisation, redaction) that allows the raw data to appear in the IA. The job is to explain any mismatch between original data and presented data.
3. **Switch into the transcript proper using turn-taking labels.** Open with a "Student:" / "Researcher:" question, followed by the participant's reply. The reply should be the minimum information needed to answer the question, no more.
4. **For non-textual evidence, embed a parenthetical stage direction.** When an answer refers to an artefact that cannot be reproduced as text (catalogue, photo, map, form), use a single bracketed past-participle clause pointing the reader to where it is stored in the appendix. This keeps the transcript readable while still surfacing the evidence.
5. **Sequence the probes from concrete to abstract.** Move questions from tangible artefacts (catalogue, founding date) → operational scope (where else does it happen?) → values (mission/vision) → realised practice (what activities were actually run?). Each probe should be answerable in one short reply; let the answers carry the analytical weight, not the questions.
6. **Use a concessive secondary clause inside scope answers.** When the participant's reply would otherwise be too clean, add "however," plus a smaller partial instance. This signals analytical nuance without breaking the brevity of the reply.
7. **Close the section on a concrete-list answer.** The final probe should ask for the realised activities/products, and the reply should list them plainly, handing the reader off to whatever interpretive section follows.
