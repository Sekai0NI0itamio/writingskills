## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1** (manufacturer's specs)
- S1 — **rationale/transition**: "To discover the capabilities of the machine for household use, the manufacturer's instructions should be followed." Hands reader onward by *justifying* the source of the next block (the bulleted specs only matter once we know we are deferring to the manufacturer).
- S2 — **list-introducer**: "On the website, it recommends:" Hands reader onward by *announcing* a structured set of parameters (the colon and list directly continue).
- Bulleted block — **evidence/specifications** (2g salt, water to Max line, 10 min, 12V/26W). Hands reader onward by *completing the authoritative spec* so the next paragraph can name what those specs *produce*.

**Paragraph 2** (manufacturer's outcome claim)
- S1 — **authority claim with citation**: "The manufacturer claims that 10 minutes of electrolysis can produce disinfectant suitable for a 'deep clean'..." Hands reader onward by *stating the expected outcome*, which creates the standard against which the next paragraph's own measurement is compared.

**Paragraph 3** (own measurement → standardisation)
- S1 — **own-measurement evidence**: "By measurement, the capacity of the machine was found to be 330mL." Hands reader onward by *confirming a single controlled variable* the writer has fixed.
- S2 — **implication/application of that measurement**: "This volume of water will be used for every trial." Hands reader onward by *locking in* the standardised volume, so the next sentence can calculate the maximum yield from exactly that 330 mL.

**Paragraph 4** (theoretical maximum)
- S1 — **conditional consequence / boundary calculation**: "If all chloride is converted to hypochlorite, the resulting NaClO concentration will be 0.772%." Hands reader onward by *setting the theoretical ceiling* the experimental trials will later be judged against.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **parameters-of-the-investigation** section. The order is:

1. **Why a published source is being trusted** (rationale for deferring to manufacturer).
2. **The authoritative parameters as a list** (mass, volume marker, time, voltage, power).
3. **What the authority promises those parameters will deliver** (manufacturer's claim, cited).
4. **Own check of one physical parameter** (measured capacity).
5. **How that checked parameter will be used in the trials** (standardisation statement).
6. **Theoretical maximum yield from those parameters** (calculation under an ideal assumption).

Why this order: rationale *justifies* the source → source *gives* the operating envelope → claim *defines* the expected outcome → own measurement *confirms* one controllable variable → standardisation *locks* that variable → calculation *sets the upper bound* the trials will be compared to. A student replicating this for a different investigation (e.g. parameters of a fermentation, a titration, a solar still) should keep this rhythm: source authority → source parameters → source outcome claim → own measured parameter → standardisation → theoretical yield.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A (rationale + authoritative list):**
"[To establish X for Y], [the source]'s [document type] should be followed. On the [source], it recommends: [parameter 1]; [parameter 2]; [parameter 3]; [parameter 4]."

- **Slot 1** (verb-led infinitive clause + noun phrase): the *purpose* of the investigation framed as "To [verb] the [noun] of [subject] for [context]". Present tense; one clause.
- **Slot 2** (short declarative with colon): a one-sentence announcement introducing a bulleted list.
- **Slot 3** (bulleted items, each a noun phrase): 3–5 short specifications in the same grammatical shape ("[quantity] of [substance]" / "[action] for [time]").
- **How to fill with a different idea**: pick any lab device or published protocol whose parameters you will defer to; write slot 1 using "To determine/establish/discover the capability/limitation/performance of [device] for [application]"; slot 2 names where the protocol lives ("In the lab manual, it specifies:"); slots 3 list 3–5 concrete operating parameters.
- **Original filled version**: "To discover the capabilities of the machine for household use, the manufacturer's instructions should be followed. On the website, it recommends: 2g of salt; Water filled up to the 'Max' line; Salt-water electrolysed for 10 minutes; 12V, 26W."
- **Demonstration fill (different idea)**: "To establish the yield of biodiesel from waste cooking oil for a school-scale investigation, the university protocol should be followed. In the lab manual, it specifies: 100 mL of oil; 20 mL of methanol with 1 g NaOH catalyst; reaction at 60 °C for 90 minutes; stirring at 400 rpm."

**SKELETON B (authority claim + citation):**
"The [authority] claims that [operating condition] can produce [output] suitable for [application]. ([Authority, year])"

- **Slot 1** (noun phrase + "claims that" + noun clause): names who is making the promise, what the input is, what the output is, and where it is meant to be used.
- **Slot 2** (parenthetical reference at end of sentence): the citation, in author/year form.
- **How to fill with a different idea**: pick any published performance claim from a manufacturer, paper, or standard; state the input the source specifies, the product/output it says results, and the real-world use it is marketed for.
- **Original filled version**: "The manufacturer claims that 10 minutes of electrolysis can produce disinfectant suitable for a 'deep clean' of bathroom surfaces and clothes."
- **Demonstration fill (different idea)**: "The supplier claims that 50 g of the catalyst pellets can produce enough hydrogen suitable for fuelling a 30-minute demonstration flight of the model aircraft."

**SKELETON C (own measurement + standardisation):**
"By [method], the [parameter] of the [apparatus] was found to be [value]. This [parameter] will be used for every trial."

- **Slot 1** (preposition-led adverbial + passive reporting clause): names the method used to verify a single parameter.
- **Slot 2** (deictic "This" + restatement of the value + future-tense commitment): locks the verified value in as the constant.
- **How to fill with a different idea**: choose the one physical parameter of your setup that *cannot* be read off the source and must be measured (volume, length, mass, area); report it in a passive past-tense clause, then re-state it as the controlled variable.
- **Original filled version**: "By measurement, the capacity of the machine was found to be 330mL. This volume of water will be used for every trial."
- **Demonstration fill (different idea)**: "By measurement, the internal diameter of the pipe was found to be 1.4 cm. This cross-sectional area will be used for every flow-rate calculation."

**SKELETON D (theoretical yield / boundary calculation):**
"If all [reactant] is converted to [product], the resulting [product] [quantity] will be [value]."

- **Slot 1** (conditional "if" clause naming the ideal assumption): the limiting case.
- **Slot 2** (main clause giving the calculated upper limit): the theoretical maximum with units.
- **How to fill with a different idea**: pick the limiting reagent in your reaction; state the 100 %-conversion assumption, then quote the calculated maximum yield in % or mol/L.
- **Original filled version**: "If all chloride is converted to hypochlorite, the resulting NaClO concentration will be 0.772%."
- **Demonstration fill (different idea)**: "If all the glucose is converted to ethanol, the resulting ethanol concentration will be 8.3% v/v."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / framing a source**: *"To discover the capabilities of the machine for household use, the manufacturer's instructions should be followed."*
- **List introduction**: *"On the website, it recommends:"*
- **Authority + citation handling**: *"The manufacturer claims that 10 minutes of electrolysis can produce disinfectant suitable for a 'deep clean'..."*
- **Evidence / own measurement handling**: *"By measurement, the capacity of the machine was found to be 330mL."*
- **Specification / locking a variable**: *"This volume of water will be used for every trial."*
- **Conditional / ideal-case calculation**: *"If all chloride is converted to hypochlorite, the resulting NaClO concentration will be 0.772%."*
- **Explanation verbs in use**: *"should be followed"* (procedural verb), *"claims"* (epistemic verb), *"was found to be"* (reporting verb), *"will be used"* (commitment verb), *"will be"* (predictive verb for a derived quantity).

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern this section relies on is: **authority-specification → authority-claim → own-confirmation → controlled variable → theoretical ceiling**. Step-by-step to apply it to a new idea:

1. **Name the operating envelope you are borrowing.** Write one infinitive-led sentence explaining *why* a published source is being trusted (e.g. "To determine the output of the apparatus for [use], the manufacturer's instructions should be followed."). This *sets up* the bulleted list.
2. **List the borrowed parameters as a bulleted block.** Use 3–5 short noun phrases, each one a quantity + material + (optional) condition. This *sets up* the outcome claim that follows.
3. **State what the source says those parameters will achieve.** Use "claims that … suitable for …" and end with a parenthetical citation. This *sets up* the need to verify or contextualise it.
4. **Report one parameter you measured yourself.** Use "By [method], the [parameter] was found to be [value]." This *sets up* the standardisation sentence.
5. **Lock that measured value in.** Use "This [parameter] will be used for every trial." This *sets up* the theoretical calculation.
6. **Calculate the theoretical maximum under 100 %-conversion.** Use "If all [reactant] is converted to [product], the resulting [product] [quantity] will be [value]." This *sets up* the later comparison with actual experimental results.
