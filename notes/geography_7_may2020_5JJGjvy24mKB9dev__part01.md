## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This section type is a **Table of Contents (Appendix sub-block)**. The ordered moves are:

1. **Parent section banner** — names the top-level group (here, "Appendix"). Sets the reader's expectation that what follows is supplementary, not core argument.
2. **First-level item with topic name** — previews the kind of support inside (background vs. calculation vs. raw data). Sets up a contrast between descriptive and numerical support.
3. **Page leader and locator** — gives the page number. Sets up the reader's ability to jump without reading.
4. **Second-level item with topic shift** — moves from one kind of support to another (e.g., background → calculations). Sets up the need to show *how* numbers were produced.
5. **Third-level parallel items (triadic pattern)** — lists siblings under the second level (Hypothesis 1, 2, 3). Sets up parity between hypotheses; signals the work tested three claims separately.
6. **Page locators for each sibling** — distributed across the column. Sets up horizontal scanning rather than vertical reading.

**Why this order:** the reader moves from broadest container → specific topic → drill-down to parallel sub-items. This mirrors the document's actual argument structure so a reader using the TOC can navigate to evidence that supports a specific claim.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**Skeleton A — Container + Topic Preview**
```
SKELETON: "[Parent label]. [First-level item name]. . . . . . . [page number].
          [Second-level item name]. . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . [page number]."
```

1. **What each slot holds:**
   - Slot 1: single-line banner naming a back-matter group (letter + word).
   - Slot 2: numbered sub-item naming a support type (noun phrase).
   - Slot 3: integer page number, right-aligned via dotted leader.
   - Slot 4: numbered sub-item at a deeper level.
   - Slot 5: integer page number.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:**
   - Slot 1: choose the letter your appendix begins with (commonly "A").
   - Slot 2: name the *first kind* of supplementary evidence (background, raw data, equipment list).
   - Slot 3: insert the actual page where it appears.
   - Slot 4: name the *second kind* of supplementary evidence (calculations, code, survey instrument).
   - Slot 5: insert its page number.
3. **Original filled version:** *"A Appendix … A.1 Background Information … 27 … A.2 Calculations … 28"*
4. **Demonstration fill (different subject):** *"B Appendix … B.1 Raw Survey Responses … 31 … B.2 Coding Manual … 34"*

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

Because this fragment is navigational, not argumentative, the "connectives" are structural rather than logical:

- **Sequencing (numerical hierarchy):** *"A.2.1 Hypothesis 1 … A.2.2 Hypothesis 2 … A.2.3 Hypothesis 3"* — uses incremented numerals to enforce reading order.
- **Containment / scope marker:** *"A Appendix"* then *"A.1 Background Information"* — the letter opens scope; the number narrows it.
- **Contrast by topic shift:** *"A.1 Background Information . . . A.2 Calculations"* — juxtaposition of "Background" vs. "Calculations" signals descriptive vs. numerical support.
- **Parity by repetition:** *"Hypothesis 1 … Hypothesis 2 … Hypothesis 3"* — identical noun shape, different numeral, asserts equal status among the three.

(There are no causal, concessive, or evidential connectives because the text performs no argument; it indexes one.)

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section relies on the **hierarchical enumeration pattern** (container → topic → parallel siblings). It does not explain a concept; it *routes* a reader to explanations. To replicate:

1. **Name the outermost container.** Pick a single capital letter and a short noun (e.g., "A Appendix"). This signals back-matter, not core body.
2. **List the first support category.** Give it a decimal number (A.1) and a descriptive noun phrase ("Background Information"). This tells them *what kind* of support is coming, not the content.
3. **Attach a locator.** Write the page number after a dotted leader so the reader can jump.
4. **Introduce the second support category.** Use the next decimal (A.2) and a noun phrase of a *different* support type ("Calculations"). The shift in noun type is the point: the reader now expects numerical/worked evidence.
5. **Drill into parallel siblings.** For each parallel unit of work, increment the final digit (A.2.1, A.2.2, A.2.3). Keep the noun *identical* across siblings so parallelism is visible.
6. **Attach locators in the same column.** Place page numbers vertically aligned so the reader can scan rows.
7. **Close the block.** End the listing cleanly with the last sibling and its page; do not narrate the contents — that is the job of the pages the TOC points to.
