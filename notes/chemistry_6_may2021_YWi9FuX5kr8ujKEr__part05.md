## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **method continuation** section — the tail end of a numbered step sequence. The logical order is:

1. **Resume a cut-off sentence** (the swirl-technique action that was mid-flow at the page break).
2. **State the colour-change endpoint** of the in-progress titration.
3. **Insert figure captions** as documentary anchors.
4. **Add the late-stage reagent** (starch indicator) with its colour cue and its stop-criterion.
5. **Record the measured volume** and justify replication for an average.
6. **Compress and replicate** the entire step block across remaining samples (parallelism).
7. **Insert a controlled waiting interval** (incubation conditions: dark, fixed time, fixed temperature).
8. **Reactivate the procedure** at the timed endpoint using a named method (Winkler) to measure the new variable (dissolved oxygen).
9. **Close with disposal/cleanup** justified on environmental/safety grounds.

**Why this order:**
- (1→2) The action must resolve into its observable endpoint before the reader can register completion.
- (2→3) Visual evidence is documented before the technique is altered.
- (3→4) A new reagent is only added near the endpoint, so it must come after the apparatus/setup is recorded.
- (4→5) Recording and replicating volumes only make sense once the titration is stopped at a known colour change.
- (5→6) Once A is averaged, the same block applies to B/C by reference — economy of words.
- (6→7) A new experimental phase (incubation) cannot begin until the titration phase is fully concluded.
- (7→8) The named method + new variable only appear at the timed endpoint, because waiting conditions only matter in relation to their measurement.
- (8→9) Disposal closes the procedure once data collection is done.

A student replicating this for a different topic should follow: **resume → endpoint cue → figures → late reagent → data record + replicate → parallel replication → controlled interval → second-cycle named method → cleanup + justification.**

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The dominant explanation pattern in this section is **procedure-with-inlined-justification**, executed as **sequential action → observable endpoint → repeat/parallel → controlled pause → closure with ethical reason**.

**Step-by-step replication for a NEW idea:**

1. **Resume a hanging action.** Pick up a sentence that is in mid-action (e.g., "swirling the conical flask…"). Let it finish naturally into its observable outcome.
2. **State the visual endpoint of that action** in one short sentence ("yellow colour was noticed"). This grounds the procedure in observation, not theory.
3. **Anchor the procedure visually** with figure captions (optional but typical at IB level). Place them between sub-steps.
4. **Introduce the late-stage reagent with three elements in one sentence:** (a) volume + drops, (b) colour it imparts, (c) the endpoint criterion it allows you to detect.
5. **Record the dependent variable** ("the volume of Na2S2O3 used… was noted") and immediately justify repetition ("repeated four more times… to get an average"). State both actions in one sentence.
6. **Compress the whole step block by reference** ("Steps i-v were repeated for…") — do NOT rewrite the steps; trust the reader.
7. **Insert a controlled waiting interval as a single declarative sentence** with five slots: sample set, passive verb, enclosed location, time period, held-constant parameter.
8. **Reactivate the procedure at the timed endpoint** by naming the standardised method + the new dependent variable + reactivating the step block by reference.
9. **Close with disposal/cleanup** in one sentence that pairs the action with an explicit reason ("hence abiding by environmental considerations of disposal").

The rhythm is: action → endpoint → reagent → record+replicate → compress → wait → reactivate → cleanup+justify. Repeating this rhythm for any wet-lab or repeated-measurement procedure will produce a method section with the same logical density as the original.
