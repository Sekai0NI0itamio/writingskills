## What This Section Does (content sequence)

The section is a **mini investigation report**, ordered as:
1. **Comparative data** (which shape won?) — *because* without a winner there is nothing to generalise.
2. **Generalisation/hypothesis** — *because* a winner invites a pattern claim.
3. **Test setup (geometric model + parameter)** — *because* the hypothesis needs a controlled object.
4. **Optimisation procedure (first derivative = 0)** — *because* the claim concerns a *maximum*, and calculus gives that.
5. **Solution reached but ambiguous** — *because* honesty about method limits is required at IB level.
6. **Alternative method chosen with reason** — *because* the flaw in step 5 demands repair; the reason ("unefficient") justifies the new tool.
7. **Final verdict (function diverges)** — *because* the new method closes the loop opened in step 2.

The WHY of this order: each move is *triggered by a gap* in the prior move — first a comparison needs a verdict, the verdict needs a generalisation, the generalisation needs a test, the test needs a flaw to reveal, and the flaw needs a fix.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / process narration**: "Through a complex series of algebraic manipulations" — compresses intermediate algebra into one step-marker.
- **Cause/consequence**: "Thus, I hypothesized that…" — converts a verdict into a claim; "thus as x tends to infinity" — converts a graph reading into an asymptotic statement.
- **Contrast / concession**: "However …doesn't indicate the nature of the function" — pivots from success to limitation.
- **Specification / clarification**: "this could be a maximum, minimum or point of inflexion" — enumerates the ambiguity introduced by the previous sentence.
- **Reason markers**: "As calculating the 2nd derivative would be unefficient" — supplies the *why* behind the methodological swap.
- **Explanation / definition verbs**: "is defined as" not used here, but "is maximised" (verb of optimisation) and "examined by" (verb of method) carry definition-of-strategy work. "Considered a top with sides" — verb of setup. "Reveals that" — verb of evidence-handling.
- **Evidence handling**: "From my calculations, I have realised that" — frames evidence as prior work; "My graph of I(x) reveals that" — frames visual evidence as authoritative.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The section uses a **"hypothesis-test loop with method repair"** pattern. To replicate:

1. **Open with comparative evidence** — name two instances that differ in one variable; declare which one won on the measured quantity. (Sets up the question.)
2. **Generalise into a hypothesis** — use "Thus, I hypothesized that the X-er the [feature], the Y-er the [quantity]." (Turns data into a claim.)
3. **State the test setup** — "To test my hypothesis, I considered [object] with [parameter relation] and [constant]." (Anchors the claim in a controllable model.)
4. **Declare the mathematical tactic** — name what condition you are solving (e.g. derivative = 0) and why it targets the extremum.
5. **Narrate the work briefly** — one sentence ("Through a series of …, I arrived at a solution") signals labour without displaying it.
6. **Surface the method's limitation** — "However [condition] doesn't indicate the nature of the function." (IB marker of critical thinking.)
7. **Swap method with reason** — "As [second-derivative / algebraic test] would be unefficient, I [plotted / graphed / numerically evaluated] …" (The "As" clause justifies the pivot.)
9. **Deliver final verdict through the new method** — "My graph …reveals that there is no maximum… thus as x tends to infinity, [quantity] tends to infinity." (Closes the hypothesis loop.)
