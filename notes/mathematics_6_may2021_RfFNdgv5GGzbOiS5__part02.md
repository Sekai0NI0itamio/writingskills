## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1 — Frame and authority**

- **S1** — Move: *Frame/announcement.* Quote: "**Below are the definitions for key terms**". The reader is told a definitional list is about to be supplied, scoping it as a glossary rather than argumentation.
- Hand-off: because the list was just *promised*, the next sentence must explain *why these particular definitions are valid* (the implicit "from where?").
- **S2** — Move: *Authority/credibility claim.* Quote: "**taken from reliable sources such as**". Establishes that what follows is borrowed rather than invented, so the reader should accept the upcoming wording as canonical.
- Hand-off: with the sourcepool (WHO, OECD, World Bank) already named, the actual definitions can now appear with their matching parenthetical citations — the source name in S2 pre-mirrors the tag at the end of each bullet.

**Paragraph 2 — The definition list (treated as one paragraph because every bullet is a parallel move)**

- **B1** — Move: *Definition of the outcome (dependent) variable.* Quote: "**the average number of years a newborn can expect to live**". Puts the investigation's measured outcome into operational form before the variable that may explain it.
- Hand-off: outcome just defined → next bullet must be the *input* that the outcome will be tested against (logical pairing of DV with IV).
- **B2** — Move: *Definition of the independent variable.* Quote: "**the total amount of money each country spends on healthcare**". Specifies the predictor in the same "term — operational phrase" shape, so the two core variables now sit in parallel.
- Hand-off: both variables are now named, but the investigation *groups* countries — the reader needs to know the grouping rules.
- **B3** — Move: *Definition of the first grouping category.* Quote: "**referred to low and middle-income countries, whose GNI per capita is**". Adds a *threshold* mechanism (a numeric cutoff) so the category is operational, not vague.
- Hand-off: the cutoff has just been set on one side of a boundary, so the next bullet must state the *other* side of the same boundary — contrast pair.
- **B4** — Move: *Definition of the second grouping category (contrast pair).* Quote: "**referred to high-income countries only, whose GNI per capita is more**". Closes the dichotomy by mirroring B3's structure and flipping the inequality, leaving the reader with a complete partition.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

A "key term definitions" section in IB coursework performs this ordered sequence:

1. **Announce the glossary** — signal that definitions are coming so the reader doesn't read them as evidence.
2. **Pre-establish source authority** — name the institutions whose wording will be borrowed, *before* any individual citation appears.
3. **Define the outcome (dependent) variable first** — so every later claim about "the rate" or "the score" has a fixed referent.
4. **Define the independent/explanatory variable second** — sits beside the DV in the reader's mind as the cause candidate.
5. **Define the grouping/classifying terms last, as a contrast pair** — only after the variables exist does the reader need to know how the sample will be split.

*Why this order:* the dependent variable is the *thing being explained*, so it must exist in the reader's head before the explanation can be parsed; the independent variable is the explanation *candidate* and comes next; grouping categories are only meaningful *across* the variables, so they are scaffolded last. The contrast-pair structure at the end means a student reading the section can immediately see the binary the analysis will compare.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

### Skeleton A — The frame/authority paragraph

`"Below are the definitions for [X] used throughout the whole [Y]. Note that all these definitions were taken from reliable sources such as [A], [B], and [C]:"`

1. **Slot 1 ("Below are the definitions for…")** — present-tense framing verb + scope noun phrase. *Fill instruction:* pick the noun that names the unit (e.g. "key terms", "core variables", "operational definitions") and the larger artefact they belong to (the essay, the report, the investigation).
2. **Slot 2 ("throughout the whole [Y]")** — singular noun naming the document. *Fill instruction:* state the artefact once; the word "whole" is reused unchanged.
3. **Slot 3 ("taken from reliable sources such as…")** — list 2–3 institutional names. *Fill instruction:* choose institutions that match the domain (WHO/OECD for health, UNESCO/World Bank for education, IMF/OECD for economics).
4. **Original filled version:** "**Below are the definitions for key terms used throughout the whole investigation. Note that all these definitions were taken from reliable sources such as WHO, OECD Data, and World Bank:**"
5. **Demonstration fill (different idea — economic topic):** "Below are the definitions for key terms used throughout the whole commentary. Note that all these definitions were taken from reliable sources such as the IMF, OECD Data, and World Bank:"

### Skeleton B — The single-variable definition bullet

`"[Term] — the [function/structure phrase that operationalises the term]. ([Source])"`

1. **Slot 1 (term)** — 1–4 word technical label, capitalised. *Fill instruction:* choose the variable's official name as it would appear on a chart axis.
2. **Slot 2 (the…)** — noun phrase that states *what is being measured* in plain words. *Fill instruction:* start with "the" and write one full phrase; avoid formula symbols here, write the meaning.
3. **Slot 3 (parenthetical source)** — short institutional tag. *Fill instruction:* use the same institution you named in Skeleton A so the tag is recognisable.
4. **Original filled version:** "**Current health expenditure per capita — the total amount of money each country spends on healthcare divided by the population number, in current US$. (OECD Data)**"
5. **Demonstration fill (different idea — education topic):** "Public expenditure per secondary student — the total amount of government funding allocated to each enrolled secondary-school pupil, expressed in current US$. (UNESCO)"

### Skeleton C — The classification-term definition bullet (with threshold)

`"[Term] — a [category-word] terminology referred to [group-A] / [group-B], whose [metric] is [inequality] [threshold] as of [date]. ([Source])"`

1. **Slot 1 (term)** — two-word class label. *Fill instruction:* pick a noun like "country", "economy", "school" that the classification acts on.
2. **Slot 2 (a [category-word] terminology referred to…)** — generic noun ("classification", "categorisation") + the income/development bracket. *Fill instruction:* write the *low* bracket in the first bullet, the *high* bracket in the second; mirror the slot.
3. **Slot 3 (whose [metric] is…)** — name the quantitative criterion (GNI, GDP, HDI, test score). *Fill instruction:* the metric must be the same one used in both contrast bullets; only the inequality flips.
4. **Slot 4 (as of [date])** — temporal anchor. *Fill instruction:* pin the threshold to a fiscal or calendar year so the cutoff is auditable.
5. **Original filled version (B3):** "**Developing countries — a country-classification terminology referred to low and middle-income countries, whose GNI per capita is less than $12,535 as of the 2021 fiscal year. (World Bank)**"
6. **Demonstration fill (different idea — literacy topic):** "Low-literacy economies — an economy-classification terminology referred to low and lower-middle-income economies, whose adult literacy rate is below 75% as of the 2023 reporting year. (UNESCO)"

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / announcing:** "**Below are the definitions for**" — frames the list.
- **Attention-marking:** "**Note that**" — flags the credibility claim that follows.
- **Specification / exemplification:** "**such as**" — introduces the list of source institutions.
- **Definition verbs:** "**referred to**" — pins a term to a category; "**defined as**" is implied by the dash structure rather than written.
- **Qualification (relative clause):** "**whose**" — attaches the numeric threshold to the category noun.
- **Restriction:** "**only**" — narrows "high-income countries" to a single income tier.
- **Temporal anchoring:** "**as of the 2021 fiscal year**" — pins the threshold to a date.
- **Scope marker:** "**throughout the whole**" — binds the glossary to the whole document.
- **Authority-handling:** the parenthetical source tag "(WHO)", "(OECD Data)", "(World Bank)" — each definition ends with a borrowed-from tag.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses the pattern: **frame → authority → operationalise each term in canonical order → contrast-pair the groupings.** To reproduce it for a new topic:

1. **Open with a frame sentence** that announces "below are the definitions for [terms]" and binds them to the document ("used throughout the whole [essay/investigation]").
2. **Add a second sentence that names the source institutions** in advance, using "Note that… reliable sources such as [A], [B], and [C]:" — this pre-authorises every citation that follows.
3. **Define the outcome variable first**, in the form "Term — the [operational phrase]. (Source)"; the operational phrase must say *what is being measured*, not the symbol.
4. **Define the explanatory/independent variable second**, in the identical "Term — the [operational phrase]. (Source)" shape, so the two definitions read as a parallel pair.
5. **Define the grouping categories last, as a contrast pair**; each one uses the form "Term — a [category] terminology referred to [bracket], whose [metric] is [inequality] [threshold] as of [date]. (Source)". The two bullets must (a) name the *same* metric, (b) use *opposite* inequalities, and (c) cite the *same* source.
6. **Close the section after the contrast pair** — no summary sentence is needed because the binary has been made explicit.
