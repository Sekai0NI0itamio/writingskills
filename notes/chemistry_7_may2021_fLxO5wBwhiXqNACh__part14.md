## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Substance identity first** — The section opens by defining caffeine as both a commercial product and a biologically active compound. This establishes *what* is being studied before any method is introduced.
2. **Extraction methods next** — Once the substance is identified, the section provides the means to obtain it: general extraction, then aqueous-specific extraction. This order ensures the reader understands *how* to get the substance before learning *from what*.
3. **Tea matrix and pH mechanism after extraction** — The section then narrows from pure caffeine to the tea plant, introducing pH as the controlling variable. This sequence works because you need to know the extraction method before understanding what affects it.
4. **Safety documentation follows the method** — After establishing what chemicals are used, the section provides safety sheets for those specific solvents. This order is logical because you justify safety protocols only after naming the hazards.
5. **Applied and analytical closure last** — The section ends with caffeine's pharmaceutical application, a definitional reference for a co-extracted compound, and an analytical measurement technique. This closes the loop: you define the substance, extract it, control the variables, handle it safely, and then measure it accurately.

**Generalizable sequence for any IB methodology Works Cited:** Define the substance → establish the core method → introduce the material context and its key variable → document safety for the chemicals → close with application, related-compound definition, and analytical validation.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern used in this section:** Authority → Contribution → Variable → Application → Validation. Each source establishes *who* says it, *what* it provides, *under what condition*, *for what purpose*, and *how it is measured*.

**Steps to replicate with a new idea (e.g., "How does temperature affect the extraction of curcumin from turmeric?"):**

1. **Name the substance with a commercial or reference anchor** — Find a product-information sheet or reference entry for curcumin (e.g., a manufacturer's data sheet) to establish its chemical identity before any method is discussed.
2. **Cite a primary extraction method** — Locate a peer-reviewed study that describes how curcumin is extracted, providing the core laboratory procedure as the second move.
3. **Introduce the key variable with a second method source** — Find a study that modifies one variable (temperature, solvent, time) and measures its effect on extraction yield, specifying the causal mechanism.
4. **Add the plant-matrix context** — Include a source that describes the turmeric root's chemical composition, explaining what curcumin must be separated from.
5. **Document safety for the chemicals involved** — Add SDS or SOP entries for each solvent or acid used in the extraction, in the order they appear in the procedure.
6. **Close with an analytical measurement technique** — Find a study that names the quantification method (e.g., spectrophotometric determination of curcumin), providing the final validation step that measures how much was extracted.
