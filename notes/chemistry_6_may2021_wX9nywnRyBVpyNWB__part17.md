## What This Section Does (content sequence)

A discussion-of-results block on a temperature-dependent equilibrium does six jobs in this order:

1. **Reverse the prior direction by mechanism first.** Name what is happening physically/electronically in the new (endothermic) case. *Why first:* every later variable inherits its direction from this mechanism, so it must be stated before any trend is claimed.
2. **State the consequence for the primary measured variable (Ecell).** *Why second:* this is the variable the reader has already met; using it as the bridge makes the parallel application to the next variable natural.
3. **Apply the same causal direction to the secondary variable (Kc) using "since."** *Why third:* a parallel "For X, since …" gives the reader a one‑to‑one copy of the earlier chain and trains them to expect the same outcome.
4. **Unpack the abstract claim into concrete concentration changes.** *Why fourth:* "greater proportion" is vague; the reader needs "concentration of reactants becomes larger / products becomes smaller" before any formula can be used.
5. **Feed the concrete changes into the written equation.** *Why fifth:* the denominator/numerator story is the only place where the inequality actually becomes a mathematical statement rather than a verbal one.
6. **Deliver the verdict with "Therefore."** *Why sixth:* now the trend is sealed and the reader expects validity evidence — which is the job of the second paragraph.

The second paragraph then does four jobs:

7. **Pivot from trend to correlation** ("Seeing that … it is important to look at the extent"). *Why:* the verdict alone is not enough — the reader expects the trend to be checked against the other measured variable.
8. **Show the regression evidence** (graph, equation, type). *Why:* correlation strength must be quantified, not asserted.
9. **Quote R² and derive the implication** ("therefore both relationships … should be negative"). *Why:* a number without an interpretation is wasted; "therefore" closes the inferential gap.
10. **Justify the implication methodologically** ("Furthermore, Kc … is calculated with the input of Ecell"). *Why:* the section closes on a "this is expected, not coincidental" beat, which is what a 6/7 marker rewards for critical thinking.

A student replicating this with another topic (e.g. pressure vs yield, pH vs rate) keeps steps 1–6 verbatim in structure and swaps in steps 7–10 wherever two measured variables need to be cross-validated.

**SKELETON A — Causal chain from mechanism to formula verdict**

`SKELETON: "[Contrast word]. This opposes [process A], [sub-process B] cancel[ling] out, thereby [consequence for variable 1]. For [variable 2], since [cause], [effect]. In the sense that [concrete change 1] and [concrete change 2]. Thinking about this in the context of [equation with citation], [numerator-side change] and [denominator-side change]. Therefore, [final verdict about variable 2]."`

1. *What each slot holds / grammatical shape:*
   - Contrast word: single adverb ("instead", "Conversely").
   - Process A: noun phrase naming the directional flow being reversed ("the flow of electrons from anode to cathode").
   - Sub-process B: gerund phrase naming what cancels ("the voltage propagating in both directions").
   - Consequence: gerund phrase with measurable result ("decreasing the overall value of the Ecell").
   - Variable 2: capitalised symbol ("Kc", "pH", "α").
   - Cause: subordinate clause after "since" ("equilibrium shifts to the left").
   - Effect: comparative clause ("a greater proportion of reactants will be present to products").
   - Concrete changes 1 and 2: parallel "concentration of X will become larger / smaller" clauses.
   - Equation: displayed formula with author/website citation in parentheses.
   - Numerator/denominator change: two short clauses joined by "and".
   - Verdict: "Therefore, the value of [variable 2] [direction] with [independent variable]."

2. *How to fill with a different idea:* Pick an equilibrium-style system (solubility, ionisation, complex formation). Identify the two opposing processes that cancel — analogue of electron flow. Pick the quotient expression (Ksp, Ka, Kf) and decide which side gains species. Keep "since …" naming the direction of shift; keep "In the sense that" for the concrete concentration statement; keep the denominator/numerator language because it is the only way the formula yields the verdict.

## Express-idea vocabulary

**Sequencing / transition into a new clause**
- *instead.* — opens paragraph as a direct contrast to the prior exothermic paragraph.
- *Seeing that … it is important to look* — pivot from a trend to its correlation check.
- *Furthermore, … therefore* — second-stage justification within one sentence.

**Cause / consequence**
- *thereby decreasing the overall value* — consequence verb attached to the cancellation mechanism.
- *since equilibrium shifts to the left* — cause marker for the proportion claim.
- *Therefore, the value of 𝐾𝑐 decreases* — verdict via consequence.

**Specification / concrete unpacking**
- *In the sense that the concentration of reacts will become larger* — translates "greater proportion" into concentration language.
- *Thinking about this in the context of the equation* — escalation marker that converts a verbal claim into a mathematical one.
- *In the context of this investigation is calculated with the input of* — localises the abstract variable to the specific calculation method.

**Evidence handling**
- *(LibreTexts)* — citation attached inline to the displayed equation.
- *Graph 5 illustrates a linear and positive correlation* — evidence presentation verb.
- *The R² is 0.958, a very strong display of correlation* — quantitative evidence with explicit strength judgement.

**Mechanism / process verbs**
- *opposes the flow of electrons* — mechanism verb naming the reversal.
- *cancel out* — mechanism verb naming the subtraction of opposing effects.
- *decreasing the overall value of the Ecell* — consequence verb in gerund form.
- *calculated with the input of* — methodological verb explaining how variable 1 is derived.

**Implication verbs**
- *illustrates* (graph) — "Graph 5 illustrates a linear and positive correlation."
- *should be* — tentative verdict verb ("both relationships … should be negative").

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The section runs a **mechanism → parallel cause → unpack → equation application → verdict → correlation evidence → methodological justification** pattern. To explain a new idea (e.g. why solubility of a salt decreases with temperature) with the same pattern, follow these numbered actions:

1. **Name the mechanism in the new direction.** Write one sentence starting with a contrast word ("instead", "Conversely") and a verb such as "opposes", "reverses", or "counteracts" to describe the physical/electron/ion process that goes the opposite way to the case already discussed.
2. **Attach a "thereby" consequence to the primary measured variable.** End the same sentence with "thereby [gerund] the overall value of [variable]." This guarantees the reader sees a measurable outcome from the mechanism.
3. **Write a "For [variable 2], since …" sentence that applies the same directional logic.** Keep the structure parallel to sentence 2 so the reader recognises the pattern; use "since" to name the cause (e.g. equilibrium shifts, dissociation shifts).
4. **Add an "In the sense that … and …" unpack sentence.** Translate the comparative claim ("greater proportion") into two concrete clauses about concentrations of the two sides.
5. **Insert a "Thinking about this in the context of the equation, …" sentence.** Show the displayed formula with a citation, and state which side (numerator or denominator) gains or loses.
6. **Close the chain with a "Therefore, the value of [variable 2] [direction] with [independent variable]." verdict sentence.** No new information — only the sign conclusion forced by step 5.
7. **Pivot with "Seeing that [source A] and [source B] display similar trends with [independent variable] …".** This is the hand-off into the correlation paragraph.
8. **Present the regression evidence** in "[Source C] illustrates a [type] correlation between [var1] and [var2] in the equation y = mx + b" form.
9. **Quote R² and write the implication.** "The R² is [decimal], [strength phrase], therefore both relationships with [independent variable] should be [sign]."
10. **Justify methodologically.** "Furthermore, [var1] in the context of this investigation is calculated with the input of [var2] values, therefore the fact that the relationships are similar is expected." This closes the section by removing any residual sense of coincidence.
