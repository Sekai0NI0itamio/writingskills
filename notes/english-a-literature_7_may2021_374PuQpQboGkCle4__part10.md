## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1**
1. **Evidence (quote + context):** "criticism. She describes how the poet, 'once sulked for a day and a night'41, because of a remark she made concerning his use of too many abstract nouns." — Hands the reader to sentence 2 by presenting a concrete textual moment that demands interpretation.
2. **Unpack (interpret the quoted word):** "The word 'sulked' reveals Eurydice's criticism concerning Orpheus' childishness." — Hands the reader to sentence 3 by establishing the characterization that the following sentence generalizes into a broader verdict.
3. **Verdict (state the overall effect):** "Carol Ann Duffy as a result portrays a childish man being mocked by an outraged woman through the used diction." — Concludes the paragraph by naming the author's overall portrayal strategy.

**Paragraph 2**
1. **Transition + Claim (introduce a new device):** "The poet also makes use of hyperbole to illustrate the extent of Orpheus' absurdity, when Eurydice, in the third stanza, lists the numerous ridiculous claims Orpheus made about himself and his work." — Hands the reader to sentence 2 by naming the device and specifying where the evidence sits.
2. **Evidence (quote):** "It is stated in the blurb found on his poetry work: 'that animals, / aardvark to zebra, / flocked to his side when he sang'42." — Hands the reader to sentence 3 by providing the textual proof that sets up the final interpretation of Orpheus' self-presentation.
3. **Verdict (state the outcome):** "Orpheus' attempt to present himself as a mystical figure is simply met with disdain from Eurydice." — Concludes the paragraph by naming the reaction that the hyperbole produces.

**Paragraph 3**
1. **Evidence + Mechanism + Implication (single complex sentence):** "In addition, the personification of the rocks at his feet, who before his apparition were 'sullen'43 and 'mute' 44, whose description symbolizes Eurydice's subordinate position in relation to her husband, are said to have 'wept wee, silver tears'45 after Orpheus' apparition." — This is the final move of the section; it introduces a third device, quotes it, explains the symbolic mechanism, and states the power-dynamic implication all at once.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Diction move first** — the word "sulked" establishes the personal, emotional core of Eurydice's scorn (childishness). This sets up the reader to accept that Orpheus is being personally mocked.
2. **Hyperbole move second** — the blurb quote escalates from personal criticism to absurdity, showing the breadth of Orpheus' self-mythologizing. This builds on the first move by widening the scope from one word to an entire body of claimed work.
3. **Personification move third** — the rocks' tears add a symbolic, environmental layer that reframes the scorn as a structural power imbalance (subordinate wife vs. husband). This comes last because it deepens the argument from "he is mocked" to "the whole world reflects her dominance."

**Generalized sequence for any student:** Move from the smallest textual unit (a word) → to a larger textual unit (a passage/blurb) → to a structural/symbolic device (personification of setting). Each step widens the scope of evidence while deepening the interpretive layer.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern name:** Quote → Unpack → Generalize (a three-step escalation from textual evidence to authorial effect)

**Step-by-step instructions to explain a NEW idea with the same pattern:**

1. **Introduce the device and its purpose** — Name the literary technique the author uses and state what it is meant to show (e.g., "The author makes use of [device] to illustrate [effect]").
2. **Anchor with a specific quote** — Insert a direct textual quotation with its location (stanza, chapter, line number) to prove the device is present.
3. **Unpack the quote's meaning** — Interpret what the quoted language reveals about a character's emotion, judgment, or trait; connect the word choice to a character attribute.
4. **State the broader verdict** — Name the author's overall portrayal strategy using "as a result" or "simply met with," specifying who is doing what to whom and through which device.
5. **Escalate with a second device (optional)** — Add a further literary technique using "also" or "in addition," following the same quote → unpack → verdict sequence to widen the argument's scope.
