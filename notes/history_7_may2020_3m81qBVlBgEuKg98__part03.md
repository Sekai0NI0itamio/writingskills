## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern used:** Context-broadening → named authority → concrete biographical evidence → implication for the work's reliability.

1. **State the broader context** your source operates within, extending from a previously discussed topic to a wider phenomenon or related concept.
2. **Name the specific person** (author, director, researcher) responsible for creating or shaping the source artifact you are evaluating.
3. **Cite one verifiable biographical or historical fact** that links that person to the subject matter — include a concrete detail (role, relationship, location) and a footnote marker.
4. **State the implication** that this connection has for the artifact's credibility, bias, or scope using a hedging verb like "may limit," "could skew," or "might compromise."
5. **Cut the sentence at the implication** if the section is designed as a fragment that leads into further analysis, ensuring the reader's attention moves to the next evaluative point.
