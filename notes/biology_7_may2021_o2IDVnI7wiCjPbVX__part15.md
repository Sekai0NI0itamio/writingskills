## What This Section Does (content sequence)

The ordered list of content moves for a reference/bibliography section:
1. **Author/agency identification** — establishes who is responsible for the claim.
2. **Year/date stamp** — sets temporal credibility (recency vs. foundational source).
3. **Title or descriptor** — names the contribution being cited.
4. **Publisher/journal/venue** — institutional anchor (journal name, organization, database).
5. **Volume/page or URL** — locator that lets a reader retrieve the source.
6. **Access date (for online sources)** — proves the link was live and verified.

**Why this order:** The reader needs to know *who* said *what* *when* before they can decide whether to follow the locator. Date without author is unanchored; locator without venue is unverifiable. The access date closes the chain by recording that the author actually consulted the live source.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern here is **provenance disclosure** — proving an outside source exists, who made it, when, and where to find it. To replicate it:

1. **Identify the claim-maker** (author or organization) — record their name exactly as published.
2. **Stamp the claim with a date** — the publication year, so the reader can judge age.
3. **Name the contribution** — give the article/report title verbatim.
4. **Anchor it institutionally** — add the journal, publisher, or issuing body.
5. **Provide a locator** — volume/issue/pages for print, full URL for web.
6. **Record retrieval** — add "Accessed [date]" if the source is online and could change.
