## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

The section is structured as **interviewer question → interviewee answer** pairs. Treating each Q&A as a "paragraph unit":

**Q&A Unit 1 (Questions 3 & 4 — exit interviews + who leaves):**

- **Move 1 (Q3 — Topic opener):** Interviewer poses a yes/no operational question about exit interviews. *"Do you conduct exit interviews with the employees leaving the company to get a feedback on reason for their resignation?"* → The word "feedback" and "reason for their resignation" hands the reader to expect a procedural/process answer.
- **Move 2 (A3 — Confirmation):** Interviewee gives minimal affirmation. *"Yes, we do."* → This brevity forces the interviewer to pivot, handing the reader to the next question.
- **Move 3 (Q4 — Refinement):** Interviewer narrows to a comparison/contrast question about performance level of leavers. *"Do you see more of high performing employees leaving your company or bottom performing employees?"* → The "or" structure hands the reader to expect a binary or mixed response.
- **Move 4 (A4 — Mixed verdict with mechanism):** Interviewee gives a hybrid answer then explains why bottom performers exit. *"It's a mix of both, when bottom performing employees are challenged, they tend to leave because they don't like to take challenges, however it is mostly a mix of both types of employees leaving."* → The "however" reframes the earlier claim, handing the reader to expect further specification about attrition distribution.

**Q&A Unit 2 (Question 5 — seniority level):**

- **Move 5 (Q5 — Categorization request):** Interviewer asks for a categorical breakdown by seniority. *"Does the employee attrition happen at the senior or junior level?"* → The binary frame hands the reader to expect a level-specific answer.
- **Move 6 (A5 — Distributional verdict):** Interviewee answers with a position on the spectrum, with hedging "most of the time" and emphasis "very less" for contrast. *"Most of the time, its on the junior to mid-level, senior level is very less."* → The asymmetry of detail (longer on junior side) hands the reader into the next benchmark question.

**Q&A Unit 3 (Question 6 — industry benchmarking, then follow-up):**

- **Move 7 (Q6 — Benchmark setup):** Interviewer introduces external comparison points (industry vs. start-ups). *"Does the employee attrition rate of your company match the attrition rate of the industry...or the attrition rate of start-ups?"* → The three-way comparison hands the reader to expect a numerical positioning.
- **Move 8 (A6 — Claim of competitive advantage):** Interviewee opens with a self-positioning verdict. *"Our attrition rate is very less compared to the industry which has attrition rate of 10-15% at a global level."* → The "very less compared to" frame hands the reader to the next comparative data point.
- **Move 9 (A6 — Industry specification):** Interviewee narrows to the local industry benchmark. *"For the IT industry in India, attrition rate is somewhere around 15%..."* → The "for the IT industry in India" specification hands the reader to the final sub-benchmark.
- **Move 10 (A6 — Start-up comparison):** Interviewee completes the benchmark trio. *"...and as far as start-ups are concerned, it is around 25%."* → This closing number hands the reader to the follow-up prompt.
- **Move 11 (Follow-up — Cause probe):** Interviewer asks a "how" question to elicit the mechanism behind the competitive position. *"How do you make sure your company's attrition is lower than that of the industry?"* → The "how do you make sure" hands the reader off-section toward causal explanation.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **semi-structured interview transcript** showing one side of a Q&A. The ordered moves are:

1. **Operational confirmation first (Q3):** establishes baseline HR practice with a closed yes/no. WHY: builds interview momentum and confirms the company follows a standard diagnostic procedure before the interviewer probes further.
2. **Segmentation question next (Q4):** narrows from "do you measure" to "who exactly is leaving." WHY: forces the interviewee to differentiate populations, surfacing performance-level patterns.
3. **Seniority-level stratification (Q5):** moves from performance axis to tenure/seniority axis. WHY: triangulates the attrition profile from a second dimension, making the picture multidimensional.
4. **External benchmarking (Q6):** pivots from internal description to external comparison. WHY: positions the company's figure against industry and start-up norms, establishing competitive standing.
5. **Triangulation of benchmarks within the answer:** own figure → global industry → domestic industry → start-ups. WHY: each successive comparison tightens the relevance of the benchmark to the company's specific market.
6. **Mechanism follow-up:** ends with a "how" prompt. WHY: shifts from descriptive (what/who/how much) to causal (why/how), which the next section of the interview would answer.

**Replication logic:** any company-profile interview can follow diagnostic-confirm → population-segmentation → stratification-dimension → external-benchmark → cause-probe.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / scope-narrowing:**
- *"For the IT industry in India"* — drills from global to local benchmark.
- *"as far as start-ups are concerned"* — pivots to a niche comparator.

**Cause / mechanism markers:**
- *"because they don't like to take challenges"* — embedded "because" clause linking trigger to behaviour.
- *"tend to leave"* — soft causal verb ("tend to") hedging the mechanism's universality.

**Contrast / concession:**
- *"however it is mostly a mix of both"* — "however" re-anchors the partial explanation back to the overall verdict.
- *"very less compared to the industry"* — "compared to" sets up implicit contrast with a baseline.

**Specification / categorization:**
- *"when bottom performing employees are challenged"* — "when" introduces a conditional subgroup.
- *"at the senior or junior level"* — binary category frame offered by interviewer.

**Hedging / frequency markers:**
- *"Most of the time"* — softens a distributional claim.
- *"somewhere around 15%"* — hedges the precision of the benchmark figure.
- *"a mix of both"* — refuses a binary, signals distribution rather than polarity.

**Verdict / positioning verbs:**
- *"is very less compared to"* — positional verdict verb phrase.
- *"tends to leave because"* — behaviour + reason one-clause construction.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section relies on the **benchmark-triangulation explanation pattern**: anchor your own figure against three external comparators in widening-then-narrowing scope, so the reader sees your number in context.

**Step-by-step instructions to explain a NEW idea with the same pattern:**

1. **Open with self-positioning.** State your own metric and its verdict direction in one clause ("Our X is [adjective] compared to [broad comparator]").
2. **Cite the broad/comparator figure.** Give the comparator's numerical range with a scope tag ("which has X at a [global/sector-wide/regional] level").
3. **Narrow to your specific context.** Use "For the [your industry/region/niche]..." to drill into the most relevant benchmark.
4. **Add a niche comparator.** Close with "and as far as [specific subgroup] are concerned, it is [figure]." This third comparator sharpens the contrast.
5. **End with a mechanism follow-up prompt** (interviewer side) — a "how do you ensure/achieve/maintain" question that pivots from descriptive to causal.

**Why this pattern works:** the widening-then-narrowing structure first locates the figure on a general scale, then proves the speaker's familiarity with the specific market, then signals awareness of an even tighter competitive set — establishing credibility in three moves without ever needing to state it explicitly.
