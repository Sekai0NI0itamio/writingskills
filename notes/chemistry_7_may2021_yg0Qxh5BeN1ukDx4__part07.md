## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1**

1. **Data tables** — *claim*: presents raw titration volumes for three samples across three trials. Quote: "Volume 9.8 9.7 9.5 9… 9.2 8.6 8.9 8.9… 9.3 9.2 9.3 8.8." Hand-off: these individual readings set up the need for a single representative value, so the next sentence averages them.
2. **Mean ∆V row** — *definition/calculation*: computes the mean titre across trials 1–3. Quote: "Mean ∆V (Na₂S₂O₃)(mL) 9.4 8.8 9.1." Hand-off: the three mean values demand a single overall average, which the next sentence supplies.
3. **Average volume** — *calculation*: divides the sum by 3 to get 9.1 mL. Quote: "Average volume of thiosulphate solution = 9.1 mL." Hand-off: this volume is the input for converting to moles, so the next sentence performs that conversion.
4. **Moles of thiosulphate** — *mechanism*: multiplies concentration × volume × stoichiometric factor. Quote: "Average number of moles of thiosulphate = 4.55 × 10⁻⁵ mol." Hand-off: thiosulphate moles are stoichiometrically linked to iodine and hypochlorite, so the next sentence states that equivalence.
5. **Moles of hypochlorite = iodine** — *evidence handling*: equates moles via reaction stoichiometry. Quote: "Average number of moles of hypochlorite = Average number of moles of iodine = 2.28 × 10⁻⁵ mol." Hand-off: these moles are the numerator for the concentration formula, so the next sentence divides by volume and mass.

**Paragraph 2**

1. **Concentration calculation** — *calculation*: divides moles by solution volume and mass to get percentage. Quote: "Concentration of NaClO = 6.79 × 10⁻³ %." Hand-off: this numerical result invites a reality check against an external standard, which the next sentence provides.
2. **CDC comparison** — *comparison/implication*: benchmarks the result against a published recommendation. Quote: "This concentration is about 15 times less than the one recommended by the CDC." Hand-off: the section ends with a contextual verdict that frames the electrolysis outcome as under-dosed relative to guidance.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Raw titration data for three samples** — establishes the empirical foundation; without measured volumes no further calculation is possible.
2. **Mean titre per sample** — reduces scatter to a single reliable value per sample, setting up the averaging step.
3. **Overall average volume** — collapses three sample means into one representative titre, providing the single input needed for mole conversion.
4. **Mole conversion of thiosulphate** — transforms volume into amount of substance using the known concentration, setting up the stoichiometric bridge.
5. **Stoichiometric equivalence to hypochlorite/iodine** — transfers the amount from the titrant to the analyte via reaction ratios, producing the quantity that actually describes the bleach.
6. **Concentration of NaClO** — normalises the mole amount by solution volume and sample mass, yielding a percentage that can be compared to a standard.
7. **Comparison to CDC recommendation** — situates the numerical result against an external benchmark, giving the section a practical verdict.

*Why this order*: each move supplies the single required input for the next — data → average → moles → equivalent moles → concentration → benchmark — so no step can precede its predecessor. A student replicating this with a different topic would follow: measure → average → convert to moles → apply stoichiometry → express as concentration → compare to a reference value.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / calculation chain:**
- "=" — links each calculation step to its result: "Average number of moles of thiosulphate = 0.005 × 9.1 × 10⁻³ = 4.55 × 10⁻⁵ mol"
- "=" — carries stoichiometric equivalence forward: "Average number of moles of hypochlorite = Average number of moles of iodine = 2.28 × 10⁻⁵ mol"
- "=" — completes the concentration formula: "Concentration of NaClO = 2.28 × 10⁻⁵ mol ÷ 0.025L = 9.12 × 10⁻⁴ mol/L = 0.0679g/L solution = 6.79 × 10⁻³ %"

**Comparison / implication:**
- "This… is about… times less than" — benchmarks the result against an external standard: "This concentration is about 15 times less than the one recommended by the CDC."

**Evidence handling:**

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern name:** *Measurement → averaging → stoichiometric conversion → concentration → external benchmark*

1. **State the raw measurements** for each trial and sample in a table so the reader can see the individual data points.
2. **Calculate the mean per sample** and then the overall average to produce a single reliable input value.
3. **Convert the average volume to moles** of the titrant using its known concentration and the reaction stoichiometry.
4. **Apply the mole ratio** from the balanced equation to equate the titrant moles to the analyte moles.
5. **Divide the analyte moles** by the solution volume and sample mass to express the result as a concentration or percentage.
6. **Compare the final value** to a published standard, guideline, or manufacturer's specification, stating the magnitude of the difference (e.g., "X times less/more than").
