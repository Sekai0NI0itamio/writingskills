## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Identifies the political stake of the source's overseers** — establishes who controlled the source and what they stood to gain.
2. **Infers bias from that stake** — draws a consequence ("hence slanted") linking authority to slant.
3. **Adds a second limitation with mechanism** — the medium itself; explains WHY the medium creates bias (editors' awareness of framing power).
4. **States the methodological consequence** — what analytical stance the limitations impose ("scepticism upon analysis").
5. **Pivots to counter-evidence** — shows the source still has value despite bias.
6. **Cites specific source language as evidence** — quotes the source's framing ("encroachment") to show what it reveals.
7. **Draws an inference from that evidence** — connects the source's framing to the broader historical argument (intervention justified by Japanese threat).
8. **Anchors the investigation in a dated event** — gives a factual starting point for the analysis.
9. **Unpacks the event in temporal context** — specifies what the event did and links it to preceding events.
10. **Lays out the analytical roadmap** — enumerates the causes the essay will examine.

**Why this order:** Source evaluation must come before the investigation because it establishes the lens through which all subsequent evidence is read. Within the evaluation, limitations are stated before counter-evidence because the reader needs to know what to be sceptical about before seeing what the source can still reveal. The investigation paragraph then pivots from source criticism to historical analysis by anchoring in fact first, then expanding context, then forecasting structure — moving from concrete event to analytical framework.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

### SKELETON 1 — Source evaluation with counter-balance
"[Authority figure — their political role]. The [source]'s [output] were hence [bias direction] towards [position on issue]. The [source]'s [value dimension] is also limited by its [characteristic]; as [contextual position], its [actors] were aware of its [power] towards [action] around such a [issue type]. This therefore imposes a [analytical stance] upon analysis of the source's content. However, the source's content demonstrates its [counter-value]; by [specific evidence from source], it [inference about what the source reveals]."

1. **Slot 1** holds an identification of a source's controller and their stake (appositive clause, past tense). **Slot 2** holds a bias claim with a consequence marker (declarative, "hence/therefore"). **Slot 3** holds a second limitation with a mechanism — a characteristic plus the reason it creates bias (compound sentence with "as" clause). **Slot 4** holds the methodological consequence — what stance the analyst must take (declarative with "This therefore"). **Slot 5** holds a contrast pivot with specific evidence and an inference (compound sentence with "However" + "by" + "it infers/reveals").
2. **To fill with a different idea:** Slot 1 — pick a source and identify who controlled it and what they stood to gain; state it as an appositive. Slot 2 — state the bias that follows from that control, using "hence" or "therefore." Slot 3 — add a second limitation (medium, audience, funding) and explain the mechanism with an "as" clause. Slot 4 — state what analytical stance this imposes. Slot 5 — pivot with "However," cite specific content from the source, and state what it reveals.
3. **Original filled version:** "Sanford Dole — the latter of whom was serving as the first President of Hawaii. The newspaper's views were hence slanted towards portraying annexation favourably. The Gazette's historical value is also limited by its medium; as the premier newspaper in 1897 Hawaii, its editors were aware of its power towards framing the discourse around such a contentious issue. This therefore imposes a degree of scepticism upon analysis of the source's content. However, the source's content demonstrates its historical value; by describing the wave of Asian migration as Japanese 'encroachment' onto Hawaii… it infers that intervention in Hawaii is as a result of Japanese intervention."
4. **Demonstration fill (different topic):** "Lord Beaverbrook — the latter of whom was serving as Minister of Information. ## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing:**
- "This therefore imposes a degree" — consequence marker linking mechanism to analytical stance
- "This authorised the annexation of" — demonstrative carrying the prior event forward into its effect

**Cause/consequence:**
- "views were hence slanted towards" — causal inference from authority to bias
- "it infers that intervention in Hawaii is as a result of" — consequence drawn from evidence
- "This authorised the annexation" — causal action of the resolution

**Contrast/concession:**
- "However, the source's content demonstrates" — pivot from limitation to value

**Specification:**
- "by describing the wave of Asian migration as" — specification through citing source language
- "five years after the overthrow of" — temporal specification placing the event in context

**Evidence handling:**
- "the source's content demonstrates its historical value" — framing evidence as proof of value
- "by describing… as Japanese 'encroachment'" — direct quotation as evidence

**Explanation verbs:**
- "is also limited by its medium" — identifies a causal factor
- "were aware of its power towards framing" — mechanism verb explaining why bias exists
- "infers that" — draws an inference from evidence
- "will discuss the role of" — forecasts analytical structure

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern: Stake-identification → bias inference → mechanism → methodological consequence → counter-evidence with implication**

This section relies on a **cause chain with counter-balance**: it builds a case for source limitation through layered causes (who controlled it → what bias that creates → why the medium amplifies that bias → what analytical stance results), then pivots to show what the source can still reveal through specific evidence.

To explain a new idea with the same pattern:

1. **Identify the stakeholder** — name who controlled or produced the source and what political or institutional role they held.
2. **Infer bias from that stake** — state what slant or framing their position creates, using a consequence marker ("hence," "therefore").
3. **Add a second limitation with a mechanism** — identify another factor (medium, audience, funding) and explain WHY it creates bias, not just THAT it does.
4. **State the methodological consequence** — say what analytical stance this imposes on anyone reading the source ("This therefore imposes…").
5. **Pivot with a contrast marker** — use "However" to signal that the source is not worthless despite its limitations.
6. **Cite specific evidence from the source** — quote or paraphrase actual content that reveals something historically significant.
7. **Draw an inference from that evidence** — state what the cited content implies about the broader historical question you are investigating.
