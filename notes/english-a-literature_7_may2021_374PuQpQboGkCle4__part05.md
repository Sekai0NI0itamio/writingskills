## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **poetry commentary paragraph block** analysing a single thematic thread (singularity/loneliness through "fragmented versions" of paired things). The repeatable sequence is:

1. **Carry-over connector** — finish the prior pairing idea so the reader's footing is stable.
2. **Add-on transition + new image-quote** — name the next fractured image and embed the textual evidence immediately ("shuffled in a dead man's shoes").
3. **Device + mechanism unpacking** — name the technique operating on that image ("Here, sibilance accentuates…") and explain *how* it produces the emotional effect.
4. **New parallel image via "also depicted, as"** — introduce a second fractured image on the same theme ("nun in a mirror").
5. **Metaphor mechanism + dual-sided implication** — explain what the metaphor encodes, then write the implication as an either/or ("can imagine, or will be allowed to have") to widen the reading.
6. **Forward-pointing "Additionally"** — gesture toward the next instance of the motif.

**Why this order works:** the section is building a *catalog* of images, so each move must (a) hand the reader a discrete new image, (b) explain the technique on that image, (c) extract the thematic implication, and (d) signal that more instances are coming. Skipping the technique step would leave the quote as mere paraphrase; skipping the implication step would leave the technique analysis hanging in mid-air.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / addition:** "Additionally, Mrs. Lazarus attempts to regain" — extends the catalog of fractured images.
- **Parallel / "also":** "The speaker's loss of a sexual partner is also depicted" — signals a second instance of the same theme.
- **Deictic anchor to the quote:** "Here, sibilance accentuates the painful sound" — pulls the reader back from the embedded quote into the analyst's voice.
- **Causal/interpretive verb:** "suggests that a life of celibacy is all" — converts the metaphor into a thematic claim.
- **Analytic hedging (binary disjunction):** "is all that she can imagine, or will be allowed to have" — opens two readings inside one sentence.
- **Nominalised identification (the unpacking verb):** "Mrs. Lazarus metaphorically identifying with a nun" — turns a quote into an interpretive clause by -ing form.
- **Evidence handling:** the embedded quotations ("shuffled in a dead man's shoes"; "nun in a mirror, touching herself.") carry the line numbers — i.e., evidence is *always* inlined inside the claim sentence, never floated.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The dominant explanation pattern here is: **quote-anchored → device-named → sensory-mechanism → thematic implication with a disjunction.**

Steps to replicate with any new image from a poem:

1. **Pre-attach the quote inside your claim sentence** — never analyse an image you haven't yet shown. Place a 3–8 word verbatim line in quotation marks *inside* the sentence that announces what the image is doing.
2. **Follow immediately with "Here," or "In this line,"** — use a deictic so the device analysis visibly hooks back to the quote you just embedded.
3. **Name one specific technique** (sibilance, monosyllables, caesura, enjambment, metaphor, juxtaposition) — never "imagery" in general.
4. **Verb the technique as an intensification** ("accentuates," "reinforces," "undermines," "exposes") so it *does* something to a named sensory quality ("the painful sound," "the cold light").
5. **Restate the quoted image in your own analytical words** so the reader sees the gap between poetic image and analytic reading.
6. **Open a second paragraph (or sentence) with "also depicted, as"** if you are adding a *parallel* image to the catalog.
7. **Convert the image into a thematic claim using "suggests that"** — turn the device analysis into a statement about the speaker's situation.
8. **Hedge the implication with a binary disjunction** ("can X, or will be allowed to Y as a [social role]") to show the poem is doing more than one thing at once.
9. **Close with a forward "Additionally,"** to keep the cataloguing logic visibly ongoing.
