## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Anchor the anomaly** — name the outlier and signal that it will be explained.
2. **Apply a numerical threshold test** — formula → substitution → result → verdict, performed twice (upper, then lower) for completeness.
3. **Describe the distribution shape** — skew, median, IQR, range, with a pointer back to the visual.
4. **Re-identify the outlier by name and value** — recall the specific data point just justified.
5. **Declare the investigative method** — name the source (online research / an authority) so the upcoming reasons carry weight.
6. **Enumerate causal factors** — bullet-style list of reasons why the cost is high.
7. **Convert one reason into a quantified example** — a single drug price in the US.
8. **Pair it with a comparator country** — "whereas" clause showing a cheaper price elsewhere.
9. **Repeat the example–comparator pair** — second drug, second foreign country, same contrastive structure.
10. **Hand to a concluding implication** — "Hence, these higher…" (the section cuts off, but the connective is set up).

**Why this order:** the anomaly must be justified statistically *before* it is explained contextually; without the threshold test the reader cannot trust the "outlier" label, so the causal reasoning in step 6 would float. The example–comparator pair is doubled to build a pattern (one instance could be coincidence; two instances establish a structural cost gap), which then licenses the "Hence" verdict.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / counting verdicts:**  
- "There is only one outlier, $10 623.85, which belongs to" — *count + category*.
- "There is no value less than the lower limit" — *null verdict*.

**Specification / narrowing:**  
- "For example, insulin for diabetes in the US costs" — *concrete instance of a general cause*.
- "Another fact from the same source, people in the US pay" — *second instance, same authority*.

**Contrast / concession (comparator workhorse):**  
- "whereas it costs only a third of that in Canada" — *home vs foreign price, fraction form*.
- "whereas people in Germany pay less than half" — *repeated "whereas", second comparator*.

**Causal / consequence hand-forward:**  
- "Hence, these higher" — *signals a closing implication clause built on the two examples*.
- "the main reasons for this are" — *names the explanatory list that the examples will instantiate*.

**Evidence handling:**  
- "After some online research, I found out that" — *source + epistemic verb framing the reasons as findings, not opinion*.
- "(Dr. Hector Florimon)" — *parenthetical authority attribution at end of comparator pair*.

**Explanation / statistical verbs:**  
- "exceeding the generated upper limit" — *verdict phrased as past-participle relation to a calculated threshold*.
- "has a negatively skewed distribution" — *classification verb "has" + shape adjective*.
- "As can be seen in the box-whisker plot" — *visual-evidence pointer verb phrase*.

**Recap / re-anchoring:**  
- "The outlier identified belongs to the United States" — *restates the anomaly before explaining it*.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern name: Statistically-Justified Anomaly → Comparative Cost Explanation.**  
The section uses *threshold-test verification* to legitimise the anomaly, then *dual example-with-comparator* to explain *why* the anomaly exists, before handing to a *consequence*.

**Steps to replicate with a NEW idea:**

1. **Name the anomaly in one short clause.** Use a definite-article re-anchor: "The [anomaly] identified belongs to [category], [value]…" This re-introduces the point already proven so it carries into the explanation.

2. **Cite how you learned the cause.** Use a source-declaration clause: "After [research method], I found out that…" so the upcoming reasons sound investigated, not asserted.

3. **List 3–4 parallel noun-phrase causes.** Comma-separated, no verbs: "[cause 1], [cause 2], [cause 3], and [cause 4]." This pre-loads categories the next sentence will instantiate.

4. **Convert one cause into a quantified example in the home category.** "For example, [item] in [home country] costs [amount] [unit]." Concrete number + time unit.

5. **Add the first foreign comparator with "whereas" and a fraction.** "whereas it costs only [fraction] of that in [comparator]." Fraction language ("a third", "less than half") is the reusable contrast device.

6. **Re-cite the same source and add a second quantified example.** "Another fact from the same source, [home country] people pay [amount] for [item]…" Different item, same country — this is what turns coincidence into a pattern.

7. **Add the second "whereas" + fraction comparator.** Repeat slot 5's structure with a different foreign country.

8. **Anchor with a parenthetical authority.** Place "(Author / Source)" at the end of the second comparator to close the evidence block.

9. **Hand forward with a consequence connector.** Open the final clause with "Hence, these higher…" so the implication rides on the two comparisons just made.

10. **(Optional preceding block) Run the threshold test first** — formula → substitution → result → verdict, mirrored for upper and lower bounds — so the anomaly in step 1 is already numerically licensed. This is the verification layer that makes the rest defensible.
