## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a Works Cited section in MLA-style format. The ordered content moves are:

1. **Author / creator slot** — establishes who produced the work; first because every citation needs a named source.
2. **Title slot** — names the specific work; required because the reader must identify what was used.
3. **Container slot** (journal, book, report, module) — situates the work in its larger publication venue; needed to locate the original.
4. **Locator / DOI slot** — provides the persistent identifier or volume/issue/page numbers; necessary for retrieval.
5. **Access date slot** — records when the source was retrieved; closes the citation for accountability.

The WHY of this order: each slot supplies information the next slot depends on. You cannot place a work in a container before naming the work; you cannot cite a DOI before naming the work that owns it; access date logically closes the entry because it references the completed act of locating the work. The order is **identity → container → locator → verification**, moving from the most specific (the work itself) outward to the act of access.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / structural markers (slot labels):**
- "vol." — "vol. 230, no. 12, 2019" — locates the journal issue.
- "no." — "vol. 230, no. 12" — counts the issue within the volume.
- "pp." — "pp. 304–313." — spans multiple pages.
- "p." — "p. 272." — denotes a single page entry.

**Cause / consequence / verification:**
- "doi:" — "doi:10.5772/intechopen.70653." — provides the unique digital object identifier as a consequence of needing a stable retrieval code.
- "DOI:" — "DOI: 10.1016/j.njas.2012.07.001." — capitalised variant for the same identification job.
- "Accessed" — "Accessed 16th August 2020." — marks the moment of verification, closing the citation.

**Specification / definition of authorship scope:**
- "et al." — "Baruah, Nijara, et al." — specifies that additional authors exist beyond the lead author.

**Specification of year-on-one location:**
- comma-separated "Year" — "2017" — pins the publication to a specific year within the container slot.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

Works Cited entries follow a **funnel pattern — identity → container → locator → verification**. Each move narrows the reader's focus from the broadest (creator) to the most concrete (retrieval moment).

Step 1: **Name the creator.** Write the author — individual(s) inverted, or institution in full — so the reader knows *who* made the source.

Step 2: **Name the work.** Place the article title in quotation marks, or the book/report title in italics-equivalent spacing; end with a terminal period to close the title slot.

Step 3: **Situate in container.** Identify the larger publication venue (journal title, book series, report series) and attach its volume/issue/year/page numbers as a comma list.

Step 4: **Provide the locator.** Add the DOI in lowercase "doi:" form (or "DOI:" capitalised if the publisher's original does), so retrieval does not require browsing.

Step 5: **Close with access.** End the entry with "Accessed" followed by the day, spelled-out month, and four-digit year, as the verification stamp of when the source was actually consulted.

Each step hands the next by dependency: the work's name demands a container; the container demands a locator; the locator demands proof of access. Repeating this five-step funnel for every source produces a uniform Works Cited list regardless of the underlying topic.
