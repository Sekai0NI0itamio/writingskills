- Hands to next sentence by **setup → outcome**: once two competing methods have been named, the reader expects a winner, so the next sentence supplies the metric that will rank them.

- Move: **forward-looking recommendation** — converts the diagnosis into a concrete future action.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **closing-method-then-evaluation** block. Its ordered moves are:

1. **Restate aim + tools together** (correlation check + 2 regressions) — gives the reader the goal and the means before any result.
2. **Layer a comparison metric** ("additionally calculated the percentage error") — supplies the yardstick needed to rank the two methods.
3. **Deliver the verdict** ("which turned out to be Equation 2") — names the winner; without this, the critique that follows has nothing to evaluate.
4. **Pivot to evaluation by naming one cause of inaccuracy** ("could be credited to the excessive rounding") — keeps the limitation narrow, fixable, and IB-flattering.
5. **Unpack the procedure that produced the error, using a "For most… but with…" contrast** — proves self-awareness by showing the default policy and the exception, plus the *intention* behind the exception.
6. **Issue a future-tense corrective** ("To make calculations more accurate in future…") — converts diagnosis into an actionable improvement.

The order matters because each move earns the next: results first (nothing to critique yet otherwise), then a single named cause (concrete), then the procedural detail (proof of understanding), then the recommendation (proof of forward thinking).

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON 1 — Aim + Method + Verdict**

`"[Verb]-ing if there was a [relationship type] then used [N] methods of [technique] to attempt to achieve my aim of finding a model for the relationship between [variable A] and [variable B]. - Slot instructions:
  - Slot 1 (gerund verb + relationship check + then used N methods of technique): state in past gerund what was first screened, then name the comparative methods used.
  - Slot 2 (aim of finding a model for the relationship between A and B): pair two concrete variables whose relationship is genuinely testable.
  - Slot 3 (additionally calculated [comparison metric]): a *second* procedural act whose only job is to rank the first set of methods.
  - Slot 4 (which turned out to be [winner]): a specific, labelled outcome — Equation 2, Model B, etc., not "the second worked better."
- Original fill: "finding if there was a linear correlation then used 2 methods of data regression… I additionally calculated the percentage error… which turned out to be Equation 2."

`"Some of the inaccuracies in [output] could be credited to the [cause]. For most [cases] I used [approach A], but with [edge cases] I chose to use [approach B], hoping it would decrease the inaccuracies. To make [process] more accurate in future, [improved approach] should be used."`

- Slot instructions:
  - Slot 1 (Some of the inaccuracies… could be credited to): pick ONE specific, fixable procedural cause; keep the hedging ("could be") to sound measured, not defensive.
  - Slot 2 (For most… but with…): contrast a default policy with an exception; the exception should be triggered by a recognisable feature ("small," "close together," "extreme").
  - Slot 3 (hoping it would decrease): a motivation clause that admits the mitigation was attempted, not guaranteed.
  - Slot 4 (To make… in future, … should be used): a single, concrete future action phrased with "should."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / addition**
- "then used 2 methods of data regression" — orders the procedural steps inside one sentence.
- "I additionally calculated the percentage error" — layers a comparison step on top of the regression work.

**Purpose / aim**
- "to attempt to achieve my aim of finding a model" — explicit purpose clause tying work to a goal.
- "so I can determine which is the most accurate model" — purpose clause justifying the extra calculation.

**Verdict / outcome**
- "which turned out to be Equation 2" — delivers the comparative winner in a single relative clause.

**Cause attribution (evaluation)**
- "could be credited to the excessive rounding" — assigns the error to one named procedural cause with hedged certainty.

**Contrast / specification**
- "For most calculations… but with values that were extremely small or close together" — default vs exception pattern.
- "For most… but with…" — the same contrast pair operating as the spine of the sentence.

**Motivation / intention**
- "hoping it would decrease the inaccuracies" — explains WHY the exception was chosen; flags the mitigation as speculative.

**Forward-looking recommendation**
- "To make calculations more accurate in future" — frames the recommendation as a future-tense improvement.
- "…should be used" — modal of obligation that closes the evaluation cleanly.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses the pattern **method-with-verdict → one-cause evaluation with future recommendation**. It is not a definition/unpack chain; it is a "finish the work, then audit it" chain.

Steps to replicate with a NEW idea:

1. **Fuse aim + tools into one sentence.** Combine "what was checked," "how many methods," and "what the aim was" so the reader sees goal and means before any result lands.
2. **Add a comparison metric with "additionally."** Pick a yardstick that allows two methods to be ranked, and name it explicitly.
3. **Deliver the verdict with "which turned out to be [winner]."** Use a specific labelled outcome, not a vague preference.
4. **Pivot to evaluation: name the inaccuracy and its cause.** Use "could be credited to" and isolate ONE fixable procedural cause (rounding, sample size, calibration, wording, etc.).
5. **Unpack the procedure with a "For most… but with…" contrast.** Show the default policy and the exception, then attach a motivation clause ("hoping it would…") that admits the mitigation was speculative.
6. **Close with a future-tense recommendation.** Use "To make [X] more accurate in future, [Y] should be used" — one action, one modal of obligation.
