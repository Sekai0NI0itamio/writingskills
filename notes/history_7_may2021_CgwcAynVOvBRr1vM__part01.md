## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1** (3 sentence-units)

1. **Context continuation + threat framing** — *"[Office, warning of violence if the Union"* — picks up a prior warning and respecifies the trigger condition ("if the Union was not replaced"), handing the reader to a named source for that warning.
2. **Authority claim + mechanism** — *"Gent warned that Malay backlash would"* — names the source (Gent) and presents a causal chain: Malay backlash → "actively assist" MCP and pro-Indonesian organizations. Hands forward by parallel clause ("while…").
3. **Concession/addition via "while"** — *"while 'Malaya-based Indonesian elements' might"* — keeps Gent as speaker but opens a second, parallel threat vector (capitalising on the tension to attack minorities). The "while" forces the reader to expect a contrasting clause, so a second agent follows.

**Paragraph 2** (2 sentence-units)

1. **Contrast ("instead") + corroborating authority** — *"instead reinforced Gent's opinions by warning"* — the dispatched moderator (MacDonald) was sent to "bring Gent back to his senses" but, by the verb "instead," he doubles down on Gent's view; this hand-off is the paragraph's pivot from one voice to two agreeing voices.
2. **Specification by named organisation + embedded evidence** — *"The PKMM, who had gained considerable support"* — zooms from abstract warning to a concrete actor (PKMM), uses a relative clause ("who had gained…") to ground its support, and ends mid-verb "threatened to" so the threat is left dangling for the next sentence to complete.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Source-anchored warning** (Gent) — establishes the alarm and gives it a named originator.
2. **Mechanism of the alarm** — explains *how* the backlash operates (assist MCP/nationalists, capitalise on tension). Sets up why the warning is credible.
3. **Parallel threat via concession marker** ("while") — broadens the danger to a second agent so the reader sees the threat is multi-front.
4. **Moderator contradicts expectation** (MacDonald dispatched to rebuke → "instead" corroborates) — escalates credibility of the warning through an *unintended* ally.
5. **Concrete agent specification** (PKMM with relative clause of support + ideology) — converts abstract danger into a named, supported organisation, priming the next sentence's verb ("threatened to…").

The order works because each move *raises the stakes without restating*: source → mechanism → second front → second authority → specific organisation. A student replicating this should keep the escalation logic (one warning is not enough; the section must show converging authorities on a named target).

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**Skeleton A — Dual-threat warning paragraph**

SKELETON: "[Source] warned that [group A] would [verb phrase 1] [target organisations], while [group B] might [verb phrase 2] [secondary targets]."

1. *Slots:* slot 1 = named authority in past tense warning verb ("warned that"); slot 2 = domestic mass actor; slot 3 = active-voice danger verb ("actively assist," "capitalise on"); slot 4 = named organisations; slot 5 = parallel foreign/minor actor with modal "might"; slot 6 = secondary victim group.
2. *Filling instructions:* pick two groups you want to show as threats — one domestic, one cross-border — and one named figure whose warning frames both. Use "would" for the more certain threat, "might" for the speculative one to mirror the original's epistemic gradient.
3. *Original fill:* "Gent warned that Malay backlash would 'actively assist' the MCP and nationalist pro-Indonesian political organizations, while 'Malaya-based Indonesian elements' might capitalize on the tension to attack minority communities."
4. *Demonstration fill (different idea):* "The 1938 Spengler Report warned that Reichstag deputies would 'parliamentarily obstruct' emergency defence bills, while paramilitary SA units might mobilise street action to intimidate coalition deputies."

**Skeleton B — Corroborating-authority pivot paragraph**

SKELETON: "[Second authority], having been dispatched to '[expected rebuke]', instead [verb of agreement] by warning of '[shared concern]'."

1. *Slots:* slot 1 = second named figure; slot 2 = past-participle clause of mission ("having been dispatched to"); slot 3 = quoted original brief in scare-quotes; slot 4 = contrast adverb "instead"; slot 5 = verb showing endorsement ("reinforced," "confirmed"); slot 6 = their own quoted warning phrase.
2. *Filling instructions:* choose a figure who was *meant* to soften or retract the first warning. Quote their original remit, then use "instead" so the reader registers the surprise, and finish with their own quoted warning to make the corroboration dramatic.
3. *Original fill:* "MacDonald, having been dispatched to 'bring Gent back to his senses', instead reinforced Gent's opinions by warning of 'Indonesian anti-European currents'."
4. *Demonstration fill (different idea):* "Bevin, having been sent to 'talk Eden out of confrontation', instead reinforced Eden's position by warning of 'Soviet designs on the Turkish Straits'."

**Skeleton C — Named-actor grounding sentence**

SKELETON: "The [ORG], who had [past-tense evidence of support] for [reason] and ideology centered on [named doctrine], [threat verb] to [infinitive of action]."

1. *Slots:* slot 1 = acronym/short name of organisation; slot 2 = relative clause of gained support ("who had gained considerable support among the X for their Y"); slot 3 = reason for support; slot 4 = named ideological doctrine; slot 5 = present/past threat verb; slot 6 = open infinitive clause that carries into the next sentence.
2. *Filling instructions:* pick an organisation, give it a constituency and a reason for its popularity, attach it to a recognisable named ideology, then open a threat verb whose infinitive you deliberately *do not finish* in this sentence so the paragraph carries forward.
3. *Original fill:* "The PKMM, who had gained considerable support among the Malay peasantry for their anti-colonial stance and ideology centered on the Indonesian nationalist philosophy of Pancasila, threatened to…"
4. *Demonstration fill (different idea):* "The Cliveden Set, who had gained considerable access to the War Cabinet for their appeasement leanings and ideology centered on Baldwin-era Conservative internationalism, threatened to…"

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / framing continuation:** none of "firstly/next" here — sequencing is done by paragraph position and by leaving the threat verb infinitive open.
- **Concession / contrast:** "while" — *"while 'Malaya-based Indonesian elements' might capitalize"* (introduces parallel threat without retracting the first).
- **Contrast / expectation reversal:** "instead" — *"instead reinforced Gent's opinions by warning"* (signals the moderator became an ally).
- **Backgrounded agency:** "having been dispatched to" — *"having been dispatched to 'bring Gent back to his senses'"* (packs prior mission into a participle so the sentence can pivot on "instead").
- **Specification / definition:** "centered on" — *"ideology centered on the Indonesian nationalist philosophy"* (pins the org to a named doctrine).
- **Evidence handling:** embedded quoted phrases in single quotation marks ('actively assist', 'Malaya-based Indonesian elements', 'bring Gent back to his senses', 'Indonesian anti-European currents') — used as the *evidentiary unit*, not paraphrase.
- **Imminent-action verbs:** "threatened to" — *"threatened to [carry into next sentence]"* (a deliberately open verb that loads the threat into the next clause).
- **Modal gradient:** "would" vs "might" — *"would 'actively assist' … might capitalize"* (marks certainty vs speculation across the two threats).

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses a **converging-authorities threat pattern**: one warning is not enough; the section shows danger by stacking named sources who *agree*, ending on a concrete organisation whose action is left deliberately unfinished so the next sentence inherits the threat.

Steps to replicate with a new idea:

1. **Open with a named first source** who warns of a general danger. Use a reporting verb ("warned that") and a quoted phrase in single quotes for the warning itself.
2. **Specify the mechanism** inside that warning: give the warning a *how* (group A → assists / enables target).
3. **Add a parallel threat with "while"** — a second actor and a second victim — to show the danger is multi-vector, not single.
4. **Introduce a second authority who was meant to disagree.** Use "having been dispatched to '[original remit]' instead [verb of endorsement] by warning of '[their own quote]'." The "instead" is the engine of credibility.
5. **Ground in a specific organisation** with a relative clause ("who had gained… for their [stance] and ideology centered on [named doctrine]"). This converts abstract warning into a named agent.
6. **End on an unfinished threat verb** ("threatened to…") so the danger visibly carries into the next sentence — the reader feels the threat is still unfolding rather than closed.
