## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section relies on a **bridge → term + formula announcement → visual equation → symbol glossary** pattern. To reproduce the same explanatory move with a new idea:

1. **Bridge** — End the previous paragraph with a stranded noun phrase that names the wider topic you are feeding into (e.g. "the efficiency of the engine"). This anchors the upcoming formula in a larger purpose.
2. **Announce** — Open a new sentence that names the quantity, brackets its symbol in parentheses, and declares "is calculated using [input A] ([symbol A]) and [input B] ([symbol B]) through the formula." Use the passive "is calculated" so the formula sounds like a given rule.
3. **Display** — Insert the equation as a centred visual (image or displayed formula), placed immediately after the announcement sentence so the reader sees the symbol-relationship before they forget it.
4. **Key** — Beneath the equation, list each symbol on its own line in the format "(symbol)- full term," repeating the order in which the symbols appear in the equation so the reader can match top-to-bottom.
5. **Stop** — Do not add interpretation here. The glossary ends the section; further analysis of the formula belongs to the next section.
