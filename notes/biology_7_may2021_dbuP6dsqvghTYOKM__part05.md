## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1**

- **Sentence 1 (Context + Authority + Claim):** "According to a study conducted by Hong Kong's Centre of Food Safety" — hands to S2 by sourcing the upcoming data point to a named institution, so the next sentence must deliver that data.
- **Sentence 2 (Evidence — quoted statistic):** "'most seaweed and seaweed products contained very high iodine levels' (Dietary)" — hands to S3 by raising the question *how high*, prompting the specific numerical follow-up.
- **Sentence 3 (Evidence — quantified figure):** "'the mean level of dried kelp was 2 600 000 µg/kg'" — hands to S4 by introducing a raw, untranslatable number that requires decoding.
- **Sentence 4 (Unpack / mechanism — unit conversion):** "which translates to 26 grams/kg, a very high amount" — hands to S5 by converting the abstract figure into a comprehensible scale, so the reader is now ready to assess real-world impact.
- **Sentence 5 (Implication — applied consequence):** "even a small amount of kombu consumed per day would meet or exceed the recommended daily limits" — hands to S6 by establishing that seaweed is iodine-rich *and* could saturate intake, so a contrast with the population's actual intake becomes the logical next move.
- **Sentence 6 (Contrast trigger):** "However" — a pivot word; hands to S7 by signalling that the conclusion above is about to be complicated.
- **Sentence 7 (Counter-evidence — population statistic):** "it was found that 'around 59% of the population [in Hong Kong] intakes below 50µg [of iodine]/day'" — hands to S8 by introducing a new dataset whose implication must now be interpreted.
- **Sentence 8 (Verdict / synthesis — clinical consequence):** "This means that there is 'the existence of mild iodine deficiency [in Hong Kong], which can result in clinical IDD'" — closes the loop by fusing S1–S5 (seaweed is rich) with S7 (people are deficient) into a single diagnostic claim.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Establish a nutritional anomaly in a defined source** (seaweed contains excess iodine) — sets the stage with a *paradox to be resolved*.
2. **Quantify the anomaly with primary data** (µg/kg figure) — converts the claim into measurable terms.
3. **Translate the raw number into intuitive units** (grams/kg) — bridges data to reader comprehension.
4. **Project the figure onto human consumption** (daily intake comparison) — anchors the data in lived behaviour.
5. **Signal a pivot** ("However") — breaks the anticipated conclusion and opens a contradiction.
6. **Introduce counter-evidence about the population** (low actual intake) — sets up the tension.
7. **Synthesize both halves into a single diagnosis** (deficiency exists) — resolves the paradox by showing that the *availability* of iodine in seaweed does not equal *consumption*.

**Why this order works:** the reader must first see that a high-iodine source exists before they can be surprised that a population is still deficient. The pivot only has force if S1–S5 are accepted; the synthesis only has weight if S7 stands. Each move creates the precondition for the next.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A — "Source Authority → Quantification → Implication"**
1. **What each slot holds:**
   - Slot 1: Institutional/named authority + qualitative claim (subject + verb + adjective).
   - Slot 2: Same authority, raw numeric figure with unit (noun phrase + unit).
   - Slot 3: Conversional re-statement of the figure in lay terms + evaluative adjective.
   - Slot 4: Real-world projection linking the figure to a daily/intake benchmark (conditional clause + comparison verb).
2. **How to fill it with a different idea:** Pick a nutrient/compound with a known concentration in a specific food; cite a government/regulatory body that measured it; quote the concentration with its SI unit; convert to grams or milligrams per kg; state what human daily consumption would imply against a WHO/RDA threshold.
3. **Original filled version:** "According to a study conducted by Hong Kong's Centre of Food Safety, 'most seaweed and seaweed products contained very high iodine levels' … 'the mean level of dried kelp was 2 600 000 µg/kg' … 'which translates to 26 grams/kg … even a small amount of kombu consumed per day would meet or exceed the recommended daily limits'."
4. **Demonstration fill (different idea — lead in dark chocolate):** "According to a study by the US FDA, 'dark chocolate bars contained measurable lead levels.' The mean concentration of lead in tested bars was 0.8 µg/g, which translates to 0.8 mg/kg. Even a 50 g serving consumed daily by a child would approach 40% of the tolerable weekly intake set by the WHO."

**SKELETON B — "Pivot → Counter-Evidence → Diagnostic Synthesis"**
1. **What each slot holds:**
   - Slot 1: Single-word transition ("However") signalling contradiction.
   - Slot 2: Quoted population-level statistic showing the *opposite* of what S1–S5 implied (subject + percentage + qualifier + unit).
   - Slot 3: Interpretive sentence ("This means…") that names a clinical/diagnostic state and its consequence (existential claim + relative clause).
2. **How to fill it with a different idea:** Use the pivot to show that despite high availability of a substance, a population does not actually consume enough; cite a survey/percentage; conclude with a named deficiency and its medical consequence.
3. **Original filled version:** "However, it was found that 'around 59% of the population [in Hong Kong] intakes below 50µg [of iodine]/day' … 'This means that there is the existence of mild iodine deficiency … which can result in clinical IDD'."
4. **Demonstration fill (different idea — vitamin D in Nordic populations):** "However, it was found that 'around 65% of the population in Sweden consumes below the EAR of 10 µg vitamin D/day.' This means that there is the existence of subclinical vitamin D insufficiency, which can result in impaired bone mineralisation in adolescents."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Authority/evidence handling:** "According to a study conducted by" (S1) — frames a claim by attributing it to a named body.
- **Quoted evidence integration:** opening quote mark after a reporting clause (S2, S3, S7) — embeds primary data inside the sentence.
- **Sequencing / chain connector:** "Furthermore" (between S1 and S2) — adds supporting data on the same axis.
- **Translation/unpacking verb:** "which translates to" (S4) — converts raw data into a comprehensible scale.
- **Evaluative intensifier:** "a very high amount" (S4) — subjectively grades the unpacked figure.
- **Implication / projection:** "would meet or exceed the recommended daily limits" (S5) — forecasts real-world impact against a benchmark.
- **Contrast / concession pivot:** "However" (S6) — single-word signaller that the prior direction is about to invert.
- **Reporting verb for counter-evidence:** "it was found that" (S7) — distances the writer from the surprising statistic while presenting it.
- **Synthesis verb:** "This means that" (S8) — explicitly tells the reader the two strands of evidence are about to be fused.
- **Clinical/diagnostic nominalisation:** "the existence of mild iodine deficiency" (S8) — turns a statistical pattern into a named condition.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The section relies on a **Paradox-Resolution pattern**: build a high-availability claim, quantify it, project it onto human behaviour, then pivot to show the population still falls short, and finally diagnose the gap as a named condition.

Step-by-step to reproduce with a new idea:

1. **Name an authority and a qualitative claim** about a food/substance containing high levels of a nutrient/compound (use "According to [institution]…").
2. **Quote a raw concentration figure** in SI units (µg/kg, mg/L, ppm) from that authority.
3. **Convert the figure into lay units** (grams, milligrams, % of a benchmark) so the magnitude is felt.
4. **Project onto daily human consumption**, comparing against a WHO/RDA threshold and using a modal verb ("would meet or exceed").
5. **Insert the pivot word "However"** as a standalone short sentence to break the implication.
6. **Quote a population-level statistic** showing that actual intake/levels are *below* a defined threshold, even though the source is abundant.
7. **Fuse both strands** with "This means that there is the existence of [named condition], which can result in [clinical consequence]."
