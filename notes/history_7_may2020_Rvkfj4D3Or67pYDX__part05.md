## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **cause-explanation paragraph** in a historiographical essay. The ordered moves are:

1. **Cause-claim** — name the additional cause and its actor (sets up everything).
2. **Historical action** — show the actor *doing* something concrete with a date (makes the cause tangible).
3. **Authority citation** — validate the cause's centrality with a historian's interpretation (turns action into a causal argument).
4. **Mechanism chain** — explain *how* the cause operated economically via linked factors (Civil War demand → Reciprocity Treaty) (proves the principle just cited).
5. **Quantitative evidence** — measure the mechanism's effect (statistic + comparison) (gives the mechanism weight).
6. **Pivot to parallel dimension** — open a new sub-cause (labour) using the same historian-plus-percentage formula (keeps the argument building).

**Why this order:** each move is the logical answer to the question the previous move raised. The claim demands action; the action demands authority; the authority demands a mechanism; the mechanism demands a number; the number opens a new angle. A student replicating this on another topic (e.g., causes of the Cuban missile crisis, causes of WWI) should: identify the cause → show it acting → cite a historian → explain the mechanism → quantify it → pivot to a second sub-cause.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

### SKELETON A — Cause Claim + Historical Action
**Template:** "[Additional/Another] [long-term/short-term] cause for [event] was the role of [actor group] in [place]; these [actor group] [action verb] in [year], [gerund phrase] with the explicit purpose of [outcome]."

- **What each slot holds:** Slot 1 = transitional phrase + adjective marking this as *one of several* causes (noun phrase); Slot 2 = the cause itself (noun phrase); Slot 3 = historical action in past tense with date; Slot 4 = purpose clause (gerund or infinitive).
- **How to fill with a different idea:** Slot 1: mark the cause as "additional" only if the essay already named another cause first. Slot 2: name an economic or political interest group (e.g., "oil interests in the Persian Gulf"). Slot 3: pick a dated overthrow/annexation/declaration event. Slot 4: state the destination political outcome the actors wanted.
- **Original fill:** *"An additional long-term cause for the annexation was the role of American sugar interests in Hawaii; these business leaders overthrew the monarchy in 1893, creating the Hawaiian Republic with the explicit purpose of United States annexation."*
- **Demo fill (different subject):** "An additional long-term cause for the 1953 coup was the role of British oil interests in Iran; these industrialists supported the removal of Mosaddegh in August 1953, coordinating operations with the explicit purpose of restoring the Shah's authoritarian rule."

---

### SKELETON B — Authority Citation + Interpretive Principle
**Template:** "Historian [Name] argues that '[phrase X] and [phrase Y] were never separate [noun]s.'"

- **What each slot holds:** historian surname (proper noun); quoted principle uses two parallel nouns linked by "and"; both nouns are bound to "never separate" to imply entanglement.
- **How to fill:** pick a credible historian on the topic; state a *bilateral* principle (two domains that the historian says were fused); use a "never separate" formulation to signal that the essay will demonstrate their linkage.
- **Original fill:** *"Historian Edward Beechert argues that 'the question of sovereignty and the welfare of the sugar industry were never separate questions.'"*
- **Demo fill (different subject):** *"Historian Ervand Abrahamian argues that 'the question of British oil concessions and Iranian political sovereignty were never separate questions.'"*

---

### SKELETON C — Mechanism Chain (Two Linked Causes)
**Template:** "[Subject] flourished in the decades after [trigger event]; the [factor 1] [verb of increase], and the [date] [policy/law name] [effect 2]."

- **What each slot holds:** Slot 1 = the industry/institution that grew; Slot 2 = an external trigger event; Slot 3 = a demand-side factor in noun form; Slot 4 = a supply-side policy named with a date.
- **How to fill:** pair a *demand shock* with a *supply-side enabling policy*; the semicolon links them as joint causes; both clauses must have verbs in past tense showing growth/enablement.
- **Original fill:** *"Sugar plantations flourished in the decades after the Civil War; the economic collapse of the American South increased demand, and the 1875 Reciprocity Treaty lifted tariffs for all United States sugar imports from Hawaii."*
- **Demo fill (different subject):** *"Oil extraction flourished in the decades after the Second World War; the devastation of European refining increased the global appetite for Middle Eastern crude, and the 1954 consortium agreement granted Western companies controlling shares of Iranian production."*

---

### SKELETON D — Quantified Evidence (Scale Proof)
**Template:** "By [end-year], [subject] had reached [number + unit], [comparison phrase: over X times its value/baseline]."

- **What each slot holds:** comparative end-date (noun phrase); subject (noun); large number with unit; ratio comparison to a baseline year/quantity.
- **How to fill:** always pair a *terminal year* with an *earlier baseline year*; the ratio ("ten times," "fivefold") must exceed the baseline by a memorable magnitude; use "had reached" + past perfect to mark a benchmark reached before the analysis date.
- **Original fill:** *"By 1890, Hawaiian sugar exports had reached 225 million pounds, over ten times its value in 1876."*
- **Demo fill (different subject):** *"By 1955, Iranian oil exports had reached 600 million barrels, over four times its value in 1940."*

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / ordering cause among others:**
- *"An additional long-term cause for the annexation was..."* — marks this as a second or third cause, not the first.
- *"By 1890..."* — marks the terminal date of the statistical benchmark.

**Cause / consequence (linking factors to effects):**
- *"the economic collapse of the American South increased demand"* — direct cause→effect verb chain.
- *"the 1875 Reciprocity Treaty lifted tariffs"* — policy→consequence ("lifted" = removed barrier).
- *"Sugar plantations flourished in the decades after..."* — temporal flourish verb establishing growth.

**Contrast / concession:** (none in this section — it builds linearly, no counter-moves).

**Specification / precision:**
- *"the explicit purpose of United States annexation"* — narrows motivation to one targeted outcome.
- *"over ten times its value in 1876"* — quantifies the comparison exactly.

**Evidence handling (authority + source):**
- *"Historian Edward Beechert argues that..."* — introduces a named scholarly voice with "argues."
- *"Stephen Kinzer argues that..."* — parallel structure, second authority.
- *"the question of sovereignty and the welfare of the sugar industry were never separate questions"* — the cited interpretive principle.

**Explanation / unpacking verbs:**
- *"the role of American sugar interests in Hawaii"* — names the cause via "role of."
- *"creating the Hawaiian Republic"* — gerund phrase unpacking the overthrow's outcome.
- *"The labour behind these sugar plantations were from..."* — locates the human agent ("behind" = the supply source).

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern this section relies on is: **Cause-claim → Historical Action → Authority Validation → Mechanism Chain → Statistical Proof → Parallel Pivot.** The student is not defining, comparing, or calculating — they are *building a layered causal argument*, one where each sentence either *substantiates* the sentence before or *opens* a new angle on the same cause.

**Replication steps to explain a NEW idea with the same pattern:**

1. **State the cause and its actor in one sentence.** Use a transition word ("An additional...," "Another...") if it is one of several causes. Name the interest group or institutional actor. *(Slot: "An additional long-term cause for X was the role of Y.")*

2. **Embed a dated historical action showing the actor doing something.** Use past tense, a specific year, and a gerund phrase naming the political outcome they sought. *(Slot: "these actors [verb] in [year], [gerund outcome] with the explicit purpose of [goal].")*

3. **Cite a historian whose interpretive principle ties the cause to the broader event.** Use "Historian [Name] argues that..." and a quoted sentence containing two parallel domains linked by "and" + "never separate." *(Slot: historian + bilateral entanglement principle.)*

4. **Translate the principle into a concrete mechanism.** Link two factors with a semicolon: a *demand shock* (verb of increase) and a *policy enabler* (named treaty/law + lifting/enabling verb). *(Slot: mechanism chain with semicolon.)*

5. **Prove the mechanism's scale with a statistic.** Use "By [year], [subject] had reached [large number], over [ratio] times its value in [baseline year]." *(Slot: past perfect + magnitude comparison.)*

6. **Pivot to a parallel sub-cause (labour, race, class, geography) using the same historian-plus-percentage formula.** Even if the sentence trails off, the move is: name the human/material substrate + "argues" + a population percentage. *(Slot: pivot to new dimension via second authority.)*

The rhythm that emerges: **claim → event → scholar → mechanism → number → new scholar on new dimension.** A student replicating this on any cause essay (Russian Revolution, decolonisation in Africa, Arab–Israeli conflict) should write six sentences hitting each of these slots in order, using semicolons within moves 1 and 4 to chain sub-clauses.
