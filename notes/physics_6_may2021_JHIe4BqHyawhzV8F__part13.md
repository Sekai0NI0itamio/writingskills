## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **results-and-discussion** section. The ordered moves are:

1. **Quantitative anchor** (one striking numerical drop) — establishes the effect *exists* before anything else.
2. **Initial verdict** on that anchor — tells the reader what to take from the data so the following graph is read with purpose.
3. **Graph introduction** (title/label) — signals a visual evidence source.
4. **Description of graph variables** — orients the reader to what is being compared.
5. **Trend claim** (linearity) — names the *type* of relationship.
6. **Directional restatement** — converts the abstract trend into a cause-effect direction.
7. **Statistical strength** (R²) — validates the trend quantitatively.
8. **Hypothesis verdict** — ties the validated trend back to the investigation's original prediction.
9. **Supporting-source introduction** (secondary plot) — broadens the evidence base beyond own data.
10. **Range specification** — pins the secondary plot to the experimental range.
11. **Paired comparison** (property A vs. property B) — uses contrast to surface nuance.
12. **Implication** (begun with "This means") — to integrate the secondary observations into the main argument.

The **why** of this order: a single number convinces, a graph shows *behaviour*, a statistic proves it, a hypothesis verdict closes the loop, and a supporting plot contextualises within real-world limits. Each move answers the implicit question left by the previous one.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

### Skeleton A — "Numerical drop → verdict"
SKELETON: *"**[Source] reveals that [quantity] under [condition A] ([value]) would [change verb] to [value] when [condition B] is applied.** This proves that **[general claim about effect].**"*

- **Slot 1** (data claim): present-tense verb ("reveals", "shows", "indicates"), a baseline value, a contrasting value, and the condition that causes the shift. *Shape:* declarative with embedded numeric clause.
- **Slot 2** (verdict): demonstrative pronoun + assertion verb ("proves", "demonstrates", "confirms") + a general statement about why the shift matters.
- **Fill with different idea:** Slot 1 — pick a measurable quantity that visibly drops/rises when a single variable changes (e.g. reaction rate when catalyst added). Slot 2 — state the real-world consequence.
- **Original filled version:** *"Table 2 reveals that the dynamic coefficient between two dry aluminum surfaces (1.422) would drop to 0.85 when engine oil is applied… This proves that lubrificantes do make the movement easier in an engine."*
- **Demonstration fill:** *"Graph 4 reveals that the time taken for potassium iodate to decolourise under room-temperature conditions (180 s) would fall to 42 s when a starch indicator is introduced. This proves that visual indicators do accelerate the perception of reaction completion in titration experiments."*

### Skeleton B — "Graph description → trend type → mechanism"
SKELETON: *"**[Graph label] shows the relationship between [variable A] and [variable B].** [Authority phrase], [variable B] is affected by [variable A] and this relationship is [type: linear / exponential / inverse]. **[As/When] [variable A] [verb of change], [variable B] [verb of opposite change].**"*

- **Slot 1** (intro): proper-noun figure label + "shows" + the two axes named.
- **Slot 2** (claim + classification): "According to" or "From the graph" + a passive verb ("is affected by") + an adjective ("linear", "positive", "non-linear").
- **Slot 3** (directional restatement): "As X increases/decreases, Y decreases/increases" — same direction logic but re-cast as a mechanism.
- **Fill with different idea:** Slot 1 — pick two measured variables. Slot 2 — pick a relationship shape that the data actually supports. Slot 3 — restate in plain cause-effect.
- **Original filled version:** *"Graph 1 shows the relationship between the experimental values of Temperature…and the calculated coefficient of dynamic friction. According to the graph, the dynamic coefficient…is affected by the Temperature…and this relationship is linear. As the temperature of the engine oil increases the dynamic coefficient of friction decreases."*
- **Demonstration fill:** *"Graph 3 shows the relationship between the concentration of sodium chloride solution and the voltage recorded by the voltmeter. From the graph, the voltage is affected by the salt concentration and this relationship is non-linear. As the salt concentration rises, the voltage output rises at a decreasing rate."*

### Skeleton C — "Statistical strength → hypothesis verdict"
SKELETON: *"**In this [graph/data], the [statistic] is [value], which suggests that [interpretation of strength].** [Transition phrase] the values presented, the hypothesis [verdict: proved / disproved / partially supported].**"*

- **Slot 1** (statistic): present-tense locator ("In this") + named statistic (R², p-value, standard deviation) + value + "which suggests" + qualitative strength.
- **Slot 2** (verdict): "Taking into account" / "Considering" + reference back to evidence + blunt hypothesis outcome.
- **Fill with different idea:** Slot 1 — pick a statistic your data yielded. Slot 2 — declare a clean yes/no on the original prediction.
- **Original filled version:** *"In this graph, the regression coefficient is R2=0.962, which suggests that the correlation of data with the trendline is very strong. Taking into account the values presented in the graph, the hypothesis states proved to be wrong."*
- **Demonstration fill:** *"In this dataset, the p-value of the t-test is 0.012, which suggests that the difference between the two means is statistically significant. Considering the means presented in the table, the hypothesis that the catalyst has no effect is rejected."*

### Skeleton D — "Supporting source → range zoom → paired contrast"
SKELETON: *"**[Source] visualizes [property] belonging to [category] (the same as [link back]).** By studying the range in which [context] occurs, specifically [range], a few conclusions can be made. **[Property A] is seen to [verb] as [variable] [verb], whereas [Property B] [different verb] [same/other direction].**"*

- **Slot 1** (source intro): "The plot/figure/chart above visualizes" + a noun phrase + a parenthetical link to the experiment's own materials.
- **Slot 2** (range zoom): "By studying…specifically" + numeric or categorical range.
- **Slot 3** (paired contrast): "whereas" forces two parallel behaviours.
- **Fill with different idea:** Slot 1 — a third-party plot describing the materials. Slot 2 — the slice relevant to your own experiment. Slot 3 — two properties that diverge in behaviour.
- **Original filled version:** *"The plot above visualizes engine oil characteristics that belong to the category 15W-40 (the same as the lubricant used in this experiment). By studying the range of this plot in which the experiment will occur, specifically the range from 20°C to 55°C, a few conclusions can be made. The kinematic viscosity is seen to drop sharply…whereas the density of the oil falls linearly…"*
- **Demonstration fill:** *"The chart above visualises the absorbance spectra belonging to the chlorophyll-a standard (the same as the pigment used in this extraction). By studying the wavelength range in which the spectrophotometer operates, specifically 400–700 nm, a few conclusions can be made. The absorbance is seen to peak sharply at 430 nm, whereas the transmittance rises gradually across the same range."*

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section runs a **"numeric proof → graphical proof → statistical proof → hypothesis verdict → supporting-source comparison"** pattern. Steps to replicate it on a new idea:

1. **Open with one striking numerical comparison** — pick a single before/after pair (control vs. condition) so the reader instantly sees that *something* changed. Use "Source reveals that X…would shift to Y when Z is applied."
2. **Deliver the verdict on that single comparison** — use "This proves/demonstrates that…" to convert the data into a one-line general claim the rest of the section will now support.
3. **Introduce a graph/figure and name the two axes** — let the figure's title do the work; the sentence merely says what is plotted.
4. **State the trend and classify its shape** — "According to the graph, [dependent] is affected by [independent] and this relationship is [linear / exponential / inverse]."
5. **Restate the trend as plain cause-effect** — "As X increases, Y decreases." (Same logic, different grammatical surface.)
6. **Validate with a statistic** — name R², p-value, or chi-square and interpret with "which suggests that the correlation/effect is very strong / significant."
7. **Issue a clean hypothesis verdict** — "Taking into account the values presented, the hypothesis [proved true / proved to be wrong / was partially supported]."
8. **Introduce a secondary supporting source** — a third-party plot/table that describes the *materials* themselves.
9. **Zoom to your experimental slice** — "specifically the range from A to B."
10. **Pair-compare two properties of that source** — use "whereas" so the reader sees two behaviours side-by-side.
11. **Open an implication clause** — "This means…" to integrate the secondary observations with the primary argument (the cut-off sentence in the original is exactly this move).
