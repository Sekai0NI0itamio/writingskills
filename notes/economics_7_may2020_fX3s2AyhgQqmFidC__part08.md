## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Diagram explanation** — establishes the baseline model with quantified outcomes (price, quantity, revenue).
2. **Critical limitation** — questions the model's assumptions and data gaps, acknowledging uncertainty.
3. **Positive impacts** — presents benefits to producers and government, linking to broader economic context.
4. **Negative impacts** — presents costs to consumers, emphasizing distributional consequences.

The order moves from **mechanical description** to **critical reflection**, then to **balanced evaluation** (pros then cons). Each move sets up the next: the diagram needs critique, the critique leads to weighing benefits and costs, and the weighing reveals winners and losers.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern: Mechanism Chain with Quantified Outcomes**

1. **Establish the baseline** — describe the initial state using a visual or data reference, naming key variables (price, quantity, equilibrium).
2. **Introduce the intervention** — state the policy or change and explain the mechanism by which it operates (e.g., tariff raises price, subsidy lowers cost).
3. **Trace the causal chain** — follow the logic step by step: intervention → price change → quantity change → distributional effects, using "as a result" or "due to" to link each step.
4. **Quantify the outcomes** — provide specific numbers, formulas, or ranges for each affected variable (e.g., revenue = (Pt − Pw)(Q3 − Q2)).
5. **Identify winners and losers** — name which groups benefit and which are harmed, explaining why based on the mechanism.
6. **Acknowledge limitations** — note what the model simplifies or what data is missing, using "however" or "especially since" to introduce critical perspective.
7. **Evaluate net impact** — weigh the positive and negative consequences, concluding with a qualified verdict that reflects uncertainty.
