## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1** (one analytical block following figure captions)

1. **Claim + specification** — "Figures 30 and 31 show increasing EIA score with distance from point 1, which in this instance is the center of the CBD." Hands the reader to the next sentence via a *because*-hook: the next line must justify the observed trend.
2. **Evidence / mechanism** — "This is because of the overall positive trend of both charts." Hands the reader by *contrast pivot*: having established the general pattern, the writer now flags what breaks it.
3. **Contrast / exception flag** — "However, the bars in the red box (fig.31) show values different from the rest, anomalous results that don't fit with the general increasing trend." Hands the reader via *logical consequence*: an anomaly + a stated trend demand quantification, so the next move introduces a test.
4. **Method transition** — "To see the strength of the trends Spearman's Rank was conducted for both." Hands the reader by *promise-of-result*: "conducted" obligates the writer to report what came out.
5. **Evidence (numerical report)** — "It was found that the R-values for OR and JE were 0.85 and 0.61, respectively." Hands the reader via *interpretive obligation*: raw numbers require meaning.
6. **Unpack / mechanism** — "Because these values are positive, the correlations are positive." Hands the reader via *elaboration cue* ("Furthermore"): the sign has been decoded, now the strength must be graded.
7. **Specification + threshold** — "Furthermore, values above 0.7 are generally considered strong, showing that the correlation was strong positive for OR and weak positive for JE." Hands the reader via *sensitivity pivot*: having graded the data, the writer now tests how the anomaly affected the grade.
8. **Hypothetical / counterfactual** — "Had the anomalous result not been included for JE, R = 0.75, therefore a strong correlation." Hands the reader via *causal reflection marker* ("Reflecting"): the counterfactual demands an explanation of the anomaly's real-world cause.
9. **Cause explanation** — "Reflecting, the EIA scores for those 4 points had been collected whilst inside due to rain, therefore lower perceptions of the EIA factors, leading to an overall lower score." Hands the reader by *synthesis pivot*: the anomaly is now resolved, so the paragraph can return to the main finding.
10. **Verdict / implication** — "With or without anomalous points, the EIA score increases with distance from the CBD in both OR and JE and hence H2 is supported by this evidence, as EI decreases with distance from the CBD." Closes the paragraph by linking evidence back to the hypothesis.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **data-analysis / hypothesis-evaluation** section. The ordered moves are:

1. **State the visible pattern from the charts** (sets up the claim that will be tested).
2. **Ground the claim in the overall trend** (justifies the observation rather than asserting it).
3. **Flag anomalies that break the trend** (creates the analytical tension that justifies a test).
4. **Introduce a statistical test to quantify the trend** (escalates from visual to numerical evidence).
5. **Report the numerical outputs explicitly with site labels** (delivers on the test's promise).
6. **Decode the sign of the coefficient** (translates raw numbers into a directional statement).
7. **Apply a published threshold to grade the strength** (turns a number into a category).
8. **Run a sensitivity / counterfactual on the anomaly** (tests robustness — would the conclusion change?).
9. **Causally explain the anomaly in real-world terms** (reflective move that defends the data, not just the maths).
10. **Synthesise back to the hypothesis, confirming or rejecting it** (verdict linking evidence to theory).

The order matters because each move *earns the right* to the next: you cannot grade a strength (7) before reporting the value (5); you cannot explain the anomaly (9) before hypothesising its removal (8); you cannot verdict (10) before both the main pattern and the anomaly are reconciled.

- **Slot holds / grammar:** a compound figure citation (noun phrase) + present-tense reporting verb + trend direction (rising/falling) + distance/time variable + appositive clause defining the variable.
- **How to fill with a different idea:** pick two comparable datasets (e.g. two transects, two time periods, two sites); identify a variable that increases or decreases; explicitly define what "Point 1" or equivalent starting point means in your study.
- **Original filled:** "Figures 30 and 31 show increasing EIA score with distance from point 1, which in this instance is the center of the CBD."

Because these values are [sign], the correlations are [sign]. Furthermore, values [above/below] [threshold] are generally considered [grade], showing that the correlation was [grade] [sign] for [site A] and [grade] [sign] for [site B].`
- **Slot holds / grammar:** purpose clause (infinitive) + past passive reporting verb + parenthetical listing of values + sign-decoding sentence + threshold-application sentence ending in a comparative grade for each site.
- **How to fill with a different idea:** pick a bivariate relationship; choose the appropriate test (Spearman, Pearson, chi-square); find or assign a literature threshold for "strong" vs "weak"; report two contrasting values so the threshold produces *different grades* (this is the analytical point).

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing:** "Furthermore" — *Furthermore, values above 0.7 are generally considered strong…*
- **Cause / consequence:** "This is because of" — *This is because of the overall positive trend…*; "Because these values are positive, the correlations are positive" (sign decoding); "therefore a strong correlation"; "therefore lower perceptions of the EIA factors"; "leading to an overall lower score"; "hence H2 is supported by this evidence".
- **Contrast / concession:** "However" — *However, the bars in the red box (fig.31) show values different…*; "Had the anomalous result not been included" (counterfactual concession); "With or without anomalous points" (concessive synthesis).
- **Specification:** "which in this instance is the center of the CBD" (appositive definition); "respectively" — *the R-values for OR and JE were 0.85 and 0.61, respectively*.
- **Evidence handling:** "Figures 30 and 31 show" (visual evidence); "It was found that" — *It was found that the R-values…*; "are generally considered" (authority/threshold evidence).
- **Explanation verbs:** "show" (reporting), "conducted" (test introduced), "considered" (grading), "collected" (data origin), "Reflecting" (interpretive move).

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern name:** *Trend-claim → justification → anomaly flag → statistical test → numerical report → sign decode → threshold grade → counterfactual → causal reflection → hypothesis verdict.*

Steps to replicate with a NEW idea:

1. **Open with a trend claim tied to two figures.** Use "Figures X and Y show [directional pattern] with [variable] from point 1, which in this instance is [definition]." This anchors the analysis in visual evidence before any numbers appear.
2. **Justify the claim.** Add "This is because of the overall positive trend of both charts." The reader must see *why* you are claiming a trend, not just that you do.
3. **Flag the anomaly with "However".** Point to specific bars/points (a "red box", a circled point) that break the trend. The anomaly is the engine of the analysis; without it, no test is needed.
4. **Introduce the statistical test.** "To see the strength of the trends [Spearman's Rank / Pearson's r / etc.] was conducted for both." Use the infinitive-of-purpose construction to justify the method.
5. **Report values explicitly per site, using "respectively".** Naming both values side-by-side sets up the comparative grading in the next move.
6. **Decode the sign first.** "Because these values are [positive/negative], the correlations are [positive/negative]." Separate sign from strength.
7. **Apply a literature threshold to grade each site differently.** "Furthermore, values above [X] are generally considered [strong], showing that the correlation was [grade] for [site A] and [grade] for [site B]." The *difference* between sites is the analytical payoff.
8. **Run a counterfactual on the anomaly.** "Had the anomalous result not been included for [site], [statistic] = [new value], therefore [re-classified grade]." This tests whether the anomaly drove the grade.
9. **Causally explain the anomaly in fieldwork terms.** "Reflecting, the [data] for those [n] points had been collected [under bad condition], therefore [weaker/stronger reading], leading to an overall [adjusted] score." This defends the data, not just the maths.
10. **Close with a synthesis verdict linking back to the hypothesis number.** Use "With or without anomalous points" to show robustness, "hence H# is supported by this evidence" to tie evidence to theory, and a trailing "as [mechanism] [consequence]" clause to state the underlying geographical/biological principle.
