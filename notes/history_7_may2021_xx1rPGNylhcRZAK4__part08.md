## What This Section Does (content sequence)
1. **Identify author(s)** – establishes who produced the work, allowing the reader to locate the source by name.  
2. **State the title** – specifies the exact work being cited, distinguishing it from other works by the same author.  
3. **Give publication details** (place, publisher, year / journal, volume, issue, pages) – provides the bibliographic data needed to find the source in a library or database.  
4. **Add access information** (DOI, URL, or database link) – supplies a direct retrieval path for online material.  
This order moves from the most general identifier (author) to the most specific locator (URL), ensuring each step builds the information necessary for the next, so another student can replicate the sequence for any topic by following the same four‑step template.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON 1 – Book entry**  
`[Author Last Name], [Author First Name]. [Book Title: Subtitle]. [Place of Publication]: [Publisher], [Year].`  
1. **Slot meanings** – Author Last Name (proper noun, capitalised), Author First Name (proper noun), Book Title (title case, may include subtitle after colon), Place of Publication (city name), Publisher (name), Year (four‑digit number).  
2. **How to fill with a different idea** – Choose a book you have read; write the author’s surname then given name, italicise the title (or use title case), note the city of publication, the publisher’s name, and the year of publication.  
3. **Original filled version** – Avrich, Paul. Kronstadt, 1921. New Jersey: Princeton University Press, 1970.  
4. **Demonstration fill** – Smith, John. The History of Ancient Rome: From Republic to Empire. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 2015.  

**SKELETON 2 – Journal article entry**  
`[Author Last Name], [Author First Name]. “[Article Title].” [Journal Name] [Volume], no. [Issue] ([Year]): [Page Range]. [DOI or URL].`  
1. **Slot meanings** – Author Last Name, Author First Name, Article Title (in quotation marks), Journal Name (italicised), Volume (number), Issue (number), Year (in parentheses), Page Range (first–last page), DOI or URL (starting with https://doi.org/ or http://).  
2. **How to fill with a different idea** – Select a journal article; list the authors, put the article title in quotes, give the journal name, volume and issue numbers, year in parentheses, page range, and finally the DOI or stable URL.  
3. **Original filled version** – Daniels, Robert. “The Kronstadt Revolt of 1921: A Study in the Dynamics of Revolution.” The American Slavic and East European Review 10, no. 4 (1951): 241-254. https://www.jstor.org/stable/2492031.  
4. **Demonstration fill** – Liu, Mei. “Climate Change Adaptation in Coastal Cities.” Journal of Urban Planning 45, no. 2 (2022): 112-130. https://doi.org/10.1080/jup.2022.045.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)
**Pattern identified:** author → title → publication details → access information (the standard reference‑citation sequence).  
**Step‑by‑step instructions to explain a new idea using this pattern:**  
1. **Name the creator(s)** – write the author’s surname followed by given name(s) exactly as they appear on the work.  
2. **Present the full title** – place the title in quotation marks for articles or italics for books; include any subtitle after a colon.  
3. **Supply the publication data** – for books: city of publication, publisher, year; for articles: journal name, volume, issue, year in parentheses, and page range.  
4. **Add retrieval information** – if the source is online, append a DOI or stable URL; otherwise omit this step.  
Following these steps yields a complete, replicable reference that moves the reader from identification to location in a logical, orderly fashion.
