## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **research-question framing + methodology + verdict** section. The sequence is:

1. **Establish prior context** (what existed before) — sets baseline the reader needs to understand disruption.
2. **Introduce disruption/pivot event** — creates the tension that needs resolving.
3. **Reframe disruption as opportunity with evidence** — converts problem into research-worthy angle.
4. **Formalize as a research question** — locks the angle into an answerable form.
5. **Announce primary data source** — establishes credibility first (insider voices).
6. **Add secondary data source** — broadens validity (customer-side voices).
7. **Enumerate scenarios/options considered** — shows thoroughness before tool selection.
8. **Name analytical frameworks** — signals the lenses that will structure the analysis.
9. **State dual-party verdict** — previews the symmetric benefit.
10. **Issue one-sided recommendation** — converts symmetry into actionable duty.

**Why this order works**: context → disruption → opportunity → question establishes *why the study matters*; then primary → secondary → options → frameworks builds methodological credibility from most authoritative to most analytical; verdict → recommendation pivots from analysis to action. Each move *earns* the next move's right to exist.

Slot 4: turn actor + gap into a "Should [actor] + [action] as [strategy]?" question.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section relies on the **"Disruption → Reframe → Question → Methodology Stack → Verdict → Recommendation"** pattern — a *pivot-then-prove* structure.

**Step-by-step replication for a NEW idea:**

1. **Establish a steady state in one temporal clause** ("until [event] set [consequence]"). Give the reader the baseline your topic disrupts.
2. **Pivot with "However"** to reframe the disruption as an opening for a specific actor. The word "opportunity" or "opening" should appear.
3. **Ground the opportunity with one piece of evidence** introduced by "due to" — a measurable decline, gap, or unmet need in the wider population.
4. **Formalize the angle as a quoted research question** beginning with "Should [actor] + [verb] + [object] as a [strategy]?" — this locks the reader into expecting an answer.
5. **Announce the most authoritative data source first** with passive "Primary research was conducted using [method] with [sample]."
6. **Add a wider-voice source** with a second passive clause and a "to + purpose verb" tail that names the *function* of that data.
7. **Enumerate competing scenarios** with a colon, listing three structural alternatives separated by commas and "or."
8. **Name the analytical tools** in a parallel list of three proper-noun frameworks, framed by "To analyse and optimise [decision]... were used."
9. **Close with a symmetric verdict** ("both [A] and [B] can benefit from each other") that signals fairness of outcome.
10. **Convert symmetry into asymmetry** in the final sentence by naming only one party and using "should" + an action verb — assigning duty to the actor with agency.

The rhythm is: **short setup → concessive pivot → evidence → question → 4-sentence method block → 2-sentence verdict-recommendation**. Match sentence counts (4-4-2) to preserve pacing.
