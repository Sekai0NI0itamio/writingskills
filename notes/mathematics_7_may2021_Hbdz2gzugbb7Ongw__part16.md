## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Present the baseline dynamics** – “Behaviour of standard double pendulum” gives a reference point for all later observations.  
2. **Introduce systematic parameter perturbations** – each subsequent paragraph varies one physical quantity (initial angles, angular velocities, masses, lengths) by a defined amount (5 % increase or “small” initial condition).  
3. **Record the effect on each angular coordinate** – for every perturbation the section lists “(a) Behaviour of θ1” and “(b) Behaviour of θ2”, isolating the response of the two degrees of freedom.  
4. **Offer limiting‑case approximations** – the final two paragraphs present simplified models (“single pendulum small angle” and “double pendulum large angle”) and again detail the behaviour of θ1 and θ2, allowing direct comparison with the fully coupled system.  

**Why this order?**  
- The baseline establishes a common reference, so the reader can judge the impact of each change.  
- Parameter changes are presented one‑at‑a‑time, which isolates the influence of each variable—essential for a methodical investigation.  
- Listing the response of each angle for every case creates a consistent data matrix that can be compared across variations.  
- Concluding with approximations links the empirical observations to theoretical simplifications, reinforcing the physical insight.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern:** *Baseline → Parameter variation → Component‑wise observation → Limiting‑case approximation*  

**Step‑by‑step instructions**

1. **State the baseline case** – give a short title that names the system in its unmodified form (e.g., “Behaviour of standard double pendulum”).  
2. **Introduce the specific change** – write a title that includes the parameter and the magnitude of the change (e.g., “Behaviour after 5% increase in θ10”).  
3. **Report the effect on each degree of freedom** – for the modified case, list two sub‑points: (a) the behaviour of the first variable (θ1) and (b) the behaviour of the second variable (θ2). Keep the wording identical across paragraphs to maintain a consistent matrix.  
4. **Offer a limiting‑case approximation** – create a final pair of paragraphs that present a simplified model (e.g., “Approximation to single pendulum: small angle”) and again detail the behaviour of θ1 and θ2.  
5. **Close with a concise summary** – briefly note how the parameter or approximation alters the overall dynamics relative to the baseline (this can be a single sentence outside the figure captions if needed).

By following this five‑step sequence, any student can replicate the logical progression observed in the section, regardless of the physical system under investigation.
