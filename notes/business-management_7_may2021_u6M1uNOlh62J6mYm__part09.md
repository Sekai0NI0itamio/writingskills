## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **diagnostic → intervention** mini-section. The ordered moves are:

1. **State the puzzling result** (e.g., a notable % who answered "none"). *Why first:* anchors the reader in a specific anomaly that the section will explain.
2. **Frame the diagnostic tool** (e.g., "We will use the positioning map in an attempt to answer…"). *Why second:* tells the reader HOW the anomaly will be resolved.
3. **Display the visual evidence** (Figure 7). *Why third:* lets the reader see the raw positioning before interpretation.
4. **Read the gap from the visual** (e.g., "a lack of 'X' and 'Y' sports is evidently missing"). *Why fourth:* converts picture into finding.
5. **Propose an intervention grounded in the gap** (e.g., introduce a sport fitting the empty cell). *Why fifth:* answers the original puzzle with an action.
6. **Triangulate with a second statistic** ("Moreover, from the statistics… also mostly male and old"). *Why sixth:* strengthens the finding by showing it matches another dataset.
7. **State the next analytical step with a consequence marker** ("Hence we are looking for…"). *Why seventh:* pivots from diagnosis to prescription.
8. **Show the prescription visually** (Figure 8). *Why last:* the reader now sees the proposed placement before the section closes.

The logic ladder: **anomaly → tool → visual → reading → proposal → corroboration → next step → visual of next step.**

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

### Skeleton A — "Tool-aimed rationale" paragraph

**SKELETON:** "We will use the [tool/method] in an attempt to answer why [specific surprising result], to find [operationalised question about the result]."

- **Slot 1 — `[tool/method]`:** a named analytical technique. Noun phrase.
- **Slot 2 — `[specific surprising result]`:** one concrete statistic or observation with a number/quote. Numeric or quoted noun phrase.
- **Slot 3 — `[operationalised question]`:** the "which/who/where" version of "why." Embedded wh-clause.
- **How to fill with a different idea:** pick ONE surprising percentage or quote from your own data; name the technique you will deploy on it; rewrite "why" as a "which group / where / who" question so the reader knows what the tool will actually hunt for.
- **Original filled version:** "We will use the product positioning in an attempt to answer why a high portion of the respondents (22.6%) responded none, to find which audience FFC may be failing to appeal, if any."
- **Demonstration fill (different idea):** "We will use a churn cohort breakdown in an attempt to answer why 18% of subscribers cancelled within 30 days, to find which acquisition channel is producing the highest-risk users."

### Skeleton B — "Visual → gap → proposal" paragraph

**SKELETON:** "After [analysing via the tool], [a category] is evidently missing. The introduction of [an offering that fills the empty cell] can help [capturing the missing segment]."

- **Slot 1 — `[analysing via the tool]`:** a temporal clause naming the tool past-tense ("after positioning from survey results"). Prepositional/temporal phrase.
- **Slot 2 — `[a category] is evidently missing`:** the empty cell expressed as an adjective pairing in quotes. Quoted adjective phrase + "is evidently missing."
- **Slot 3 — `[an offering that fills the empty cell]`:** a noun phrase modified by a relative clause referencing the empty attributes ("a sport which can fit within the category of…").
- **Slot 4 — `[capturing the missing segment]`:** verb phrase expressing the mechanism ("help gaining the missing potential customers"). Gerund phrase.
- **How to fill with a different idea:** run your matrix; identify the EMPTY cell with two adjective labels; name an intervention that would sit in it; specify the segment it would reach in one gerund phrase.
- **Original filled version:** "After positioning from survey results, a lack of 'masculine' and 'old' sports is evidently missing. The introduction of a sport which can fit within the category of 'old' and 'masculine' can help gaining the missing potential customers."
- **Demonstration fill (different idea):** "After segmenting by price sensitivity, an 'affordable premium' tier is evidently missing. The introduction of a mid-priced bundle which can fit between the current 'basic' and 'pro' tiers can help capturing the price-sensitive but feature-hungry segment."

### Skeleton C — "Triangulate → decide" paragraph

**SKELETON:** "Moreover, from [second dataset], [the same group] are also [descriptor A] and [descriptor B], coinciding with our mapping. Hence we are looking for [the next deliverable] which can theoretically fit [a placeholder shown in the next visual]."

- **Slot 1 — `[second dataset]`:** the source noun ("from the statistics").
- **Slot 2 — `[the same group] are also [descriptor A] and [descriptor B]`:** the group's overlap with the map's axes.
- **Slot 3 — `coinciding with our mapping`:** a participle phrase that EXPLICITLY ties the second dataset to the first tool.
- **Slot 4 — `Hence we are looking for…`:** the consequence move (single sentence, present continuous).
- **Slot 5 — `[placeholder shown in the next visual]`:** ends with a colon pointing at the next figure.
- **How to fill with a different idea:** bring a second table that profiles the anomaly group; list 2 descriptors that match the map axes; use the word "coinciding with" to stitch datasets; end with "Hence we are looking for…" and a colon that promises the next visual.
- **Original filled version:** "Moreover, from the statistics, the large portion of respondents who responded 'None' are respondents are also mostly male and old, coinciding with our mapping. Hence we are looking for a new sport which can theoretically fit the following:"

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / progression**
- *"After positioning from survey results"* — temporal anchor that opens evidence reading.
- *"Moreover, from the statistics"* — additive connector that launches triangulation with a new source.

**Cause / consequence**
- *"Hence we are looking for a new sport"* — single-word consequence pivot turning evidence into the next step.

**Specification / targeting**
- *"in an attempt to answer why… to find which audience"* — the colon-less rephrasing of "specifically, we want to know which group."
- *"which can fit within the category of 'old' and 'masculine'"* — relative clause that PINS the proposal to the empty cell's attributes.
- *"the following:"* — colon-as-pointer to the next figure.

**Evidence handling**
- *"from the statistics"* — source-flagging prepositional phrase.
- *"coinciding with our mapping"* — participation phrase that EXPLICITLY stitches two datasets together ("this dataset agrees with the visual").
- *"a lack of 'masculine' and 'old' sports is evidently missing"* — modal of visibility ("evidently") to mark the visual reading as a finding.

**Explanation verbs**
- *"will use… in an attempt to answer"* — frames the tool as explanatory.
- *"can help gaining the missing potential customers"* — mechanism verb ("help [gerund]") that links intervention → captured group.

**Contrast (set up by negation of absence)**
- *"is evidently missing"* — implicit contrast: present elsewhere, absent here.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The section's pattern is: **ANOMALY → TOOL DECLARATION → VISUAL → GAP-READING → PROPOSAL → CORROBORATION → NEXT STEP DECLARED → NEXT VISUAL.** It is a *visual-then-verbal interpretation* pattern, where every analytic claim is anchored to (and triggered by) a figure.

Step-by-step to replicate with a NEW idea:

1. **Pick one specific number or quote that puzzled you** (e.g., "27% of customers ticked 'other'"). State it as a noun phrase at the end of your opening clause.
2. **Declare the tool you will use on it** in a "We will use [tool] in an attempt to answer why [anomaly]" sentence — this previews the method before showing it.
3. **Insert Figure A** (the raw output of the tool). Do not interpret yet.
4. **Read the visual aloud in one sentence**, naming the EMPTY cell with two quoted adjectives (e.g., "'premium' and 'female' is evidently missing"). This sentence is your finding.
5. **Propose a specific intervention that occupies that empty cell**, expressed as "The introduction of [X] which can fit within the category of [A] and [B] can help [gerund: capturing the missing segment]." Keep subject = intervention, verb = "can help [gerund]."
6. **Triangulate**: bring a SECOND dataset that profiles the anomaly group and explicitly stitch it with the phrase "coinciding with our [tool]." Use "Moreover" to open this sentence.
7. **Pivot with "Hence"** to declare the next analytical deliverable: "Hence we are looking for [X] which can theoretically fit the following:" — end with a colon.
8. **Insert Figure B** that delivers on the colon's promise. Stop.

The key mechanic: **every claim must trace back to either a number/quote (sentence 1) or a figure (Figures 7 and 8).** The two figures act as bookends; the prose between them does the labelling and the stitching.
