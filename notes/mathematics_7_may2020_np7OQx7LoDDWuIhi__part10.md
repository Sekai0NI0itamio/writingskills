## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **model-validation section**. The ordered moves are:

1. **Authoritative source introduction** (cite who supplies the "true" model) — sets up the benchmark against which the student's own work will be measured.
2. **Benchmark formula display** (show the equation symbolically) — gives the reader a concrete artefact to evaluate.
3. **Substitution → numerical result** (plug in, state answer with s.f.) — turns the formula into a comparable number.
4. **Comparison with own value + reassurance verdict** (close to / faithful to) — closes the first test.
5. **Parallel transition** (*Similarly* / *Again*) — signals a second, identical test rather than a new idea.
6. **Second benchmark formula + result** (often with a *for conciseness* note about omitted sub-functions) — preserves the same evaluation logic on a different quantity.
7. **Second comparison + verdict** — replicates the judgement, now generalised.
8. **Anticipated-direction handoff** (*As expected, …*) — launches whatever claim the validation now permits.

WHY this order: each step earns the reader's trust for the next. The source has to be named before its formula can be used; the formula must be shown before the substituted number can be trusted; the substituted number must exist before comparison is meaningful; the first comparison must be reassuring before a parallel claim is allowed; two consistent comparisons make a generalised expectation natural.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / parallel moves:** *"Similarly, the surface area could be calculated"* (S6) — opens the second identical test; *"Again, my value"* (S9) — re-runs the comparison verb without restating the structure.
- **Authority / source-handling:** *"According to Yamamoto, the volume of revolution"* (S2) — names the external reference and immediately links it to a calculable quantity; *"given by Yamamoto in his paper"* (S6) — re-grounds the second formula in the same source so no new citation is needed.
- **Mechanism / procedure verbs:** *"can be calculated as"* (S2) — signals a formula is coming; *"Substituting the values of 𝑎 and 𝑏"* (S4) — narrates the mechanical step from symbolic to numeric; *"using (14)"* (S6) — references an equation by tag rather than rewriting it.
- **Comparison / closeness markers:** *"This is close to my calculated value"* (S5); *"is faithful to the 'true' value"* (S9) — two near-synonyms used to avoid repetition while keeping the same evaluative job.
- **Concession / scope-limitation:** *"not included here for conciseness"* (S6) — admits an omission and justifies it procedurally, not apologetically.
- **Expectation markers:** *"As expected, my"* (S10, cut-off) — announces that the next claim is now licensed by the prior verification.
- **Evaluation / verdict adjectives:** *"reassuring"* (S5) — converts numerical closeness into an emotional/credibility judgement.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section relies on the **Authority → Closed-form → Substitution → Parallel comparison** pattern. Steps to replicate on a new idea:

1. **Name an external authority** that provides a closed-form (symbolic) expression for the quantity you measured. Write *"According to [Authority], the [quantity] can be calculated as:"* so the reader knows the next artefact is sourced, not invented.
2. **Display the formula** on its own line so it is read as an object, not buried in prose.
3. **Narrate the substitution** explicitly: *"Substituting the values of [parameters], we get [result] ([s.f.])."* This tells the reader the number is actually computed, not asserted.
4. **Close the first test** with a single sentence that places your own number next to the model's number and assigns it an evaluative adjective (*close / reassuring / faithful*). Do not over-explain — one adjective does the work.
5. **Open a parallel second test** with *Similarly*, reusing the **same** authority (state this) and a *for conciseness* scope note if sub-expressions would clutter the page. Show only the final form of the second formula.
6. **State the second result** on its own line, then **repeat the comparison** with *Again* and a near-synonym of the first evaluative adjective — variation in wording, identical logical job.
7. **Hand off** with *As expected* to the generalisation the two tests now permit. Do not introduce a new idea here — only consolidate what the verifications have earned.

The discipline is: every sentence either *sets up* the next comparison, *performs* it, or *consolidates* it; no sentence introduces a new variable of meaning.
