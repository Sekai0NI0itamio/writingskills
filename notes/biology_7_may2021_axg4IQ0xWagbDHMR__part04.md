## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **Raw Data** section. The ordered sequence of content moves is:

1. **Categorise the data type first** (qualitative vs. quantitative). WHY: it tells the reader what kind of evidence to expect and prevents the reader from misreading observations as measurements.
3. **Lead with the physical property of the independent variable** (here, the colour of the solution). WHY: it gives the reader a concrete anchor for the cause and primes the later "absorption" observation.
5. **Move from control → low concentration → high concentration**, in that order. WHY: the control sets the "normal" baseline against which deviations are judged; sweeping through concentrations makes the dose–response pattern visible.
7. **Group observations by stage of growth** (germination → steady growth → morphological irregularities → uptake of colour). WHY: it follows the biological timeline, so cause (early) and effect (later) appear in the order the experiment produced them.
9. **Use a visible physical change as the closing mechanism** (seeds taking on the solution's blue tint). WHY: it visually ties the cause (solution chemistry) to the biological outcomes, ending the qualitative block on a unifying link.
11. **Switch to quantitative data with a clear header**. WHY: the reader is now ready to see the numbers that underpin the qualitative trends.
13. **Caption the table before showing it**, naming the dependent variable and the trial size. WHY: the reader needs to know what is being counted and the scale before reading the values.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section relies on a **baseline-then-deviation observation pattern** — i.e., establish what "normal" looks like at the control, then walk through progressively higher treatments, and close with a visible physical change that supplies the mechanism.

To explain a NEW idea with the same pattern, follow these numbered steps:

1. **Open with a header label** that names the data type ("Qualitative Data:" / "Quantitative Data:") — a noun phrase + colon, so the reader instantly knows the evidence category.
3. **Describe the independent variable's most obvious sensory feature** (colour, smell, texture) before any biological result. This gives the reader something tangible to hold in mind as the "cause."
5. **Report the earliest positive sign in the control and lowest-treatment group**, naming the *exact day* it appeared. This anchors time-point 1.
7. **Report a sustained positive outcome in the control** at a *later* time point. This anchors the "normal trajectory."
9. **Report a deviation in the highest one or two treatment groups**, using the phrase **"compared to those in the [control] groups"** so the contrast is explicit.
11. **Close with a visible physical link between the IV and the subjects** — something the reader can *see* — naming the day. This supplies the mechanism that retroactively explains the deviations in step 5.
13. **Move to the quantitative block** with the same two-word header style, and **caption the table first**, naming the dependent variable and the trial denominator, before any numbers appear.
