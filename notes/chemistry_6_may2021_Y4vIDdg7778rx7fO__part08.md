## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Establish alphabetical author ordering** — first entry (Afeefy) determines that subsequent entries follow surname order; this lets the reader locate any source without scanning.
2. **Mix source types deliberately** — journal articles (Skinner, Rossini) → textbooks (Derry, Owen) → websites (Boudreaux, Owlcation, GCSE) → online calculators (EndMemo). Variety demonstrates the breadth of evidence behind the investigation.
3. **Include access dates and URLs for online sources** — "October 2020," "<https://…>" flags retrievability, which is required for digital citations.
4. **Distinguish format by punctuation rather than prose** — periods, commas, and italics (implied) carry the structural load, so no explanatory sentences are needed.
5. **End without a closing remark** — the bibliography terminates by alphabetical exhaustion, not by summary; the reader's job is to verify, not to be persuaded.

The order matters because: alphabetical sequencing makes verification efficient; type-variety proves triangulation; access dates prove the data was live when used.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A (journal paper citation)**
"[Surname, First Initial.] '[Title in quotation marks.]' [Journal Name] ([Year]): [page range]. [Format tag]."

1. *Slots:* author block (inverted name) → quoted article title → italicised journal name + year in parentheses → colon-led page numbers → optional format descriptor (e.g., "Paper").
   *Fill:* state the lead author surname-first, comma, then initials with periods; place the article title inside straight double quotes; give the journal in italics, year in parentheses, pages after a colon; end with a format word if the citation type is unusual.
   *Original:* "H. A. Skinner, A. Snelson. 'The heats of combustion of the four isomeric butyl alcohols.' Transactions of the Faraday Society (1960): 1776-1783. Paper."
   *Demo fill (different idea):* "Smith, J. R. 'The activation energies of tertiary halide substitutions.' Journal of Organic Chemistry (2014): 412-419. Paper."

**SKELETON B (web page citation)**
"[Author or site name]. [Title - brief descriptor]. [n.d. or Year]. [Access Month Year]. <URL>."

1. *Slots:* author/site field → title with hyphenation if implied → date or "n.d." → access date → angle-bracket URL.
   *Fill:* put either a person or an organisation in the author slot; write a title that names the page topic; if no publication date exists, write "n.d."; always close with the date the page was last accessed and the live link.
   *Original:* "Boudreaux. Alcohols - Organic Chemistry. n.d. October 2020. <https://www.angelo.edu/...>."
   *Demo fill (different idea):* "ChemGuide. Nucleophilic Substitution Mechanisms. 2019. November 2023. <https://www.chemguide.co.uk/mechanisms/sn1.html>."

**SKELETON C (textbook citation)**
"[Author surname], [First initial]. [Edition if applicable]. [Place of publication: Publisher, Year]. [Format tag]."

1. *Slots:* inverted author → optional edition numeral or "2nd" → city colon publisher comma year → format descriptor ("Document," "Print").
   *Fill:* invert the author name, add edition only when not the first, write the city followed by a colon, then the publisher and year; close with a one-word format tag indicating the medium.
   *Original:* "Owen, Steve. Chemistry for the IB Diploma. 2nd. Cambridge University Press, 2014. Document."
   *Demo fill (different idea):* "Brown, T. L. Chemistry: The Central Science. 14th. London: Pearson, 2018. Print."

**SKELETON D (data-table / reference-collection citation)**
"[Lead author], [et al. or co-authors]. [Title of dataset]. [Year]. [Container or editor info]. [Format]. [Access date]."

1. *Slots:* lead author → co-author marker → title → year → editors/container → medium → access month and year.
   *Fill:* give the primary author, append "et al." only if there are three or more; italicise the data-set title; list editors after "Eds." when the dataset sits inside a larger compilation; add access date because the resource is online.
   *Original:* "Afeefy, Hussein Y., Joel F. Liebman and and Stephen E. Stein. Neutral Thermochemical Data. 2018. Eds. P.J. Linstrom and W.G. Mallard. WebBook. October 2020."
   *Demo fill (different idea):* "Lide, David R., et al. CRC Handbook of Chemistry and Physics. 2020. Eds. W. M. Haynes. WebBook. September 2023."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

Because this is a bibliography, the section relies on **citation-punctuation signals**, not discursive connectives. Grouped by job:

- **Sequencing (alphabetical, not temporal):** no "firstly/next" — ordering is conveyed by *surname-first inversion*: "Afeefy, Hussein Y.," "Boudreaux," "Derry, Lanna."
- **Authorisation/attribution:** "et al." in "Derry, Lanna, et al." — handles multi-author compression.
- **Specification of medium:** "WebBook." in "WebBook. October 2020" — names the container for digital data; "Paper." after "(1960): 1776-1783" — names physical format.
- **Date precision / evidence handling:** "n.d." in "EndMemo. n.d." — handles missing publication date; "October 2020" after URLs — handles last-accessed date for live verification.
- **Explanation verbs:** none present — bibliography does not explain; punctuation alone ("Eds.," "Document," angle-bracket URL) performs explanation-by-symbol.
- **Cause/consequence connectives:** none — the section is not argumentative.
- **Contrast/concession:** none — entries are additive, not comparative.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses the pattern **attribution → container → retrieval stamp** — i.e., who said it, where it lives, when the speaker consulted it.

Steps to apply to any new source:

1. **Identify the author's name in the citation-required format** (surname, given-initial; or organisation name verbatim). Write it as the lead of the entry.
2. **State the title of the work exactly as it appears**, using quotation marks for article/paper titles and italics (or no special marker in plain text) for standalone works.
3. **Name the container** — journal name with year in parentheses and colon-led page numbers for papers; city colon publisher comma year for books; "Eds." list followed by the host publication for data tables.
4. **Append a format tag** ("Paper," "Document," "Print," "Web page," "Html page") when the medium is not obvious, so the reader instantly sees how the source was retrieved.
5. **For web sources, add the access date** ("October 2020") and wrap the URL in angle brackets, proving the resource was live when consulted.
6. **Do not write sentences** — let punctuation (periods, commas, colons, parentheses) carry the structural meaning; the entry ends when the format tag or URL closes.
