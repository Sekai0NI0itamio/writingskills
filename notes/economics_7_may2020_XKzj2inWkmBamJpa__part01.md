## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1**
1. *"from Q1 to Q2."* — **Transition fragment** (tail of a prior move). Hands to the reader by pointing at a price/quantity location, forcing the next sentence to explain what changes there. The reader must continue because the fragment alone is unanchored.
2. *"After the tariff quantity imported has fallen"* — **Claim + evidence (cause)**. "After the tariff" installs the causal event; the reader is now handed to "what the importers do about it" (the next sentence), because the reduced import quantity begs the question of who funds the gap.
3. *"They must pay a fixed tax to the government"* — **Mechanism (cause)**. The "thus" that follows turns the tax into the gateway for the verdict in the same sentence, handing forward by labelling the taxed area (B+C) for later reuse.

**Paragraph 2**
1. *"The minimum amount of revenue for international producers"* — **Definition/baseline claim**. Sets a cost threshold so the reader can compare the next sentence against it.
2. *"however, after the tariff, quantity Q2 is produced by local producers"* — **Contrast ("however") + specification**. Hands forward by substituting the producer and bumping the cost (E+F → E+F+A), so the next sentence can state the consequence.
3. *"thus from society's standpoint the revenue from area A is lost."* — **Implication (consequence)**. "Thus" converts the cost gap into a loss classification; the reader is pushed toward a second, parallel loss.
4. *"Area D is lost as well, because consumer surplus is lost"* — **Evidence/unpack (cause)**. "Because" unpacks why D disappears; the reader is handed to the qualifier that prevents D from reappearing elsewhere.
5. *"and this doesn't become producer surplus."* — **Specification/negation**. Closes off the rerouting possibility so the verdict can land cleanly.
6. *"Thus the area A+D is the welfare loss associated with the tariff"* — **Verdict (synthesis)**. Adds A and D into the final label; hands forward to a paragraph that shifts perspective from society to producers.

**Paragraph 3 (paragraph 4 in document order)**
1. *"The domestic producers benefit in this situation as they now face less competition"* — **Claim + mechanism (cause)**. "As" unpacks why domestic producers gain; hands forward by dangling an unquoted benefit that the next clause will name.
2. *"but can rather keep 'promoting healthy and stable development of the sector.'"* — **Authority-backed evidence (quoted source)**. The quotation supplies the textbook's framing; hands forward by leaving "this development" undefined.
3. *"This development means, that producers expand their businesses and open new factories,"* — **Implication/unpack (definition of the quote)**. "This development means" forces the reader to see the quote operationalised, and the trailing comma invites the next sentence to continue the chain.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Quantify the post-tariff change** — fix the new import volume against the old. Sets up everything that follows because every welfare area is defined relative to quantities.
2. **Account for the government's gain** (area B+C). Establishes one of the three "buckets" of a tariff diagram before any losses are named, so the reader can see what is collected before what is wasted.
3. **State the international producer's baseline cost** (E+F). Establishes a benchmark so the *increase* required of local producers can be exposed as a cost.
4. **Contrast international vs. local producers after the tariff** (E+F vs. E+F+A). Generates the gap (area A) that becomes the deadweight loss.
5. **Declare area A as a society-level loss**. First half of the welfare verdict.
6. **Unpack area D as lost consumer surplus** with a "because" chain. Adds the second half, while *negating* any offsetting producer gain to keep the math clean.
7. **Combine A + D into the welfare loss verdict**. Final synthesising label so the reader leaves the paragraph with one takeaway.
8. **Pivot to the producer side** ("The domestic producers benefit"). Signals a perspective shift away from society.
9. **Insert an authoritative quote** to legitimise the producer-benefit claim. Borrows the textbook's language so the student's own inference doesn't need to defend itself alone.
10. **Operationalise the quoted phrase** ("expand their businesses and open new factories"). Translates abstract "development" into concrete microeconomic behaviour, setting up whatever follows.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

### Skeleton A — "Quantify → tax → label the green area"
**SKELETON:** "After the [policy event] [metric] has [changed] from [old] to [new]. [Agents] must [pay/do X] to [authority] for each [unit], thus [area] represent the [authority outcome] ([colour label])."

1. *Slots & shapes:*
   - Slot 1: policy event (noun phrase).
   - Slot 2: metric (noun, e.g. "quantity imported").
   - Slot 3: change verb (past participle, e.g. "fallen").
   - Slot 4 & 5: old and new expressions (math-style, Q4–Q1 → Q3–Q2).
   - Slot 6: agents (pronoun, "they").
   - Slot 7: action (modal + verb, "must pay").
   - Slot 8: authority (noun, "the government").
   - Slot 9: unit noun ("unit").
   - Slot 10: area label (letters, "B+C").
   - Slot 11: outcome category (noun, "government revenue").
   - Slot 12: colour/visual tag in parentheses.
2. *How to refill:* Pick any policy that imposes a per-unit charge (subsidy to consumers, unit tax, cap-and-trade permit). Find a before/after quantity pair already on your diagram. Keep the "thus" linking the payment to a labelled area; the colour tag mirrors whatever shade you shaded that area.
3. *Original fill:* "After the tariff quantity imported has fallen from Q4-Q1 to Q3-Q2. They must pay a fixed tax to the government for each unit they sell, thus area B+C represent the government revenue (green area)."
4. *Demo fill (different idea):* "After the subsidy quantity purchased has risen from Q1–Q2 to Q1–Q3. Consumers must receive a fixed payment from the government for each unit they buy, thus area B+C represent the government expenditure (blue area)."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / framing the causal moment:** "After the tariff" (quantity imported has fallen), "after the tariff, quantity Q2 is produced" — installs the policy as the time-stamp that licenses every later claim.
- **Cause / consequence:** "thus area B+C represent the government revenue", "thus from society's standpoint the revenue from area A is lost", "Thus the area A+D is the welfare loss" — three escalating uses of "thus" to convert mechanism into verdict.
- **Contrast / concession:** "however, after the tariff, quantity Q2 is produced by local producers" — single pivot word that swaps the agent and unmasks the cost gap.
- **Specification / additive marking:** "Area D is lost as well", "and this doesn't become producer surplus" — "as well" and "and" stack a second loss onto the first; the "doesn't" actively forecloses an offsetting counter-argument.
- **Evidence handling:** quoted authority — *'promoting healthy and stable development of the sector.'* — and the immediately preceding footnote citation (Finamore, 2014, p. 202). Both signal source-backed legitimacy before the student's own unpacking begins.
- **Perspective tag:** "from society's standpoint" — explicitly relocates the judging agent so the welfare verdict reads as objective rather than partisan.
- **Explanation verbs / definitional connectives:** "represent" (the green area), "is lost as well, because" (unpacking D), "This development means, that" (defining the quoted phrase).

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The dominant pattern is **diagram-walkthrough → comparative cost wedge → societal loss verdict → authority quote → operational unpacking**. Replicate it in five steps:

1. **Pin the diagram state after the policy.** Open with a quantity or price change introduced by "after the [policy]" so the reader knows the visual frame. This step sets up every later label.
2. **Account for one of the diagram's labelled areas as a gain.** Use "thus" + "represent" + parenthetical colour tag so the reader can match your words to the shaded region on the page.
3. **Build a baseline-vs-after cost comparison with a single "however".** Name the original agent's cost/production threshold, then swap in the new agent with a strictly higher required figure. This is the move that *generates* the welfare loss.
4. **Convert the gap into a verdict using "thus" + "from [stakeholder]'s standpoint".** Then add a *second* parallel loss using "as well, because" + a mechanism clause, *and* close off any plausible offset with "and this doesn't become [other surplus]". End by summing the two areas under one welfare label.
5. **Pivot to the winning agent and quote a textbook phrase.** Use "The [agents] benefit in this situation as…" to install a positive claim, then immediately anchor it with a verbatim quote in single quotation marks. Follow the quote with "This [abstract noun from quote] means, that [agents] [concrete verb] and [concrete verb]," so the abstraction becomes behaviour the reader can picture.
