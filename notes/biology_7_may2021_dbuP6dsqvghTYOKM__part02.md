## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1** (single paragraph, 4 sentences)

1. **Context bridge + authority evidence** — "worldwide, it being 'a significant health problem in 118 countries' (Kapil)" → *Hands off by scope-narrowing: "worldwide" plus a country count funnels the reader from a global claim to one specific substance the problem concerns.* The next sentence begins with "Iodine is essential..."
2. **Definition + mechanism** — "Iodine is essential for producing hormones that the thyroid gland uses" → *Hands off by cause-and-consequence: a deficiency claim ("essential for") needs the reader to know what happens when it goes wrong, which the next sentence supplies with "excess or deficiency... may be detrimental."*
3. **Consequence with evidentiary quotation** — "excess or deficiency in iodine intake may be detrimental to one's health, 'lead[ing] to a great number of symptoms' (Institute for Quality and Efficiency in Health Care [IQWiG])" → *Hands off by escalation: the phrase "the most severe cases" in the next sentence signals a worsening on the same continuum.* List items (goitre, hypothyroidism) cue the reader that a worse category is coming.
4. **Extreme-case evidence** — "The most severe cases of IDD may lead to 'Brain damage and irreversible mental retardation' (Kapil 267)" → *Hands off by pivot-word "Furthermore": the reader now expects an additional — not merely a worsened — consequence, which the next sentence delivers with a different disease (thyroid cancer).*

1. **Contrast/qualification with paired evidence** — "thyroid cancer has been linked to both under and overconsumption of iodine" followed by two opposing quotations ("ATC... decreased... however... PTC increased") → *Hands off by solution-pivot: having documented the harm, the reader is logically positioned to ask "what is being done?", and the next sentence begins exactly with "To combat...".*
2. **Intervention as response to the established problem** — "'universally effective… salt iodization programmes' (Micronutrient Deficiencies) have been implemented" → *Hands off by concession: "However," at the end opens the door to a limitation or complication, which the next section supplies.*

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This section executes a **"scale-up of severity + paired-counterintuitive-evidence + intervention response"** introduction. The order matters because each move only works once the previous one has framed it:

1. **Global-scale claim with authority statistic** — establishes that the issue is widespread and serious, so any detail about one nutrient feels weighty.
2. **Biological definition of the nutrient** — gives the reader the mechanism that makes deficiency matter at all; without this, symptoms would feel arbitrary.
3. **Symptom consequence + quotation** — converts the mechanism into lived effects; the quotation borrows institutional credibility.
4. **Most-extreme-case evidence** — escalates severity to its worst plausible outcome, raising the stakes so that a public-health response feels necessary.
5. **Counterintuitive paired data (A decreased, however B increased)** — prevents the paragraph from reading as one-sided; the contrast *within* the evidence is what makes the topic interesting rather than merely alarming.
6. **Existing intervention presented as the response** — closes the loop: problem established → response implemented.

A student replicating this for any public-health/environmental topic would move: global scale → what the substance/factor is → why it matters biologically → worst documented outcome → counterintuitive nuance → current real-world response.

Slot 2: describe two common symptoms using lay-accessible terms. Slot 3: find one institutional source quoting a permanent, severe outcome.
   3. *Original filled version:* "Iodine is essential for producing hormones that the thyroid gland uses; excess or deficiency in iodine intake may be detrimental to one's health, 'lead[ing] to a great number of symptoms' (IQWiG)... 'Brain damage and irreversible mental retardation' (Kapil 267)."

**Skeleton B — "Counterintuitive paired-data paragraph"**
   SKELETON: "[Condition] has been linked to [factor], — '[metric A] decreased in many [regions] with the introduction of [intervention]', however '[metric B] increased' ([Source])."

   1. *What each slot holds:* Slot 1 = a specific disease subtype (noun phrase); Slot 2 = a modifiable exposure or behaviour; Slot 3 = a quoted trend showing improvement; Slot 4 = a quoted counter-trend showing deterioration; Slot 5 = a parenthetical authority.
   2. *How to fill with a different idea:* Slot 1: choose a condition that splits into subtypes (e.g. skin cancer, leukaemia). Slot 2: pick a public-health intervention that could plausibly help some subtypes while shifting incidence to others. Slot 3/4: locate two opposing data points in a single review article; quote each in 5–10 words.
   3. *Original filled version:* "thyroid cancer has been linked to both under and overconsumption of iodine — 'the prevalence of anaplastic thyroid cancer (ATC)... decreased in many countries with the introduction of iodized salt', however 'papillary thyroid cancer (PTC) increased' (Lund and Wu)."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Cause / mechanism:** "essential for producing", "may be detrimental", "may lead to" — used to convert a definition into a consequence ("Iodine is essential for producing hormones"; "excess or deficiency in iodine intake may be detrimental").
- **Severity escalation:** "The most severe cases... may lead to" — ramps the reader from moderate to irreversible outcomes.
- **Sequencing / addition:** "Furthermore" — adds an *additional* consequence rather than a worsening one ("Furthermore, thyroid cancer has been linked...").
- **Contrast / counterintuitive pairing:** "however" — placed *mid-sentence inside a quotation block* to stage opposing data points within a single evidence claim ("ATC... decreased... however... PTC increased").
- **Evidence handling:** parenthetical author–page citations after quoted phrases ("(Kapil)", "(Kapil 267)", "(Lund and Wu)", "(IQWiG)", "(Micronutrient Deficiencies)") — every consequence carries a named source.
- **Purpose / intervention framing:** "To combat the prevalent problem" — frames the next clause as a *response* to the preceding evidence.
- **Definition verb:** "is essential for" — performs definition-by-function rather than dictionary-style definition.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses a **"definition → consequence → extreme case → counterintuitive nuance → response"** pattern. Replicate with any health/environmental topic by following these steps:

1. **Anchor globally with one statistic** — open with a numerical scope claim ("a problem in N nations") so the topic reads as serious.
2. **Define the factor by function** — write one sentence giving the substance/agent and one clause stating what biological role it plays.
3. **List moderate harms with one quotation** — convert the definition into consequences, citing an institutional source for the symptom cluster.
4. **Escalate to the worst plausible outcome** — open the next sentence with "The most severe cases..." and quote an irreversible harm with a citation.
5. **Add a *different* consequence with counterintuitive data** — use "Furthermore" to introduce a separate disease, then quote two opposing trends joined by "however" so the evidence itself contains a twist.
6. **Close the loop with the existing intervention** — begin with "To combat..." and quote an evaluative adjective ("universally effective", "scalable") plus the intervention's delivery mechanism, citing the organisation responsible.
7. **End on "However,"** — leave a dangling concession that the next section must resolve, signalling that the argument is not yet finished.
