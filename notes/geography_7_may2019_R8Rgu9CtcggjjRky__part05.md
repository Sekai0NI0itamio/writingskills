## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **bridging/reasoning + evidence + verdict section** sitting between two data sources (textual reasoning and photo analysis on one side, a quantitative Figure 9 on the other). The order is:

1. **Reasoning carry-over** — picks up an unresolved trade-off from the previous section (sacrificed sidewalk for road).
2. **Concession + inference** — narrows the trade-off ("Even so… not wide enough") then *infers* the unstated priority (more sidewalk than trees).
3. **Authority-led comparison** — cites a named source and gives two paired measurements (Ginza vs Omotesando) so the reader has *numbers, not adjectives*.
4. **Asymmetric contrast** — adds a *constraint difference* (one must reduce, the other already has trees), making the comparison explanatory rather than decorative.
5. **Implication/verdict** — "Thus…" closes the comparison into a flag (sidewalk width = obstacle).
6. **Cross-source transition** — "Figure 9 also displays a similar pattern" *links the verdict to a new data set*, so the next evidence is read through the lens just established.
7. **Quantified split + prior-reasoning verdict** — gives two grouped numbers, recalls a previously given reason ("as it was explained above"), and closes with "as expected."
8. **Section summary opener** — "Overall…" begins the roll-up of the whole line of argument.

Why this order: each move *earns* the right to make the next. The concession only makes sense after the trade-off is named; the authority is needed because the inference in step 2 was hedged ("it is fair to assume"); the asymmetric contrast only counts once both raw numbers are on the table; the verdict only lands once the contrast is established; the cross-source transition only reads as confirmation if the obstacle has just been flagged.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / adding**
- "**Additionally, while** Ginza area would have to reduce" — used to layer a second, contrasting fact onto the first number.
- "**also displays a similar pattern as** photo analysis" — used to chain a new data source back to a prior method.

**Cause / consequence / inference**
- "**For this reason, it is fair to assume that** the government had allocated" — flags a hedged inference built on the preceding concession.
- "**Thus, the sidewalk width could be a possible** obstacle" — collapses comparison into a single named verdict.
- "**therefore this result is** as expected" — converts recalled reasoning into a justification for the data.

**Contrast / concession**
- "**Even so, the width of the road itself** is not wide enough" — concedes the trade-off was reasonable, then undermines it.
- "**while** Ginza area would have to reduce this 6.25m… **Omotesando area** – which is already known as…" — paired "while" + parenthetical reputation tag sets up the asymmetry.

**Specification / precision**
- "**compared to** 8.1m at Omotesando" — narrows a general claim to a measured pair.
- "**already has 2.65m of trees allocated within** 8.1m" — nests a sub-measurement inside the container measurement.

**Evidence handling**
- "**According to the** "Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism"" — names the authority before the number.
- "**as it was explained above,** site E is aimed" — explicitly recalls earlier reasoning as evidence.

**Summation / verdict**
- "**Overall, the area of green spaces did** not increase" — opens the cross-section roll-up.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section runs on a **"hedged inference → authority + paired numbers → asymmetric contrast → verdict → cross-source confirmation"** pattern. To replicate it on a new idea:

1. **Name a trade-off inherited from earlier in the essay** in one short clause (state what was sacrificed and for what).
2. **Insert a concession** ("Even so…") that shows the trade-off is *still* inadequate by some external standard — this earns the right to infer a hidden priority.
3. **State the inferred priority** using "For this reason, it is fair to assume that [agent] allocated [X] over [Y] in order to [pressure]." Hedging language ("it is fair to assume") is essential — it signals the next move will supply proof.
4. **Cite a named authority** ("According to the [Body]…") and give **two paired measurements** for two comparable cases (Site A's number vs Site B's number, with units).
5. **Add an asymmetric contrast** ("Additionally, while Site A would have to [change]… Site B – which is already known as [reputation] – already has [X]"). The parenthetical reputation tag is what turns a raw comparison into an explanation.
6. **Close the paragraph with "Thus,"** and name the measured variable as "a possible obstacle for [Site A] to have improved [outcome]." Verdict must echo the variable introduced in Step 4.
7. **Open the next paragraph by linking the verdict to a new data source**: "[Figure] also displays a similar pattern as [prior method]." This is the bridge move.
8. **Quantify the split** in that new source ("Group 1 remained low, having an average of X%, while Group 2 exceeded Y%") and **recall the earlier reason** ("as it was explained above…") so the outlier group is justified, not unexplained.
9. **Close with "therefore this result is as expected"** — converting the recalled reason into a verdict for the new data set.
10. **Open the roll-up with "Overall,"** to signal the section's argument is about to be consolidated.
