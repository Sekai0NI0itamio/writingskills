## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is an **error-diagnosis / back-calculation** section of an evaluation. The order is:

1. **Error observation + suspected cause** — first establish WHAT was wrong (off by 10³) and WHAT variable is to blame. Sets up the reader's question: *why?*
2. **Mechanism** — give the physical reason the blamed variable is wrong. Converts the suspicion into a testable claim.
3. **Proof-pointer** — explicitly tell the reader a calculation will now *demonstrate* the mechanism. This is the bridge that justifies the math appearing.
4. **Rearranged symbolic equation** — show the working algebraically, isolating the variable whose value is being investigated.
5. **Numerical substitution + final figure (with sig figs)** — deliver the "true" value that matches literature, proving the diagnosis.

*Why this order works:* You cannot ask for a back-calculation until the reader knows (a) that an error exists, (b) which variable to back-calculate for, and (c) the physical mechanism that makes the error predictable. Each move narrows the reader's uncertainty one notch.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- "Rearranging (2) for the terminal velocity" — labels the algebraic move.
- "Substituting in values for the variables" — labels the numeric move.

- "perhaps due to the balls' terminal velocity" — hedged causal attribution.
- "which means that the balls perhaps did" — consequence introduced by a measurement fact.

- "The reason as to why the viscosity value" — formal opening for an inference.

- "for the viscosity of saltless water" — narrows the substitution context.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern is: **ERROR OBSERVED → CAUSE HYPOTHESIS → MECHANISM → BACK-CALCULATION → LITERATURE MATCH.** It is a reverse-engineering / verification pattern, not a forward derivation.

To replicate with a NEW idea:

1. **State the discrepancy.** Open with what your measured/calculated value got wrong and by how much (quantify: factor, percentage, or magnitude).
2. **Name ONE suspect variable.** Pick the single intermediate variable whose error would propagate into your wrong answer.
3. **Defensively rank it.** Add "as this variable is the most likely to have been altered due to [design feature]" so the reader sees your reasoning chain.
4. **Give the mechanism.** Quote a concrete number from your setup (a distance, a time, a mass) and follow with "which means that…" to state the physical reason.
5. **Announce the demonstration.** Write one sentence beginning "This is demonstrated in the calculations below, which determine the 'true' [suspect variable] value…"
6. **Show the algebraic rearrangement.** Begin with "Rearranging (equation number) for [suspect variable]:" and isolate it.
7. **Substitute and close with literature match.** Begin with "Substituting in values…" and end with the numerical result, an approximation sign, units, and "(n s.f.)" in parentheses.
