## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1 — Numbered procedure (steps 1–8)**

1. **Step 1 — Reagent preparation.** Sentence: "Prepare a standard solution of hydrogen peroxide with a concentration of 0.1 moldm-3 by diluting a 1.0 moldm-3 sample of the liquid in a volumetric flask."  
   *Move:* **setup of input substance** (names substrate, locks concentration via dilution ratio, names apparatus).  
   *Hand-off:* Because the substrate is now quantified, the next sentence must establish the *environment* in which the reaction will run — it specifies the controlled variable that the rest of the procedure will later vary.

2. **Step 2 — Apparatus + controlled variable.** Sentence: "Set up a water bath with a temperature of 348.0K, with a boiling tube attached to a gas syringe within it. This gas syringe will be held up by a clamp."  
   *Move:* **environment definition** (states the IV's starting value, names the reaction vessel, names the gas-collection instrument, names the support).  
   *Hand-off:* With apparatus ready and temperature fixed, the next sentence must deliver the substrate *into* the prepared vessel — the only logical next action is loading reactant.

3. **Step 3 — Substrate load.** Sentence: "Measure 25 cm3 of the diluted hydrogen peroxide using a measuring cylinder, and pour it into the boiling tube."  
   *Move:* **introduction of reactant** (volume, dispenser, destination).  
   *Hand-off:* Substrate alone does not react at the required rate; the catalyst must be added *with* the timer started — the catalyst sentence bundles trigger + initiation.

4. **Step 4 — Catalyst addition + timer trigger.** Sentence: "Pour 10 cm3 of 0.1 moldm-3 iron (III) chloride into the boiling tube. At the same time, start the timer of a stopwatch."  
   *Move:* **initiation event** (catalyst volume and concentration, simultaneous clock start).  
   *Hand-off:* Reaction now running; the procedure cannot yet terminate, so the next move is the *open observation* window.

5. **Step 5 — Open observation.** Sentence: "Record any observations as necessary."  
   *Move:* **qualitative capture during the run.**  
   *Hand-off:* Step 5 leaves the run open-ended; step 6 must therefore supply the **stop criterion** that closes the run.

6. **Step 6 — Stop criterion.** Sentence: "Stop the timer once 10 cm3 of oxygen gas has been collected, as per the gas syringe."  
   *Move:* **endpoint definition** (gas volume threshold, instrument source).  
   *Hand-off:* One trial has now a defined duration; reliability logic forces the next move to be replication.

7. **Step 7 — Replication.** Sentence: "Repeat steps 3 to 5 thrice, for a total of four experimental attempts."  
   *Move:* **repeat for reliability** (note: range deliberately stops *before* the stopwatch-stop in step 6 — i.e., the timed run is the thing being repeated).  
   *Hand-off:* One temperature value is now exhausted; the procedure must move to **varying the IV**.

8. **Step 8 — IV sweep.** Sentence: "Repeat steps 2 to 7, using a water bath with temperatures of 343.0K, 338.0K, 333.0K and 328.0K."  
   *Move:* **enumeration of remaining IV levels** (descending order — chosen so the bath can cool rather than be re-heated; logic embedded in the ordering, not stated).  
   *Hand-off:* Procedure finished; the only remaining job is **source attribution**.

**Paragraph 2 — Attribution**

9. **Single sentence.** "This experimental procedure was adapted from the Job Foundation of Thermodynamics."  
   *Move:* **source citation** (signals that the design is inherited, not original — frames academic honesty).  
   *Hand-off:* none — terminal sentence.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

The section executes the canonical IB Chemistry method structure, in this order:

1. **Reagent preparation first** — because every later quantity must be traceable to a defined stock concentration.
2. **Apparatus + initial IV value** — because reaction conditions must be set before any chemical is introduced.
3. **Substrate introduction** — because the vessel must contain reactant before catalyst is added.
4. **Catalyst + simultaneous timer start** — because rate measurement requires a synchronised initiation event.
5. **Open observation window** — because qualitative notes accompany the quantitative reading.
6. **Quantitative stop criterion** — because "rate" needs an unambiguous endpoint to be measurable.
7. **Replication at that IV** — because a single trial cannot support a rate claim.
8. **Sweep across remaining IV values** — because the experiment's purpose is the relationship between IV and rate.
9. **Source attribution** — because academic honesty requires acknowledging borrowed designs.

*Why this order:* each move creates a precondition for the next. You cannot measure a rate without a defined substrate (1→3), cannot run a reaction without a controlled environment (2→4), cannot time without a trigger (4→6), cannot average without repetition (6→7), cannot plot IV vs rate without varying the IV (7→8). Attribution comes last because it closes the method, it does not enable it.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A — Reagent preparation slot**
`"[Verb of preparation] a [standard solution / stock] of [substance] with [concentration] by [transformation verb+ing] a [stock value] sample of the [liquid/solid] in a [volumetric apparatus]."`

1. **Slot grammar:** imperative verb + qualifier ("standard") + of-substance + with-concentration + by-method + in-apparatus.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** Pick the substrate you will use; state its working concentration and the stock concentration you will dilute from; pick the volumetric vessel (volumetric flask, pipette + volumetric flask). Keep "diluting" if concentration drops, or swap to "dissolving" if you are making from solid.
3. **Original fill:** "Prepare a standard solution of hydrogen peroxide with a concentration of 0.1 moldm⁻³ by diluting a 1.0 moldm⁻³ sample of the liquid in a volumetric flask."
4. **Demonstration fill (different subject):** "Prepare a standard solution of sodium thiosulfate with a concentration of 0.05 moldm⁻³ by diluting a 0.5 moldm⁻³ sample of the liquid in a volumetric flask."

**SKELETON B — Apparatus + IV setup slot**
`"Set up a [environmental apparatus] with a [controlled variable value], with a [vessel] attached to a [measurement instrument] within it. This [measurement instrument] will be held up by a [support]."`

1. **Slot grammar:** Set up + environmental noun + with-value, + secondary attachment. Followed by a demonstrative-restating sentence that specifies how the instrument is supported.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** Identify what variable you are controlling (temperature, pH, light intensity), name the apparatus that controls it (water bath, buffer jacket, lamp housing), name the reaction vessel, name the measurement instrument, name its support (clamp, stand, boss).
3. **Original fill:** "Set up a water bath with a temperature of 348.0K, with a boiling tube attached to a gas syringe within it. This gas syringe will be held up by a clamp."
4. **Demonstration fill (different subject):** "Set up a light-proof box with a lamp placed 15 cm from a test tube attached to a colorimeter probe within it. This probe will be held steady by a clamp."

**SKELETON C — Initiation event slot (bundles trigger + clock)**
`"[Transfer verb] [volume] of [concentration] [reagent] into the [vessel]. At the same time, [initiate the measurement device]."`

1. **Slot grammar:** Imperative + volume + of-concentration-reagent + into-vessel. Second clause: temporal adverbial ("At the same time") + imperative starting the clock/probe.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** Pick the reagent whose addition is your rate-defining moment; specify its volume and concentration; pick the timer or data-logger you will start.
3. **Original fill:** "Pour 10 cm3 of 0.1 moldm⁻³ iron (III) chloride into the boiling tube. At the same time, start the timer of a stopwatch."
4. **Demonstration fill (different subject):** "Add 5 cm3 of 1.0 moldm⁻³ hydrochloric acid into the beaker. At the same time, start the data logger recording pH."

---

**SKELETON D — Quantitative endpoint slot**
`"Stop the [measurement] once [numerical threshold] of [product / quantity] has been [collected / reached], as per the [measurement instrument]."`

1. **Slot grammar:** Stop + measurement device + once-threshold + of-quantity + as-per-instrument.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** Identify what product or quantity marks the end of one trial; choose its threshold value; name the instrument you read it from.
3. **Original fill:** "Stop the timer once 10 cm3 of oxygen gas has been collected, as per the gas syringe."
4. **Demonstration fill (different subject):** "Stop the timer once the solution turns fully opaque, as per the light sensor reading below 5 % transmittance."

**SKELETON E — Replication + IV sweep slot (composite)**
`"Repeat steps [X] to [Y] [number] times, for a total of [number] experimental attempts. Repeat steps [A] to [B], using a [apparatus] with [variable values] of [list]."`

1. **Slot grammar:** Two sentences. First = repeat block + total count. Second = expand the IV by repeating the whole block with new values listed.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** Choose which step range you will repeat (be careful — stop *before* the stop criterion if the stop criterion belongs to the repeated event). Then list the new IV values in a sequence (logical: descending temperature so equipment can cool without re-heating).
3. **Original fill:** "Repeat steps 3 to 5 thrice, for a total of four experimental attempts. Repeat steps 2 to 7, using a water bath with temperatures of 343.0K, 338.0K, 333.0K and 328.0K."
4. **Demonstration fill (different subject):** "Repeat steps 2 to 4 twice, for a total of three experimental attempts. Repeat steps 1 to 5, using a buffer solution with pH values of 4.0, 5.0, 6.0 and 7.0."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / time ordering**
- "At the same time" — used in step 4 to bind catalyst addition to stopwatch start ("At the same time, start the timer").
- "once" — used in step 6 to mark the stop condition ("once 10 cm3 of oxygen gas has been collected").
- "for a total of" — used in step 7 to convert repeated runs into a sample size ("for a total of four experimental attempts").

**Specification / quantification**
- "with a concentration of" — step 1 ("with a concentration of 0.1 moldm-3").
- "with a temperature of" — step 2 and step 8 ("with a temperature of 348.0K", "with temperatures of 343.0K…").
- "with a boiling tube attached to" — step 2 (specifies the assembly).

**Method / means ("how")**
- "by diluting" — step 1 ("by diluting a 1.0 moldm-3 sample"), encoding the transformation that produces the working solution.

**Evidence / instrument source**
- "as per" — step 6 ("as per the gas syringe"), attributing the reading to a specific instrument.

**Imperative procedure verbs**
- "Prepare", "Set up", "Measure … and pour", "Pour … and start", "Record", "Stop", "Repeat" — one verb per step, signalling action without explanation.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section relies on a **conditioned procedural enumeration pattern**: each step imposes a precondition that the next step consumes, and the numbering itself becomes the logical spine. The reader does not need connectives because the *order* carries the logic.

To explain a new procedure with the same pattern:

1. **Name and quantify the substrate first** — state what chemical you start with, at what concentration, made how.
2. **Set the environmental conditions second** — name the apparatus, lock the IV at its first value, assemble the measurement rig.
3. **Load the substrate into the prepared vessel** — specify volume, dispenser, transfer.
4. **Bundle the trigger with the catalyst (or vice versa)** — the moment the rate-defining substance enters, start the clock in the *same* step.
5. **Open an observation window** — tell the reader to record qualitative notes while the reaction runs.
6. **Define a quantitative stop criterion** — pick the threshold (volume, colour, mass, voltage) and name the instrument that reads it.
7. **Replicate at the current IV** — state how many repeats and the resulting total.
8. **Sweep the IV** — list the remaining values in a sensible order (so equipment transitions are physically efficient).
9. **Close with source attribution** — one sentence stating whose procedure you adapted.

The pattern works because it mirrors the *physics of the experiment*: substrate before environment before initiation before measurement before repetition before variation. A student replicating this skeleton with a different topic (e.g. enzyme rate vs pH, or reaction rate vs light intensity) needs only to swap reagents, IV unit, and endpoint quantity — the *order* of moves stays identical.
