## What This Section Does (content sequence)

For an **outlier discussion in a statistical analysis**, the moves should run:

1. **Locate the outlier numerically** (identify it, give the value, state where it falls relative to the threshold) — *because the reader needs the anomaly pinned before any explanation makes sense*.
2. **Offer an external/authority explanation** for why this country is high (research, citation, quote) — *because statistical outliers need a why, and authority gives credibility*.
3. **Add quantitative specifications** (GDP per capita, population, surplus) — *because one authority claim needs triangulated factual support to feel rigorous*.
4. **Translate a raw statistic into a mechanism** (surplus → exports > imports → high productivity) — *because a number alone doesn't explain; you must show what the number means*.
5. **Chain the mechanism to a human/economic consequence** (productivity → income → jobs) — *because the reader must see the so-what for ordinary people*.
6. **Close with "Therefore" / a verdict sentence** tying back to the original outlier — *because the causal chain has now earned the conclusion*.

The order is **anomaly → authority → triangulation → mechanism → consequence → verdict**. Each step supplies what the next needs: the anomaly motivates the explanation; the authority raises a follow-up; the follow-up forces a number; the number needs interpreting; the interpretation needs a human payoff; the payoff licenses the closing claim.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A (statistical summary paragraph):**
"[End of observation about where data clusters]. Additionally, this distribution has a [statistic 1] of [value], a [statistic 2] of [value], and an [statistic 3] of [value]."

1. *Slot 1* — visual observation completed from prior sentence (verb phrase, present tense, referencing a previously named feature). *Slot 2* — "Additionally" + triple list of summary statistics, each phrased "a [name] of [number]" (parallel noun phrases).
2. *How to fill with a different idea:* Slot 1: state where the bulk of your dataset visually sits; Slot 2: pick three descriptive statistics (e.g. mean, median, std dev; or Q1, Q3, IQR) and give concrete numbers for each.
3. *Original fill:* "fall around the low values… Additionally, this distribution has a median value of $125.55, a range of $964.89, and an IQR of $288.35."
4. *Demo fill (different topic):* "cluster between 60 and 75 as the scatter shows. Additionally, this distribution has a mean of 67.4, a standard deviation of 5.2, and a range of 22 points."

**SKELETON B (outlier identification + authority evidence):**
"There appears to be only one outlier or an extreme value, [value], which belongs to [case] exceeding the upper limit of [threshold]. I did some research on this matter and found out that [case]'s [variable] is indeed considered [superlative], which [authority] says '[quoted authority phrase]' ([source])."

1. *Slot 1* — hedged claim ("There appears to be") + numerical outlier + identification of the case + the threshold it exceeds. *Slot 2* — personal-research pivot ("I did some research…") + the variable being praised + "which [authority]" sub-clause containing an in-text quote.
2. *How to fill:* Slot 1: name your outlier case, give its value, compare it to a calculated threshold (Q3 + 1.5×IQR); Slot 2: pick one of the case's standout features, attribute it to a named authority, and embed a short quote in the sentence.
4. *Demo fill (different topic):* "There appears to be only one outlier, Kenya at 92%, which exceeds the upper limit of 78%. I did some research and found that Kenya's mobile-money penetration is indeed among the highest globally, which the World Bank describes as 'a remarkable financial inclusion story' (World Bank)."

**SKELETON C (numeric triangulation → mechanism → consequence):**
"[Case] has a [metric 1] of [value] for its [population descriptor]. In addition to that, the case runs a [metric 2] of [value], which implies that [case] [verb meaning of metric] indicating [abstract concept]. This [abstract concept], in turn, [downstream effect on people/economy]."

1. *Slot 1* — fact with one metric (per-capita figure + population descriptor). *Slot 2* — "In addition to that" + second metric + "which implies" causal clause translating the number into an abstract concept. *Slot 3* — "in turn" + chained consequence using a different agent ("people", "workers").
2. *How to fill:* Slot 1: choose a per-unit metric (per capita, per household); Slot 2: choose a second metric that you can unpack into a mechanism (a surplus, a rate, a ratio); Slot 3: push the abstract concept to a human-scale consequence (incomes, jobs, access).
4. *Demo fill (different topic):* "Vietnam has a literacy rate of 95% for its 97 million population. In addition to that, the country invests 5.7% of GDP in education, which implies that Vietnam prioritises human-capital formation, indicating strong workforce readiness. This readiness, in turn, secures higher wages and more skilled employment for young workers in manufacturing."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / addition**
- "Additionally, this distribution has…" — stacks parallel statistics.
- "In addition to that, the country runs…" — adds the next supporting fact.

**Causal / consequence**
- "which implies that the country exports more than it imports indicating high economic productivity" — number → concept.
- "This high economic productivity, in turn, secures higher incomes" — chains one step further.
- "Therefore, the…" — signals the verdict sentence that the chain has been building toward.

**Hedging / identification**
- "There appears to be only one outlier" — cautious claim before evidence.
- "exceeding the upper limit of $631.045" — locates the anomaly against a calculated threshold.

**Evidence handling**
- "I did some research on this matter and found out that…" — frames the next clause as research-based.
- "which the UN secretary-general Ban Ki-moon says 'a model for many countries' (United Nations)" — in-line authority + citation.
- "The country has a GDP per capita of $8,821.8…" — raw numerical citation.

**Specification / unpacking**
- "or an extreme value" — gives the technical synonym for the lay term "outlier".

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern used: anomaly → authority quote → triangulated fact → causal unpack → human-scale consequence → "Therefore" verdict.**

To replicate with a NEW idea:

1. **Pin the anomaly numerically.** Name one outlier or extreme value, give its figure, and locate it against the calculated threshold (e.g. Q3 + 1.5×IQR). One sentence.
2. **Hand to an authority.** Use "I did some research / according to / as noted by" and embed a short in-text quote from a named institution or person, with a citation in parentheses.
3. **Triangulate with a second fact.** Supply one more per-capita or per-unit number that supports the same picture; use "In addition to that" or "Furthermore" as the hinge.
4. **Unpack one of your numbers.** Use "which implies that… indicating [abstract concept]" — turn the raw figure into what it means.
5. **Push the concept to a human consequence.** Use "This [concept], in turn, [verb] [consequence for people]." Make the consequence land on ordinary citizens, workers, or patients, not abstract indicators.
6. **Close with a "Therefore" verdict.** Tie the chain back to the original outlier — explain why the outlier is now *expected*, or what its presence signifies about the dataset as a whole.
