## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **method continuation section** (mid-procedure, after initial setup is already done). The ordered content moves are:

1. **Time/transition marker** (e.g., "Next, after few hours") — tells the reader *when in the procedure* this block occurs and links backward.
2. **Overall method statement** (name the technique: "redox titration… Winkler method") — announces *what kind of work* is about to be shown.
3. **Identification of samples** ("on Flasks A, B, and C") — establishes *which objects* the steps will be performed on.
4. **Reagent sequence with exact volumes** ("6 drops of 1% MnSO4", "6 drops of H2SO4", "6 drops of KI") — gives *precise, reproducible quantities* in the order they must be added.
5. **Physical handling actions inline** ("inverted and shaken", "carefully added", "left to settle") — shows *how the experimenter physically manipulates* the sample at each step.
6. **Wait/time conditions** ("left to settle for 10 minutes", "left until settled precipitate solubilised") — controls *when the next step is valid*.
7. **Observable result check** ("brown precipitate of MnO2 sedimented", "brownish solution") — confirms *the chemistry actually happened* before moving on.
8. **Transfer + next-apparatus setup** ("transferred to a 250 cm³ conical flask… burette was filled") — prepares *the sample for the titration proper*.

Order matters because each step (4–7) **depends on the previous step's success**; the reagent order is dictated by the chemistry (Mn²⁺ must bind O₂ before acid releases I₂), and each result check (7) justifies proceeding to the next reagent or apparatus. A student replicating this for any analytical method should keep: **time cue → method name → target samples → reagents in chemical order → physical handling → wait → visual check → transfer**.

This section uses the **chronological-method-with-inline-confirmation** pattern: every chemical action is paired with either a wait condition or a visual observation that proves the step worked before the writer moves on. To replicate this pattern for a NEW procedure:

4. **State the first reagent with full precision** — concentration, volume, target vessel — and attach one physical-handling verb ("inverted and shaken", "vortexed", "swirled").
5. **Attach a wait condition and a visible result** ("left for X minutes until [colour/state] appeared") — the result sentence justifies continuation.
7. **Add the next reagent(s)** using "Then" as the bridge, repeating the volume-concentration-handling-result loop.
