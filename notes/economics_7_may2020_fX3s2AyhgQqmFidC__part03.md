## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **diagram-annotation section**. The ordered content moves are:

1. **Visual orientation by colour** — because the reader cannot see the graph, each line/shape must be named by appearance first.
2. **Formal term + plain-English unpacking for each labelled element** — because colour alone carries no economic meaning.
3. **Baseline equilibrium before any intervention** — because the whole argument is a before/after comparison, the "before" anchor must be locked in.
4. **Second curve/element introduced in parallel grammatical form** — because two identically-structured sentences invite the reader to compare the two objects directly.
5. **Spatial position stated, then explained causally** ("above as…higher than…: there is an externality") — because the vertical gap between curves *is* the externality, and the cause must be named.
6. **Consequence of the gap named** (welfare loss) — because once a divergence exists, its welfare implication must be stated.
7. **Policy goal stated** (shift MPC up to MSC) — because stating the goal bridges the descriptive diagram into the prescriptive mechanism of the tax.

This order is necessary because each move presupposes the previous: you cannot explain a shift without first showing two curves, and you cannot show two curves meaningfully without naming the cause of their gap.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern this section relies on is: **Visual orientation → Parallel definition → Spatial comparison → Causal mechanism → Consequence naming → Policy-intent bridge.**

Numbered replication steps:

1. **Orient the reader to the diagram visually.** Name each coloured line, area, or shape in the order the reader should scan it (e.g. "The curve in blue is…", "The triangle in cyan is…").
2. **Give each visual a formal term with acronym and a plain-English unpacking** using "is equal to" / "refers to" + a noun-phrase definition.
3. **Lock in a baseline state before any intervention** (an equilibrium point, an initial value), introduced with "as such" or "therefore" so it reads as a logical consequence of the definition.
4. **Introduce the second curve/area using the SAME grammatical rhythm** as the first definition. Parallel structure makes the comparison automatic.
5. **State the spatial relationship between the two objects** ("above", "below", "to the right of"), then **justify it causally** with "as [cause A] is [higher/lower] than [cause B]" and **name the concept** that the gap represents ("there is an externality").
6. **Name the welfare consequence of the gap** using "represents the [welfare term], the [unpacking of who bears it and why]".
7. **Bridge into the intervention** by stating its GOAL using "With the introduction of [policy], the goal is to [shift X] in order to [meet Y]". Do not yet explain how the policy works — just announce its target.

The reason this order works: the reader cannot understand a shift without first having two curves to compare; cannot compare without seeing them defined; and cannot judge the welfare meaning without the cause of the gap being named.
