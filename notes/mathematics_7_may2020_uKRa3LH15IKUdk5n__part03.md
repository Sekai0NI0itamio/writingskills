## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is an **axiom-application → derivation** section. The ordered moves are:

1. **State the equilibrium principle** (a system with non-zero net force will move until it is balanced).
2. **Apply it to the specific system** by listing every force vector on the towfish.
3. **Formally write the equilibrium equation** as a display.
4. **Pick out one term** (here, T⃗) and assert that *this particular term* carries physical meaning.
5. **Justify the claim** by linking the term to a geometric/physical chain (colinearity → angle → depth).
6. **Decompose the remaining forces** by component (which axes they live on).
7. **Derive an explicit expression** for the highlighted term from that decomposition.
8. **Loop back to the geometric chain** so the derived expression is given a physical interpretation.

**Why this order works**: each move sets up the next by narrowing scope — from a general physical principle, to a specific system, to one term within that system, to a component-level derivation of that term, to a physical reading of the result. A reader who tries to skip a step is forced to backtrack, so the logic is self-sealing.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing**: "Thus" — opens "Thus: −T⃗ = R⃗ + F⃗g + F⃗B".
- **Cause / consequence**: "since the tension vector and the ideal wire are always colinear"; "and thus determines the depth of the towfish"; "Since R⃗ only has a non-zero x component".
- **Specification / narrowing**: "at all times the vector sum of … must be 0" (the general axiom pinned to this system); "the tension vector T⃗ colinear with the wire is" (naming the specific form of T⃗).
- **Evidence handling**: "as shown in figure 4" — anchors the geometric claim in a labelled diagram.
- **Explanation verbs**: "is significant when considered along with" (claim of relevance); "determines" (used twice as a causal verb — angle → depth, and component → vector form).
- **Implicit connectors**: the semicolon ";" is used twice to chain subordinate justifications inside a single sentence, letting the writer stack mechanism steps without breaking the sentence.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern used**: *Principle → Specific Application → Formal Equation → Significance of One Term → Geometric/Mechanism Chain → Component Decomposition → Derived Expression → Physical Reading.*

Step-by-step to replicate with a new idea:

1. **State the governing principle** in one sentence, including what happens if the principle is violated (so the reader sees why it must hold).
2. **Pin the principle to the specific system** by naming every relevant quantity in a single list — usually four terms.
3. **Write the formal equation** as a centred display introduced by "Thus:" so the reader sees the claim made symbolic.
4. **Choose one term** from that equation and assert its *physical* significance in a "This property of X is significant when considered with Y" sentence.
5. **Open a mechanism chain** with "since …" giving a structural/geometric fact that ties the term to an intermediate observable (angle, ratio, orientation).
6. **Chain a second "thus"** so the intermediate observable is linked to a final physical outcome (depth, phase, height, speed), and cite the supporting figure.
7. **Decompose the *other* terms** by axis/component in a "Since A only has … and B, C only have …" sentence, so the reader sees why the highlighted term takes a particular form.
8. **Display the resulting expression** for the highlighted term.
9. **Loop back** to the mechanism chain from Step 5–6 and re-anchor the *derived expression* to its physical meaning, closing the loop between algebra and geometry.
