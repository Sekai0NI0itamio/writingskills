## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1** (numbered item 1)
- Move 1 (item label): *"The method at which the ball is dropped"* — names the variable being controlled.
- Hands to next sentence via the dash "—": the reader expects justification.
- Move 2 (purpose → threat → method, packed in one sentence): *"in order to ensure that the ball does not gain any extra acceleration from the throw of my hand, the ball has to be 'dropped'"* — purpose ("in order to ensure"), threat ("extra acceleration from the throw"), operational method ("dropped" by letting gravity accelerate the ball from the same place).
- Hands to Paragraph 2 via enumeration closure: item 1 closed by the imperative, item 2 follows.

**Paragraph 2** (numbered item 2)

- Hands via the dash to a justification clause.
- Move 2 (threat → imperative): *"The volume of the mass of the ball cannot change as it would directly affect the velocity observed, thus this has to be kept constant"* — threat ("would directly affect the velocity") and then the imperative conclusion ("thus this has to be kept constant").

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

A "Controlled Variables" sub-section performs this ordered sequence:

1. **List the variables that must be held constant** — one per numbered item; the label acts as a micro-heading.
2. **For each variable, explain the mechanism of interference** — state HOW the variable would distort the dependent measure if allowed to vary (e.g., "would directly affect the velocity", "as a result of pressure").
3. **State the operational constraint** — either a method ("dropped from the same place") or a fixed value ("at 250ml").
4. **(Often) link the control to an error class avoided** — connects the constraint back to a named methodological error (parallax, air resistance, etc.).

Why this order: the label commits to WHAT is being controlled; the mechanism earns the reader's belief (it justifies the control rather than asserting it); the constraint delivers the operational instruction the lab partner can act on; the error class gives methodological purpose. Omitting mechanism turns the section into an unjustified list; omitting the constraint turns it into theory with no procedure.

## Paragraph Skeletons

**Skeleton A — Consequence-driven control**
SLOT: *[Variable label]* — *[threat clause: variable would affect dependent measure as a result of mechanism], [connector] this must [also/therefore] be kept constant [optional: at VALUE].*

Slot instructions:
1. Label slot — noun phrase naming one variable (grammatical shape: bare noun / noun phrase, capitalised or article-led).
2. Threat slot — conditional consequence with an inlined mechanism ("would affect [X] as a result of [Y]").
3. Connector slot — "thus" / "hence" / "therefore".
4. Constraint slot — modal imperative + optional quantified value.

Original fill: *"Volume of Glycerol - The volume of the glycerol would also affect the velocity of the ball in the fluid as a result of pressure, hence this must also be kept constant at 250ml."*

Demo fill (different idea): *"Temperature of Water Bath — The temperature of the water bath would alter the rate of enzyme activity as a result of kinetic-energy effects on the substrate, therefore it must be kept constant at 37 °C."*

---

**Skeleton B — Purpose-driven control**
SLOT: *[Variable label]* — In order to ensure that *[error class] are [avoided/eliminated], [operational method or fixed distance].*

Slot instructions:
1. Label slot — noun phrase, often a "Distance/Position of X and Y" construction.
2. Error-class slot — methodological error name ("parallax errors", "systematic bias", "heat loss to surroundings").
3. Method slot — a fixed procedure, distance, or arrangement.

Original fill (truncated): *"Distance between measuring cylinder and camera - In order to ensure that any possible parallax errors are…"*

Demo fill: *"Position of the Light Source — In order to ensure that no shadows fall across the reaction vessel, the lamp is fixed at 30 cm directly above the beaker."*

---

**Skeleton C — Threat-with-cause, imperative close**
SLOT: *[Variable label]* — *[Variable] cannot change as it would [verb] the [dependent variable] observed, thus this has to be kept constant.*

Slot instructions:
1. Label slot — noun phrase led by "The" or proper noun.
2. Threat slot — present-tense consequence about the dependent measure.
3. Imperative slot — "this has to be kept constant" (no quantified value needed).

Original fill: *"The mass and the size of the ball - The volume of the mass of the ball cannot change as it would directly affect the velocity observed, thus this has to be kept constant."*

Demo fill: *"Concentration of HCl — The molarity of the hydrochloric acid cannot change as it would directly alter the reaction rate observed, thus this has to be kept constant."*

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing** — numerical enumeration "1.", "2.", "3.", "4." (each item label opens with a number) — drives the list forward without prose connectors; the dash "-" after the label is the recurring joiner.

**Cause / consequence** —
- *"in order to ensure that the ball does not"* (item 1) — purpose marker.
- *"as it would directly affect the velocity observed"* (item 2) — causal claim.
- *"would also affect the velocity of the ball"* (item 3) — causal claim.
- *"as a result of pressure"* (item 3) — mechanism marker.
- *"In order to ensure that any possible parallax errors are…"* (item 4) — purpose marker.

**Contrast / concession** — none used; the section is constructive throughout.

**Specification** —
- *"at 250ml"* (item 3) — quantifies the constraint.

**Evidence handling** — none; this is procedural, not evidential.

**Explanation verbs / modals** —
- *"has to be 'dropped' by letting gravity"* (item 1) — operational verb.
- *"this has to be kept constant"* (item 2) — modal imperative.
- *"must also be kept constant"* (item 3) — modal imperative.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

Pattern used: **VARIABLE → MECHANISM OF INTERFERENCE → CONSTRAINT** (a justification-anchored control block).

To replicate for any new controlled variable:

1. **State the variable** as a noun-phrase label (one per item; number it).
2. **Open the justification clause** with either "in order to ensure…" (purpose) OR "cannot change as it would…" (threat).
3. **Name the dependent measure distorted** (velocity, rate, absorbance, period, yield).
4. **Insert a mechanism phrase** ("as a result of [physics/chemistry principle]") so the interference is mechanistically explained, not asserted.
5. **Close with an imperative** — "thus this has to be kept constant" or "hence this must also be kept constant".
6. **Optional: append the fixed value** — "at 250ml", "at 30 cm", "at 37 °C".
7. **Move to the next item by numbering, not by prose** — the list itself is the connector; no transitional sentence is needed between items.

This yields the section's signature shape: a label-anchored micro-paragraph per variable, each just long enough to justify the control and short enough to remain a scannable list.
