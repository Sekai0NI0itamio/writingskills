## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **method section** organised as a **two-phase imperative protocol** preceded by a one-paragraph header. The standard order, and the reason each move sets up the next, is:

1. **Source attribution** — names where the protocol comes from so the reader can verify or replicate the design.
2. **Single-sentence aim** — names the *one* quantity the experiment will yield, so every later step can be judged as necessary or not.
3. **PPE rule** — installed before any handling step, so the writer is not interrupting mid-procedure.
4. **Data-convention N.B.** (unit conversion) — patches a likely error in the raw data the reader is about to see, declared before the steps that produce that data.
5. **Setup phase (Part I)** — assembles the *physical world* the experiment needs: liquids measured → liquids containerised → bath filled → bath heated → platform placed → vessels positioned → sensors added → wait for equilibrium. Each step is the *prerequisite* of the next.
6. **Data-collection phase (Part II)** — assembles the *measurement chain*: electrode A prepped → electrode B prepped → both placed → bridge measured/cut/soaked/placed → meter powered → value recorded → meter shut down. Each step is the *trigger condition* for the next.

A student replicating this for a different experiment should: **(a)** open with origin + single-sentence aim + PPE + any unit/convention N.B.; **(b)** write a Setup block whose steps march in the order "things must physically exist before the next thing can act on them"; **(c)** write a Data-Collection block whose steps march in the order "the circuit/instrument must be built and powered before a reading can be taken, and shut down after."

### Skeleton A — Method Header
**SKELETON:** "[Section number]. Method: Adapted from [source protocol] ([institution]). The direct objective of the method is to find [target quantity] ([unit]). [PPE items] should be worn throughout. N.B. [measurement type], measured in [unit A] ([symbol]), were converted to [unit B] ([symbol]) by [operation] ([citation])."

1. **Slot contents / grammar:**
   - Slot 1: section number + colon + past-participle phrase naming the source + parenthetical institution (noun phrase).
   - Slot 2: "The direct objective of the method is to find" + noun phrase + parenthetical SI unit.
   - Slot 3: comma-list of PPE objects + modal "should be worn throughout" (imperative-mood sentence).
   - Slot 4: "N.B." + noun phrase naming what was measured + past-passive "were converted to" + parenthetical citation.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** keep the slot count and grammar identical; only the *materials* change. Slot 1: pick a real lab manual entry. Slot 2: name the single deliverable of the experiment (e.g. rate constant, Rf value, absorbance). Slot 3: list the actual PPE for that experiment. Slot 4: pick the one conversion the reader would otherwise get wrong.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / imperative-chaining**
- "Using two 50±5cm3 measuring cylinders, **measure out** 50cm3 of" — open-then-verb imperative chaining apparatus to action.
- "Add each solution to a separate 100cm3 **beaker**" — "each" signals parallel actions across the two reagents.
- "Fill the 2dm3 measuring jug with water from the tap **and pour** into" — "and" chains two imperatives inside one step.
- "**First**-numbered steps 1, 2, 3…" — explicit ordinal numbering as the primary sequencing device.

**Cause / consequence (purpose-clause "for…to be")**
- "Start a stopwatch for 5 minutes for **complete thermal equilibrium to be reached**" — "for…to be reached" states the physical purpose of the wait.
- "Place the strip (salt bridge) in between the two half cells…**making sure** both ends" — "making sure" frames the placement as a *consequence condition*.
- "Add the boiled water to the kettle until **298.15K is reached**" — "until" + past passive marks a target state.

**Specification / N.B. notes**
- "N.B. Mercury based thermometer readings…**were converted to Kelvin (K)** by the addition of" — "N.B." + past passive + parenthetical unit for inline specification.
- "N.B at this point, **half of the beakers should be submerged**" — "at this point" anchors the rule to a specific step.
- "N.B. at least three quarters of the electrodes should be **submerged**" — "at least" quantifies the rule.
- "**Using the Ruler (±0.05cm)**, on the zinc foil" — parenthetical uncertainty after the instrument name.

**Evidence / authority handling**
- "**Adapted from** C2507 Intensive General Chemistry – E10" — "Adapted from" names the protocol origin.
- "**according to** the measurements" (paraphrased: "according to the measurements" → "**according to** the measurements" appears in the cutting step) — grounds the cut dimension in a prior measurement.
- "(**Lumenlearning**)" — parenthetical citation for the conversion rule.

**Definition / aim verbs**
- "**direct objective of the method is to find** Ecell (V)" — "is to find" defines the single deliverable.
- "**defined as** the salt bridge" (implicit in "Place the strip (salt bridge)") — parenthetical renaming acts as an in-line definition.

**Imperative / procedure verbs (the action lexicon)**
- Measure, Add, Fill, Pour, Place, Start, Attach, Cut, Clean, Soak, Turn on, Record, Turn off. (All quoted in context above.)

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses a **protocol-with-prerequisites** pattern, not a definition → unpack → example pattern. To replicate it for a new idea (any wet-lab or apparatus-based method), follow these steps in order:

1. **Open with the source.** Write "[Section]. Method: Adapted from [named protocol] ([institution])." This one move tells the reader the design is not improvised and locates the full reference.
2. **Name the single deliverable.** Write one sentence: "The direct objective of the method is to find [quantity] ([unit])." The deliverable must be a *single measurable thing*; if you have two, you have two methods.
3. **Front-load PPE.** Write one sentence listing the actual protective equipment with the modal "should be worn throughout." Place it *before* the steps so it never interrupts a handling action.
4. **Patch the one conversion the reader is most likely to misread.** Write a single "N.B." sentence in past passive with a parenthetical citation. This handles the convention that raw data will need before any step runs.
5. **Build Part I: the physical world.** Write 8–10 imperative steps in this exact internal order: (a) measure each reagent with stated tolerance, (b) put each in its own labelled container, (c) fill the bath, (d) heat the bath, (e) place the platform, (f) position the vessels on the platform, (g) insert the sensors, (h) wait for equilibrium. Each verb's *object* must already exist from the previous step.
6. **Insert one inline N.B. inside Part I** specifying the immersion/submersion rule — this is the only safety-critical geometry note and belongs at the moment it becomes relevant.
7. **Build Part II: the measurement chain.** Write 12–15 imperative steps in this exact internal order: (a) prepare electrode/sample 1 in paired imperatives (attach → measure → cut → attach), (b) prepare electrode/sample 2 the same way, (c) clean both, (d) immerse each, (e) insert one inline N.B. for the submersion rule, (f) build the bridge/connection with paired measure-cut-soak-place imperatives, (g) power the instrument, (h) record the reading, (i) return the instrument to its safe state and shut down.
8. **Carry an explicit tolerance in every measurement step.** Every "measure / cut / weigh / pipette" verb must be followed by a value with a ± symbol and the instrument's own uncertainty in parentheses.
9. **Keep the verb tense flat past-imperative for steps, past-passive for N.B.s.** Mixing tenses is the most common flow-breaker in this section; the reader's eye is trained by the present imperative sequence to track the procedure as a list of actions, and any tense shift signals a new kind of statement.
10. **Close the loop with a shut-down step.** End the data-collection list with a step that returns the instrument to its idle state — this hands the reader off to whatever (cleanup, Part III, or results) comes next and signals that the data point is final.
