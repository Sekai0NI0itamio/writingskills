## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1 (policy-overview continuation)**

1. **Fragment/continuation** — *"ensure further economic growth"* — tails an unfinished clause from a prior sentence, handing the reader onward only by context (the writer assumes the policy just named will do X).
2. **Claim (side-effect)** — *"Lowered interest rates contribute towards"* — asserts a problem caused by monetary policy, handing forward by raising an unresolved cost (why mention a side effect without an answer yet?).
3. **Claim with consequence** — *"Trade tensions are lowering confidence in the economy, resulting in fewer investments"* — establishes a cause→effect chain, handing forward by accumulating yet another cost that demands a remedy.
4. **Solution claim** — *"Additionally, interventionalist supply side policies could aid Australia"* — pivots with **Additionally** from problems to remedy, bundling two functions (correct market failures + ensure growth) in one move, and hands the reader out of the paragraph by closing the loop on the earlier "ensure growth" tail.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **situational-diagnosis section** that precedes evaluation or recommendation work. The ordered content moves are:

1. **Residual policy recap** — first, the writer closes a prior policy paragraph by tying it back to growth (so the reader carries the policy thread).
2. **Problem accumulation (cost side)** — second, the writer lists two side-effect/cost claims (housing prices; investment confidence) to build the case that the situation needs explaining.
3. **Policy suggestion** — third, the writer previews the next remedy (supply-side intervention) using **Additionally**, so the reader knows a solution is coming.
4. **Figure introduction** — fourth, the writer announces the diagram so the abstract claims now have a visual referent.
5. **Curve/label definition** — fifth, the writer names the LRAS, point Yp, and the target intersection, because every later sentence depends on these labels.
6. **Current-state diagnosis** — sixth, the writer names the condition (recessionary gap).
7. **Geometric mechanism + target specification** — seventh, the writer explains *why* it's a gap by locating the SRAS∩LRAS target.
8. **Actual vs. target contrast** — eighth, the writer marks the current point (Yc|Pc) against the target, leaving the size of the gap for the next sentence.

**Why this order works**: the reader cannot interpret a diagram before the policy context is set (moves 1–3), cannot read labels before they are defined (5 before 6–8), and cannot grasp "below target" until the target itself has been located (7 before 8). The whole sequence front-loads context so the diagram does explanatory, not decorative, work.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / addition**
- *"Additionally, interventionalist supply side policies could aid…"* — stacks a second remedy on top of an earlier one.
- *"Currently, the economy is in a recessionary gap"* — time-marker that anchors the diagnosis to the present.

**Cause / consequence**
- *"Trade tensions are lowering confidence in the economy, resulting in fewer investments"* — present-continuous cause + "resulting in" + consequence noun phrase.
- *"contribute towards the social problem of inflated housing market prices"* — "contribute towards" frames the link as contributory, not sole.

**Contrast / opposition**
- *"Instead, the current eqilibrium is at (Yc|Pc)"* — **Instead** flips from the target (sentence 4) to the actual.
- *"which is below the target output and price level"* — embedded spatial contrast.

**Specification / location**
- *"located at (Yp|Pp), which is where the short run aggregate supply (SRAS) intersects the LRAS curve"* — pair of coordinates + relative-clause that specifies the geometric spot.
- *"shown by the long run aggregate supply (LRAS) curve, and marked as point Yp"* — name + abbreviation + label.

**Evidence handling / figure-handling**
- *"Figure 1 portrays the Australian economic situation"* — opens the figure's interpretive frame.

**Explanation verbs**
- *"is shown by"* (the LRAS curve) — identifies a visual referent.
- *"portrays"* — announces the figure's function.
- *"could aid … and correct … while ensuring"* — bundles two remedy-functions into one move.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The section relies on a **diagram-reading explanation pattern**: *figure-introduction → label-definition → named diagnosis → geometric mechanism (where the target lives) → contrast (where we actually are)*. To replicate it for a NEW idea:

1. **Open the figure.** Write one short present-tense sentence that names the figure number and announces what situation it depicts (e.g. *"Figure N portrays the [topic] situation."*). This hands the reader into the visual.
2. **Define the reference labels.** In a second sentence, give the full name + abbreviation of the curve or line that represents the *target* state, and assign it a single-letter label (e.g. *"…shown by the [name] ([abbr]) curve, and marked as point [label]."*). The reader now has a vocabulary for what follows.
3. **Name the diagnosis.** In a third sentence, use **Currently** to stamp the diagnosis in the present tense and reach for an established term (e.g. *"recessionary gap," "negative output gap"*). Hand forward by leaving the magnitude unstated.
4. **Locate the target geometrically.** In a fourth sentence, explain *why* the current state falls short by locating the target on the diagram using a coordinate pair + a relative clause ("which is where X intersects Y"). This is the mechanism move — it converts the named diagnosis into a picture.
5. **Contrast with the actual.** Open the fifth sentence with **Instead** and give the current coordinate pair, then a relative clause that states the *direction* of the gap (below/above the target). Stop one sentence short of quantifying the gap — that gap-size sentence becomes the natural lead-in to the next section.

Follow these five steps in order and the reader is carried from "what diagram am I looking at?" to "where exactly is the problem on it?" without ever losing the labels you introduced.
