## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern:** Spatial observation → causal hypothesis → specific confirming instance → interpreted implication (a "zoom-out" chain that moves from what you see, to why it might be, to where it's most visible, to what it means).

1. **Name the analytical tool and what it reveals.** State which figure or data view you are using and what type of understanding it provides (e.g., "Figure X provides a spatial/temporal understanding of [phenomenon] in [context]").
2. **State the observed pattern and cross-reference for consistency.** Report the directional trend you see and cite another figure or dataset that confirms it (e.g., "[Variable] appears to [pattern], as noted in [reference]").
3. **Propose a causal mechanism.** Use a hedging verb ("suggests," "could be due to") to name the likely driver and assign it a functional role (e.g., "[Source] suggests this could be due to [cause], which serves as [role]").
4. **Narrow to a specific instance.** Use "in particular at [sites/instances]" to pinpoint where the pattern is most pronounced and name the specific variable that shifts (e.g., "as [context] progresses, in particular at [instances], [variable] [change]").
5. **Draw the final implication.** Use "This indicates" to connect the specific evidence back to a broader interpreted consequence (e.g., "This indicates [implication], suggesting [broader consequence]").
