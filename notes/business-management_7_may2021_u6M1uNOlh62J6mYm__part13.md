## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a Conclusion section built as a *weighed-balance recap*. The ordered moves are:

1. **Acknowledge the drawback first** (the counter-argument concession). Sets up the rest as a *defence* rather than pure cheerleading; the writer earns credibility by admitting the inconvenient point before answering it.
2. **Show stakeholder overlap with positive feedback**. Establishes that the two parties are *already aligned*, so the proposal is not a forced fit.
3. **Quantify beneficiary demand** ("large majority"). Moves from qualitative alignment to a measurable advantage.
4. **Supply an external macro-cause for demand** (pandemic-driven declining fitness) **plus survey evidence**. Generalises beyond the school into a societal trend, then anchors it in primary data.
5. **Identify a gap in current provision** (absence of an "old-masculine" sport) and frame it as lost revenue.
6. **Name one concrete instance of a sport that fills the gap** and apply a strategic tool (market share / star). Demonstrates how theory meets practice.
7. **Dismiss the remaining pricing objection** (no discriminatory pricing needed).
8. **Final overall verdict** ("Therefore, the re-concession is highly recommended").

Why this order: each move *narrows the defence*. The reader is taken from (a) "yes, there's a drawback" → (b) "but parties agree" → (c) "and customers want it" → (d) "and society needs it" → (e) "and you lack a product" → (f) "and here's the specific product that fits the matrix" → (g) "and price won't be a barrier" → (h) verdict. A student replicating this should order their bullets from broadest justification to most specific operational point, ending on the verdict.

- Slot 2: a stakeholder-alignment claim with embedded evidence (e.g. "Both [parties] overlap in their mission … with positive feedback").
- Slot 3: a consequence signalled by "and thus / therefore" (e.g. "and thus a re-concession is likely").

How to fill with a different idea: slot 1 = pick the *one* realistic downside of your proposal and name it in a noun phrase beginning with "A/An [action] by [method] is [negative] for [stakeholder]"; slot 2 = name two bodies whose stated aims already match, plus a short evidence phrase ("positive feedback from X"); slot 3 = write a "thus/therefore" sentence that delivers the verdict of this cluster.

Original: "A re-concession by utilising real time lessons of the school is inconvenient for the school. Both businesses overlap in their mission and vision statements with positive feedback from school staff, and thus a re-concession is likely."

Demo fill (different idea — proposing a school canteen switch to a local farm supplier):
"A switch to sourcing from the regional farm co-op is logistically inconvenient for the canteen staff. Both the school and the co-op share sustainability aims in their mission statements with positive feedback from the PTA, and thus the switch is feasible."

**Skeleton B — Beneficiary demand with macro-cause and survey evidence**
- Slot 1: beneficiary claim ("The [proposal] may help [stakeholders'] satisfaction with …").
- Slot 2: quantifier of demand introduced by "given that a large majority would consider …".
- Slot 3: external cause introduced by "due to the [trend] as a result of [event]".
- Slot 4: evidence anchor ("which is reflected within the surveys").
- Slot 5: implication for the business ("an advantage the [firm] can take").

How to fill: slot 1 = pick the stakeholder group whose satisfaction rises; slot 2 = insert a percentage or "large majority / most" of an action verb (would consider / would buy / would attend); slot 3 = name a current societal trend + its trigger; slot 4 = name the data source that captures the trend; slot 5 = state the strategic takeaway for the firm.

Original: "The concession may help the students' and parents' satisfaction with sports of the school, given that a large majority would consider attending professional lessons. There is a high demand for sports due to the declining fitness of individuals as a result of the pandemic, which is reflected within the surveys and is an advantage the FFC can take."

There is a high demand for digital skills due to the expanding AI job market as a result of automation, which is reflected within the student interest surveys and is an advantage the partner firm can take."

**Skeleton C — Gap → concrete instance → matrix verdict**
- Slot 1: gap claim ("There is a lack of a[n] '[type]' … which is causing a loss of potential customers and thus sales").
- Slot 2: concrete instance of a product/service that fills the gap.
- Slot 3: strategic-tool application ("can be considerably benefited with [tool position], potentially making it a [BCG category]").

How to fill: slot 1 = name the missing category in the offer with a quoted label and frame its absence as lost revenue; slot 2 = name one specific product that occupies that category; slot 3 = apply a strategic model (BCG matrix, Ansoff, Porter) and name the resulting quadrant.

Original: "There is a lack of an 'old-masculine' sport which is causing a loss of potential customers and thus sales for the FFC. Pilates is a sport which can be considerably benefited with market share due to the concession, potentially making it a star."

Demo fill (different idea — proposing an after-school music programme with a recording studio partner):

**Skeleton D — Objection dismissal with verdict**
- Slot 1: an operational objection ("The price set by the [firm] is appropriate").
- Slot 2: a negative of the form "and does not require any discriminatory [policy]".

How to fill: slot 1 = identify the operational variable that might worry the reader (price, scheduling, materials); slot 2 = negate the need for a discriminatory or extra-specific policy using "does not require any …".

Original: "The price set by the FFC is appropriate and does not require any discriminatory pricing specific for the concession."

Demo fill (different idea — proposing a mobile-app loyalty scheme with the same partner):

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / signposting**
- "given that a large majority would consider attending" — conditional specifier; signals a quantifier will follow to back the previous claim.

**Cause / consequence**
- "due to the declining fitness of individuals as a result of the pandemic" — compound cause chain; embeds one cause ("as a result of the pandemic") inside another ("due to … declining fitness").
- "which is causing a loss of potential customers and thus sales for the FFC" — cause-to-consequence with "and thus" linking two losses (customers → sales).
- "and thus a re-concession is likely" — short verdict connector.
- "Therefore, the re-concession is highly recommended" — final synthesis marker.

**Concession / contrast**
- Implicit "despite / although" structure: starts with the inconvenience ("inconvenient for the school") then pivots — no explicit connective, but the *position* of the bullet functions as the concession move.

**Specification / narrowing**
- "in particular / specifically" not used; instead the writer narrows by *naming an instance* ("Pilates is a sport which …").

**Evidence handling**
- "which is reflected within the surveys" — evidence-as-reflection connector.
- "with positive feedback from school staff" — embedded evidence inside the alignment claim.

**Explanation / definition verbs**
- "defined as" not present, but strategic-tool language is used: "considerably benefited with market share due to the concession, potentially making it a star" — verb chain of application ("benefited with …, potentially making it") that ties the instance to the BCG quadrant.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern this section uses is **weighted-balance recap with a theory-tool climax**: each bullet is a self-contained reason that *tilts the balance* in favour of the recommendation, and the chain ends by *applying a strategic model* to one concrete instance before a final verdict.

To replicate the pattern on a new idea, follow these steps:

1. **Open with the honest drawback.** Write one bullet naming the *one* realistic negative of your proposal in a noun phrase beginning with "A/An [proposal] by [method] is [negative] for [stakeholder]."
2. **Add a stakeholder-alignment bullet.** Name two bodies whose stated aims match, plus a short piece of evidence ("positive feedback from X"), and close with "and thus [proposal] is likely/feasible."
3. **Add a beneficiary-demand bullet.** State who benefits, then introduce the quantifier with "given that a large majority would [verb]."
4. **Add a macro-cause bullet.** Introduce the external trend with "due to [trend] as a result of [event]," then anchor it with "which is reflected within [data source]," and close with "an advantage [the firm] can take."
5. **Add a gap bullet.** State "There is a lack of a '[type]' … which is causing a loss of potential customers and thus sales for [the firm]."
6. **Add a model-application bullet.** Name a concrete product/service that fills the gap, then apply a strategic tool in one sentence: "can be considerably benefited with [tool metric] due to [proposal], potentially making it a [model category]."
7. **Add an operational-objection bullet.** Dismiss pricing or logistics: "The [variable] set by [firm] is appropriate and does not require any discriminatory [policy] specific for [proposal]."
8. **Close with a synthesis verdict.** One short sentence: "Therefore, [proposal] is highly recommended."
