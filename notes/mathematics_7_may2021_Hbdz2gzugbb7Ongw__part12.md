## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1**
- **S1** "There are several evident properties for all parameters seen in the graphs:" — *signposting claim*: flags that a multi-item enumeration is coming. Hands the reader to the bullets by directly announcing them.
- **Bullets** ("no clear period…/sharp maxima…/no clear relationship…") — *evidence/specification*: three distinct observable features that deliver the count promised by S1. Hands the reader onward by closing the bullet block and visually separating from the next move.

**Paragraph 2**
- **S1** "These properties can also be seen in online demonstrations of double pendulums. (Neumann, 2002)" — *authority/corroboration*: borrows outside validation for the just-listed bullets. Hands to the next sentence by shifting register from "what we see" to "how the next data was made."
- **S2** "The trajectory was calculated by finding x1 , x2 , y1 , y2 at each moment using equations (9) to (12)." — *method/transition*: explains the procedure that produced the next figure. Hands the reader forward by handing them to the figure the sentence describes.

**Paragraph 3**
- **S1** "Again, multiple conclusions can be drawn:" — *recursive claim*: mirrors the opening move of Paragraph 1, signalling that another enumeration is coming. Hands to bullets.
- **Bullets** ("asymmetrical…continuous circular arc…/no clear pattern…/reach a height above the original") — *evidence/specification*: three trajectory-level findings, with the last one left slightly "fatter" so it can be picked up. Hands the reader to the deep-dive by leaving the final bullet ready for unpacking.

**Paragraph 4**
- **S1** "The last property can be explained via energy conservation." — *mechanism-flag*: singles out the final bullet and names the governing principle. Hands the next sentence as the start of an explanation it has just announced.
- **S2** "As the one falls and loses energy, the other gains energy and can get back up to or over its initial height." — *unpack (causal chain)*: shows the energy swap frame by frame. Hands the next sentence as the observable consequence of that chain.
- **S3** "This allows the lower mass to form large loops." — *implication*: ties the abstract mechanism back to the behaviour already listed. Hands the next sentence by opening the door to generalisation.
- **S4** "This phenomenon continues throughout all other trials." — *verdict/scope-extension*: lifts the finding out of the single trial and into the whole investigation. Closes the section by closing the loop with "all other trials" the reader knows are coming.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Signpost an enumeration** ("There are several evident properties…") — sets up a multi-item list the reader can scan.
2. **List bullet-level observations from graph 1** — delivers what was promised in step 1.
3. **Corroborate with an external authority** ("These properties can also be seen in online demonstrations…(Neumann, 2002)") — borrows credibility from outside the experiment.
4. **Bridge to the next figure with a method note** ("The trajectory was calculated…") — explains how the next visual was produced so its bullets are interpretable.
5. **Re-summon the enumeration move** ("Again, multiple conclusions can be drawn:") — re-uses the same scaffolding for the second figure, so the reader recognises the rhythm.
6. **List bullet-level observations from graph 2** — same job as step 2, applied to a new dataset.
7. **Flag the most striking bullet for deep-dive** ("The last property can be explained…") — selects one item from the list and elevates it.
8. **Name the underlying principle** ("…via energy conservation") — supplies the physical law that will run the next few sentences.
9. **Unpack the cause chain** ("As the one falls and loses energy, the other gains…") — walks the mechanism step by step.
10. **Reconnect mechanism to observable consequence** ("This allows the lower mass to form large loops.") — lands the abstract principle back in the bullet language.
11. **Extend scope across the whole investigation** ("This phenomenon continues throughout all other trials.") — closes by generalising beyond this one trial.

Order rationale: observation-before-explanation is mandatory (you cannot unpack what you have not yet listed); corroboration-between-enumerations is the only place an external source fits without interrupting a deep-dive; deep-dive-last because it depends on the enumeration it picks from; scope-extension absolutely last because it would be premature before the mechanism is established.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A — "Signpost then list, then corroborate"**
"[Signpost sentence naming N evident properties] for all parameters seen in the [data source]: [bullet list of N distinct features]. These properties can also be seen in [external source type] of [phenomenon]. ([Authority, year])"

1. **Slots:** (i) quantifier + adjective + noun ("several evident properties"); (ii) scope phrase ("for all parameters seen in the graphs"); (iii) 3 bullets, each a short noun-phrase observation; (iv) external corroboration noun phrase ("online demonstrations"); (v) bracketed author+date.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** slot 1 = "several evident properties"; slot 2 = "for all parameters seen in the [dataset, e.g. frequency–amplitude plot]"; slot 3 = three short observations you can literally point to on the figure; slot 4 = a public source (tutorial site, textbook figure, video) that shows the same features; slot 5 = a real reference.
3. **Original fill:** "There are several evident properties for all parameters seen in the graphs: [no clear period; sharp maxima and minima; no clear relationship between behaviours]. These properties can also be seen in online demonstrations of double pendulums. (Neumann, 2002)".
4. **Demonstration fill (different subject):** "There are several evident properties for all parameters seen in the waveforms: [the signal never returns to baseline; spikes cluster in pairs; spike height does not decay over time]. These properties can also be seen in sample ECG traces of atrial fibrillation. (Smith, 2019)".

**SKELETON B — "Recursive enumeration on a second figure"**
"Again, multiple conclusions can be drawn: [bullet list of three observations about a second piece of data, with the last one flagged as the most striking]."

1. **Slots:** (i) recurrence marker ("Again"); (ii) lead noun ("conclusions"); (iii) bullet 1 (a stable/repeating feature); bullet 2 (an irregular feature); bullet 3 (the feature to be deep-dived).
2. **How to fill:** open with "Again" so the reader recognises the move from skeleton A; vary bullet 1 between "what is regular" and bullet 2 between "what is chaotic"; make bullet 3 the one that surprises or needs justification.
3. **Original fill:** "Again, multiple conclusions can be drawn: [upper mass sweeps out an asymmetrical, continuous circular arc…; the motion of the lower mass has no clear pattern…; both masses can reach a height above the original]."
4. **Demonstration fill (different subject):** "Again, multiple conclusions can be drawn: [the response amplitude saturates near the resonance frequency; the phase lag crosses π/2 over a narrow band; the system keeps oscillating long after the driving force stops]."

**SKELETON C — "Deep-dive one bullet via principle → mechanism → observable → scope"**
"The last property can be explained via [principle]. As [cause clause], [consequence clause]. This allows [observable behaviour already listed in the bullets]. This phenomenon continues throughout [the rest of the investigation]."

1. **Slots:** (i) reference word ("The last property"); (ii) governing principle ("energy conservation"); (iii) cause clause (subject loses X); (iv) consequence clause (subject gains X); (v) re-statement of the bullet in mechanism terms; (vi) scope phrase ("all other trials").
2. **How to fill:** select the bullet you most want to justify; name a single physical/mathematical principle; write the two clauses as a mirrored "loses/gains", "drops/rises" structure so they read as a chain; restate the bullet using words from the bullet itself to make the link obvious; finish with a phrase that says "this still held later."
3. **Original fill:** "The last property can be explained via energy conservation. As the one falls and loses energy, the other gains energy and can get back up to or over its initial height. This allows the lower mass to form large loops. This phenomenon continues throughout all other trials."
4. **Demonstration fill (different subject):** "The last property can be explained via conservation of momentum. As the cue ball strikes and transfers momentum, the target ball gains speed and can travel past the pocket line. This allows the target ball to scatter the side balls. This phenomenon continues throughout all other shots in the run."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / signposting**
- "There are several evident properties for all parameters seen in" — *opens an enumeration*
- "Again, multiple conclusions can be drawn" — *re-opens an enumeration on the next dataset*

**Cause / consequence**
- "As the one falls and loses energy, the other gains" — *mirrored cause/effect clause*
- "This allows the lower mass to form" — *links mechanism to consequence*
- "can get back up to or over its initial height" — *consequence as observable*

**Specification (rather than explicit connectives)**
- Bulleted lines after "There are several evident properties" — *specification by listing*

**Evidence handling**
- "These properties can also be seen in online demonstrations of" — *outside corroboration*
- "(Neumann, 2002)" — *bracketed authority*

**Explanation verbs / framing**
- "can be explained via energy conservation" — *names the governing principle*

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

Pattern: **enumeration → flag → principle → mirrored cause/effect chain → observable restatement → scope extension**

1. **Signpost N observations** in one sentence ("There are several evident properties…").
2. **List the observations as bullets** (3 items, each short, each pointed at the figure).
3. **(Optional) Corroborate** the list with an outside source plus a bracketed citation.
4. **Open the second figure with the same signposting phrase** ("Again, multiple conclusions can be drawn:") to mark recurrence.
5. **List three more bullets**, deliberately leaving the last one to be deep-dived.
6. **Flag that last bullet** with the phrase "The last property can be explained via [principle]."
7. **Name one governing principle** (a conservation law, a limiting case, an idealised model — something compact).
8. **Write the mechanism as a mirrored cause/effect pair** ("As X loses [quantity], Z gains [quantity]…") so the reader feels the balance.
9. **Restate the bullet** in mechanism terms ("This allows [observable].") using the bullet's own words so the link is unmistakable.
10. **Extend the scope** with "This phenomenon continues throughout [rest of dataset/trials/conditions]." to close the section by generalising.
