## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1**

**Move 1 — Continuation (carries from prior text):** "these laws enforceable."
→ This is a fragment completing a clause from the previous section. It functions as a *handoff* — the previous paragraph was making a claim about laws, and this fragment closes that thought so the new paragraph can pivot. The reader is handed forward by the dangling claim, which the next sentence now expands.

**Move 2 — Contrast (two-perspective framing):** "Some highlight the importance of strong institutions, while others place importance on an approach based in civil society and education."
→ This sentence maps *both sides of a debate* side by side. Its job is to plant a dichotomy (top-down institutional vs bottom-up civil-society) so that the section has a tension to build on. The reader is handed to S3 by *logical consequence*: given these two camps, the question of enforcement becomes worth raising.

**Move 3 — Transition + Central Question (motivation move):** "With that in mind, why is the enforcement of laws protecting the LGBT minority in Brazil so problematic?"
→ The phrase "With that in mind" is a *consequence connective* that converts S2's debate into a research problem. The sentence form shifts from declarative to interrogative, signalling the section is now posing its driving question. Hand-off to S4 is *direct answer*: a question like this demands a scope statement.

**Move 4 — Scope/Focus Statement (commitment move):** "For this internal assessment, the political issue that I will be focusing on is the enforcement of the human rights of the LGBT population in Brazil."
→ This *answers* the prior question by narrowing it. The phrase "For this internal assessment" frames the move as academic commitment; "I will be focusing on" is the classic IA declaration formula. Nothing follows, so this sentence *closes* the section by staking the topic.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This section is a **transition / focus-statement paragraph** that performs four ordered moves:

1. **Carry-over fragment** — picks up a half-finished thought from the prior paragraph so the new paragraph starts mid-thought and stays cohesive.
2. **Two-perspective contrast** — names opposing scholarly/activist approaches to the issue, building a tension the IA can resolve.
3. **Motivated question** — uses "With that in mind" to convert the contrast into a single research question, signalling the IA's driving problem.
4. **Scope narrowing** — explicitly commits the IA to one specific angle, closing the section.

**Why this order works:** the fragment grounds the reader in continuity; the contrast supplies the intellectual stakes; the question makes those stakes urgent; the scope statement picks the side of the tension the IA will answer. A student replicating this on a *different topic* (e.g. deforestation enforcement, indigenous land rights) only needs: a continuation fragment → two camps → one "why is X so problematic?" → one "I will focus on Y" commitment.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A — Focus-statement paragraph**
"[Fragment continuing prior idea]. Some highlight the importance of [Approach A], while others place importance on an approach based in [Approach B]. With that in mind, why is [phenomenon] so problematic? For this [assessment type], the [topic] that I will be focusing on is [specific focus]."

1. **What each slot holds:**
   - Slot 1 (fragment): noun phrase + past-participle; declarative tail completing a prior clause.
   - Slot 2 (contrast): full sentence using *some…while others…* parallelism, with two parallel noun phrases naming opposing camps.
   - Slot 3 (question): full sentence beginning with *With that in mind*, ending in *?*, with the contested phenomenon as subject.
   - Slot 4 (scope): full sentence beginning *For this…*, ending *is* + noun phrase of the focus.

2. **How to fill with a different idea:**
   - Slot 1: finish whatever noun + adjective + verb idea you started in the previous paragraph (e.g. "these protections enforceable").
   - Slot 2: pick two camps that genuinely disagree about your issue (institutional vs cultural, federal vs local, punitive vs restorative).
   - Slot 3: write the single most interesting *why* question those two camps raise together.
   - Slot 4: choose one concrete slice (one population, one law, one region) inside that question.

3. **Original filled version:** "these laws enforceable. Some highlight the importance of strong institutions, while others place importance on an approach based in civil society and education. With that in mind, why is the enforcement of laws protecting the LGBT minority in Brazil so problematic? For this internal assessment, the political issue that I will be focusing on is the enforcement of the human rights of the LGBT population in Brazil."

4. **Demonstration fill with a different idea:** "these protections enforceable. Some highlight the importance of federal legislation, while others place importance on an approach based in indigenous self-governance. ## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern this section relies on is a **Context → Debate → Driving Question → Commitment** opening for an IA. To replicate on a new idea:

1. **Carry a fragment.** End your prior paragraph on a half-finished thought (e.g. "these reforms", "this framework"). Begin this paragraph with the word that closes it ("these reforms workable"). One noun phrase, no full sentence.
2. **Map the debate.** Write one sentence with *some…while others…* parallelism. Pick two genuinely opposed camps, each named by a short noun phrase (institutions vs civil society; legislation vs self-governance; deterrence vs rehabilitation).
3. **Convert debate to a question.** Begin the next sentence with the connective *With that in mind,* then write a single *why/how* question whose subject is the contested phenomenon. End with *?*.
4. **Commit the focus.** Begin the final sentence with *For this [essay/assessment],* then name the specific population, law, region, or mechanism the IA will analyse. Use *I will be focusing on is* + concrete noun phrase. Do not hedge.

The logic path is: *inherited claim → split into two camps → camps generate urgency → urgency resolves into a researchable scope.* Each step supplies what the next needs; nothing is decorative.
