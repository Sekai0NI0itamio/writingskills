## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The dominant pattern in this section is **evidence-chain under a claim**, working *cause-first*, *instance-second*, *escalation-third*. To reproduce it on a new idea:

1. **Open with the systemic cause.** Name the policy / trend / season that is creating pressure. Use present-perfect or present tense so it feels ongoing.
2. **Drop one concrete, numbered example** ("Year 1s, 49 students"). The number is non-negotiable — it forces the listener to picture a real room.
3. **Convert the number into a threshold verdict** ("the limit"). Use first-person hedge *"I would say"* so it reads as judgment, not measurement.
4. **Reveal the coping action** and its own numbers (split 40 + 10). This shows the strain is *managed*, not solved.
5. **Pivot with a list marker** ("The other thing…", "One more thing…") and stack the remaining unmet needs.
6. **For each need, give a current-state sentence** that names what is wrong with what they have. Do not describe the ideal — describe the failure.
7. **End the most serious item with a sensory mechanism**, not an adjective. Replace "bad" with "if you fall you cut your skin to the bone."
8. **Close that cluster with a cheap-fix verdict** so the reader is left thinking the absence is a choice.
9. **When the topic shifts, reframe before you evidence.** Open with *"I don't think X is due to A, I think X is due to B."* Refusing the question's premise is the move that earns trust.
10. **Evidence the new cause with a descending series** — best case → middle case → worst case — so the curve itself is the proof. Land on the value verdict ("crucial and important") only after the descent.
