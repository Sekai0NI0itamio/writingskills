## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1**
1. "producers. Therefore, to protect the domestic industry and avoid unemployment, China's Commerce Ministry has chosen to impose really high protective tariffs on Australian wine." — **Claim + justification**: states the policy action and its stated rationale. It hands the reader to the next sentence by establishing the intervention whose effects must now be traced.

**Paragraph 2**
1. "The potential effect of this tariff is shown below:" — **Transition/setup**: signals that a diagram will carry the analysis forward. It hands the reader to the diagram by promising visual evidence for the claim just made.

**Paragraph 3**
1. "Before the tariff imposition, 0Q4 of wine was being demanded and supplied at PA+Subsidy, where 0Q1 was domestically supplied and Q1Q4 was imported." — **Baseline/context**: establishes the pre-intervention market equilibrium. It hands the reader to the next sentence by setting the "before" state that the "after" state will be compared against.
2. "After '... a year-long investigation ...', a tariff of 212% (worst-case-scenario) is imposed on imports, resulting in extra cost to Australian producers." — **Intervention/mechanism**: introduces the policy shock with its magnitude. It hands the reader to the next sentence by creating the causal trigger whose consequences are now traced.
3. "Hence, imports fall from Q1Q4→Q2Q3, and domestic supply increases from 0Q1→0Q2." — **Direct effect**: shows the quantity shifts caused by the tariff. It hands the reader to the next sentence by establishing the volume changes that then drive price changes.
4. "As a result, the price of wine increases from PA+subsidy→PA+tariff, and quantity supplied/demanded falls from 0Q4→0Q3." — **Secondary effect**: traces the price and quantity consequences flowing from the volume shifts. It hands the reader to the next sentence by completing the market-level outcomes and opening space for distributional effects.
5. "The government receives the tax revenue of a shaded (green) rectangular area; and additionally, there occur" — **Additional consequence**: begins to unpack who gains (government revenue) and what else follows. It hands the reader forward to whatever deadweight loss or welfare analysis continues beyond the excerpt.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **States the policy action and its justification** — establishes what was done and why, giving the reader a clear subject and purpose before any analysis begins.
2. **Promises a diagram as evidence** — signals the analytical tool that will visualise the claim, setting up a structured before-and-after comparison.
3. **Establishes the pre-intervention baseline** — defines the starting equilibrium (price, quantities, domestic vs. imported shares) so the reader has a reference point.
4. **Introduces the intervention with its magnitude** — specifies the tariff rate and its origin, creating the causal shock that the rest of the section traces.
5. **Traces the direct quantity effects** — shows how imports and domestic supply shift first, because volume changes logically precede price changes in a supply-demand mechanism.
6. **Traces the secondary price and quantity effects** — shows the resulting market price and total quantity, completing the core diagram narrative.
7. **Identifies government revenue and additional welfare effects** — moves from market outcomes to distributional consequences, which is the final analytical layer.

**Why this order:** each move supplies the necessary input for the next — you cannot state effects without first defining the baseline and the shock; you cannot show price changes before quantity changes because the quantity shifts are what push the price. A student replicating this with any tariff or tax topic would follow the same sequence: justify → set up diagram → before → shock → direct effects → secondary effects → welfare/distribution.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON 1:** "[Justification phrase]. Therefore, to [policy objective], [Actor] has chosen to [policy action] on [target]."

1. **Slots:**
   - Slot 1 (justification phrase): a noun fragment naming the affected group or problem (grammatical shape: noun phrase, often plural noun).
   - Slot 2 (policy objective): an infinitive phrase stating the goal (grammatical shape: "to + verb + object").
   - Slot 3 (Actor): the institution or body taking action (grammatical shape: proper noun / noun phrase).
   - Slot 4 (policy action): the verb phrase describing the measure (grammatical shape: verb + adjective + noun).
   - Slot 5 (target): the specific good, country, or sector affected (grammatical shape: adjective + noun).
2. **How to fill differently:** Slot 1: name the group harmed (e.g., "local farmers"); Slot 2: state the protective goal (e.g., "to shield domestic producers"); Slot 3: name the governing body (e.g., "the EU Commission"); Slot 4: describe the trade measure (e.g., "has decided to levy anti-dumping duties"); Slot 5: name the imported good (e.g., "on Chinese steel").
3. **Original filled:** "producers. Therefore, to protect the domestic industry and avoid unemployment, China's Commerce Ministry has chosen to impose really high protective tariffs on Australian wine."
4. **Different fill:** "local farmers. Therefore, to shield domestic producers from cheap imports, the EU Commission has decided to levy anti-dumping duties on Chinese steel."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Cause / Consequence:**
- "Therefore, to protect the domestic industry" — "Therefore" signals that the tariff action follows as a consequence of the problem named.
- "Hence, imports fall from Q1Q4→Q2Q3" — "Hence" signals that the quantity shift is a direct consequence of the tariff.
- "As a result, the price of wine increases from PA+subsidy→PA+tariff" — "As a result" signals that the price change is a consequence of the volume shifts.
- "resulting in extra cost to Australian producers" — "resulting in" signals an immediate consequence of the tariff imposition.

**Sequencing:**
- "Before the tariff imposition" — anchors the baseline in time before the intervention.
- "After '... a year-long investigation ...'" — anchors the intervention in time after the investigation.

**Addition / Specification:**
- "and additionally, there occur" — signals that further effects beyond government revenue are about to be named.

**Mechanism / Description verbs:**
- "is imposed on imports" — "is imposed" names the mechanism of the policy.
- "was being demanded and supplied" — "was being demanded and supplied" describes the market state at equilibrium.
- "is shown below" — "is shown" directs the reader to visual evidence.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern name:** Before-and-after causal chain — a diagram-driven explanation that moves from baseline → shock → direct effects → secondary effects → distributional consequences.

**Step-by-step instructions to explain a NEW idea with the same pattern:**

1. **State the intervention and its justification** — name the policy action, who enacted it, and why, using a "Therefore, to [objective], [Actor] has [action]" structure.
2. **Set up the diagram reference** — write a transition sentence that tells the reader a visual shows the effects (e.g., "The effect of [X] is shown below:").
3. **Define the pre-intervention baseline** — state the starting equilibrium price and quantities, splitting total quantity into domestic and imported shares using "Before [X], [Q] was being [verb] at [P], where [domestic] was [verb] and [import] was [verb]."
4. **Introduce the shock with its magnitude** — name the policy rate and its origin, using "After [context], [magnitude] is [imposed/removed] on [target], resulting in [immediate effect]."
5. **Trace the direct quantity effects** — show how imports and domestic supply/demand shift using "Hence, [variable] [direction] from [start]→[end], and [variable] [direction] from [start]→[end]."
6. **Trace the secondary price and quantity effects** — show the resulting market price and total quantity using "As a result, [metric] [direction] from [start]→[end], and [metric] [direction] from [start]→[end]."
7. **Identify government revenue and additional welfare effects** — name who gains or loses (tax revenue, deadweight loss) using "The [actor] receives [benefit]; and additionally, there occur [further effects]."
