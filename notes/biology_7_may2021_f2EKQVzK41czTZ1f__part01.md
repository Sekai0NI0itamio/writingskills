## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1 — Independent / Dependent variables**

1. Move: *Identify independent variable, naming levels.* Quote: "Independent variables: concentrations of microalgae (Chlorella zofingiensis) extract". This hands to the next by leaving the *amounts* unstated — the reader needs the doses, so a parenthetical listing of percentages is the only way to finish the sentence.
2. Move: *Specify the three treatment levels as data.* Quote: "(0.5%, 0.1%, 0.01%)". Hands off by ending the independent-variable block; nothing more to add about inputs, so the paragraph must now announce what those inputs act *on*.
3. Move: *Announce the dependent variable category.* Quote: "Dependent variable: the height (cm)". Hands off by implying a co-measurement — if height is measured, weight will be too, so the next clause simply lists it.
4. Move: *Add the second dependent measure with unit.* Quote: "and the fresh weight (gr)". Hands off by closing the variables block — having named inputs and outputs, the reader is primed for the *third* condition of the experiment, the things held constant.

**Paragraph 2 — Controlled Variables (bullet list, not prose)**

1. Move: *Section heading announces the category.* Quote: "Controlled Variables:". Hands off by inviting a list — the next sentence cannot be a claim, it must be the first held factor.
2. Move: *First controlled factor: water.* Quote: "amount of water applied to plants via drip irrigation was constant". Hands off by establishing that *inputs* are controlled; the next factor naturally extends to the *medium*.
3. Move: *Second controlled factor: soil source.* Quote: "The soil used was provided from the greenhouse". Hands off by moving from *delivery* to *dosage* — logical adjacency shifts from environment to application.
4. Move: *Third controlled factor: treatment volume, frequency, duration.* Quote: "10 ml per week over the course of 3 weeks". Hands off because duration of application implies duration of the whole experiment — the next item closes that loop.
5. Move: *Fourth controlled factor: experiment length for all groups.* Quote: "the course of the experiment was identical to all treated groups". Hands off by ending on *time*; the last item logically closes on *replication*, which is the remaining design safeguard.
6. Move: *Fifth controlled factor: sample size.* Quote: "a constant number of 10 repetitions per treatment; overall a population of 50 plants". Hands off because replication defines the statistical unit; the next section must now supply the *physical means* to carry out the design.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is the **Variables and Apparatus section** of an IB Biology IA. It must perform, in order:

1. **State independent variables with exact levels.** Sets up what is being manipulated — every later variable (dependent, controlled) is defined *relative to* this manipulation.
2. **State dependent variables with units.** Only makes sense *after* the IV, because the DV is "what changes in response to" the IV.
3. **State controlled variables (medium → dosage → time → replication).** Only meaningful *after* IV and DV exist — controlled variables exist to isolate the IV→DV relationship. Ordering moves from *external environment* inward to *temporal and statistical safeguards*.
4. **List apparatus in functional order: workspace → subjects → solvents → treatments → preparation vessels → precise liquid tools → application/recording → growing medium → pots → small accessories → measurement → handling → closing volumetric check.** Only possible *after* the variables are fixed, because each tool corresponds to a variable (e.g., pipettes → precise dosage of treatment; ruler → height measurement; scale → fresh weight).

The WHY of the order: variables establish the *logical design* (what is tested, what is measured, what is held equal) before apparatus establishes the *physical means*. Apparatus then walks the reader through the *experiment as it would be performed*: set up the table, get the plants, mix the solutions, apply them, grow them, measure them.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON 1 — IV/DV paragraph**

SKELETON: "Independent variables: [name of manipulated factor with species/strain in parentheses] ([level 1], [level 2], [level 3]). Dependent variable: [measure 1 with unit] and [measure 2 with unit]."

1. *Slot 1 — IV name:* grammatical shape = singular noun phrase, optionally with taxonomic binomial in parentheses. Fill instruction: pick the substance or condition you will vary; identify the species/strain if it is biological.
2. *Slot 2 — IV levels:* grammatical shape = parenthetical comma-separated list of numeric or categorical levels. Fill instruction: choose three doses, concentrations, or categories — three is the IB-conventional minimum.
3. *Slot 3 — DV measures:* grammatical shape = two noun phrases joined by "and", each carrying its unit in parentheses. Fill instruction: choose one morphometric/physical measure and one mass/length-type measure that respond to the IV.

*Original fill:* "Independent variables: concentrations of microalgae (Chlorella zofingiensis) extract (0.5%, 0.1%, 0.01%). Dependent variable: the height (cm) and the fresh weight (gr)."

**SKELETON 2 — Controlled Variables list**

SKELETON: "Controlled Variables: [environment/delivery]; [growth medium]; [dosage and frequency over duration]; [experiment duration for all groups]; [sample size per group]; [total population]."

1. *Slot 1 — environment:* grammatical shape = noun phrase describing the delivery system. Fill instruction: name the way the treatment reaches the organism (irrigation, misting, injection).
2. *Slot 2 — medium:* grammatical shape = noun phrase naming the substrate and its source. Fill instruction: identify the soil, agar, or solution and where it came from.
3. *Slot 3 — dosage/frequency/duration:* grammatical shape = "[volume] per [time unit] over the course of [duration]". Fill instruction: state exact volume, frequency, and total duration of treatment application.
4. *Slot 4 — experiment duration:* grammatical shape = clause stating equal time for all groups. Fill instruction: assert parity of timing.
5. *Slot 5 — replication:* grammatical shape = "a constant number of [n] repetitions per treatment; overall a population of [n × groups]". Fill instruction: state replicates per group and the total.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / enumeration**
- *Colon after category header* — "Independent variables: concentrations of microalgae…" — used to launch a labelled set.
- *Comma-separated parenthetical list* — "(0.5%, 0.1%, 0.01%)" — used to exhaustively enumerate levels.
- *Bulleted lines beginning with a dash* — "- Irrigation; amount of water applied…" — used to mark discrete controlled factors.

**Specification / precision**
- *Numerical tolerances with ±* — "100 ml graduated cylinder ± 0.5 ml" — used to attach instrument precision to each measuring tool.
- *Parenthetical units after a measure* — "the height (cm)" — used to bind unit to quantity.
- *Parenthetical provider with credential* — "provided by Dr. Eran Barak which specializes in Innovative Plant Nutrition" — used to authenticate biological/chemical inputs.

**Evidence handling / sourcing**
- *Source attribution through "provided by"* — "provided by Dr. Eran Barak" — used to trace the treatment to a credentialed supplier.
- *Honest limitation flag in parentheses* — "(no label was provided)" — used to disclose a missing identifier.

**Causal / logical connectors**
- *Semi-colon linking parallel controlled statements* — "controlled by an irrigation system" / "provided from the greenhouse" — used to chain related specifications within one bullet.
- *Final "overall" summary clause* — "overall a population of 50 plants" — used to collapse the per-group figure into the total.

**Categorisation verbs**
- *"defined as / identified as"* — implicit through "Independent variables:" and "Dependent variable:" headings; the colon *is* the categorisation device.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The dominant explanation pattern in this section is **DESIGN DECLARATION → LOGICAL ORDERING → EQUIPMENT-IN-USE ORDERING** (i.e., *here are the conceptual pieces of the experiment, and here are the physical pieces, in the order they will be touched*). It is not a *definition → unpack → example* pattern; it is a *structural enumeration* pattern whose internal logic is *what affects what*, then *what you grab first when you walk in the door*.

To replicate this pattern for any new IA Variables & Apparatus section:

1. **Name the independent variable in one noun phrase and append its exact levels in a parenthetical list of three.** This sets the manipulation; every later decision hangs from it.
2. **Name the dependent variable as two measures joined by "and", each with its unit in parentheses.** The DV only makes sense once the IV is fixed.
3. **Open a "Controlled Variables:" heading and list, in this fixed sequence: (a) delivery/environment, (b) substrate, (c) dosage + frequency + duration of treatment, (d) equal experiment duration for all groups, (e) replicates per group + total population.** This sequence moves from outer environment inward to statistical safeguard.
4. **Open an "Apparatus:" heading and list tools in the order they will be physically encountered: workspace → subjects → solvent → treatments (with provider + credential) → bulk preparation vessels → precise volumetric tools (each with ± tolerance) → application tool → recording tools → substrate → pots/containers → small accessories → measurement tools (each with ± tolerance, plus any labelling gap) → handling tools → closing volumetric tool.** This sequence mirrors the *chronology of use*, which lets the reader mentally rehearse the experiment.
5. **Attach a tolerance (± value) to every measuring device and a credential to every biological/chemical input supplied by another person.** These small annotations are the section's mechanism for demonstrating rigour.
6. **End the apparatus list on a volumetric tool that bookends the earlier one.** This gives the list a satisfying closure and signals the experiment is ready to run.
