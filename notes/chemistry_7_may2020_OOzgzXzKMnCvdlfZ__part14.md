## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

The bibliography operates as a single ordered list rather than discursive paragraphs, but its **grouping sequence** carries a hidden logic.

**Group 1 — Primary data + procedural source (entries 1–2)**
- Move 1 (data anchor): "*24-hour Time Series of Mean Sea Level Pressure in Hong Kong*" — establishes the **original empirical source** the investigation draws from (weather readings). Hands the reader on because a data point alone needs procedural backing for replication.
- Move 2 (procedural anchor): "*Decomposition of Hydrogen Peroxide by Various Catalysts*" — supplies the **experimental method** the student followed. Hands forward by pivoting from "what was measured" to "what was performed."

**Group 2 — Background textbook (entry 3)**
- Move 3 (curricular authority): "*Horner, Mark, et al. Physical Sciences Grade 12*" — gives the **canonical school-level framing** of the chemistry involved. Hands on because curriculum framing must now be deepened by specialist literature.

**Group 3 — Specialist catalysis literature (entries 4–6, 8)**
- Move 4 (historical mechanism paper): "*The catalytic decomposition of hydrogen peroxide by ferric perchlorate*" — provides **early kinetic data** on the same reaction.
- Move 5 (advanced thesis): "*KINETICS OF THE Fe(III) INITIATED DECOMPOSITION OF …*" — escalates to a **higher-level investigation** of the Fe³⁺ pathway.
- Move 6 (modern applied paper): "*Catalytic Decomposition of Hydrogen Peroxide on Iron Oxide: Kinetics, Mechanism, and Implications*" — supplies **current mechanistic theory**, completing the depth-of-field on the topic. Hands forward because mechanism theory now requires a safety layer.

**Group 4 — Safety resource (entry 7)**
- Move 7 (risk control): "*CLEAPSS HazCards*" — inserts the **health-and-safety authority** that justifies lab conduct.

**Group 5 — Foundational and supplementary texts (entries 9–10)**
- Move 8 (foundational theory): "*Pauling, L. C. … General Chemistry*" — anchors **underlying chemistry principles**.
- Move 9 (chapter-specific reference): "*Chapter 15: Reaction Rates and Chemical Equlibrium*" — specifies the **reaction-rate subsection** the supplemental manual supports, closing the reference set.

(Hand-off logic across groups: **empirical → procedural → curricular → specialist → safety → foundational** — each group narrows or broadens the evidentiary base the previous one opened.)

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

A bibliography in IB coursework performs a **credibility triangulation** move-list. The standard order is:

1. **Primary empirical source first** — the raw data the student personally extracted (e.g. weather readings, school lab measurements). Sets up: "the experiment was real."
2. **Procedural / methodology source** — the lab sheet or protocol that standardises the method. Sets up: "the method was repeatable."
3. **General textbook** — the syllabus-aligned reference for background theory. Sets up: "the chemistry is curriculum-legitimate."
4. **Specialist academic papers (chronological or thematic)** — peer-reviewed work that deepens the same reaction or phenomenon. Sets up: "the student went beyond the textbook."
5. **Safety authority** — institutional hazard guidance. Sets up: "the risk assessment is externally validated."
6. **Foundational chemistry reference** — a standard general-chemistry text that anchors the molecular-level explanation. Sets up: "the mechanism is theoretically grounded."

The WHY of this order: each tier **converts one possible examiner objection into a citation** — *where did the data come from? how did you do it? is this chemistry right? is this chemistry deep? was it safe? does the mechanism make sense?* Moving from concrete-to-abstract keeps the bibliography readable instead of alphabetically chaotic.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A — Web data source:**
`"[Source Title describing the dataset]. (n.d.). Retrieved [Month Day, Year], from [full URL]."`

1. **Slot 1** — Source title naming the dataset; noun phrase in title case.
2. **Slot 2** — "Retrieved" date plus access date; establishes currency.
3. **Slot 3** — Direct URL; full link for verification.
4. **HOW to fill with a different idea:** Pick a free public dataset (e.g. "Daily Solar Radiation in Singapore"), note its publisher's website, give the date you actually opened the page, paste the URL exactly as it appears.
5. **Original filled version:** *"24-hour Time Series of Mean Sea Level Pressure in Hong Kong. (n.d.). Retrieved November 10, 2019, from https://www.hko.gov.hk/wxinfo/ts/display_element_pp_e.htm."*
6. **Demonstration fill (different idea):** *"Daily Average PM2.5 Concentration in London. (n.d.). Retrieved March 4, 2023, from https://londonair.org.uk/LondonAir/Default.aspx."*

**SKELETON B — Peer-reviewed journal article:**
`[Authors]. ([Year]). [Article title in sentence case]. [Journal Name in italics], [Volume]([Issue]), [Page range]. doi: [DOI]`

1. **Slot 1** — Author surname-initials list, et al. if >2.
2. **Slot 2** — Year of publication in parentheses.
3. **Slot 3** — Title of the article; ends without period.
4. **Slot 4** — Journal name (italics), volume(issue), pages.
5. **Slot 5** — DOI string for permanent lookup.
6. **HOW to fill:** Find a journal article on the topic's mechanism; copy authors, year, title, journal vol/issue/pages exactly; paste DOI from the article's metadata.
7. **Original:** *"Kremer, M. L., & Stein, G. (1958). The catalytic decomposition of hydrogen peroxide by ferric perchlorate. Transactions of the Faraday Society, 55, 959. doi: 10.1039/tf9595500959"*
8. **Demo fill:** *"Smith, J. R., & Patel, A. (2014). Kinetics of iodide oxidation by peroxydisulfate in aqueous media. Journal of Physical Chemistry A, 118(22), 3841–3850. doi: 10.1021/jp500772x"*

**SKELETON C — Institutional safety / guideline resource:**
`[Acronym] ([Year]). [Resource Name]. [Domain]. Available at: [URL] [Accessed Day Month Year].`

1. **Slot 1** — Issuing-body acronym and publication year.
2. **Slot 2** — Resource title in italics.
3. **Slot 3** — Domain (no protocol).
4. **Slot 4** — "Available at" URL plus bracketed access date.
5. **HOW to fill:** Identify the safety body relevant to your experiment (CLEAPSS, HSE, NIOSH), pull the exact resource title, record the access date.
6. **Original:** *"CLEAPSS (2016). CLEAPSS HazCards. science.cleapss.org.uk. Available at: http://science.cleapss.org.uk/Resources/HazCards/ [Accessed 10 Nov. 2019]."*
7. **Demo fill:** *"HSE (2022). EH40 Workplace Exposure Limits. hse.gov.uk. Available at: https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/books/eh40.htm [Accessed 15 Sep. 2022]."*

**SKELETON D — Edited book chapter:**
`[Author]. ([Year]). "[Chapter title in quotes]." [Book Title in italics], [Edition], [Publisher].`

1. **Slot 1** — Author.
2. **Slot 2** — Year, chapter title in quotation marks.
3. **Slot 3** — Parent book title (italics), edition if not first, publisher.
4. **HOW to fill:** Identify the textbook that contains the chapter on your topic; quote the chapter title; note edition.
5. **Original:** *"Burns, R. (2003). "Chapter 15: Reaction Rates and Chemical Equlibrium." Study Guide and Selected Solutions Manual: Fundamentals of Chemistry, 4th Edition, Prentice Hall."*
6. **Demo fill:** *"Atkins, P. (2010). "Chapter 8: Electrochemistry." Physical Chemistry, 9th Edition, Oxford University Press."*

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

A bibliography has **no connectives** in the discursive-essay sense; its "logic" is signalled by punctuation, ordering, and three expression patterns:

- **Source identification verbs / phrases** — used to label the type of resource:
  - *"Retrieved … from"* — citation verb: *"(n.d.). Retrieved November 10, 2019, from https://www.hko.gov.hk…"*
  - *"Available at"* — resource locator: *"Available at: http://science.cleapss.org.uk/Resources/HazCards/"*
  - *"[Accessed …]"* — access timestamp: *"[Accessed 10 Nov. 2019]."*
- **Authorship / authority signals** — establish who stands behind the claim:
  - *"et al."* — multi-author shorthand: *"Horner, Mark, et al. Physical Sciences Grade 12."*
  - *"doi:"* — permanent identifier marker: *"doi: 10.1039/tf9595500959"*
  - *"[On the catalysis of hydroperoxide]"* — translation bracket signalling original-language work: *""Über die katalyse des hydroperoxydes" [On the catalysis of hydroperoxide]."*
- **Sequencing signals via typographic order** — the bibliography's only "firstly / next" is **alphabetical by lead author surname** (Horner → Kremer → Kwan → Lin → CLEAPSS → Haber → Pauling → Burns), with anonymous corporate authors slotted by acronym.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The bibliography's underlying pattern is **claim → anchor → triangulation** — but applied not to ideas but to the **evidence an idea rests on**. To replicate the same evidentiary anchoring for a NEW topic, follow these steps:

1. **Name the phenomenon you investigated** in one noun phrase (e.g. "rate of iodide oxidation").
2. **Cite the raw dataset** you personally pulled — author/website, no date if continuously updated, retrieval date, full URL.
3. **Cite the lab protocol or method guide** that told you how to perform the experiment.
4. **Cite the syllabus textbook** chapter that gives the standard-level explanation.
5. **Cite 2–3 peer-reviewed journal articles** on the *same* reaction or a closely analogous one, oldest first if you want a historical arc, newest first if you want recency.
6. **Cite the safety authority document** (CLEAPSS, HSE, SDS) that legitimises your hazard handling.
7. **Cite one foundational general-chemistry text** to anchor molecular-level claims.
8. **Order entries alphabetically by lead surname** (or, for websites, by corporate author); preserve the punctuation pattern `[Author]. ([Year]). [Title]. [Source]. doi/URL`.
9. **Proof-check every DOI and URL** by clicking once — examiners verify.
