## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Context + Definition**: Establishes the article’s topic and defines key terms (government spending, expansionary fiscal policy) — sets up the analytical framework.
2. **Theory Explanation**: Explains the multiplier mechanism and AD/AS model — provides the economic model to be applied.
3. **Application to Policy 1**: Applies the theory to wage subsidies — demonstrates how the model explains real-world policy.
4. **Application to Policy 2**: Applies the theory to cash transfers — extends analysis to a second policy using MPC logic.
5. **Financing Consequences**: Introduces crowding out and monetary policy interaction — adds complexity and evaluates trade-offs.
6. **Sticky Price Analysis**: Introduces wage/price stickiness and compares policy effectiveness — deepens theoretical critique.
7. **Conclusion**: Summarizes short-run effectiveness and long-run caveats — synthesizes findings.

Each move builds on the previous: definitions enable theory, theory enables application, applications reveal consequences, consequences prompt critique, critique leads to synthesis.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern**: Definition → Theory/Mechanism → Application → Consequence → Caveat → Resolution

1. **Define** the key economic concept using a textbook-style quotation or formal phrasing.
2. **Explain** the theoretical mechanism using cause-and-effect language and directional arrows (e.g., AD1 → AD2).
3. **Apply** the theory to a specific real-world policy or example, citing concrete figures or details.
4. **Trace** the consequences through the economy, linking micro effects to macro outcomes.
5. **Introduce** a limitation, side-effect, or conflicting force that complicates the initial analysis.
6. **Resolve** or mitigate the caveat by introducing a complementary policy or contextual factor.

This pattern ensures ideas are grounded in theory, illustrated with evidence, and critically evaluated.
