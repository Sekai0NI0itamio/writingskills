## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **grouped descriptive analysis** section. The order is:

1. **Name the test performed** (outlier check) — sets up every statistic that follows as a *consequence* of that test.
2. **Describe Group A's distribution** with shape + centre + spread + IQR — gives the reader an anchor before comparison.
3. **Verdict on Group A's outliers** — closes the open thread from step 1.
4. **Describe Group B's distribution** with the same triplet of statistics, but flagged for its unusual structure ("median of the same value as the first quartile") — invites direct contrast with Group A.
5. **Verdict on Group B's outliers**, with named cases — gives the outliers a face so the reader cares about step 6.
6. **Justify the anomalies** ("by doing some research") — the *cause* move that explains the names just dropped.
7. **Zoom out to a combined view** — synthesis that puts both groups on one axis, which only *works* because steps 2–5 have already separated them.

The logic: **separate the two groups first, explain their differences, then unite them on a single scale** — so the unifying visual lands with contrast already loaded into the reader's head.

1. *Slots:* noun-phrase group label; shape adjective; three statistic-value pairs (centre, range, IQR-style).
2. *How to fill:* slot 1 = a clear binary category from your data; slot 2 = the literal shape word ("positively"/"negatively"); slots 3–5 = three summary numbers you have actually computed, named in the order *centre → spread → IQR*.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing:** "Lastly" (used in "Lastly, again to better understand…") — marks the closing move of the section.
- **Claim/evidence framing:** "it was found that" (used in "it was found that the distribution had a negatively skewed shape") — presents a finding as a settled result rather than opinion.
- **Specification / addition:** "Additionally" (used in "Additionally, a range of 9 years and an IQR of just 1 year were observed") — stacks a second layer of evidence onto the prior claim.
- **Contrast-by-comparison:** "with the median of the same value as the first quartile" (used in "the median of the same value as the first quartile, 82 years") — flags an *unusual* structural feature without naming it as a problem yet.
- **Verdict / evidence handling:** "being the outliers" (used in "the US and Saudi Arabia being the outliers") — names the cases the reader is about to see explained.
- **Cause / justification:** "to explain these cases" (used in "Different statistics were given to explain these cases") — converts a bare fact into a *motivated* investigation.
- **Purpose / implication:** "to better understand the distribution of the data in general" (used in "again to better understand the distribution of the data in general") — frames the next visual as answering a *stated need*.
- **Intensifier for shape claim:** "a very strong positively skewed distribution" — front-loads the *degree* of skew so the numbers that follow land as *evidence for* the intensity, not as surprises.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern is **split-compare-explain-unify** (grouped statistical description → anomaly justification → combined visualisation). To reproduce it on a new idea:

1. **Name the diagnostic test you ran first** so the reader knows *why* the numbers that follow are the numbers that follow. (e.g. "and tests for outliers.")
2. **Describe Group A** in one sentence that packs four moves: shape (adjective-skewed), centre (median), spread (range), and middle-spread (IQR), in that order. The order is *shape → centre → spread → middle-spread* because the reader needs the *qualitative* verdict before the numbers, otherwise the numbers float.
3. **Verdict on Group A's outliers** in one short, separate sentence. The brevity is the point: it tells the reader this group is *uncomplicated* and frees attention for Group B.
4. **Describe Group B** using the same four-move template, but flag any *structural oddity* (e.g. median = a quartile) in-line so the reader notices the contrast with Group A *without you having to say "in contrast"*.
5. **Name Group B's outliers explicitly** (1–3 named cases). Naming is what creates the *need* for the next move.
6. **Justify the named outliers** in a single short sentence that names (a) the *type* of source you used, (b) *what* you are explaining, and (c) the *action verb* of consulting that source. This is the cause move — it answers the question the names just raised.
7. **Zoom out** with a sequencer ("Lastly"), restate the *purpose* of the new visual, name the visual type, give the total N, and the variable name — in that order. The purpose clause must come *before* the visual, so the reader knows *what the visual is doing*, not just *what it is*.
