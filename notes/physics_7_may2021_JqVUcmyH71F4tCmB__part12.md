## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A — "Model announcement + paired equations"**
> "The equations for the lines of best fit for [variable pair] are the following: [Equation 1] [Equation 2]"

1. *Slot 1* (declarative clause): states that what follows is the fitted model — grammar: simple present, "The equations… are the following:" (colon introduces list).
2. *Slot 2* (display equation 1): a formula for the first variable — grammar: closed-form expression with constants.
3. *Slot 3* (display equation 2): a parallel formula for the second variable — grammar: same structure, different coefficients.

- **How to fill with a different idea**: pick two related measurable quantities (e.g. spring period and damping factor), generate a best-fit line/curve for each, then present them in the same one-line-intro + two-equation pattern.
- **Original fill (verbatim)**: "The equations for the lines of best fit for best fit are the following:" followed by Eq. (27) and Eq. (28).
- **Demonstration fill**: "The equations for the regression curves of oscillation period T and decay constant τ are the following: T(m) = 2.09 − 0.83·e^(−0.42m) + 0.014m ; τ(m) = 0.71 − 0.69·e^(−0.39m) − 0.0006m."

**SKELETON B — "Comparative verdict → cause open"**
> "As it can be observed, [variable A] suffers deviations of up to [max A] from our trials, whereas [variable B] suffers deviations as great as [max B]. This is because [mechanism]."

1. *Slot 1* (evidence-handling frame): "As it can be observed," — soft authority opener for a claim drawn from data.
2. *Slot 2* (magnitude + comparison): state the worse-but-tolerable deviation for one variable, introduced with "suffers deviations of up to".
3. *Slot 3* (contrast partner): "whereas [variable B] suffers deviations as great as [max B]" — pair with the much larger deviation.
4. *Slot 4* (cause pivot): "This is because…" — one-sentence mechanism that accounts for the gap.

- **How to fill with a different idea**: choose two measured outputs of an experiment where one is highly sensitive to initial conditions and one is robust; quote each worst-case percentage; then name the physical cause of the asymmetry.
- **Original fill (verbatim)**: "As it can be observed, the burnout velocity suffers deviations of up to 16.5% from our trials, whereas the maximum altitude suffers deviations as great as 205.0%. This is because the burnout…"
- **Demonstration fill**: "As it can be observed, the spring period suffers deviations of up to 4.2% from our trials, whereas the damping coefficient suffers deviations as great as 38.7%. This is because the period depends only on mass and stiffness, whereas damping is sensitive to the air-flow turbulence around the oscillating plate."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing / introduction**: *"are the following"* — "The equations for the lines of best fit… are the following:"
- **Evidence handling**: *"As it can be observed"* — "As it can be observed, the burnout velocity suffers…"
- **Contrast / concession**: *"whereas"* — "…whereas the maximum altitude suffers deviations as great as 205.0%."
- **Cause / consequence**: *"This is because"* — "This is because the burnout…" (introduces mechanism)
- **Magnitude specification**: *"suffers deviations of up to"* — "suffers deviations of up to 16.5%"
- **Magnitude specification (escalation)**: *"as great as"* — "suffers deviations as great as 205.0%"

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

Pattern: **data evidence → paired model display → comparative verdict → cause chain**.

1. **Display the evidence** in a numbered table showing paired variables and their deviation percentages.
2. **Cite the visual fit** (figure caption) so the reader knows a model exists.
3. **Announce the model**: one sentence — "The equations for the lines of best fit for [X] are the following:" — followed by the two closed-form equations displayed in sequence (variable A, then variable B).
4. **Issue a comparative verdict** by extracting the worst-case deviation of each variable from the table, using "suffers deviations of up to" for the milder one and "suffers deviations as great as" for the worse one, joined by "whereas".
5. **Open the cause chain** with "This is because…" and name the physical mechanism that makes the second variable far more sensitive — keep it to one or two clauses before continuing in a follow-on sentence.
