## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a Table of Contents, so the content moves are structural, not argumentative. The order is:

1. **Major spine first.** Top-level chapters (Research Proposal, Introduction, Main Results, Analysis, Conclusion, Recommendation, Bibliography, Appendix) appear before any sub-entries. *Why:* the reader needs the overall skeleton before the sub-parts make sense — sub-entries are read as "this belongs inside that."

2. **Research Proposal unpacked before any other chapter gets sub-entries.** Research Question → Rationale → Theoretical Framework → Methodology → Anticipated Difficulties → Action Plan → Changes Made → Executive Summary. *Why:* the proposal is the only section with a built-in internal sequence (question → justification → theory → method → limits → timeline → revisions → summary), and listing it last in sub-form would orphan its logic.

3. **Other chapters left un-indented until Analysis and Discussion.** *Why:* Analysis is the only chapter that genuinely has multiple named tools (Cashflow Forecast, Profitability Ratio, Decision Tree, Force Field Analysis); the others are single-block chapters and listing fake sub-headings would clutter.

4. **Analytical tools listed before appendices.** *Why:* the appendices supply the *raw data* the tools operate on — listing tools first makes the appendices read as "evidence for the above," not as floating attachments.

5. **Appendices enumerated in the order they will be referenced.** Interview first (primary qualitative data), then the three cash-flow forecasts in chronological sequence (2019-20 → 2020-21 → 2021-22), then the customer survey last. *Why:* primary source first, time-ordered artefacts next, supplementary survey last — a *source-priority + chronology* ordering.

6. **Header band (course code + exam session) and page locator** frame the list. *Why:* a TOC must self-identify so it can be lifted out and still mean something.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

### SKELETON A: "Major-spine entry" (one line in a Table of Contents)
   `[Section Name] ........................................................................................................ [page #]`

1. **Slot 1 — Section Name:** noun phrase, Title Case, the canonical chapter title. **Slot 2 — Dot leader + page number:** repeated full stops forming a visual gutter, then a right-aligned Arabic numeral. The dot leader's job is purely spatial.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** pick the largest analytical unit of the document (e.g. "Methodology," "Findings," "Discussion"); write it in Title Case; count the page it will begin on; do not invent the page — match it to the actual draft.
3. **Original filled version:** "Main results and Findings ..................................................................................................................... 8"
4. **Demonstration fill with a different idea:** "Limitations of the Model ..................................................................................................................... 11"

### SKELETON B: "Sub-entry inside a parent chapter"
   `[Sub-component Name] ........................................................................................................ [page #]`
   indented one tab further than the spine line.

1. **Slot 1 — Sub-component Name:** noun phrase shorter than its parent, Title Case. **Slot 2 — Page number:** the page on which that sub-component actually opens. **Slot 3 — Indent:** consistent tab-in so the reader's eye groups sub-entries under their parent.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** choose a named sub-section that exists inside one of your parent chapters (e.g. inside "Findings": "Pilot Result," "Full Trial Result"); match its real starting page; maintain the tab indent.
3. **Original filled version:** "Profitability Ratio .............................................................................................................................. 12"
4. **Demonstration fill with a different idea:** "Sensitivity Analysis ......................................................................................................................... 13"

### SKELETON C: "Annotated appendix entry (number + colon + descriptor)"
   `Appendix [#]: [descriptor that names what the artefact is and where it came from] ........................................................................ [page #]`

1. **Slot 1 — "Appendix [#]:"** fixed label with Arabic numeral and colon. **Slot 2 — Descriptor:** noun phrase that does *three jobs* in sequence — (a) what the document is, (b) who made/provided it, (c) what time period or scope it covers. **Slot 3 — Page number.** 
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** number your appendices sequentially; in the descriptor, stack the three jobs — type ("A cash-flow forecast"), author ("made by me"), source ("based on the data provided by the owner"), and scope ("for the year 2019-2020"); verify the page.
3. **Original filled version:** "Appendix 4: A cash-flow forecast made by me based on the economic changes data regarding inflation and predicted change is online sales revenue for the year 2021-22."
4. **Demonstration fill with a different idea:** "Appendix 3: A break-even chart constructed by me using the variable cost data supplied by the operations manager for fiscal year 2022–2023."

### SKELETON D: "Inline metadata tag inside an appendix entry"
   `[Field name]: [Value].`

1. **Slot 1 — Field name:** a single-word label in Title Case followed by colon. **Slot 2 — Value:** a short noun phrase. **Slot 3 — Period:** closes the tag.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** identify the conventional metadata fields an appendix needs (Location, Date, Respondent, Sample size); for each, write `Field: Value.` on its own indented line directly beneath the appendix title.
3. **Original filled version:** "Location: His Work place."
4. **Demonstration fill with a different idea:** "Date of Interview: 14 March 2023."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

Because this is a TOC, the vocabulary is structural rather than argumentative.

- **Sequencing / ordering (implicit):** the section uses *positional ordering* rather than connectives — the list itself is the sequencer. The chronological stacking of Appendix 2 → 3 → 4 ("for the year 2019-2020" → "for the year 2020-21" → "for the year 2021-22") is the only inline time-marker and it does the work of *firstly / next / finally* without words.
- **Specification / narrowing:** the colon after each appendix number ("Appendix 1: Interview with the Co-owner, Mr. Tejas Choudhary") and the colon after field labels ("Location: His Work place.") — colons replace "*that is*" and "*namely*."
- **Source attribution (evidence handling):** "based on the data provided by the owner" and "based on the economic changes data" — prepositional phrases doing the job of "*according to*" and "*as reported by*."
- **Possessive authorship (responsibility marker):** "made by me" appears three times in Appendix 2, 3, 4 — replaces "*constructed by the researcher*" and signals primary vs. secondary source.
- **Definitional packaging:** every appendix title uses the pattern `[Artefact] + made by [author] + based on [source] + for [scope]` — a compact definitional formula replacing "*X is defined as Y*."
- **Implication / scope marker:** "for the year 2019-2020," "for the year 2020-21," "for the year 2021-22" — temporal scope phrases doing the work of "*covering the period*."
- **No contrast, no cause, no concession vocabulary is needed** — a TOC states, it does not argue.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section does not *explain* a concept; it **classifies and locates** content. The pattern is **identity → location → parent-child grouping → source/scope tag**.

Step-by-step to reproduce the pattern on a new IA document:

1. **Identify the spine.** List the 6–9 largest chapters of the IA in the order they actually appear (Proposal → Introduction → Findings → Analysis → Conclusion → Recommendation → Bibliography → Appendix).
2. **Assign real page numbers.** Walk through the draft and write down the page each chapter begins on; do not estimate.
3. **Decide which chapters need sub-entries.** Only unpack a chapter into sub-entries if it genuinely contains multiple named parts (e.g. Analysis often does; Introduction usually does not).
4. **Group sub-entries under their parent.** Place each sub-entry indented one tab further than its parent, directly beneath it, so the eye reads the grouping.
5. **Order sub-entries by their internal logic**, not alphabetically — by procedure sequence (question → rationale → method), by analytical tool used, or by chronology.
6. **For appendices, use the three-job descriptor.** In each appendix title, stack *what it is + who made/provided it + the time period or scope it covers*.
7. **Add inline metadata tags** beneath any entry that needs them (Location, Date, Respondent), each as `Field: Value.` on its own indented line.
8. **Frame the list** with a course/exam header and a page locator so the TOC is self-identifying.

The underlying pattern: *name the unit, point to its page, group it under its parent, and tag it with the minimum metadata a reader needs to decide whether to turn there.*
