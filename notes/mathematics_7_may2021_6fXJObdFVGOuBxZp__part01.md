## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This section executes a **paired-concept introduction** in this fixed order:
1. **Announce both concepts together** (signals they are linked and will be contrasted).
2. **Define concept A** with an equation, then immediately **unpack the equation into mechanism** (what each variable *does* physically), then attach a figure citation.
3. **Contrast-marker transition** into concept B, framing it as an **analog of a familiar concept** so the reader maps new onto known.
4. **Define concept B** with its own equation.
5. **Enumerate 2 special features** of concept B — one qualitative, one quantitative that links back to concept A (this is the linking move).
6. **Forward-reference** a later section that will explore the properties in depth.

**Why this order**: naming both concepts first prevents the reader wondering what is coming next; defining the simpler/mechanical concept (torque) before the more abstract one (angular momentum) gives the reader a concrete anchor; the contrast marker then makes the second definition feel earned; the property list + cross-link ties the two together; the forward reference keeps the section tight without losing rigor.

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

- **Sequencing**: *"I must first understand"* — sets the order of exposition.
- **Cause / consequence**: *"is the result of applying a force"* — turns an equation into a causal explanation.
- **Contrast / concession**: *"On the other hand"* — explicit paired-concept pivot.
- **Specification / enumeration**: *"There are 2 special features"* — signals an itemised list is coming.
- **Definition verbs**: *"defined as"* (appears twice — once per concept, anchoring both paragraphs).
- **Deferral / forward reference**: *"This will be explored and discussed"* — closes a section without losing the thread.
- **Analogy marker**: *"is the rotational analog of"* — converts an unfamiliar term into a familiar mapping.
- **Evidence handling**: bracketed numeric citations *[2], [3]* placed at sentence-end so they verify the whole claim.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section uses the **paired-concept introduction pattern** (announce pair → define A with mechanism → contrast to B defined by analogy → list B's properties → forward-reference). Steps to reproduce with a new idea:

1. **Name the phenomenon** you are opening with, then announce the *two* concepts the reader needs in one sentence (use "first understand X and Y").
2. **Define concept A** in the form *"[Term], defined as [equation] ([notation aside deferred to a later section]), …"* — embed a parenthetical that defers sub-detail so the current flow is not interrupted.
3. **Unpack the equation into mechanism**: write *"is the result of [physical action], where [variable] is [meaning]"* and attach a figure and citation.
4. **Pivot with "On the other hand"** to concept B and **frame it as an analog** of a concept the reader already knows (*"is the [domain] analog of [familiar concept]"*), then give its equation.
5. **Enumerate 2 properties** of concept B using *"There are 2 special features to [term]: [qualitative], and [quantitative with equation]"* — make the quantitative property **link back to concept A** so the pair is tied together.
6. **Close with one forward-reference sentence**: *"This will be explored and discussed in more detail in section X.Y."*
