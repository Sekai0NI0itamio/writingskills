## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This section performs **one function: it locates sources**. The sequence is:

1. **Alphabetical grouping by author surname** (sets up easy scanning by a marker looking up a citation).
2. **Within each entry, a fixed micro-order**: Author → Title (italicised) → Place of publication → Publisher → Year → (optional Volume/Part descriptor for edited collections).
3. **A second Stockwell entry placed after Roff**, breaking pure alphabetical order — this functions as a **topical cluster**: the two Stockwell works (1995 edited documents; 1979 monograph) sit together so a reader treating "Stockwell on the Malayan Union" can find both.
4. **The activist source (People's Constitutional Proposals, 2017) sits last**, separated by the page number "11" — this visually distinguishes a **non-academic/contemporary source** from the historical scholarship above.

**Why this order**: alphabetical-then-topical lets the marker verify citations quickly (the primary IB requirement for a bibliography) while still signalling that the student consulted a deliberate range of source-types (colonial records, regional histories, resistance studies, nationalist origins, contemporary political proposals).

**Replication rule** for another student with a different topic: list academic monographs alphabetically by author surname first; group multi-work authors together; place non-academic / contemporary / activist sources last with a visible separator so the marker sees the source-range.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**SKELETON A — Single-author monograph entry**
`[Author Surname], [First Name]. [Title]. [Place]: [Publisher], [Year].`

1. **Slots and shapes**:
   - Slot 1: Surname + comma + given name(s) (proper nouns).
   - Slot 2: Title in italics, terminated by a full stop (noun phrase).
   - Slot 3: City of publication (proper noun).
   - Slot 4: Publisher name (proper noun).
   - Slot 5: Four-digit year (numeral).
2. **How to fill with a different idea**: pick one book you actually used; copy its title page data verbatim; do not paraphrase place or publisher; keep the comma between surname and given name(s) — IB examiners check this.
3. **Original filled**: *"William Roff, The Origins of Malay Nationalism. Canberra: ANU, 1965."*
4. **Demonstration fill (different subject)**: *"E.P. Thompson, The Making of the English Working Class. London: Victor Gollancz, 1963."*

**SKELETON B — Two-author co-authored entry**
`[Surname 1], [First Name 1], and [First Name 2] [Surname 2]. [Title]. [Place]: [Publisher], [Year].`

1. **Slots and shapes**:
   - Slot 1: Surname + comma + given name of first author.
   - Slot 2: coordinating "and" + given name first + surname second of co-author (note: surname comes LAST for the second author only).
   - Slot 3: Title (italicised noun phrase).
   - Slot 4: Place: Publisher, Year.
2. **How to fill**: identify whether your source has two or three authors; if two, follow this order; if three+, place the first author surname-first and subsequent authors given-name-first, joined by "and" — never "et al." in a bibliography (only in footnotes).
3. **Original filled**: *"Barbara W. Andaya, and Leonard Y. Andaya. A History of Malaysia. London: Red Globe Press, 2017."*
4. **Demonstration fill**: *"John Keegan, and Richard Holmes. Soldiers: A History of Men in Battle. London: Hodder & Stoughton, 1985."*

**SKELETON C — Edited volume / part-of-series entry**
`[Editor Surname], [First Name], ed. [Series Title], [Volume descriptor]. [Subtitle of specific part]. [Place]: [Publisher], [Year].`

1. **Slots and shapes**:
   - Slot 1: Editor name + ", ed." (signals edited, not authored).
   - Slot 2: Series title + Volume number (noun phrase).
   - Slot 3: Subtitle stating what this specific volume/part covers (often a date range).
   - Slot 4: Place: Publisher, Year.
2. **How to fill**: for any source that is one volume inside a multi-volume series, you MUST include both the series title and the volume/part identifier; otherwise the marker cannot retrieve the source.
3. **Original filled**: *"A.J. Stockwell, British Documents on the End of Empire. Series B Volume 3, Malaya. Part 1: The Malayan Union Experiment 1942-1948. London: HMSO, 1995."*
4. **Demonstration fill**: *"Michael Crowder, ed. Cambridge History of Africa, Volume 8: 1940-1975. Cambridge: Cambridge University Press, 1984."*

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

This section contains **no connective or argumentative vocabulary** — by design, a bibliography carries no logical hand-off language. What it does use is **citation-punctuation vocabulary**:

- **Author–title separator**: comma after surname-given name (e.g. *"William Roff, The Origins"*).
- **Title–publication-data separator**: full stop after italicised title (e.g. *"A History of Malaysia. London"*).
- **Place–publisher separator**: colon (e.g. *"London: Red Globe Press"*).
- **Publisher–year separator**: comma (e.g. *"ANU, 1965"*).
- **Series/volume marker**: comma + numeral (e.g. *"Series B Volume 3, Malaya"*).
- **Date-range marker**: hyphen with no spaces (e.g. *"1942-1948"*).
- **Edition marker**: ", ed." for edited collections (implicit in the Stockwell 1995 entry's series framing).

There are **zero sequencing, causal, contrastive, or specification connectives** because the section performs no argument.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

This section **does not explain an idea** — it cites sources that do. Therefore the "explanation pattern" is not pedagogical but **evidentiary**: the section performs a *citation chain* that allows a reader to retrace the student's research.

Step-by-step replication for a NEW topic (e.g. "The Cuban Missile Crisis"):

1. **Step 1 — Identify the foundational primary source series** (analogous to Stockwell's *British Documents*): pick one edited documentary collection; cite as Skeleton C, placing the volume/part descriptor after the series title.
2. **Step 2 — Add the standard regional/national history textbook** (analogous to Andaya & Andaya): cite as Skeleton B; use the most recent edition because IB examiners expect up-to-date scholarship.
3. **Step 3 — Add a single-author specialist monograph on the specific event** (analogous to Cheah on resistance): cite as Skeleton A; choose a work focused tightly on your topic, not a broad survey.
4. **Step 4 — Add an origins/intellectual-history work** (analogous to Roff on nationalism): cite as Skeleton A; this provides the longer background.
5. **Step 5 — Add a second work by the same author as Step 1** (analogous to Stockwell 1979): cite as Skeleton A; cluster it visually near the first work of that author.
6. **Step 6 — Add a comparative/transitional overview** (analogous to Kratoska): cite as Skeleton A; this provides regional context.
7. **Step 7 — Add one contemporary, non-academic source** (analogous to *People's Constitutional Proposals*): cite as Skeleton A but place last with a visual break (page number, blank line, or heading); this signals the student consulted sources *beyond* academic monographs.

The underlying logic is: **breadth of source-type, ordered for retrievability, not for argument**.
