## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**Pattern:** *Cause‑Effect Chain with Evidence & Comparison*  

1. **State the mechanism** – describe the technical change (e.g., a tax shifting a supply curve).  
2. **Declare the policy goal** – say what the change is meant to achieve (e.g., move equilibrium to a socially optimal point).  
3. **Unpack the implication** – explain what the change means for observable variables (price ↑, quantity ↓).  
4. **Introduce a positive attribute** – claim a merit of the policy (e.g., speed, cost‑effectiveness).  
5. **Provide concrete evidence** – give a specific, quantifiable fact that backs the attribute (e.g., a timeline, a statistic).  
6. **Contrast with an alternative** – compare the policy to another approach to emphasize the advantage.  
7. **Add a concession** – use a contrast word (“However,”) to signal a limitation.  
8. **Elaborate the limitation** – explain why the drawback matters, using a reason or contextual detail.  

**Applying the steps to a new idea (e.g., a subsidy for renewable energy):**  

1. *Mechanism*: “The government introduces a per‑kilowatt subsidy for solar panels, shifting the supply curve downward.”  
2. *Goal*: “The aim is to lower the market price of solar energy to encourage adoption.”  
3. *Unpack*: “This means consumers will face lower upfront costs, raising the quantity demanded toward the social optimum.”  
4. *Positive attribute*: “A strong advantage is that subsidies can be rolled out rapidly.”  
5. *Evidence*: “The subsidy will be active within 2 months of enactment.”  
6. *Comparison*: “Compared to building new fossil‑fuel plants, this approach achieves emission cuts much faster.”  
7. *Concession*: “However, funding constraints may limit the scale of the subsidy.”  
8. *Elaboration*: “It is difficult to estimate the long‑run budget impact, especially as renewable technology costs are still falling.”  

Following these steps reproduces the logical flow demonstrated in the examined section.
