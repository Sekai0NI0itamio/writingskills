## Paragraph Flow (move by move)

**Paragraph 1**

1. **Sentence 1 — Specification/Evidence (list).** *"The temperature values that were tested were 328.0K, 333.0K, 338.0K, 343.0K and 348.0K."*
   Hands to next by **raising an implicit "why these?" question** — the reader sees the numbers and naturally asks what the justification is.

2. **Sentence 2 — Rationale/Unpack (cause).** *"These values were chosen as the high temperatures would decrease the time taken for the reaction to occur."*
   Hand-off by **partial answer + leftover gap**: speed has been explained, but reliability has not — the reader expects a second justification.

3. **Sentence 3 — Rationale/Extension (additive cause).** *"Furthermore, the use of five different values increases the reliability of any trendline that my results infer."*
   "Furthermore" **stacks** a second, separate reason (statistical reliability) on top of the first; paragraph closes because both practical and statistical reasons are now covered.

**Paragraph 2**

1. **Sentence 1 — Definition/Identification.** *"The dependent variable of this investigation is the time taken for 10cm3 of oxygen gas to be produced."*
   Hand-off by **naming the thing**, which forces the next sentence to explain the *mechanism* of capture.

2. **Sentence 2 — Mechanism/Apparatus.** *"This will be measured through a gas syringe with a bung connected to the boiling tube that the reaction occurs in."*
   Hand-off by **HOW-to-measure** answered, which leaves the reader asking *WHY this variable* — natural setup for the next justification sentence.

3. **Sentence 3 — Justification/Evidence (equation).** *"This dependent variable has been chosen as one can find the rate of reaction using the previous equation: r = k[H2O2][Fe3+]…"*
   Hand-off by **bridging variable → equation**: having shown that rate → k, the reader expects the next move to explain what k *does* in the wider plan.

4. **Sentence 4 — Implication/Connection to aim.** *"Calculating k is integral in plotting the graph of ln k vs 1/T, which can be multiplied by the negative of the gas constant to result in the activation energy value."*
   Closes the paragraph by **telescoping outward** from k to the graph to the final Ea value — the paragraph's logical destination.

## What This Section Does (content sequence)

This is a **variables-justification sub-section of a method**. The order is:

1. **Specify independent-variable values first** (concrete numbers). → Sets up the reader to need to know *why those numbers*.
2. **Justify the choice of values** (practical reason, then statistical reason). → Closes any doubt about selection.
3. **Name the dependent variable** with a measurable quantity. → Pivot from independent to dependent.
4. **Describe the measurement apparatus**. → Shows operationalising the variable is feasible.
5. **Justify the dependent variable via a rate equation**. → Connects the variable to a number that can be plugged into analysis.
6. **Tie the calculated quantity back to the investigation's aim** (here, Ea). → Shows the variable feeds the ultimate goal.

WHY this order: it moves from **what you controlled → why those choices → what you measured → how → why that measurement matters → how it feeds the final answer**. A reader can audit each choice only once the previous one is on the table, so the sequence is causally nested, not decorative.

Replicable general sequence for any variables section:
1. State the controlled values concretely.
2. Justify them in two layers (practical + scientific/statistical).
3. Name the dependent variable + its unit/threshold.
4. Describe how it will be captured (instrument + setup).
5. Link the dependent variable to a calculable quantity via a known equation.
6. Link that quantity to the section's ultimate purpose.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

### SKELETON A — Independent-variable justification paragraph

   SKELETON: "[The independent variable] values that were tested were [v1, v2, v3, v4 and v5]. These values were chosen as [practical reason linking values to speed/clarity]. Furthermore, the use of [N] different values increases the [statistical quality] of any [trendline/conclusion] that my results infer."

1. **Slot 1 — List of values (concrete, specific, numerical).**
   *How to fill:* Pick 4–6 evenly spaced values within a chosen range. State them with units, separated by commas, last joined by "and".
   *Original:* "The temperature values that were tested were 328.0K, 333.0K, 338.0K, 343.0K and 348.0K."
   *Demonstration fill (different idea):* "The concentrations of HCl that were tested were 0.10 M, 0.20 M, 0.30 M, 0.40 M and 0.50 M."

2. **Slot 2 — Practical justification, introduced with "as" (subordinate clause giving a speed/efficiency or visibility reason).**
   *How to fill:* Add one sentence beginning "These values were chosen as [extreme of range] would [make the reaction faster/slower/larger/smaller]."
   *Original:* "These values were chosen as the high temperatures would decrease the time taken for the reaction to occur."
   *Demonstration fill:* "These values were chosen as the low concentrations would slow the reaction enough for a colour change to be timed accurately."

3. **Slot 3 — Statistical justification, introduced with "Furthermore" (additive, about reliability/precision of trend).**
   *How to fill:* Add one sentence starting "Furthermore, the use of [N] different values increases the [reliability/precision] of any [trendline/gradient] that my results infer."
   *Original:* "Furthermore, the use of five different values increases the reliability of any trendline that my results infer."
   *Demonstration fill:* "Furthermore, the use of five different values increases the precision of any gradient that my results infer."

### SKELETON B — Dependent-variable definition + apparatus + equation-link paragraph

   SKELETON: "The dependent variable of this investigation is the [quantity + unit + threshold]. This will be measured through a [instrument] with a [connector] attached to the [vessel] that the reaction occurs in. This dependent variable has been chosen as one can find the [intermediate quantity] using the [equation], in which [specified conditions hold]. Calculating [intermediate] is integral in [next analytical step], which can be [final operation] to result in the [overall aim]."

1. **Slot 1 — Definition of dependent variable (noun phrase with threshold).**
   *How to fill:* State what is being measured, in what amount (e.g. volume, mass, time), and to what threshold (e.g. 10 cm³). End with a period that signals a closed definition.
   *Original:* "The dependent variable of this investigation is the time taken for 10cm3 of oxygen gas to be produced."
   *Demonstration fill:* "The dependent variable of this investigation is the time taken for 5.0 cm³ of hydrogen gas to be collected."

2. **Slot 2 — Apparatus/measurement mechanism (instrument + connector + vessel).**
   *How to fill:* Use "This will be measured through a [instrument] with a [connector] attached to the [vessel] that the reaction occurs in." Keep one clause per apparatus piece.
   *Original:* "This will be measured through a gas syringe with a bung connected to the boiling tube that the reaction occurs in."
   *Demonstration fill:* "This will be measured through an inverted measuring cylinder with a rubber stopper attached to the flask that the reaction occurs in."

3. **Slot 3 — Justification linking variable → equation (introduced with "as").**
   *How to fill:* State the rate (or general) equation explicitly, then list the held-constant conditions in a subordinate clause ("in which … are of [value] [unit]").
   *Original:* "This dependent variable has been chosen as one can find the rate of reaction using the previous equation: r = k[H2O2][Fe3+], in which the concentration of both H2O2 and Fe3+ are of 0.1 moldm-3."
   *Demonstration fill:* "This dependent variable has been chosen as one can find the rate of reaction using the previous equation: r = k[CH3COOH][CH3OH], in which the concentration of both CH3COOH and CH3OH are of 0.5 mol dm⁻³."

4. **Slot 4 — Telescoping implication (intermediate → analytical step → overall aim).**
   *How to fill:* Chain three actions: "Calculating [X] is integral in [Y], which can be [Z] to result in [overall aim]."
   *Original:* "Calculating k is integral in plotting the graph of ln k vs 1/T, which can be multiplied by the negative of the gas constant to result in the activation energy value."
   *Demonstration fill:* "Calculating k is integral in plotting the graph of ln k vs 1/T, which can be multiplied by the negative of the gas constant to result in the activation energy value."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / listing**
- *"These values were chosen as…"* — opens the rationale slot after the list of tested values.
- *"Furthermore, the use of five different values…"* — stacks a second, statistical reason on top of the first.

**Cause / consequence (subordinate "as")**
- *"These values were chosen as the high temperatures would decrease the time…"* — "as" = cause clause that justifies the preceding list.
- *"This dependent variable has been chosen as one can find the rate of reaction…"* — "as" = cause clause that justifies the named dependent variable.
- *"…which can be multiplied by the negative of the gas constant to result in the activation energy value."* — "to result in" = consequence clause closing the chain.

**Specification / quantification**
- *"in which the concentration of both H2O2 and Fe3+ are of 0.1 moldm-3"* — "in which" specifies the held-constant conditions of the prior equation.

**Explanation / link verbs**
- *"The dependent variable of this investigation is the time taken for 10cm3…"* — "is" = equative definition (announces identity).
- *"This will be measured through a gas syringe with a bung connected…"* — "will be measured through" = mechanism-of-measurement verb stack.
- *"Calculating k is integral in plotting the graph of ln k…"* — "is integral in" = significance verb marking the move from intermediate to analytical step.

**Evidence handling**
- *"using the previous equation: r = k[H2O2][Fe3+]"* — colon introduces a written formula as evidence supporting the chosen variable.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

The pattern used here is **CONTROLLED-LIST → JUSTIFICATION → DEPENDENT-VARIABLE DEFINITION → APPARATUS → EQUATION-LINK → AIM-LINK** — a *variables-justification chain* where each step earns the next.

To replicate it for any new experiment:

1. **List the independent-variable values.** Choose 4–6 evenly spaced points in your chosen range; write them as a comma-separated series with units and a closing "and".
2. **Justify those values practically.** One sentence beginning "These values were chosen as [extreme] would [practical effect]."
3. **Add a second justification (statistical).** One sentence beginning "Furthermore, the use of [N] different values increases the [reliability/precision] of any [trendline/gradient]…"
4. **Define the dependent variable.** Sentence pattern: "The dependent variable of this investigation is the [quantity] for [threshold + unit]."
5. **Describe the measurement apparatus.** Sentence pattern: "This will be measured through a [instrument] with a [connector] attached to the [vessel] that the reaction occurs in."
6. **Link the variable to an equation.** Sentence pattern: "This dependent variable has been chosen as one can find the [intermediate] using [equation], in which [conditions]."
7. **Telescope to the overall aim.** Sentence pattern: "Calculating [intermediate] is integral in [plotting/graphing], which can be [operation] to result in [final quantity]."

The mechanism: each step answers the unspoken "so what?" of the previous one — values raise "why?", justification closes it; the variable raises "how?", apparatus answers it; the apparatus raises "why this variable?", the equation answers it; the equation raises "what for?", the aim answers it.
