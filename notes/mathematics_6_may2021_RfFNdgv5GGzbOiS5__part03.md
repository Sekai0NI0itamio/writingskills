## What This Section Does (content sequence)

1. **Re-open the bias problem (move 1 of para 1).** The section never re-introduces the topic cold — it uses "Therefore" to inherit a flaw from the previous section. This sets up *why* a procedural fix is needed.
2. **Name the tool as the fix (still move 1).** Immediately after the bias, the writer names a specific instrument (www.random.org/lists). Order: problem → tool. This sets up the need to explain the tool.
3. **Walk through the tool's procedure for the FIRST group (para 1, sentence 2).** Pool size → platform → selection rule. Order: example-by-example, with the first group fully unpacked. This sets up the natural shortcut of "same again" for the second group.
4. **Apply the shortcut to the SECOND group (para 1, sentence 3).** "The same method was repeated" plus the mirror number (25) and the new pool (83). Order: full first, compressed second. This is efficient and parallel.
5. **Anchor back to the source ("provided by the World Bank Data").** Order: closes the methodology loop with an authority/source tag.
6. **Zoom out to justification + aim (para 2).** "Therefore" + sample size as proportion ("nearly ¼") + restated variables. Order: procedure → consequence → aim restated. The aim is restated AT THE END so the reader exits the section knowing exactly what the data will be used for.

**Why this order works:** Bias → tool → procedure-for-group-A → procedure-for-group-B → source → quantified justification + aim. Each move is a tighter ring around the same idea; the reader is never asked to hold more than one new piece of information at a time.

## Paragraph Skeletons (replicable templates)

**Skeleton A — "Bias-to-tool opener" (Paragraph 1, opening sentence)**

`SKELETON: "Therefore, to [remove the flaw X] while [doing the action] and to [give Y the benefit], [specific tool/strategy] has been used to [achieve the procedural goal]."`

1. **Slot meanings & grammar:**
   - Slot 1 (`to [remove the flaw]`): infinitive verb phrase naming the problem from the prior section (e.g. "to remove the bias", "to avoid a self-selected sample").
   - Slot 2 (`while [doing the action]`): -ing clause naming the main action being corrected (e.g. "while choosing", "while recruiting participants").
   - Slot 3 (`and to [give Y the benefit]`): second infinitive giving the *fairness* or *equivalence* reason (e.g. "to give every country an equal chance").
   - Slot 4 (`[specific tool/strategy]`): a concrete, named instrument (a website, formula, software, rubric) — the more specific the more credible.
   - Slot 5 (`has been used to [achieve the procedural goal]`): passive reporting structure ("has been used to randomly choose…") ending in the output of the tool.
2. **How to fill with a different idea:** Slot 1 — name the weakness of your previous step in 2–4 words (bias, subjectivity, convenience). Slot 2 — name the action that currently has the flaw. Slot 3 — name who/what benefits from fixing it (every participant, every case). Slot 4 — pick a real, named tool (a randomiser, a rubric, a formula). Slot 5 — name the output the tool produces.
3. **Original fill:** "Therefore, to remove the bias while choosing and to give every country an equal chance of being picked, www.random.org/lists - an online random sampling generator has been used to randomly choose 25 countries from each category."
4. **Demonstration fill (different idea):** "Therefore, to remove the subjectivity while grading the responses and to give every student an equal chance of being marked fairly, the College Board's official analytic rubric has been used to assign each response a score from 1 to 6."

**Skeleton B — "Procedure walk-through" (Paragraph 1, middle sentence)**

`SKELETON: "To do this, all [pool size] [items] provided were placed on [tool] which then [action the tool performs], and then [selection rule]."`

1. **Slot meanings & grammar:**
   - Slot 1 (`all [pool size]`): exact population number ("all 135 developing countries") — specificity is doing credibility work.
   - Slot 2 (`provided`): past participle attributing the source mid-sentence (often a dataset, list, or bank).
   - Slot 3 (`placed on [tool]`): past tense reporting verb.
   - Slot 4 (`which then [action]`): relative clause describing the tool's behaviour.
   - Slot 5 (`the first [N] were selected`): selection rule giving a numeric cut-off.
2. **How to fill:** Slot 1 — count the population you actually have. Slot 3 — pick a tool with a verb associated with it ("assorted", "shuffled", "ranked"). Slot 5 — decide your sample size before writing and state it as a number.
3. **Original fill:** "To do this, all 135 developing countries provided were placed on the online generator www.random.org/lists which then assorted them in random order, and then the first 25 were selected to be the investigation representatives."
4. **Demonstration fill:** "To do this, all 84 essays collected during the pilot were uploaded to Turnitin Draft Coach which then flagged any passage matching its database, and the first 20 flagged essays were kept for closer review."

**Skeleton C — "Parallel-procedure shortcut" (Paragraph 1, closing sentence)**

`SKELETON: "The same method was repeated to [action verb] [N] [items] out of [total], [source attribution]."`

1. **Slot meanings & grammar:**
   - Slot 1 (`The same method was repeated`): formulaic phrase signalling economy.
   - Slot 2 (`to [action verb]`): infinitive mirroring the verb in skeleton A's slot 5.
   - Slot 3 (`[N] [items] out of [total]`): compressed number pair showing sample/total ratio.
   - Slot 4 (`[source attribution]`): trailing prepositional phrase locating the pool.
2. **How to fill:** Mirror slot-by-slot from the previous procedure. Change only the category, the numbers, and the source. The compression ratio of this sentence vs. the previous one IS the point.
3. **Original fill:** "The same method was repeated to randomly choose 25 developed countries out of 83, provided by the World Bank Data."
4. **Demonstration fill:** "The same method was repeated to randomly select 20 short stories out of 47, provided by the school's internal reading list."

**Skeleton D — "Consequence + proportion + aim restatement" (Paragraph 2)**

`SKELETON: "Therefore, this method enabled me to [action] [proportion] of [total population] to study the correlation between [variable A] and [variable B] both for [group 1] and [group 2]."`

1. **Slot meanings & grammar:**
   - Slot 1 (`this method enabled me to`): first-person reflection linking procedure → purpose.
   - Slot 2 (`[proportion] of [total population]`): a fraction or percentage that justifies the sample size.
   - Slot 3 (`to study the correlation between [A] and [B]`): the research aim in one clause.
   - Slot 4 (`both for [group 1] and [group 2]`): a parallel "both…and…" tag ensuring no group is dropped.
2. **How to fill:** Slot 2 — calculate the fraction your sample represents of the total (here 50/218 ≈ ¼). Slot 3 — name the two IV/DV terms exactly as they appear in your introduction. Slot 4 — name the two subgroups in the same wording you used in the procedure.
3. **Original fill:** "Therefore, this method enabled me to investigate nearly ¼ of all the world countries provided by the World Bank Data to study the correlation between the 'Life Expectancy at Birth' of a country and its 'Health Expenditure per Capita' both for developing and developed countries."
4. **Demonstration fill:** "Therefore, this method enabled me to investigate roughly ⅓ of all the poems in the anthology to study the correlation between the use of enjambment and the reader's sense of urgency both for pre-1900 and post-1900 poems."

## Express-Idea Vocabulary

**Sequencing / procedural connectors**
- *"To do this"* — opens the procedure walk-through after the tool is named.
- *"The same method was repeated"* — compresses the second group's procedure.
- *"and then the first 25 were selected"* — chained temporal connector inside the procedure sentence.

**Cause / consequence**
- *"Therefore, to remove the bias while choosing"* — carries consequence logic from the prior section.
- *"Therefore, this method enabled me to investigate nearly ¼"* — consequence drawn from the whole procedure.

**Specification / clarification**
- *"an online random sampling generator"* — appositive that specifies the tool's category.
- *"both for developing and developed countries"* — "both for…and…" ensures no subgroup is omitted.

**Authority / source handling**
- *"provided by the World Bank Data"* — passive attribution embedded mid-sentence and again at the end of para 1.
- *"all 135 developing countries provided"* — passive + past participle distancing the writer from the selection.

**Explanation / mechanism verbs**
- *"has been used to randomly choose"* — passive reporting of tool → output.
- *"which then assorted them in random order"* — relative clause explaining the tool's mechanism.
- *"enabled me to investigate"* — causal verb linking method → scope of study.

**Definition / labelling**
- *"investigation representatives"* — labels the chosen units so they reappear later in the write-up.
- *"the correlation between the 'Life Expectancy at Birth' of a country and its 'Health Expenditure per Capita'"* — variables re-introduced in their original quoted form.

## How to Explain an Idea (replication steps)

**The pattern this section uses: PROBLEM → NAMED TOOL → UNPACKED FIRST INSTANCE → COMPRESSED SECOND INSTANCE → SOURCE TAG → QUANTIFIED JUSTIFICATION + RESTATED AIM.**

This is the "method-justification" pattern: a procedure is presented not as a list of steps but as a *response* to a flaw, then closed with a numerical justification of why the sample is sufficient.

To replicate it with a new idea:

1. **Open with a "Therefore" hook that points at a flaw.** Name the specific weakness of whatever you did before (bias, subjectivity, unrepresentativeness). Use the connective "Therefore" to inherit logic from the prior section.
2. **Immediately name the tool that fixes the flaw.** One sentence. Use a specific named instrument (a website, a software, a formula) — not a vague strategy. Pair it with a fairness reason ("to give every X an equal chance").
3. **Unpack the tool's procedure for the FIRST group in full.** Pool size → where the pool came from → what you did with the tool → the numeric cut-off. Show every step the first time so the reader knows the rule.
4. **Compress the same procedure for the SECOND group.** Use the formula "The same method was repeated to…" and supply only the changed numbers and source. The contrast in length between step 3 and step 4 IS the point — it shows consistency.
5. **Re-anchor the source at the end of the procedure paragraph.** A trailing "provided by X" closes the loop so the reader trusts the pool.
6. **Open the final sentence with "Therefore, this method enabled me to…"** then move to (a) the fraction the sample represents of the total, and (b) the research aim restated verbatim with its two variables. The "both for X and Y" tag must close the section to guarantee the reader exits with the two groups clearly held in mind.

The underlying logic path is: *flaw acknowledged → tool chosen → tool demonstrated once → tool demonstrated again → source anchored → sample size justified → variables restated.* A reader following this path never has to ask "why this method?" or "what is this for?" — both questions are answered in the same two paragraphs.
