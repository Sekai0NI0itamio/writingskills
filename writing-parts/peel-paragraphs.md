# PEEL Paragraphs: The Evidenced-Based Academic Skill

## Purpose
PEEL paragraphs ensure academic writing meets evidentiary and analytical requirements by structuring arguments logically. This framework forces the writer to state a claim, substantiate it with authoritative sources, and critically examine its implications. Ultimately, it creates cohesive, defensible prose that connects discrete ideas back to the overarching thesis.

## Structure / Moves in order
1. **Point:** Make and introduce the reader to your core argument.
2. **Evidence:** Back up the point with examples or academic sources (e.g., textbooks, journals).
3. **Explanation:** Explain how the evidence supports the point; critically analyse why, how, and what the impact is if the theory is or isn't applied.
4. **Link:** Connect this point to the next paragraph's argument or back to the main thesis.
*(Optional 5th move for PEELC):* **Critical Point:** Apply critical thinking to the literature (e.g., note limitations, methods, or recency).

## Sentence shapes with slot anatomy
*   **Point Shape:** "[Claim about X], which suggests that [implication]." — Slot 1 [Claim about X] is the core argument; Slot 2 [implication] is the consequence of the claim.
*   **Evidence Shape:** "As [Author/Source] states, '[quote or data]', demonstrating [subject]." — Slot 1 [Author/Source] is the academic authority; Slot 2 [quote or data] is the specific proof; Slot 3 [subject] is the topic area.
*   **Explanation Shape:** "This occurs because [reason], meaning that [impact on thesis]." — Slot 1 [reason] is the analytical mechanism; Slot 2 [impact on thesis] is the consequence for the overall argument.
*   **Link Shape:** "Consequently, [next point/main thesis], which requires further examination of [upcoming topic]." — Slot 1 [next point/main thesis] is the bridging concept; Slot 2 [upcoming topic] is the subsequent argument.
*   **Critical Point Shape (PEELC):** "However, [limitation/method/recency], indicating that [caveat]." — Slot 1 [limitation/method/recency] is the specific critical flaw; Slot 2 [caveat] is the adjusted understanding.

## Rules
*   **Do introduce your point explicitly:** The source mandates making and introducing the reader to your point before offering proof.
*   **Do use academic sources for evidence:** The source specifies backing up points with textbooks, journals, or specialist products.
*   **Do critically analyse in the explanation:** The source requires explaining why, how, and the impact of the theory being applied or not applied.
*   **Do apply critical thinking (PEELC):** The source instructs writers to apply critical thinking skills to the literature, commenting on recency, methods, limitations, or barriers to implementation.
*   **Do maintain paragraph length consistency:** The source stresses aiming for paragraphs to be about the same size across the whole piece of work for readability.

## Don'ts / Defect to fix
*   **Don't write overly short paragraphs (1-3 sentences):** The source warns these are unlikely to provide the required analysis and critical points, making the work 'clunky' to read.
*   **Don't write overly long paragraphs:** The source states they overload the reader with information and become hard to follow.
*   **Don't leave evidence unexplained:** The source dictates that evidence must be followed by explanation of how it supports the point; simply dropping a quote without analysis fails the PEEL structure.
*   **Don't treat the critical point as strictly final:** The source notes that the critical thinking component (PEELC) does not necessarily need to be at the end of the paragraph.

## Quality bar
1. *Explicit Point:* Does the paragraph open with a clear, introduced claim?
2. *Academic Evidence:* Is the claim backed by a textbook, journal, or specialist source?
3. *Critical Explanation:* Does the writer explain *why* and *how* the evidence impacts the argument, rather than just summarizing it?
4. *Effective Link:* Does the closing sentence connect the argument to the next point or the main thesis?
5. *Consistent Length:* Is the paragraph roughly the same length as other paragraphs in the essay (avoiding 1-3 sentence or excessively long blocks)?
6. *Critical Evaluation (if PEELC):* Does the paragraph comment on limitations, methods, or recency of the research used?
