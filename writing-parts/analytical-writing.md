# Analytical Writing

## Purpose
Analytical writing introduces an essay, establishing the relevance of the topic, summarising what is already known, identifying a gap or debate, and stating the writer’s aim and argument. It creates a coherent research space for the analysis that follows and signals how the essay will be organised.

## Structure / Moves (in order)

1. **Establish context and importance** – Show why the topic matters for the discipline or broader society.  
2. **Review prior work** – Summarise existing knowledge and findings relevant to the topic.  
3. **Identify a niche** – Point out a gap, controversy, limitation, or unresolved question in the literature.  
4. **State purpose and thesis** – Declare the essay’s aim, central claim, or hypothesis.  
5. **Outline method (if applicable)** – Briefly describe the research design or approach used.  
6. **Explain significance** – Articulate the expected contribution or implications of the work.  
7. **Preview structure** – Indicate how the essay is organized (e.g., “The paper proceeds as follows…”).

## Sentence Shapes with Slot Anatomy

1. **Importance to the discipline**  
   > `[X] is a key issue in / driver of / factor in …`  
   Slots: **[X]** (topic), **[relationship/role]** (e.g., “key issue in”, “driver of”).

2. **Summary of prior research**  
   > `Recent evidence suggests that …`  
   Slots: **[claim about existing knowledge]**.

3. **Gap or controversy**  
   > `Much uncertainty still exists about the relationship between …`  
   Slots: **[aspect lacking clarity]**.

4. **Thesis statement**  
   > `In this paper, I argue that …`  
   Slots: **[central claim]**.

5. **Purpose & aim**  
   > `The aim of this essay is to explore the relationship between [X] and [Y].`  
   Slots: **[X]**, **[Y]**.

6. **Structural preview**  
   > `The essay has been organised in the following way.`  
   Slots: (no additional slots; followed by enumeration).

## Rules (Do’s)

- **Establish relevance** – Cite disciplinary or societal importance (source: “establishing importance of the topic for the world or society”).  
- **Review literature** – Summarise what is already known (source: “referring to previous work to establish what is already known”).  
- **Identify a niche** – Explicitly state gaps, controversies, or limitations (source: “identifying a knowledge gap in the field of study”).  
- **State clear purpose/thesis** – Declare the essay’s aim or argument (source: “stating the focus, aim, or argument of a short paper”).  
- **Provide methodological outline** – Briefly describe approach when relevant (source: “describing the research design and the methods used”).  
- **Explain significance** – Articulate expected contributions (source: “explaining the significance of the current study”).  
- **Preview structure** – Signal essay organisation (source: “outlining the structure of the paper or dissertation”).

## Don’ts / Defects to Fix

- **Vague opening without relevance** – Fails to orient the reader; the essay’s rationale is unclear.  
- **No explicit thesis or purpose** – Leaves the reader without a guiding claim; weakens focus.  
- **Over‑detailed literature review** – Buries the niche; dilutes the essay’s direction.  
- **Omitting gaps or debates** – Undermines justification for the study; the writer appears uninformed.  
- **Promising more than the work delivers** – Raises expectations, leading to criticism of over‑reach.  
- **Neglecting key‑term definitions** – Creates ambiguity; readers may misinterpret scope.  
- **Using informal or personal tone** – Breaks academic conventions; reduces credibility.

## Quality Bar

1. The introduction clearly states the essay’s purpose and central argument.  
2. The importance of the topic is justified with disciplinary or societal context.  
3. A specific gap, controversy, or limitation is identified and explained.  
4. The essay’s structure is previewed, guiding the reader through subsequent sections.  
5. Key terms are defined or clarified where they first appear.  
6. The tone is formal, concise, and free of unsupported claims.
